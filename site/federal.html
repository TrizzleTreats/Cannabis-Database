Federal law, as it stands

Every statement here carries its citation. Dates matter more than usual on this page: two of these provisions change in December 2026, and one enforcement deadline falls at the end of it. Checked against the audited federal report in this project on 14 November 2026.

Scheduling is split

State-licensed medical marijuana and FDA-approved products are Schedule III from 28 April 2026, under a final rule at 91 FR 22714.

All adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. The broad 2024 rescheduling proposal was never finalised.

So "rescheduled" is only true of the medical side. A state's adult-use programme is in the same federal position it was in before.

Tax: 280E is lifted for medical only

Because Schedule III is outside 280E's reach, the deduction bar is generally lifted for state-licensed medical operators. Adult-use operators remain fully subject to it.

A business holding both a medical and an adult-use licence has to split its activity to take the medical treatment — the licence alone does not do it.

Hemp: the definition changes on 11 December 2026

PL 119-37 §781 took effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026.

After 11 December 2026 the definition moves to total THC and a 0.4 mg per container cap.

That cap is the provision most likely to change what a hemp product may contain. Anything written about hemp before 11 December 2026 needs re-reading after it.

Two deadlines to watch

31 December 2026 — USDA's enforcement of DEA-registered laboratory testing for hemp, delayed to this date.

11 December 2026 — the medical-marijuana appropriations rider runs through this date.

What there is no federal law for

Banking. There is no federal cannabis banking statute. Access to banking remains a matter of each institution's own risk appetite and federal guidance.

How states interact with a federal change

Some states follow a federal schedule change automatically or by board action — Idaho's §37-2702(d) is an example. Others block it: Tennessee's Public Chapter 789 does.

Whether a tribal medical programme counts as "state-licensed" for the Schedule III treatment is unresolved, and this project marks it so rather than guessing.
