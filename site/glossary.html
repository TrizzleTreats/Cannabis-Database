Glossary

The words this site uses, and what they mean here. Where a regulator uses its own word for something, the site shows that word too: the standard terms below are for searching across states, never a replacement for the regulator's wording. For federal law (Schedule I and III, section 280E, the hemp definition), see Federal law.

Reading the figures

Official source
A government website: a regulator, a legislature, a tax agency, an official data portal. News, blogs, law firms and legal-information sites are never a source of truth here.
Read, checked
The date a figure was taken from its source. Licence records carry the date the regulator's file was read; facts about the law carry the date the state's report was audited. A date is never the day this site was rebuilt.
Not yet confirmed
The research found a figure, but the audit could not confirm it from an official source, so it is not shown. The page gives the reason instead.
Not published
The regulator does not publish this item at all, for example a status or an address. An empty status is never read as Active.
Licence record
One row as the regulator publishes it. Some regulators publish one row per location or per licence term, and some include applicants, so a count of records is not always a count of licences. Each state page says which.
Active + Provisional
The default search: records whose standard status is Active or Provisional. Other statuses are one click away.
Issued, approved, licensed, operating
Different measures. A licence can be issued before a business opens, and approved before it is issued. They are never added together into one count.
County
The county the premises are in, with its source on every row. An independent city, such as Baltimore City or St. Louis City, is its own county-equivalent, never folded into the county of the same name. Connecticut uses its traditional eight counties.

Legal status (the home-page map)

Adult use and medical
Both are legal under state law. Retail sales may still be months away; the state's card says when.
Medical only
A comprehensive medical programme; adult use is illegal.
Limited medical (low-THC) only
A medical programme that state law limits to low-THC products; adult use is illegal.
No medical programme
Neither, beyond at most a narrow exception for CBD or low-THC oil.

The map shows state law only. Federal law differs, and tribal law governs on tribal land.

Standard licence types

Sixteen types, so one search can span every state. A licence that authorises several activities (a vertically integrated licence) is filed under its most consumer-facing one, and lists the rest beside it.

Cultivation
Growing cannabis, including smaller micro-cultivation licences (the size cap is kept separately).
Nursery
Producing immature plants, clones and seeds for other licensees.
Manufacturing
Making cannabis products: extraction, infusion, edibles, pre-rolls, vapes, packaging.
Retail
Selling to consumers or patients, including medical dispensaries and hybrid stores.
Delivery
Delivering to consumers or patients.
Transport & Distribution
Moving or wholesaling products between licensees. Distributors sell to businesses, not to the public.
Testing Lab
Testing products for potency and contaminants.
Microbusiness
Several activities (for example growing, making and selling) under one small licence. Not the same as a micro-cultivator, which only grows.
Consumption Lounge
A licensed place to consume cannabis on site.
Research
Licensed research and development.
Caregiver
A person licensed or registered to grow or supply medical cannabis for patients.
Event Organizer
A licence to run cannabis events (California).
Operator
A company licensed to run a licensed business on the licensee's behalf (Colorado).
Hemp Grower
Growing hemp, under a state plan or the USDA's own licence. A USDA licence is federal, not state.
Hemp Processor
Processing hemp or making hemp products.
Hemp Retail
Selling hemp products, such as hemp-derived THC or CBD, to the public.

Standard statuses

Ten statuses, roughly from most to least live. The regulator's own word is always shown beside the standard one.

Active
Licensed and in good standing.
Provisional
A conditional or provisional licence, granted before final approval to operate.
Limited Operations
Licensed, but allowed only some activities for now.
Pending
An application or renewal not yet decided.
Suspended
Temporarily not allowed to operate, including a temporary closure.
Expired
The licence term ended without renewal.
Inactive
Not currently in use, as the regulator records it.
Surrendered
Handed back voluntarily by the licensee.
Canceled
Ended administratively.
Revoked
Taken away as an enforcement outcome.

Business directory categories

Seventeen categories of businesses that serve the industry. A business that holds a cannabis licence itself is in the licence database instead. Each entry is also tagged Local, Regional or National.
