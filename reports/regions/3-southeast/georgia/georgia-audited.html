Georgia Cannabis & Hemp Research — Audited Final Report

Last audited: 24 October 2026. This is a separate fact-checking audit of the Georgia Cannabis & Hemp Research Parts 0–12 report (accessed 2026-10-24). The auditor did not write the original report.

Federal baseline — used as given, not re-audited. Scheduling is split: under the final rule at 91 FR 22714 (effective 28 April 2026), FDA-approved cannabis products and state-licensed medical marijuana are in Schedule III; all other marijuana, including all adult-use, remains Schedule I. The broader 2024 rescheduling proposal is NOT final. IRS 280E: Treasury has said rescheduling generally removes 280E as a bar for businesses no longer dealing in Schedule I/II substances (guidance forthcoming); adult-use activity remains fully subject to 280E. Hemp: PL 119-37 §781 takes legal effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026 to only the provisions excluding cannabinoids that are not naturally produced or are synthesized; the full total-THC definition and 0.4 mg per container cap apply after 11 December 2026. USDA has delayed enforcement of the DEA-registered-laboratory hemp testing requirement until 31 December 2026. The federal medical-marijuana appropriations rider is in force through 11 December 2026. No federal cannabis banking law has been enacted. Schedule III application to Georgia licensees: the federal rule's text on its face covers state-licensed medical marijuana, which includes Georgia's state-licensed medical programme, but whether a specific Georgia producer/dispensary that has not obtained DEA registration gets Schedule III / 280E treatment remains [VERIFY] — GMCC had, on secondary reporting, received no written DEA guidance, and no Georgia DOR guidance was located. All other marijuana in Georgia is treated as Schedule I for federal purposes. No statement in the original report was found asserting a contrary federal scheduling rule as law; where the original report stated a Georgia-specific Schedule III/280E conclusion for a specific operator, it has been downgraded to [VERIFY] in Part 2.

Method. Primary sources only for confirmation: Official Code of Georgia Annotated via the state's official public access site and bill pages on legis.ga.gov, Rules and Regulations of the State of Georgia (rules.sos.ga.gov), GMCC, Georgia Department of Public Health, Georgia Board of Pharmacy, Georgia Department of Agriculture, Georgia Department of Revenue, Georgia Secretary of State, court records for licence-award challenges, and official county, consolidated city-county and city sites/codes. Part 1 — Laws

1.1 Legal status — what is / is not legal

  • Legal (medical only, registered patients/caregivers): possession/purchase of medical cannabis products totalling ≤12,000 mg THC in manufacturer-labelled pharmaceutical containers, from a GMCC-licensed dispensary or a Board of Pharmacy-licensed independent pharmacy, with a physical or electronic Medical Cannabis Patient Registry card; out-of-state card recognised only if the holder has been present in Georgia <45 days. DPH: "Is marijuana now legal? No, marijuana is not legal in Georgia" (quoted via secondary summary of DPH FAQ) and "OCGA §31-2A-18 authorizes patients with qualifying health conditions and their caregivers to legally possess medical cannabis that contains up to 12,000 milligrams of tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)" — https://dph.georgia.gov/low-thc-oil-registry/patients-and-caregivers . Index, 2026-10-24.
  • Legal (hemp): hemp and consumable hemp products meeting the Georgia Hemp Farming Act (total delta-9-THC ≤0.3% dry weight, see 1.5), sold by GDA-licensed establishments to persons 21+, except retail hemp flower/leaves (banned), food products for sustenance, and alcoholic-beverage hemp products (see 1.5). Source: GDA Hemp Program https://agr.georgia.gov/hemp-program . Index, 2026-10-24.
  • Illegal: all adult-use/recreational marijuana possession, sale, cultivation, and home grow (patients included); smoking of medical cannabis; infused food products; any marijuana outside the registry/pharmaceutical-container rules; unlicensed hemp sales. No Georgia adult-use licence type exists in law — any such type in a bill is PROPOSED only (see 1.9, 4.6).
  • Federal overlay (fixed baseline, not re-researched): state-licensed medical + FDA-approved products = Schedule III eff. 28 Apr 2026 (91 FR 22714); adult-use = Schedule I; all other Georgia marijuana = Schedule I for federal purposes. Application to Georgia licensees [VERIFY]: the fixed baseline says "state-licensed MEDICAL marijuana" moved, which on its face covers Georgia's state-licensed medical programme, but industry/tax sources flag that DEA registration may be a further condition and GMCC reported (MJBizDaily, 7 Oct 2026) that it had "no written guidance from the DEA" on the registration process.   • Definition / measurement (current Georgia law, pre-federal-cliff): hemp = Cannabis sativa L. and parts/derivatives/extracts/cannabinoids/isomers/acids/salts with total delta-9-THC concentration ≤0.3% on a dry weight basis; "total delta-9-THC" counts THCA after decarboxylation (secondary: O.C.G.A. § 2-23-3.1 / GDA sampling rules — [VERIFY] section number). "Legal limit" = lesser of 0.3% or the 7 U.S.C. § 1639o limit. Crop over limit = disposal/remediation. Source: GDA Act PDF (above); LegalClarity hemp guide https://legalclarity.org/is-hemp-legal-in-georgia-laws-and-regulations-explained/ . Index, 2026-10-24.
  • State response to new federal definition / 12 Nov & 11 Dec 2026 dates: No Georgia statute or GDA guidance responding to PL 119-37 §781 / PL 119-103 §2019 was located in this run — [UNAVAILABLE/VERIFY]. GDA's hemp programme page (read 2026-10-24) still links "Miller Amendment" and rescheduling news items but states no Georgia implementation rule for the federal total-THC definition or the 0.4 mg/container cap. The fixed federal baseline therefore governs the forward risk: after 11 Dec 2026, full total-THC definition + 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply federally, which would make most current Georgia consumable-hemp THC products federally non-compliant even though Georgia's own 0.3% + milligram-cap framework has not (yet) been amended. Georgia's SB 33 hemp substitute (which would have added a 0.3% total-intoxicating-cannabinoid cap and synthetic-cannabinoid provisions) did not become law (see gaps §1).
  • Consumable hemp products — what is sold now (SB 494 framework): gummies, beverages, tinctures (oil-based), topicals, vapes/inhalables that meet testing/labelling/packaging rules. Potency caps (GDA Rules Ch. 40-32-5, secondary summaries — [VERIFY] current rule text for each): gummies ≤10 mg delta-9 THC per piece / ≤300 mg per package; beverages ≤10 mg total delta-9 THC per 12 fl oz, container ≤12 fl oz (Rule 40-32-5-.06); tinctures/topicals under the 0.3% total delta-9 concentration limit. 1.7 Seed-to-sale tracking (medical)

Part 3 — Local Rules

3.1 Authority — counties, consolidated governments, cities: which level does what

  • Georgia counties have home rule for purely local matters (Ga. Const.); cities have home-rule ordinance power; consolidated city-county governments exercise both city and county powers within their boundaries (subject to any remaining separate municipalities inside the consolidated area, e.g., Winterville/Bogart in Athens-Clarke, and unincorporated-area rules in Augusta-Richmond/Columbus-Muscogee/Macon-Bibb where relevant — [VERIFY] each charter for carve-outs).
  • State law reserves controlled-substance criminal law, medical-cannabis licensing (GMCC/Board of Pharmacy) and hemp licensing (GDA) to the state. Local governments may: (a) adopt lower local penalties for ≤1 oz marijuana possession enforced by their own officers in their own courts (the decriminalisation ordinances below — they do not legalise, do not bind county/state/federal officers, and do not change state penalties); (b) use zoning to regulate where dispensaries/hemp/CBD/smoke shops locate, including additional buffers, use classifications and special/conditional-use permits, within state buffers; (c) impose local business-licence/occupation taxes and local sales taxes; (d) impose moratoriums while studying zoning (time-limited, see 3.3). Local governments may not authorise what state law prohibits (e.g., adult-use sales, home grow) or license medical-cannabis production/dispensing themselves.

4.1 GMCC production licences

  • Class 1 (max 2): indoor cultivation + manufacture of medical cannabis/products. Holders: Botanical Sciences, LLC and Trulieve GA, Inc. (Trulieve Cannabis Corp. subsidiary). Fees: $25,000 application (non-refundable) + $200,000 initial + $100,000 annual renewal. Available capital: $1.5m (bond / irrevocable letter of credit / comparable surety per Rule 351-3-.01(4); MPP summary: $1.5m). Must be operational within 12 months or licence may be revoked (Class 2 rule text; Class 1 equivalent [VERIFY]). No new Class 1 window is open — cap fully issued; replacement licences only if a licence is revoked/surrendered or an additional licence is authorised under § 16-12-214 (§ 16-12-221). Sources: MJBizDaily 7 Oct 2026 https://mjbizdaily.com/news/georgia-medical-cannabis-continues-to-grow-rapidly-despite-lack-of-dea-guidance/618548/ ; MPP https://www.mpp.org/states/georgia/georgia-medical-cannabis-law-summary/ ; Rule 351-3-.01 via SOS PDF (above). Index, 2026-10-24.
  • Class 2 (max 4): indoor cultivation limited to 50,000 sq ft + manufacture. Holders: FFD GA Holdings (Fine Fettle), TheraTrue Georgia, Natures GA, Treevana Remedy. Fees: $5,000 application + $100,000 initial + $50,000 annual renewal. Applicant must be a Georgia corporation/entity, maintain a Georgia bank/credit-union account, show ≥$1.25m available cash reserves on application/award, submit production + security (24/7 video/intrusion monitoring) plans, and pass GCIC/FBI background review (owners/officers/employees: no disqualifying convictions except felonies >10 years old, non-drug-related, or expunged/pardoned — per § 16-12-212(b) summary). Revoked if not operational within 12 months of award. No person/entity may hold >1 Class 2 licence or an ownership interest in another licence type under the same part (ownership-transfer bar: no transfer within 5 years of issuance; later transfers need GMCC approval + transfer fee, per MPP). 4.2 Dispensing licences

  • GMCC retail dispensing licences: issued to Class 1/Class 2 production licensees for retail outlets; initially 5 per production licensee (30 total), +1 per licensee at 25,000 registry patients and +1 per licensee for every further 10,000 patients (§ 16-12-206(a)(2)). Current: 22 active dispensing licences (Sept 2026, see Part 0.3); licences are annual and non-transferable (§ 16-12-206 title). Dispensing licences must be dispersed throughout the state (statutory dispersal requirement). Fees set by GMCC by location/demand — [VERIFY] current schedule.

12.3 Impaired driving law and testing

Verbatim law — O.C.G.A. § 40-6-391(a) (Justia republication): “(a) A person shall not drive or be in actual physical control of any moving vehicle while: (1) Under the influence of alcohol to the extent that it is less safe for the person to drive; (2) Under the influence of any drug to the extent that it is less safe for the person to drive; (3) Under the intentional influence of any glue, aerosol, or other toxic vapor to the extent that it is less safe for the person to drive; (4) Under the combined influence of any two or more of the substances specified in paragraphs (1) through (3) of this subsection to the extent that it is less safe for the person to drive; (5) The person's alcohol concentration is 0.08 grams or more at any time within three hours after such driving or being in actual physical control from alcohol consumed before such driving or being in actual physical control ended; or (6) Subject to the provisions of subsection (b) of this Code section, there is any amount of marijuana or a controlled substance, as defined in Code Section 16-13-21, present in the person's blood or urine, or both, including the metabolites and derivatives of each or both without regard to whether or not any alcohol is present in the person's breath or blood.” Verbatim — § 40-6-391(b): “The fact that any person charged with violating this Code section is or has been legally entitled to use a drug shall not constitute a defense against any charge of violating this Code section; provided, however, that such person shall not be in violation of this Code section unless such person is rendered incapable of driving safely as a result of using a drug other than alcohol which such person is legally entitled to use.”

Per se vs impairment — correct Georgia statement: Georgia has no numeric per se THC blood limit (no ng/mL threshold equivalent to alcohol's 0.08). It has two different theories in same statute, and they must not be collapsed: (1) “Less safe” (§ 40-6-391(a)(2)): State must prove drug rendered driver less safe to drive. This is operative theory for most contested drug-DUI cases, and secondary DUI analysis states positive THC result is evidence of presence at testing, not impairment at driving. (2) “Any amount” (§ 40-6-391(a)(6)): presence of any amount of marijuana/controlled substance, incl. metabolites/derivatives, in blood or urine. This clause is subject to subsection (b) and has been substantially narrowed by case law: Love v. State, 271 Ga. 398, 517 S.E.2d 53 (1999), held former (a)(6) framework unconstitutional on equal-protection grounds because it criminalised mere metabolite presence without impairment for persons without a prescription, while legally entitled users faced an impairment test. Post-Love secondary analysis states mere presence in system does not automatically equal DUI guilt; State must prove marijuana rendered driver less safe. Current charging practice under (a)(6) as it stands after Love and later amendments is [VERIFY] in current case law and prosecutor practice — do not advise a client that (a)(6) is either dead or fully operative without that check.

Legally entitled users (incl. Georgia medical patients and lawful hemp users) are not immune: § 40-6-391(b) expressly says legal entitlement is not a defence, but adds “rendered incapable of driving safely” proviso for a drug person is legally entitled to use (other than alcohol). A Georgia registry card is therefore not a defence to a DUI charge, and hemp-derived THC (delta-8, delta-10, THC-O, lawful hemp) that impairs driving is within (a)(2). Secondary first-offence DUI penalty summaries (fine $300–$1,000, up to 12 months jail, ≥40 hours community service, mandatory risk-reduction programme) are secondary and [VERIFY] against current § 40-6-391(c) penalty text, which was not re-quoted in Run 2. Testing — what is actually measured: Georgia DUI chemical tests governed by O.C.G.A. § 40-6-392 (methods approved by GBI Division of Forensic Sciences). GBI DOFS Toxicology Section tests blood/urine/tissues for drugs, incl. cannabinoid confirmation by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry distinguishing delta-9-THC (psychoactive), 11-hydroxy-THC (active metabolite), and 11-nor-9-carboxy-THC (inactive metabolite, THC-COOH). Carboxy-THC can persist for days/weeks in regular users and is not, by itself, evidence of recent impairment. No roadside THC breathalyser with Georgia statutory threshold was located; officers rely on driving behaviour, field sobriety / ARIDE / Drug Recognition Expert observations, and blood/urine testing. This does not create a hemp-DUI defence: lawful hemp THC that impairs driving is still within (a)(2), and a test cannot distinguish hemp-derived from marijuana-derived THC.

12.6 Gun ownership conflicts for registry patients

Georgia state law — no patient-specific gun ban, but federal-prohibition bridge: Georgia law is silent on firearm ownership by medical-cannabis cardholders as such; Georgia does not require licence to own firearm, and since SB 319 (2022) a “lawful weapons carrier” may carry handgun openly or concealed in permitted places without Weapons Carry License (WCL). WCL still needed for reciprocity carry in other states. Georgia WCL application does not ask federal Form 4473 controlled-substance question. Georgia WCL disqualifier that imports federal law — verbatim, O.C.G.A. § 16-11-129(b)(2)(E): “No weapons carry license shall be issued to … Any person who is prohibited from possessing or shipping a firearm in interstate commerce pursuant to subsections (g) and (n) of 18 U.S.C. Section 922.” Related Georgia disqualifiers incl.: felony conviction (unless pardoned); pending felony proceedings; fugitive status; conviction of offence arising from unlawful manufacture/distribution of controlled substance; conviction of misdemeanour involving use/possession of controlled substance where person has not been free of restraint/supervision and free of second such misdemeanour conviction for at least 5 years before application. A marijuana misdemeanour conviction therefore has Georgia WCL consequence independent of any card. Federal prohibition — text unchanged by Schedule III: 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(3) prohibits possession/shipment of firearms by person who is “unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance.” ATF Form 4473 asks: “Are you an unlawful user of, or addicted to, marijuana or any depressant, stimulant, narcotic drug, or any other controlled substance?” with warning marijuana use/possession remains unlawful under federal law regardless of state medicinal/recreational legalisation. Secondary federal analysis states Schedule III does not remove medical users from § 922(g)(3) text, because statute reaches users of any controlled substance, incl. Schedule III drugs such as ketamine and anabolic steroids. No ATF safe harbour for Georgia medical patients was located in Run 1 — [VERIFY]. United States v. Hemani, No. 24-1234 (decided 18 June 2026, Gorsuch) — narrow as-applied limit, NOT blanket restoration (sourced context only; do not overstate): Supreme Court held it unconstitutional under Second Amendment to use § 922(g)(3) to criminalise in-home possession by regular marijuana user on status-only theory, with no showing of active simultaneous intoxication or dangerousness. Holding is as-applied and narrow: does not strike § 922(g)(3) on its face, does not address persons intoxicated at time of possession, does not address felony-conviction disarmament, and does not by itself answer Form 4473 purchase question, WCL question under § 16-11-129(b)(2)(E), or whether Georgia registry patient who is currently using medical cannabis may lawfully purchase from an FFL. Practical Georgia statement: Georgia patient faces live conflict — Georgia carry law does not itself ask about cannabis, federal purchase/possession law still contains § 922(g)(3), and Hemani narrows only status-only in-home possession prosecutions without danger/intoxication showing. No Georgia guidance reconciling Hemani with WCL issuance was located — [UNAVAILABLE]; patients must not be advised Hemani “restores” gun rights generally.

12.7 Pet and veterinary products

No FDA-approved CBD/cannabis product for animals, and no Georgia veterinary cannabis rule located: FDA has not approved CBD for any use in animals; pet CBD is sold as unapproved product, and veterinarians' ability to discuss/recommend CBD varies by state practice act. Secondary veterinary guidance states safest answer is product-, animal-, and dose-dependent, with reported effects incl. sedation, dry mouth, blood-pressure drops, ataxia, liver-enzyme changes, and interactions with anti-seizure, sedative, pain, and liver-metabolised drugs. Georgia food/feed bar (binding Georgia rule for animal products): GDA adopts federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act by reference and will not allow GDA-licensed establishments to make food/feed products that would be deemed “adulterated” under FD&C Act. Because FDA approved Epidiolex (CBD) as drug, adding CBD to human food or animal food/feed renders product adulterated under FD&C Act § 301(ll); FDA has granted GRAS status only to hulled hemp seeds, hemp seed oil, and hemp seed protein powder for human food, and those three have not received GRAS status for animal feed. GDA's position (2019 guidance, carried in Digital Library of Georgia record) is until FDA changes regulations, Department cannot licence or endorse production of CBD-adulterated food/feed products. Hemp-seed (no-CBD) animal product is different question from CBD pet tincture/treat — [VERIFY] on specific product's composition and GDA Food Safety position. Georgia veterinary regulation: Georgia Veterinary Practice Act, O.C.G.A. Title 43, Ch. 50, and State Board of Veterinary Medicine (Secretary of State's office; six members, five licensed veterinarians + one public member) govern veterinary licensure/discipline generally. No Georgia Board of Veterinary Medicine guidance, rule, or disciplinary position specifically on cannabis/CBD discussion, recommendation, or dispensing for animals was located — [UNAVAILABLE]. Do not import another state's veterinary cannabis shield law (e.g. California AB 2215-style discussion protection) into Georgia. Investigational, not approved — CannEpil (Oct 2026, secondary, dated): on 5 Oct 2026, Endovia/Lupvindol announced FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine determined proposed use of CannEpil (investigational CBD + THC oral solution) for control of pain associated with bone cancer in dogs qualifies as “minor use” under Minor Use and Minor Species (MUMS) programme, making it eligible to pursue conditional approval. CannEpil has not been approved or conditionally approved. This does not make any Georgia retail pet CBD product an approved animal drug. Practical pet rules following from Run 1: pet may not be given Georgia medical cannabis (registry products are for registered human patient/caregiver only, in labelled pharmaceutical containers); THC exposure in dogs is associated with lethargy, ataxia, urinary incontinence, vomiting, hyperesthesia in veterinary secondary sources; human CBD products containing xylitol, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, or unsafe essential oils must not be given to pets. Retail hemp flower/leaves ban and 21+ consumable-hemp age rule apply to human retail, not as pet-product licence — there is no Georgia “pet hemp licence” type located.

12.8 Low-THC / medical cannabis in schools

No Georgia school-administration statute was located. Georgia has no equivalent of New Jersey / New Mexico / South Dakota school medical-cannabis administration statutes that authorise designated caregiver to administer on school grounds, require written administration plan, or bar school personnel from storing/administering while permitting private caregiver location. Do not cite another state's school policy as Georgia law. Historical Georgia practice (pre-SB 220 low-THC era, secondary reporting of WABE/NPR-affiliate coverage, via Ganjapreneur): Georgia public schools could not store medical cannabis products; registered patient's parent/caregiver had to take student off campus to administer, because “by law, the only person whose name is on the registration card issued by the Department of Public Health for cannabis oil may store the oil,” and schools cited federal Safe and Drug-Free Schools concerns and jeopardy to federal funding. Houston County was reported as telling family student “can't even have it on campus”; Bibb County was reported as having no policy until confronted with case; Georgia School Boards Association was reported as not having seen issue arise. Source secondary, undated in extract, low-THC-era facts — historical practice only, [VERIFY] current district policy. What SB 220 does and does not change for schools: SB 220 expanded “caregiver” to include adult designated by patient and health care institution where patient receives care, and allows electronic/physical cards valid 5 years. Nothing in SB 220 summaries found creates right to possess, store, or administer medical cannabis on school property, obliges school nurse to administer it, or overrides school's drug policy. Whether specific Georgia school district now permits off-campus caregiver administration during school day, private on-campus location for caregiver, or IEP/504 accommodation is district-policy question — [UNAVAILABLE] as statewide rule; check specific district's student medication and drug policies. Adjacent school-zone criminal law — scope warning: O.C.G.A. § 16-13-32.4 makes it felony to manufacture, distribute, dispense, or possess with intent to distribute controlled substance or marijuana in, on, or within 1,000 feet of real property owned/leased to public/private elementary/secondary school or school board used for elementary/secondary education (1st conviction up to 20 years / $20,000; 2nd/subsequent 5–40 years / $40,000, with mandatory 5-year minimum that may not be suspended unless otherwise provided). Verbatim operative text: “It shall be unlawful for any person to manufacture, distribute, dispense, or possess with intent to distribute a controlled substance or marijuana in, on, or within 1,000 feet of any real property owned by or leased to any public or private elementary school, secondary school, or school board used for elementary or secondary education.” It is no defence school was not in session, property was used for other purposes, or offence took place on school vehicle; private-residence affirmative defence exists where conduct was entirely within private residence, no person 17 or younger was present, and it was not for financial gain. This section does not criminalise simple possession by registered patient, and does not by itself authorise or forbid school administration — do not cite it as school medical-cannabis rule. Related school rules that do apply: vapor products — Georgia law makes it unlawful to knowingly use vapor product within school safety zone (Tobacco 21 summary, § 16-12-171 context). Student possessing non-medical marijuana, paraphernalia, or hemp THC products at school remains subject to ordinary school discipline and Georgia controlled-substance law; registered minor patient's lawful possession outside school does not create right to possess/use at school on any source found.
