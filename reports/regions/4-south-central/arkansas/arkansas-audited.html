Part A — Audit

Audit of the original Parts 0–12 report

Last audited: 28 October 2026. This audit was run as a separate pass from the writer. Primary sources only were used for Confirmed and Corrected verdicts: the Arkansas Constitution (Amendment 98), the Arkansas Code and bill/Act pages on arkleg.state.ar.us, the Arkansas Register and Administrative Rules (Secretary of State / codeofarrules.arkansas.gov), the Medical Marijuana Commission, DFA and its Alcoholic Beverage Control Division, the Arkansas Department of Health, the Arkansas Department of Agriculture, the Secretary of State for ballot measures and certified results, Arkansas Supreme Court opinions, federal court dockets and opinions for hemp litigation (including the Eighth Circuit), the Attorney General's official opinions, and official county and city sites and codes. Nothing was confirmed from news, law-firm blogs, trade press or AI summaries; where only a secondary source exists the claim is marked Unverified. The federal baseline in the brief was used as given and was not re-audited; only the report's statement of it and Arkansas's responses to it were checked.

Verdict counts (these sum exactly to the number of rows audited): 209 rows audited — Confirmed 92, Corrected 36, Removed 1, Unverified 80. A 'Corrected' row means the rewritten report below uses the corrected wording and the original wording must not be quoted as current. A 'Removed' row means the claim is deleted from the rewritten report. An 'Unverified' row means the claim is kept only with its flag, exactly as the rewritten report shows it.

How conflicting figures were decided. Where the original listed several numbers for the same thing, the most authoritative official source was chosen and the other figures were removed as current totals: ADH FY2025 prevails over the secondary MMC-renewal summary for licence counts; the certified Issue 4 result (Ballotpedia, officially certified) prevails over the UA Extension 73-county unofficial totals; the ADH live page figure of 119,210 active cards as of 24 October 2026 prevails over the 17 October 119,452 figure, which was not found on the ADH page; and 40 is the constitutional dispensary cap, not a licensed total. Dated historical figures are retained only with their dates and sources.

Election status at audit date. 28 October 2026 is before the 3 November 2026 election. No 2026 ballot result is reported in this file, and no 2026 result has been invented. The 2026 position is: no cannabis measure was found certified for 3 November 2026, and the four certified measures are legislature-referred measures, none cannabis (see the Part 9 ballot row).

Personal data. No personal phone number or email for an individual licence holder is reproduced in this file. Where the official Agriculture hemp list publishes such details for individual growers, this file gives company/county/count only and links to the official source.

  • Hemp: PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026. After 11 December 2026, the full total-THC hemp definition and the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply.
  • USDA enforcement of DEA-registered lab testing for hemp is delayed to 31 December 2026.
  • The federal medical marijuana appropriations rider runs through 11 December 2026.
  • There is no federal cannabis banking law.

How this applies to Arkansas: Arkansas's programme is state-licensed medical marijuana, so its cultivation facilities, dispensaries and processors fall inside the Schedule III medical category and federal §280E is generally lifted for that activity from 28 April 2026. Any non-medical possession — and any adult-use activity, which Arkansas does not license — remains Schedule I federally and fully subject to §280E. Arkansas already allowed ordinary state deductions in practice (see Part 2), so the federal change mainly changes the federal return, not the Arkansas return. No Arkansas DFA or Attorney General guidance implementing Schedule III was found Unavailable. One secondary summary (CannBus Q3 2026) states the 28 April 2026 Schedule III order "includes Arkansas's program" — consistent with the baseline, but Treasury/IRS implementing guidance for Arkansas licensees was not found on an official Arkansas page.

1.1 Legal status — medical only

Legal for qualifying patients and designated caregivers with a registry card, through licensed dispensaries only: acquisition, possession (≤2.5 oz usable marijuana), use, delivery, transfer and transportation for medical use — Amendment 98 §2(11) definition of "medical use" and §3 protections.

Not legal for non-patients: any possession, use, cultivation, sale or transfer of marijuana outside Amendment 98 is a Schedule VI controlled-substance offence under the Uniform Controlled Substances Act, Ark. Code §§5-64-101 et seq. (see §1.3). There is no adult-use / recreational licence, no decriminalisation tier, and no home-grow right for patients.

Marijuana remains Schedule VI under Arkansas state law — distinct from the federal Schedule III (medical) / Schedule I (adult-use) split in the baseline above. Do not conflate the two schedules.

Verbatim — Amendment 98 §3(a)

"A qualifying patient or designated caregiver in actual possession of a registry identification card shall not be subject to arrest, prosecution, or penalty in any manner or denied any right or privilege, including without limitation a civil penalty or disciplinary action by a business, occupational, or professional licensing board or bureau, for the medical use of marijuana in accordance with this amendment if the qualifying patient or designated caregiver possesses not more than two and one-half ounces (2 1/2 oz.) of usable marijuana."

§23 permits the General Assembly to amend Amendment 98; §26 lists Acts amending it. 1.5 Intoxicating hemp: Act 629, Act 934, litigation, CBD, beverages, age, testing, labelling

Act 629 of 2023 (SB 358) — intent and structure

Verbatim — Act 629 (Act text, index)

"(a) It is the intent of the General Assembly to prohibit the production and sale of intoxicating substances derived from industrial hemp. (b) The General Assembly recognizes that the cultivation of hemp for industrial use, such as home and building construction, should remain authorized under the 'Arkansas Industrial Hemp Production Act.'"

Source: Act 629 (PDF)

In substance (secondary summaries agreeing; full codified text Verify): Act 629 (i) narrowed the state definition of industrial hemp by tying the 0.3% delta-9 limit to the hemp-derived CBD content on a dry-weight basis rather than to the hemp/product weight — the "0.3% of CBD" formulation, the provision most often quoted and most in need of verification against the enrolled Act before quoting as current law, because Act 934 later reworked the framework; (ii) added delta-8, delta-9 (in the synthetic/converted context), delta-10, delta-6a,10a THC and their acetate esters and other psychoactive substances derived from them to Schedule VI; (iii) prohibited growth, processing, sale, transfer and possession of industrial hemp containing those substances; (iv) contained a trigger / alternative set of provisions (Act 629 §§6–14 in secondary accounts) that took effect if the first set was enjoined, and a continuous-transportation savings clause; (v) initially banned sale to under-21s, extending to all persons from 1 Aug 2023 (secondary — Verify dates in enrolled Act).

Products covered: delta-8, delta-10, delta-6a,10a, THC-O (acetate esters), HHC and other converted/semi-synthetic cannabinoids, and synthetically converted delta-9 — at any concentration for the synthetic/converted categories, per secondary summaries. THCA: THCA flower was not expressly named in Act 629 (secondary, agreeing); its status under Act 629 alone was a grey zone until Act 934.

Litigation — Bio Gen LLC v. Sanders (E.D. Ark. → 8th Cir.)

  • September 2023: the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Arkansas preliminarily enjoined Act 629, finding likely success on Supremacy Clause (2018 Farm Bill preemption) and Due Process (vagueness) claims.
  • 24 June 2025: the Eighth Circuit, No. 23-3237, vacated the injunction, reversed denial of the Governor's/Attorney General's motion to dismiss on sovereign-immunity grounds, and remanded. Holdings (Justia summary, Verified live): the Farm Bill did not expressly preempt Act 629 because its savings clause allowed continuous transportation through Arkansas; Act 629 did not conflict with the Farm Bill's purpose because federal law allows states to regulate hemp production more stringently; Act 629 was not unconstitutionally vague ("continuous transportation," "synthetic substance," "psychoactive substances" sufficiently clear); the Governor/AG lacked a sufficient enforcement connection for Ex parte Young purposes.
  • Current status: the injunction is gone; Act 629 is enforceable and is being enforced (see the AG release below). The case was remanded for further proceedings — it was not a final judgment on all claims at the Eighth Circuit stage. A later final judgment on the pleadings in the underlying suit is reported by the AG (April 2026, below), which is what triggered Act 934 certification. Any post-remand docket entries after April 2026 are Unavailable — follow-up: check the E.D. Ark. docket (the district-court case number was not confirmed in opened sources — Verify; do not cite a district-court number from memory).

CBD products

Non-intoxicating CBD within the consumable-hemp definition (≤1 mg total THC/container, >15:1 CBD:THC, full-spectrum / listed-cannabinoid product) sold by ATC-permitted sellers to buyers 21+ is the permitted channel. 1.6 State response to the new federal hemp definition and its 12 Nov / 11 Dec 2026 dates

Finding: No Arkansas statute, rule, AG opinion or agency guidance responding specifically to PL 119-37 §781 / PL 119-103 §2019 was found in the sources searched. That absence is itself the finding — Unavailable; do not infer a response.

Analysis (labelled as analysis, applying the federal baseline to Arkansas law as found):

  1. Arkansas does not need the federal change to ban intoxicating hemp — Acts 629/934 already do so, and Bio Gen held the 2018 Farm Bill does not preempt that stricter state regime. The federal dates therefore do not create Arkansas's ban.
  2. The federal change narrows what counts as federal "hemp" after 11 Dec 2026 (total-THC + 0.4 mg/container). For Arkansas the practical pinch is the reverse of most states: Arkansas's permitted consumable-hemp ceiling (1 mg total THC/container) is above the federal 0.4 mg cap. A product that is lawful consumable hemp in Arkansas (e.g. 0.5–1.0 mg/container, >15:1) could therefore lose federal "hemp" status after 11 Dec 2026 even though it remains within Arkansas's state definition — with consequences for interstate shipment, federal enforcement and banking that Arkansas law alone cannot cure. This interaction has not been addressed in any Arkansas guidance found Verify / Unavailable.
  3. For cultivation, the Agriculture programme's testing regime is state-administered under USDA approval (Dec 2021). Whether Arkansas will conform its hemp-plan testing to total-THC / DEA-lab requirements by the USDA 31 Dec 2026 enforcement date is Unavailable — follow-up: Arkansas Dept. of Agriculture hemp programme and the USDA AMS Arkansas plan page.
  4. The 12 Nov 2026 date (PL 119-37 §781 as limited by PL 119-103 §2019) matters in Arkansas mainly for the non-plant-derived / synthetic cannabinoid exclusion category in secondary accounts; no Arkansas guidance distinguishing 12 Nov vs 11 Dec products was found.
  5. Schedule III / §280E interaction (see Part 0): Arkansas medical licensees are the intended beneficiaries of the federal Schedule III move; Arkansas's own income-tax treatment already allowed ordinary deductions (see Part 2), so no Arkansas conforming statute is needed for state purposes. Whether DFA has issued Schedule III / §280E guidance to licensees: Unavailable.

1.9 Employment and drug testing protections for patients

Amendment 98 §6(f) (via §2 definitions and §6 text, index — official DFA compilation):

  1. Anti-discrimination rule: "An employer shall not discriminate against an applicant or employee in hiring, termination, or any term or condition of employment, or otherwise penalize an applicant or employee, based upon the applicant's or employee's past or present status as a qualifying patient or designated caregiver." This applies to employers with 9+ employees in Arkansas in 20+ calendar weeks in the current/preceding year (§2(22) definition of "employer," DFA text Verified live).
  2. Carve-outs — no cause of action / not prohibited: establishing a substance-abuse / drug-free workplace policy (including a compliant drug-testing programme) and acting under it; acting on a good-faith belief the patient possessed/used marijuana on the employer's premises or during work hours, or was under the influence there/during hours — provided a positive test result alone cannot be the sole basis for that good-faith belief; and excluding a qualifying patient from a safety-sensitive position based on a good-faith belief of current use of marijuana. "Current use" is presumed on a positive test (§2(20)).
  3. Permitted employer actions under the carve-out include monitoring/supervision, reassignment, paid/unpaid leave, suspension/termination, requiring completion of a substance-abuse programme before return, refusing to hire, or any combination.
  4. What the amendment does NOT require: an employer to accommodate ingestion in the workplace or an employee working under the influence; a government medical-assistance programme or private insurer to reimburse medical marijuana costs unless federal law requires it; a property owner to allow use on its property or admit an inebriated visitor; a landlord to permit smoking on leased property (but a landlord may not prohibit non-smoked medical use); a public school to permit a student in violation of discipline policy on a positive test / good-faith impairment belief (§6(b), HB 1935 engrossed text, index).

Safety-sensitive definition: The full §2 definition of "safety-sensitive position" was not extracted — secondary summaries describe it broadly (heavy/powered equipment, hazardous/flammable materials, food/medicine handling, direct patient care, firearms, work where a lapse could cause injury/death) — Verify the exact definition before advising on a specific job. 3.1 Authority — what counties and cities can and cannot do

Verbatim — Amendment 98 §14(b) (DFA text via index snippet, confirmed in DFA compilation)

"(b) This section does not allow a city, incorporated town, or county to prohibit the operation of any dispensaries or cultivation facilities in the city, incorporated town, or county unless such a prohibition is approved at an election under Article 5, § 1, of this constitution."

In plain English: (a) Reasonable zoning is allowed — a city/town/county may enact reasonable zoning regulations for dispensaries/cultivation facilities, provided (per 2017-era MMC/Little Rock summaries of §14) those regulations are the same as those for a retail pharmacy — Verify the "same as pharmacy" wording in the current §14 text, as the snippet above only confirms subsection (b). (b) Outright prohibition only by election — no council or county quorum-court ordinance alone can ban a dispensary or cultivation facility; only a voter-approved prohibition at an Art. 5 §1 election can. (c) No other local ban power follows from §14.

For hemp/CBD/smoke/vape shops, §14 does not apply — those are ordinary retail businesses subject to general zoning, business-licence and tobacco/vape retailer rules, and (for consumable hemp) ATC permitting; a city may regulate them through general police/zoning powers, but no §14-style local-option election is required or provided. 10.2 Interstate issues

General rule (all marijuana, all directions): transporting marijuana across any state line is unlawful under federal law even if both states have legalised it in some form, and Arkansas law only protects marijuana obtained from an Arkansas-licensed dispensary by an Arkansas (or visiting-patient) cardholder. Secondary legal summaries concur: "transporting marijuana across state lines violates both state and federal law" and, for Missouri, "Do not bring Missouri cannabis home with you… regardless of whether both states have legalized it." Sources: legalclarity.org — Arkansas; legalclarity.org — Missouri Index

Hemp shipments — a different regime, still Arkansas-controlled:

  • Through-shipment: Bio Gen held the 2018 Farm Bill savings clause allows "continuous transportation of hemp through Arkansas" — i.e. through-transport is protected, but Arkansas may regulate production/sale more strictly inside the state.
  • Into Arkansas for sale: banned products (delta-8/10, synthetics, intoxicating hemp) may not be shipped/transferred into Arkansas; DFA/ATC enforcement includes seizures and retailer warnings, and compliance packets (COA, bills of lading, licences) must be producible on demand (secondary enforcement summaries). Permitted non-intoxicating hemp/CBD (≤0.3% delta-9) and permitted consumable hemp (ATC permit + 1 mg / 15:1 limits) are the only hemp products lawfully sold in Arkansas. After 11 Dec 2026 the federal 0.4 mg/container cap (fixed baseline) will be stricter than Arkansas's 1 mg ceiling for purposes of federal hemp status — no Arkansas guidance on reconciling the two was found (Unavailable, as in Run 1).
  • Out of Arkansas: no Arkansas statute found authorising export of medical marijuana. Amendment 98 text (secondary summary) allows a dispensary to transfer to an out-of-state dispensary only "if permissible under federal law" — which, for marijuana, it is not. Do not advise export.

Out-of-state patients buying IN Arkansas — YES, via a visiting-patient card only (not by presenting a home-state card): the ADH page (verified live) states "Visiting patients must apply online," fee $50, 90 days. Secondary ADH-FAQ summaries agree: $50, 90 days, application may take up to 14 days, the condition must also qualify in Arkansas, proof of home-state registration required. A conflicting secondary summary says "30 days per application" (Veriheal) — ADH's own 90-day fee-table entry is adopted; the 30-day figure is not. Visiting-patient eligibility in Amendment 98 summaries: non-resident, or resident <30 days, with a qualifying Arkansas condition. Purchase limit once carded: the same 2.5 oz / 14 days.

11.5 Waste disposal, including non-compliant hemp destruction

Medical waste (ABC rule RR 18.1 / 20 CAR): store in a secure, limited-access area; weigh and record in the Inventory Tracking System; notify ABC at least 72 hours before rendering unusable and disposing (20 CAR §810-1801(d)(1)) — the 3-business-day vs 72-hour wording differs between rule compilations; Verify the current rule text and use the newer compilation's 72 hours only with that flag. Render unusable under video surveillance by grinding and mixing with other ground materials so the mixture is at least 50% non-cannabis waste by volume. Acceptable compostable mix: food waste, yard waste, vegetable-based grease/oils, agricultural materials, biodegradable products/paper, clean wood, fruits/vegetables, plant matter. Non-compostable/landfill/incineration mix: paper, cardboard, plastic, soil, non-recyclable plastic, broken glass. Liquid waste and chemical/hazardous (pesticide) waste must be disposed of under all applicable federal/state/local laws. Source: Cornell — ABC disposal rule Index

Hemp — non-compliant destruction (2 CAR §31-111): if no retest is requested, or a retest is >0.3% THC, the area/lot represented "shall be subject to the following disposition: (i) Industrial hemp stalks (denuded) may be harvested, processed, and used for fiber and/or any other lawful purpose; or (ii) Industrial hemp seed may be harvested, processed, and rendered nonviable for food products" (provided certified-seed criteria are met). If plant parts are commingled before results are known, all commingled parts shall be destroyed if >0.3%. No plants/parts from a tested planting may be marketed until released by Agriculture. Source: 2 CAR §31-111 Index

  • Older (2021/2022) Agriculture guidance adds a tier: pre-harvest ≥1.0% total delta-9 THC = non-compliant, mandatorily destroyed in full (lot + unharvested crop of that variety), Destruction Request Form within 7 days of notice, segregation/labelling, possible USDA/law-enforcement reporting, and remediation/retest pathways for lots between 0.3% and 1.0% (remediated material retest, grain/seed/fibre salvage with destruction of leaf/floral verified by Agriculture). That tier is guidance, not codified §31-111 text — Verify the current Agriculture Sampling/Testing/Remediation/Disposal Guidelines (2025 version) before advising a grower which tier applies in 2026. Source: 2025 Sampling/Testing/Remediation/Disposal Guidelines (PDF) Index
  • Hot hemp is not a licence-revocation event if destroyed per the part: "An analytical testing of THC levels greater than [0.3%] shall not result in revocation of a license so long as the crop is destroyed in accordance with this part" (2 CAR §31-102).

12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke shops

  • Drug paraphernalia — A.C.A. §5-64-443 (2025 text, via Justia): "(a) A person who possesses drug paraphernalia with the purpose to use [it] to inject, ingest, inhale, or otherwise introduce into the human body a controlled substance or to store, contain, conceal, or weigh a controlled substance… is guilty of: (1) A Class A misdemeanor; or (2) A Class D felony if [methamphetamine/heroin/fentanyl/cocaine] and previously convicted…" "(b) A person who uses or possesses with the purpose to use drug paraphernalia to plant, propagate, cultivate, grow, harvest, manufacture, compound, convert, produce, process, prepare, test, analyze, pack, or repack a controlled substance… is guilty of: (1) A Class D felony; or (2) A Class B felony if [those drugs]." In practice: pipes/rolling papers for use = Class A misdemeanour (up to 1 year, up to $2,500 fine, secondary summaries); grow/manufacture equipment = Class D felony (up to 6 years, up to $10,000). Patient exception: paraphernalia used lawfully for medical marijuana by a cardholder within Amendment 98 limits is protected as medical use; paraphernalia for non-medical marijuana is not. Source: §5-64-443 (Justia) Index
  • Smoke shops / head shops: no Arkansas "smoke shop licence" was found. A shop selling tobacco needs an ATC tobacco permit; a shop selling consumable hemp needs an ATC consumable-hemp retailer permit (§7.2); a shop selling paraphernalia with intent for illicit drug use risks §5-64-443/444 (delivery to a person <18 who is ≥3 years younger = Class A misdemeanour, or Class B felony if in furtherance of a felony — secondary summary). A shop selling only non-intoxicating hemp/CBD, tobacco (permitted), and general merchandise, without intoxicating hemp, operates under ordinary retail + ATC permits, not a cannabis licence. Verify the local business-licence / zoning for any specific shop; Fort Smith tobacco-retailer buffer discussions (500 ft proposal, secondary) are local proposals, not statewide law — do not cite them as state law.

12.2 Minimum age — hemp products (and medical)

  • Consumable hemp: 21+. Under the Act 934 framework (Run 1 + secondary in Run 2): "minor" = under 21 for consumable hemp; sale/provision/purchase/use/possession by a minor is prohibited, subject to specified enforcement/employment exceptions. This is a hemp-specific 21 rule, not a general CBD age for non-consumable topicals — Verify whether non-consumable hemp topicals/cosmetics are age-restricted; Act 629-era commentary excluded cosmetics/animal products/raw flower/dietary supplements from the "hemp-derived product" definition, so the 21 rule's scope by product type is Verify in current ATC rules. Source: mycannabis.com — CBD in Arkansas Index, secondary
  • Medical marijuana: patient 18+ (minors only via a parent/guardian caregiver, with physician risk explanation + written consent + caregiver control of acquisition/dosage/frequency — Amendment 98 §105(b) summary). Caregiver 21+. Dispensary/cultivation owners/officers 21+. Smoking medical marijuana under 21 is prohibited in secondary Act 740 summaries — Verify the current §5-71-231 text for the under-21 smoking clause before citing it as a separate offence.

12.3 THC driving limits and impairment testing

  • No per-se THC limit in Arkansas law. A.C.A. §5-65-103 makes it unlawful to operate/be in actual physical control of a motor vehicle while intoxicated; "intoxicated" = influenced/affected by a controlled substance/intoxicant "to such a degree that the driver's reactions, motor skills, and judgment are substantially altered and the driver… constitutes a clear and substantial danger…" The fact a person "is or has been entitled to the use of that drug… under the laws of this state does not constitute a defense" (§5-65-103). Alcohol has a 0.08 per-se prong; marijuana has no equivalent ng/ml prong in the statute as summarised. Source: Drugged Driving Resources — Arkansas statute summary (PDF) Index
  • Testing method: no reliable roadside THC breath device; enforcement relies on observations, field sobriety, drug-recognition-expert (DRE) evaluation, and blood/saliva/urine analysis (secondary legal sources). Metabolites may persist weeks after use — that creates risk but is not itself the offence; the State must prove intoxication/impairment, not mere presence, under the impairment definition above. Source: dougnorwood.com Index

Conflict flagged, not adopted: the "5 ng/ml" claim

One secondary source (StateRegsToday DUI page) claims "a legal limit of 5 ng/ml THC," and a 2017 bill summary (SB 130) proposed a 5 ng/ml presumption (≥5 ng presumed intoxicated, <2 ng presumed not) — that bill is a proposal, not enacted law as summarised, and the 5 ng claim conflicts with the statute summary and with multiple law-firm sources stating "Arkansas law presently has no legal limit, known as a per se limit, for THC." Adopted position: no per-se limit; Verify the current §§5-65-103/204 text on arkleg before advising on a blood-THC number.

Penalties (secondary, Verify the current §5-65-111): first DWI: unclassified misdemeanour, 24 hours–1 year imprisonment, $150+ fine, 6-month licence suspension; repeat offences escalate (7 days–1 year / 90 days–1 year / 1–6 years in older summaries — ranges differ by source). Refusal of a chemical test: implied-consent suspension (180 days first, 2 years second in 5 years, 3 years third — secondary, Verify §5-65-205). Drug-conviction licence suspension: 6 months (12 months for CDL) under §27-16-915 in secondary summaries — Verify.

12.4 Expungement and sealing for marijuana offences

  • No cannabis-specific automatic expungement. Arkansas uses petition-based sealing under the Comprehensive Criminal Record Sealing Act of 2013, A.C.A. §§16-90-1401 et seq. (Act 1460 of 2013). Secondary surveys concur: "Arkansas has no marijuana-specific expungement program, but some marijuana convictions may qualify under the state's broader record-sealing laws" (Marijuana Herald 2026 survey) and "no automatic expungement… must file a petition" (GISPI).
  • Eligibility (secondary summaries of §§16-90-1404–1419, Verify the current text): most misdemeanours (including low-level marijuana possession, Class A misdemeanour <4 oz) are sealable after full sentence completion (fines, costs, restitution, licence-reinstatement fees paid); for misdemeanours the court shall seal unless the prosecutor shows clear-and-convincing evidence against sealing (§16-90-1415(a) summary); no filing fee (§16-90-1419 summary); the prosecutor has 30 days to object or the court may grant without a hearing. Non-violent Class C/D felonies and certain drug felonies (including Class A/B drug felonies in some summaries) are sealable after sentence completion; violent Class C/D felonies: 5-year wait; ineligible: Class Y, most Class A/B (non-drug), felony sex/violence, unclassified felony >10 years max, and (in some summaries) 3+ prior felonies. Sealed ≠ destroyed: records are sequestered, treated as confidential, still available to criminal-justice agencies as law permits; sealed conduct may be denied by the applicant for most purposes.
  • First-offender / drug-court / diversion: discharge/dismissal without adjudication under the First Offender Act / drug court may lead to sealing as part of that process (secondary).
  • What Amendment 98 / Issue 4 did NOT do: neither Amendment 98 nor the failed Issue 4 contained an expungement provision (Issue 4 summaries expressly note "no provisions to remove criminal records"). Do not tell a client Amendment 98 expunged their record.
  • Possession penalties for context (secondary, Verify §§5-64-419/440): <4 oz = Class A misdemeanour (up to 1 year, $2,500); 4+ prior convictions under the same statute escalates to a Class D felony; ≥4 oz = felony tiered by weight (4 oz–10 lb Class D, 10–25 lb, 25–100 lb, 100–500 lb higher felonies, 500+ lb trafficking Class Y in some summaries — weight tiers differ slightly between secondary sources, Verify). Enhanced penalties within 1,000 ft of schools/childcare etc. and for delivery to a minor (doubling / +10 years in some summaries) — Verify §§5-64-406/436–440.

12.5 Consumption rules

  • Where medical use is prohibited (Amendment 98 + Act 740 + Act 271): a public place; public transport; school/daycare/preschool/primary/secondary school; college/university; drug/alcohol treatment facility; community/recreation centre; correctional facility; any place smoking tobacco is prohibited; in the presence of a person <14; knowingly in the presence of a pregnant woman; where smoking is likely to cause a non-authorised person to be under the influence; inside a motor vehicle/aircraft/motorised watercraft/vehicle drawn by power other than muscle power (smoking).
  • Act 271 (HB 1452, approved 12 Mar 2025) makes two Class C misdemeanours (official Act PDF): (1) unlawful smoking in a prohibited place as above (§5-71-231 as amended); (2) possession of uncontained medical marijuana in a motor vehicle (§5-71-232): "'Uncontained' means not contained in a jar, bag, wrapping or other container obtained from a dispensary licensed under [Amendment 98]"; unlawful if (1) the form is readily usable through smoking, (2) in a vehicle on a public highway/right-of-way, (3) within an area readily accessible to the driver in a seated position. Class C misdemeanour. Source: Act 271 (PDF) Index
  • Practical consequence: keep medical marijuana in its original dispensary container, out of driver reach (e.g. boot/trunk), and do not smoke in a vehicle — even as a passenger in a moving vehicle on a public highway, the smoking ban applies.
  • Tasks under influence: performing a task under the influence where it would constitute negligence/professional malpractice is prohibited (Amendment 98 summaries); operating/controlling a motor vehicle/aircraft/boat while under the influence is prohibited (see §12.3).
  • Home / private consumption: private-home consumption by a cardholder (non-smoking forms where smoking is otherwise allowed, and smoking where tobacco smoking is not prohibited and no <14/pregnant person is present) is the lawful default; public consumption is not lawful even for cardholders. No consumption lounge / on-site dispensary consumption authorisation was found — Verify if a lounge is claimed (none in MMC rules summaries).
  • Hemp consumption: smoking hemp flower is not separately authorised as a consumable-hemp product; intoxicating hemp smoking/vaping products are banned (Act 629/934). Non-intoxicating CBD use is not subject to the medical smoking-place bans, but general smoking bans (tobacco-free places, schools, state property) still apply to smoking any product.

12.6 Visitor and out-of-state patient rules

Visiting-patient card: ADH online application, $50, 90 days, up to 14 days processing (ADH page verified live + secondary FAQ summaries). The holder must hold a home-state card/recommendation, the condition must qualify in Arkansas, and the patient must be a non-resident or <30 days resident (Amendment 98 summaries). No Arkansas resident may use a visiting-patient card as a shortcut — residents use the standard $50 patient process. Visiting patients buy at Arkansas dispensaries only, 2.5 oz / 14 days, and may not take Arkansas marijuana home across a state line. Arkansas patients visiting other states: see the §10.2 table (Missouri/Oklahoma/Louisiana/Mississippi/Tennessee/Texas). An Arkansas card alone does not guarantee purchase elsewhere; check the destination's temporary/visitor rule and never transport across state lines.

12.7 Landlord and tenant rules

Amendment 98 (f)(1) — verbatim (secondary reproduction of the Amendment text)

"(f)(1) A school or landlord shall not refuse to enroll, refuse to lease to, or otherwise penalize an individual solely for his or her status as a qualifying patient or designated caregiver unless doing so would put the school or landlord in violation of federal law or regulations."

Source: Amendment 98 text (PDF) Index

  • Limits: (a) a landlord may prohibit smoking on the premises (as with tobacco) — the protection is against status discrimination, not a right to smoke in a rental. MPP summary: "The amendment allows landlords to prohibit onsite cannabis smoking, but they may not prevent nonsmoked consumption of medical cannabis." (b) Federal-law exception: federally subsidised / public housing landlords may be compelled by federal law to enforce federal drug-free rules — that is the stated exception, and it is the main practical limit for public-housing tenants. Verify HUD/public-housing rules for a specific tenancy; do not advise a public-housing tenant that Amendment 98 overrides federal housing law. (c) Lease violations unrelated to status (damage, nuisance, illegal activity beyond medical use, non-payment) remain enforceable. (d) Home cultivation is prohibited regardless of landlord consent — a lease cannot authorise it. Source: MPP — Arkansas overview (PDF) Index
  • For hemp: no landlord protection for hemp growing was found; hemp growing on rented land requires the landowner's written acknowledgement of Agriculture access terms (2 CAR pt 31: the licensee "shall obtain in writing from the owner of any leased or rented field or structure the owner's acknowledgement they will abide to the terms listed in this section").

12.8 Gun ownership conflicts for patients

  • State protection — Act 757 (2023), effective 1 Aug 2023 (secondary): a person's status as a medical-marijuana patient/caregiver cannot be used to deny a licence to carry a concealed handgun in Arkansas, and ADH may not share patient status with law enforcement for a concealed-carry investigation. Secondary summaries: "Medical cardholders and state-licensed caregivers may legally purchase and carry a concealed gun [under Arkansas law]" (LeafWell) — that statement is state-law only.
  • Federal prohibition — still operative (fixed-baseline context: Schedule III from 28 Apr 2026 does not remove the §922(g)(3) bar, which covers controlled substances generally): 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) prohibits a person who is an "unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance" from possessing firearms/ammunition. ATF Form 4473, Q.21f, asks about unlawful use of marijuana and carries a bold warning that marijuana use "remains unlawful under Federal law regardless of whether it has been legalized or decriminalized for medical or recreational purposes in the state where you reside." Answering "yes" = denial at a licensed dealer; answering "no" while using = a federal false-statement felony (penalties stated as up to 10 years in one secondary, up to 15 years in another — Verify the current §924 penalty; do not quote a single number as settled).
  • Framing (not legal advice): a patient must choose between (i) state concealed-carry licensing (protected by Act 757) and (ii) federal firearms possession/purchase risk (prohibited by §922(g)(3) as ATF interprets it). No Arkansas law can grant an exemption from federal law. This conflict is unresolved in Arkansas law and was not resolved by Schedule III rescheduling in the fixed baseline. Verify any post-April 2026 ATF guidance if a current federal position is needed — none was found in Run 2.

12.9 Pet and veterinary products

  • No FDA-approved cannabis animal drug; no Arkansas veterinary cannabis rule found. FDA (Jan 2025 Federal Register notice, secondary summary): "no FDA-approved, conditionally approved or indexed animal drugs contain CBD" and "significant data gaps exist on CBD and other cannabis-derived products in animals." Veterinarians may use FDA-approved human drugs (e.g. Epidiolex) extra-label where medically appropriate, but generally cannot prescribe/recommend/sell unapproved hemp/cannabis pet products as drugs (secondary veterinary summaries).
  • Arkansas-specific: no Arkansas Veterinary Medical Examining Board rule on CBD was located in Run 2 — Verify with the Board. What is Arkansas law: (a) hemp-derived pet products sold in Arkansas must meet Arkansas hemp law (≤0.3% delta-9, no intoxicating cannabinoids, ATC testing/labelling if consumable-hemp); (b) Act 629-era commentary excluded products intended for animal consumption from the "hemp-derived product" definition — that exclusion's current status after Act 934 is Verify (do not assume pet products are unregulated or, conversely, that they are ATC-permitted consumables); (c) THC is toxic to dogs/cats — human medical-marijuana edibles (especially chocolate/coffee/raisin-containing) are a poisoning risk, and giving a pet a patient's medical marijuana is not a lawful veterinary use and is not protected by the patient's card.
  • Practical rule for owners: talk to the veterinarian about interactions (CBD inhibits cytochrome P450, may alter anticonvulsant dosing in cats/dogs — secondary veterinary summary), do not dose from human products, and keep all marijuana products out of pet reach. No Arkansas pet-CBD dosage, licence, or product directory was verified.

12.10 Medical marijuana in schools

  • Enrolment protection vs use ban — both true, different questions. Enrolment: Amendment 98 (f)(1) (above): a school "shall not refuse to enroll… or otherwise penalize an individual solely for his or her status as a qualifying patient or designated caregiver unless doing so would put the school… in violation of federal law or regulations." (MPP/Fisher Phillips summaries.)
  • Use/possession on school property: prohibited. Amendment 98 / Act 740 prohibit use/possession/smoking on school, daycare, preschool, primary/secondary school, college/university, and drug/alcohol treatment, community/recreation and correctional premises (see §12.5). University policies implement this — the UAFS Drug-Free Schools policy: "Students are not permitted to possess, smoke, or otherwise engage in the medical use of marijuana on campus in accordance with Arkansas Act 740. Act 740 further prohibits the smoking of marijuana for medical purposes in places where the smoking of tobacco is prohibited by state law, and all state property is to be considered tobacco free." Source: UAFS Drug-Free Schools policy (PDF) Index
  • Administration at school (e.g. a school nurse giving a minor's dose): no Arkansas school-administration authorisation was found. Minors' medical use is via a parent/guardian designated caregiver who controls acquisition/dosage/frequency — that does not create a right to administer on school grounds during the school day, and federal Drug-Free Schools / state tobacco-free property rules point the other way. Verify with the Arkansas Department of Education / a specific district's policy before advising a family; do not advise that a school must administer medical marijuana.
  • Employees (teachers/staff): Amendment 98 employment protection (status) applies, but schools may bar on-the-job use/impairment and enforce drug-free-workplace policies (see §11.8/§12.5). The ASU-Beebe handbook example (§11.9) bars being under the influence during classes/clinicals.
  • Tax-revenue link (not a use rule): SB 59 (2025) medical-marijuana tax revenue funds free school breakfasts — that is a funding use of tax money, not a permission to use marijuana in schools. Do not conflate the two.
