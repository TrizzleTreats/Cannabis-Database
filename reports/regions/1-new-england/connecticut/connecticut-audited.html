Summary

Connecticut has both a medical programme (since 2012, Conn. Gen. Stat. Ch. 420f, §21a-408 et seq.) and an adult-use (recreational) programme (legalised June 2021 by Public Act 21-1, June Special Session — SB 1201, the Responsible and Equitable Regulation of Adult-Use Cannabis Act, "RERACA"; possession legal 1 July 2021; adult-use retail sales began 10:00 a.m., Tuesday 10 January 2023). The regulator is the Department of Consumer Protection (DCP). Seed-to-sale tracking is BioTrack. Adults 21+ may possess 1.5 oz of cannabis plant material on their person (plus 5 oz in a locked container at home or in a locked glove box/trunk), with statutory equivalencies of 7.5 g concentrate or 750 mg THC in other products for the 1.5 oz limit, and may home-grow 3 mature + 3 immature plants (12 per household cap) since 1 July 2023 (medical patients since 1 October 2021).

The tax system changed fundamentally on 1 October 2026: the former THC-content tax (§12-330ll — $0.00625/mg plant material, $0.0275/mg edibles, $0.009/mg other) was replaced by PA 26-68 with a flat 10.75% statewide adult-use cannabis tax on gross receipts, charged in addition to the 6.35% state sales tax and the 3% municipal tax (§12-330mm, remitted to the municipality) — a combined 20.1% on adult-use sales in DRS's worked example. Medical (palliative) cannabis is exempt from all three. Connecticut has no county government and no county tax; local regulation is entirely municipal (169 towns/cities), principally zoning under §21a-422f. Municipalities may prohibit or restrict cannabis establishments by zoning, but may not prohibit delivery or transport (§21a-422g) and cannot prohibit lawful possession.

Latest official licence counts (DCP Adult-Use Cannabis Licensing Report, FY2026 Q1, period 1 July–30 September 2025, report dated 1 November 2025): 112 final licences issued cumulatively under RERACA in 53 towns, plus 134 provisional licences issued cumulatively. Latest official sales headline figures: $274 million combined in calendar 2023 (DCP); $293,600,093 in 2024 and $290,029,618 in 2025, recomputed from the official data.ct.gov weekly sales dataset ucaf-96h6 (see Part 6). Statewide cannabis tax collections (DRS Annual Report FY25): FY2022-23 $4,675,642; FY2023-24 $20,509,089; FY2024-25 $22,657,073.

Major 2024–2026 changes: PA 24-76 / PA 24-115 created the moderate-THC hemp and THC-infused beverage framework (beverages in package stores with an endorsement from 1 July 2024); PA 25-166 (2025) raised adult-use potency caps, created the infused-beverage wholesaler licence and the statewide enforcement task force, and standardised retail hours; PA 26-8 (HB 5350, 2026) plus fixer PA 26-100 (HB 5222) rewrote cannabis/hemp/beverage law effective mainly 1 October 2026 — including out-of-state medical patient reciprocity, infused beverages up to 5 mg THC/container in package stores and 10 mg in cannabis retailers, removal of the concentrate potency cap while retaining the 35% flower cap; PA 26-68 §264 enacted the 10.75% flat tax.

Possession — §21a-279a(a) verbatim (opened):

3.2 Municipal authority — §§21a-422f and 21a-422g (opened verbatim)

Correction to the mission's premise: municipal zoning authority is §21a-422f and referendum/delivery protection is §21a-422g. §21a-422m is hospitals, not municipal authority.

§21a-422f(b): a municipality may, by zoning amendment or ordinance, (1) prohibit the establishment of a cannabis establishment, (2) establish reasonable restrictions on hours and signage, or (3) establish restrictions on the proximity of cannabis establishments to any establishment listed in §30-46(a)(1) (church, public/parochial school, convent, charitable institution, hospital, veterans' home, armed-forces camp/barracks/flying field). The zoning official must report changes to the OPM Secretary and DCP within 14 days. §21a-422f(c): if a municipality is silent, an establishment is "zoned as if for any other similar use". §21a-422f(d): new restrictions do not apply to existing establishments for 5 years if there is no licence-type conversion. §21a-422f(e): a special permit / affirmative approval is required for any retailer or micro-cultivator. History: PA 21-1 §148, eff. 1 July 2021; PA 22-103 deleted the former population-based retailer/micro-cultivator cap subsections (there is therefore no longer a state-imposed per-town retailer cap in §21a-422f; towns may still cap by zoning — see West Hartford, 3.5).

§21a-422g(a): referendum on petition of ≥10% of electors. §21a-422g(b) verbatim: "No municipality shall prohibit delivery of cannabis to a consumer, qualifying patient or caregiver when the delivery is made by a retailer, hybrid retailer, dispensary facility, delivery service, micro-cultivator or other person authorized… No municipality shall prohibit the transport of cannabis to, from or through such municipality…" §21a-422g(c): no municipal host agreements, required donations, or conditioning official action on payments. §21a-422g(d): a municipality may charge a retailer/hybrid retailer for opening public-safety costs for up to 30 days, capped at $50,000.

Opt-in/opt-out characterisation: Connecticut is not an opt-in state (contrast Maine). The default is that cannabis establishments are treated like similar uses; a municipality must affirmatively act by zoning/ordinance to prohibit or restrict establishments, and even a prohibiting municipality cannot prohibit possession, delivery or transport. 4.4 Social equity programme

  • Social Equity Council (SEC): established under RERACA (§21a-420d); verifies social-equity status, identifies DIAs (from 1 Aug 2023), approves EJVs/DIA cultivators/lottery SE selectees, and approves workforce development plans.
  • Criteria (statute + DCP report, consistent): social equity applicant = ≥65% owned and controlled by individual(s) with (i) average household income <300% of the state median household income over the last 3 tax years, AND (ii) DIA residence for ≥5 of the past 10 years OR ≥9 years before age 18. EJV partner individual(s) meet the same income/residency criteria; the EJV entity must be ≥50% owned and controlled by them.
  • Equity Joint Ventures (§21a-420u): an EJV may be any establishment type other than a cultivator; not subject to the lottery; must be SEC-approved. Each licensed producer, §21a-420o cultivator and dispensary facility may create at most two EJVs. Micro-cultivators licensed under §21a-420aa may create one EJV after commencing cultivation, on application + $500,000 conversion fee. Reduced conversion fees are conditioned on timely EJV creation: producers $1.5M (2 EJVs) vs $3M; dispensaries $500k (1 EJV) vs $1M — originally within 14 months, extended to 24 months by PA 25-166. Outcomes to 30 Sept 2025 (DCP report): of 4 converted producers, 1 missed the EJV deadline and owed the $1.5M balance; of 17 converted dispensaries, 4 missed and owed the $500k balance. 55 provisional and 42 final licences had been issued to EJVs cumulatively.
  • Social Equity Partner (§21a-420l): ≥65% SE-owned entity partnering with a producer; producer provides 5% grow space, mentorship + all overhead for ≥5 years, and 100% of profits. Zero applications received to 30 Sept 2025.
  • SEC Micro-Cultivator programme (§21a-420d(l)): SEC + DCP to select SE applicants for not more than two micro-cultivators with producer mentorship, without lottery/RFP. Zero applications received to 30 Sept 2025.
  • DIA Cultivators (§21a-420o): SEC authorised 26 applicants; 14 applied for provisional; 2 final cultivator licences issued; 3 withdrew to the §21a-420aa micro-cultivator route (1 final issued). Remaining applicants had until 31 Dec 2026 to use the PA 25-166 §8 micro-cultivator route or the §9 hemp-producer partnership route (1 Jan 2026–31 Dec 2027 final licences, on a hemp producer's lot outside a DIA, under a lease with a producer continuously licensed since 1 Jan 2024, who must surrender the hemp licence and hold no ownership/control/management role for 7 years).
  • Workforce development: §21a-421g workforce training programme; §21a-421f business accelerator; labour peace agreements (§21a-421d area) and project labour agreements for covered projects ≥$5M; SEC-approved workforce development plans are a conversion requirement. In Sept 2026 the SEC approved 170+ organisations for a $36M three-year community reinvestment programme [SECONDARY — CannBus reporting an official action; VERIFY on SEC/DCP].

11.4 Security & transport

  • Video (P&P, eRegulations PR2024-040, index, verbatim excerpts): cameras directed at "all approved secure locations, dispensing and sale areas, and any other area where there is cannabis"; entry/exit cameras angled for "clear and certain identification"; recordings "retained for at least thirty days", available for immediate viewing at department direction; if aware of pending investigation/proceeding, retain unaltered copy until closed/released; recordings must produce clear colour HD still/video with embedded date/timestamp, exportable in standard formats (.png/.jpg/.bmp/.gif), archived in authenticated proprietary format; erase recordings before disposal/sale of establishment (except retained copies).
  • Closure security (§21a-421j-16, PR2024-053, index): closed establishments securely locked, security system activated, immediate detection + notification to security company/law enforcement + designated key employee; only key employee may deactivate; cannabis in approved secure location; no sales while closed. Sept 2024 P&Ps removed mantrap requirement and closed-doorway/separate age-verification room requirements for retailers (Shipman, index).
  • Storage: PA 24-76 §21: location deemed secure if it satisfies controlled-substance storage regs (approved vault/safe/separate secure locked area); establishments must prevent diversion/theft/loss and return cannabis to secure location at end of business day (OLR, index).
  • Transport (§21a-421j-36, PR2024-040, index, verbatim excerpts): "minimum of two transporting agents per transport vehicle containing more than two pounds of cannabis flower and cannabis trim or the equivalent"; an agent must remain with vehicle at all times it contains cannabis (single agent may leave locked vehicle only to complete a transfer/delivery); agents carry transportation credentials (driver's licence + employee licence/registration + copy of establishment licence + all shipping manifests); vehicle may contain no cannabis without a manifest; establishment must inspect/test security systems, secure communications and GPS daily before first departure, with written certification (name, VIN, date…). Seed-to-sale system is BioTrack (Run 1; secondary sources also call the state interface "CATS — Cannabis Analytic Tracking System, operated through BioTrack", index).
  • Delivery (Sept 2024 update, index): delivery licensees may use personal vehicles if cannabis stored securely, out of sight, not easily accessible (trunk not required); co-delivery with hemp products and infused beverages permitted.

11.7 Sampling, giveaways, discounts, promotions

  • Discount advertising ban (PA 24-76 §22, OLR index verbatim): establishments generally may not "advertise or market a discounted price or other promotional offer as an inducement to buy cannabis" (non-medical), except (1) inside a dispensary/retailer/hybrid building, (2) through a delivery service, or (3) on the retailer's website where cannabis may lawfully be ordered. The governing provision is §21a-421bb(b)(15) (added by PA 24-76 §22), and a violation is an unfair or deceptive trade practice under CUTPA, §42-110b (§21a-421bb(f)). A reported 2026 DCP letter to retailers and a "$5,000 fine" figure are secondary only — [UNVERIFIED].
  • Hybrid equity rule (§21a-421j-15(i), PR2024-053, index): "A hybrid retailer shall not offer a sale or discount for a specific type or product of cannabis if the sale or discount terms, of equal or greater value, are not offered to qualifying patients and caretakers."
  • Giveaways / free product: Moderate-THC: bill text (HB 6855, index) — "No cannabis establishment or moderate-THC hemp product vendor… shall gift or transfer any moderate-THC hemp product at no cost to a consumer as part of a commercial transaction." General cannabis free-sample ban text was not separately opened [VERIFY in P&Ps §21a-421j-15/16]; vendor sampling in trade contexts is governed by lab-selection sampling rules (Part 11.1), not consumer sampling.
  • Retailer inducements banned: §21a-421aa(a) — retailers/hybrids may not accept payment/compensation from cultivators/manufacturers/packagers to carry/promote product, and may not enter exclusivity/preferential-treatment contracts (index, Part 10.3 source).
  • What retailers may sell (§21a-421j-16(h), index): only (1) cannabis/products, (2) paraphernalia, (3) manufacturer hemp products, (4) non-food/beverage (other than water), non-nicotine, non-lottery, non-weapon, non-live merchandise not appealing to under-21s.

11.8 Drug testing — licence holders & staff (distinct from Run 1 general employment law)

  • Backers/key employees: state + national criminal-history checks, possible financial-history check (§21a-421a(b), index); employees (non-key): annual registration before commencing employment (§21a-421a(a)); changes to application information must be notified to DCP within 5 business days (§21a-421a(c), index).

12.1 Paraphernalia & smoke shops

  • Paraphernalia sale by licensed retailers is expressly permitted (§21a-421j-16(h)(2), Part 11.7). Sale/delivery of cannabis or paraphernalia to under-21s is prohibited (§21a-421aaa, Ch. 420h heading, index).
  • No standalone smoke-shop licence for paraphernalia was found. Smoke shops enter the system only via: (a) Moderate-THC Vendor certificate (Part 7) if selling moderate-THC products, (b) DCP registrations for adjacent products (e.g. electronic-cigarette dealer registration — DCP summarily suspended "R&R Convenience Smoke LLC" registration, Norwalk, 1 Aug 2025, and "Vape R Us" e-cigarette dealer registration, New Haven, 30 July 2025; DCP news index), and (c) enforcement for unlicensed THC sales.
  • Enforcement (secondary republication of AG releases, index): AG Tong v. RZ Smoke, Inc. — stipulated judgment $2M ($375,000 paid, $1.6M suspended), Oct 2026, for distributing high-potency cannabis to smoke shops/gas stations/convenience stores without DCP licence; May 2023 Suffield warehouse search found 11 pallets, ~$792,000 retail value; Jan 2024 CUTPA action filed. Sources: https://www.mmjdaily.com/article/9881988/connecticut-attorney-general-tong-announces-settlement-with-large-wholesale-company/ — index, secondary. [VERIFY on portal.ct.gov/ag].
  • CBD-only retail: no permit/licence needed if products contain no THC (DCP FAQ, Part 7.2).

12.2 Driving — THC limits & impairment testing

  • No per se THC limit — confirmed (two secondary legal sources agreeing, statute text index). §14-227a(a): "No person shall operate a motor vehicle while under the influence of intoxicating liquor or any drug or both…" — the per se prong ("elevated blood alcohol content") is alcohol-only: 0.08% (0.04% commercial; 0.02% under-21 under §14-227g). Source: FindLaw reproduction of §14-227a, index: https://codes.findlaw.com/ct/title-14-motor-vehicles-use-of-the-highway-by-vehicles-gasoline/ct-gen-st-sect-14-227a/

12.7 Landlord & tenant

  • §47a-9a (Ch. 830, CGA, index verbatim): "(a)… a landlord or property manager may not refuse to rent to a prospective tenant or an existing tenant, or otherwise discriminate… based on a past conviction for possession of a cannabis-type substance under section 21a-279a or for a past conviction for possession of four or fewer ounces of cannabis plant material… (b)… a landlord or property manager may not prohibit the possession of cannabis or the consumption of cannabis, except a landlord or property manager may prohibit smoking of cannabis or use of an electronic cannabis device or cannabis vapor product… (c) A landlord or property manager may not require a tenant to submit to a drug test. (d) Exceptions: (1) roomer not leasing entire residence; (2) residence incidental to detention/medical/geriatric/educational/counselling/religious service; (3) transitional housing or sober living facility; (4) compliance would violate federal law or cost landlord a federal monetary/licensing benefit."   • Medical tenants: §21a-408p(b)(2) — landlord may not refuse to rent/take action "solely" because tenant is a qualifying patient/caregiver (DCP FAQ, index: https://portal.ct.gov/cannabis/knowledge-base/articles/mmp/patient-faq/can-a-landlord-refuse-to-rent-to-someone-or-take-action-against-a-tenant — answer: "No.").

12.9 Pets & veterinary products

  • Establishments: prohibited. §21a-421aa(b) verbatim: "No cannabis establishment shall produce, manufacture or sell cannabis that is intended for use or consumption by animals." (LawServer/CGA, index).
  • Hemp for animals: DoAg producer programme covers hemp for non-human-consumable products incl. animal food as a processor/producer activity (secondary CT Hemp Overview, index); DCP licence is only for human consumables (§22-61m). No CT-specific rule on CBD/hemp pet products at retail, and no veterinary cannabis authorisation, was found [UNAVAILABLE / VERIFY — CT veterinary practice act / DoAg animal-food rules not researched].

12.10 Medical cannabis in schools

  • Mission cited "§10-212" — operative section is §10-212a (administration of medications in schools). No provision in §10-212a text retrieved authorises school-nurse administration of medical marijuana; the section lists who may administer "medicinal preparations, including such controlled drugs as the Commissioner of Consumer Protection may, by regulation, designate" on written prescriber order + parent/guardian authorisation (Justia text, index: https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-10/chapter-169/section-10-212a/).
  • Palliative use is prohibited on school grounds: §21a-408a(b)(2) — palliative use may not occur "on the school grounds of any preschool, elementary or secondary school", on a school bus, in the workplace, in any public place, or in direct line of sight of a person under 18 exposing them to secondhand smoke (school-board policy reproductions + DPH symposium materials, index). CAS Legal Mailbag (Shipman & Goodwin, index): use of medical marijuana on school grounds "is not protected under PUMA. Conn. Gen. Stat. §21a-408a(b)(2)(c)"; employers may prohibit use during work hours / discipline for it (§21a-408p(b)(3)).
  • Conclusion: a school nurse administering medical cannabis to a student on school grounds is not authorised under the provisions found; administration would conflict with §21a-408a(b)(2). [VERIFY — full current §21a-408a text + any SDE guidance were not opened].
