PART 1

Laws

1.1 Legal status

  • Adult-use / recreational: legal for adults 21+ under the RI Cannabis Act, Ch. 21-28.11 (personal use at § 21-28.11-22). Retail only from licensed retailers / hybrids.
  • Medical: legal for registered qualifying patients under the Edward O. Hawkins and Thomas C. Slater Medical Marijuana Act, Ch. 21-28.6 (enacted P.L. 2005, ch. 442/443, effective Jan 2006; current chapter as amended, notably in 2022).
  • Hemp: legal and licensed under the Hemp Growth Act, Ch. 2-26, as amended effective 29 June 2025 (P.L. 2025, ch. 278, art. 3, § 1).
  • Decriminalisation legacy: the pre-2022 decriminalisation (2013) is now largely superseded for lawful 21+ amounts by § 21-28.11-22; the Controlled Substances Act, Ch. 21-28, § 21-28-4.01 as amended still penalises over-limit possession (Part 1.3).

1.3 Possession, home grow, public consumption, product types

A person 21+ “shall not be arrested, prosecuted, penalized, sanctioned or disqualified… for: (1) Possessing, using, purchasing from a licensed cannabis retailer, or processing one ounce (1 oz.) or less of cannabis, or the equivalent amount in the form of cannabis concentrate; (2) Within any residence, possessing, cultivating or processing not more than a total of three (3) mature cannabis plants and up to a total of three (3) immature cannabis plants per dwelling unit for personal use and as long as all security requirements as promulgated by the commission are complied with. These limits shall apply no matter how many persons reside at the premises; (3) Within the person's primary residence, possessing up to ten ounces (10 oz.) total of cannabis per resident, in addition to any live cannabis plants lawfully kept…; (4) Assisting another person who is 21+…; or (5) Giving away… up to one ounce (1 oz.)… to a person 21+, as long as the transfer is not advertised or promoted to the public.” § 21-28.11-22(a) — verified verbatim. History: P.L. 2022, ch. 31/32, eff. May 25, 2022. Official text

Public consumption — § 21-28.11-22(e) Verified: “Nothing… shall be construed as authorizing the smoking or vaporizing of cannabis in any public place. The smoking or vaporizing of cannabis is prohibited in any public place that prohibits the smoking or vaporizing of tobacco products as well as any place that prohibits the smoking or vaporizing of cannabis including by rule, regulation, or by local ordinance.” § 21-28.11-29(a)(2) also bars smoking/vaping in school buses and public transport, on school grounds, in correctional facilities, in licensed drug-treatment facilities, and where smoke significantly adversely affects children. § -29(a)(3): no operating a motor vehicle, aircraft or motorboat under the influence — “However, a person shall not be considered to be under the influence solely for having cannabis metabolites in his or her system.”

Home-grow rules — 560-RICR-10-15-1.6 (SOS summary verified; section text via secondary reproduction): indoor only; grow area locked/secured against under-21 access; reasonable efforts to keep plants not visible from the street or public areas and to prevent odour significantly altering the odour outside; max 3 “plant sets” per dwelling; no registration required for adult-use home grows; solvent extraction with compressed flammable gas is prohibited for personal growers.

Over-limit penalties — § 21-28-4.01 Verify — the misdemeanour/felony tiers below are from a secondary reproduction; do not rely on them without opening the official section: >1 oz outside a residence = misdemeanour (up to 1 yr / $200–$500); >10 oz in a residence (excl. live plants) = misdemeanour; excess live plants <25 = misdemeanour, ≥25 plants = felony (up to 3 yrs / $5,000); ≤2 oz by ages 17–20 = civil offence $150 + forfeiture + drug-awareness/community service. Civil offence — confirmed in the audit (Q37a): possession of 1 oz or less by a person 18+ “shall constitute a civil offense” under § 21-28-4.01(c)(2)(iii) — $150 civil penalty + forfeiture (1st/2nd violation within 18 months). This is the 2013 decriminalisation provision (P.L. 2012, ch. 221 & 233).

1.5 Hemp

“‘Hemp’ or ‘industrial hemp’ means the plant Cannabis sativa L. and any part of that plant, including the seeds thereof and all derivatives, extracts, cannabinoids, isomers, acids, salts, and salts of isomers, whether growing or not, with a delta-9 tetrahydrocannabinol concentration of not more than three-tenths percent (0.3%) on a dry weight or per volume basis regardless of moisture content, and which satisfies the requirements of this chapter.” § 2-26-3(9) — verified verbatim. § 2-26-3(10): hemp-derived consumable CBD products “shall only be sold to persons age twenty-one (21) or older.” Official text

Do not conflate two tests: the statutory plant definition is delta-9-based; post-harvest product compliance in RI is measured as total THC under the regs/guidance below.

Licensing — § 2-26-5 Resolved in Run 2 — official text read: growers, handlers, licensed CBD distributors and licensed CBD retailers must hold a Commission hemp licence; the application includes names/addresses and a certificate of analysis for seed/plants. The pre-2025 fees are confirmed as current: grower/handler $2,500 per 2 years + $250 non-refundable application fee (§ 2-26-5(e)); a grower/handler may obtain one CBD distributor and/or one CBD retailer licence at no additional cost (§ 2-26-5(f)); all other distributor/retailer applicants pay $500/year (§ 2-26-5(g)). Full hemp licence detail is in Part 7.

Federal change & RI’s response

  • PL 119-37, § 781 (division B; signed 12 Nov 2025) redefines federal hemp to a total THC standard (incl. THCA) at 0.3% dry weight, excludes synthetic/manufactured cannabinoids, and caps final hemp-derived cannabinoid products at 0.4 mg total THC per container. § 781’s own commencement date is 12 Nov 2026 — but PL 119-103, § 2019 (signed 2 Sep 2026) does not delay it: until 11 Dec 2026, § 781 applies only to products containing cannabinoids “not capable of being naturally produced” by the plant (the § 297A(1)(C)(ii)(I) and (1)(C)(iv)(I) categories). Two dates for RI readers: from 12 Nov 2026 the non-naturally-producible exclusions apply; from 11 Dec 2026 the rest of § 781 applies, including the total-THC/THCA definition and the 0.4 mg/container cap. 1.6 Packaging, labelling, advertising, delivery, zoning & buffers

  • Packaging / labelling (560-RICR-10-10-2.6–2.7): child-resistant (Poison Prevention Packaging Act, 16 CFR Part 1700), tamper-evident, resealable for multi-serving, no fluorescent/neon colours, protects from contamination, no toxic impartation; an exit package is required if the inner container is not child-resistant. Labelling must include (per a secondary compilation of the 30 Jan 2026 CCC guidance Verify): business + licence name/number, “tested” statement, seed-to-sale ID, cannabinoid profile, Poison Control number, five required warnings, universal symbol ≥1″×1″. Edibles additionally: mg per serving/package, ingredients/allergens.
  • Advertising — § 2.8 read in Run 2 Resolved in Run 2: the old DBR medical regime (no TV/radio/print, no billboards/public-space ads) was replaced. Current rule (560-RICR-10-10-2 § 2.8): online (21+ age-gated), broadcast, print, outdoor/billboards and other CCC-approved media are permitted; every ad must carry the universal symbol in colour, the licence number and “For Ages 21+ and medical cannabis patients”, and must target an audience ≥85% reasonably expected to be 21+. Prohibited: curative/unproven therapeutic claims; targeting under-21s; child-attractive images/fonts; fluorescent/neon colours; images of children/minors; displaying consumption/use/transfer; encouraging excessive consumption; combining with alcohol/nicotine/other controlled-substance content; placement within 500 ft of a pre-existing K-12 school; depicting persons under the influence, pregnant or breastfeeding; trademark violations. 1.8 Employment & drug testing

“Nothing… shall be construed to require employers to accommodate the use or possession of cannabis, or being under the influence of cannabis, in any workplace or… while an employee is performing work, including remote work. Employers may implement drug use policies which prohibit the use or possession of cannabis in the workplace or while performing work [or] being under the influence… provided that unless such use is prohibited pursuant to the terms of a collective bargaining agreement, an employer shall not fire or take disciplinary action against an employee solely for an employee's private, lawful use of cannabis outside the workplace and as long as the employee has not and is not working under the influence of cannabis except to the extent that: (1) The employer is a federal contractor or otherwise subject to federal law… such that failure to take such action would cause the employer to lose a monetary or licensing related benefit…; or (2) The employee is employed in a job… that is hazardous, dangerous or essential to public welfare and safety” — for such jobs the employer may prohibit use within the 24-hour period prior to a scheduled shift; examples listed: aircraft/watercraft/heavy equipment/commercial vehicles/school buses/public transport operation, explosives, public-safety first responders, emergency/surgical medical personnel. § 21-28.11-29(d) — verified verbatim, official statute. Official text

§ -29(e): employers may still act for violation of a workplace drug policy or working under the influence. § -29(b)(2): nothing requires an employer “to accommodate the medical use of marijuana in any workplace” — contrast § 21-28.6-4(c) (secondary text): no school/employer/landlord may penalise a person solely for status as a cardholder (landlord cultivation exception). Drug testing: § 28-6.5-1 — full official text read in Run 2 Resolved in Run 2: an employer may test only where there are reasonable grounds from specific job-performance aspects plus specific contemporaneous documented observations; a first positive result leads to referral to an RI-licensed substance abuse professional, not termination on that basis — but an employee whose testing shows continued use despite treatment may be terminated (§ 28-6.5-1(a)(3)). 3.1 Level of government — clear answer

City/town (municipal) level only. Rhode Island has 39 cities & towns and 5 counties with no county government and no county cannabis regulation, licensing or tax found in any source. State law operates through municipalities: the 3% local excise goes to the city or town of delivery (§ -13(c)); zoning compliance is municipal; the 2022 opt-out was by municipal referendum. The geographic zones in § 21-28.11-10.3 are state licensing zones composed of municipalities — they are not local governments.

3.2 Opt-in / opt-out, moratoriums, caps

Structure (the Act, as described in CCC/legislative materials): municipalities that did not already host a licensed cannabis business could put the statutory question to voters at the Nov 2022 election: “Shall new cannabis related licenses for businesses involved in the cultivation, manufacture, laboratory testing and for the retail sale of adult recreational use cannabis be issued in the city (or town)?” Existing compassion centres / cultivators / manufacturers / labs were grandfathered, and their host towns could not opt out of those existing operations. 31 of 39 municipalities held the referendum; 8 did not (Central Falls, Cranston, Exeter, Foster, Pawtucket, Portsmouth, Providence, Warwick — per contemporaneous reporting Secondary).

7.3 Requirements (statute § 2-26-5(a)–(c) + rule §§ 1.6–1.8)

  • Every grower, handler, distributor and retailer must hold a CCC hemp licence (§ 2-26-5(a)–(c)).
  • Application: name/address of supervisor and of partners/consultants; seed/plant certificate of analysis showing ≤ the § 2-26-3 delta-9 THC maximum; GPS facility location, seed-to-sale tracking and security layout; cultivation/extraction method; pre-planting verification of ≤0.3% THC dry weight; purchase agreement with a handler/processor/distributor/retailer.
  • NCIC fingerprint criminal-history check via State Police / Attorney General / local law enforcement (§ 2-26-5(c)(7)); disqualifying felonies listed (Ch. 21-28 felony, murder, manslaughter, 1st/2nd-degree sexual assault, child molestation, kidnapping, arson, mayhem, robbery, burglary, breaking & entering, assault with a dangerous weapon, felony assault), subject to the Fair Chance Licensing Act § 28-5.1-14. CCC Bulletin 2026-3 Index: NCIC required prior to initial licensure, with a continuing duty to notify CCC of disqualifying convictions; hemp compliance contact CCC.HempCompliance@ccc.ri.gov.
  • Grower applications: the pre-transfer rule text (§ 1.6(F)) sent all grower applications to DEM for review before issuance — a pre-transfer arrangement. Since 29 June 2025 the Commission (CCC) is the licensor under § 2-26-5, and no current primary source gives DEM an application-review role (audit Q31b). Zoning compliance, seed-to-sale + recall procedures, owner/lessee statement, COA attestations required.
  • Licence is for one location only; non-contiguous growing areas need separate licences; records kept 5 years (§ 1.8).
  • CCC may temporarily suspend new hemp applications/issuance for market study, oversight, or public health/safety/environmental reasons; renewals may continue (§ 2-26-5(h)–(i)).

7.4 Cultivation, testing, operations (rule §§ 1.9–1.15)

  • Sampling/testing: mandatory annual + periodic inspections; lot sampling 30 days prior to harvest by a testing agent (not the licensee), flowering tops 5–8 inches; every lot sampled/tested (except performance-based sampling). >0.3% total THC = prima facie non-compliance; the lot is barred from commerce and may be ordered destroyed at licensee expense; written retest request within 10 days at licensee cost; labs report non-compliant results/disposal to USDA.
  • Post-harvest acceptable THC (consumables): 1 mg/serving, 5 mg/package, or 0.3% dry weight — consistent with the Run 1 CCC Guidance eff. 8 Dec 2025 (oral products max 1 mg total THC/serving, max 5 mg/package; tinctures max 100 mg/package; flower under 1% total THC; inhaled concentrates under 5 mg/package).
  • Prohibitions: conversion to delta-9 THC / synthetic cannabinoids “strictly prohibited” unless CCC variance (§ 1.8(I)); child-appealing shapes and cannabis-leaf shapes banned; no combining with alcohol/tobacco/nicotine/synthetics; opaque child-resistant packaging; labelling must show THC/CBD per serving and per package in red, bold, underlined type > size 6, with the FDA warning verbatim; no disease claims without FDA approval; no advertising targeting under-21s; retail sales 21+ with government ID, separate display area.
  • Extraction (§ 1.12): butane extraction prohibited per § 2-26-8(b); allowed: mechanical, non-volatile/food-grade solvent, professional closed-loop CO₂, volatile solvent if CCC-authorised/approved, other CCC-approved methods; flammable/heat methods need DEM + State Fire Marshal/local fire approval.
  • Security: firearms prohibited except AG-licensed guards under Ch. 5-5.1 or law enforcement; emergency plan required.
  • Pesticides: FIFRA § 3-registered OR minimum-risk under 40 CFR § 152.25(f); DEM registration verified.
  • Destruction: grind + mix to ≥50% non-hemp waste, to a licensed RI solid waste facility or CCC-approved alternative.
  • Reporting (§ 1.13): grower end-of-year report by Dec 1; handler by Dec 31; distributor/retailer end-of-year report (sales, manifests, COAs, off-site venues/festivals); CCC reports grower status to USDA monthly and files an annual report by Dec 15; licence info transmitted to USDA via the HeMP system within 30 days (7 CFR § 990.7).
  • Penalties (§ 1.15): negligent violation → corrective action plan; 3 negligent violations in 5 years = ineligible to produce hemp for 5 years; conduct beyond negligence → suspension/revocation + penalties under § 2-26-10 + report to the RI AG and US Attorney within 30 days; administrative penalties up to $1,000/violation/day (no immediate threat) or $2,500/violation/day (immediate threat or unlicensed activity).

11.1 Testing, failures, recalls, labs

  • Quarantine-first system (§ 2.26): all product is quarantined until it passes required testing. Dried batch ≤15 lbs, single strain; concentrates sampled only when fully homogenised with no further processing. The lab uploads results to the tracking system and issues a COA within 2 business days; the COA expires after 1 year, after which product is treated as untested and needs a full retest. Failed batches are kept separate/identifiable.
  • Retest/remediation: retest by the same lab, using the retained sample or a CCC-approved resample, batch unaltered. Remediation only with CCC approval (sole discretion; CCC considers public health and the licensee’s failure history), then full retesting required. If no retest/remediation, the batch “must be immediately destroyed” per § 2.25 and logged in the tracking system. (Secondary, rhodeislandcannabis.org, consistent in spirit: microbiological/water-activity failures may be remediated on written request; heavy-metal/pesticide failures may not — retest only.)
  • Test categories (RIDOH 216-RICR-60-05-6 § 6.21, Index): finished plant material — cannabinoid potency (Δ9-THC, CBD, THCa, CBDa), microbiological (aerobic bacteria, coliforms, bile-tolerant gram-negative bacteria, pathogenic E. coli, Salmonella, yeast/mould), water activity, pesticides, metals (As, Cd, Pb, Hg); extracts/concentrates/infused products — potency, residual solvents, pesticides, metals.
  • Lab licensing/independence: labs are licensed by RIDOH under 216-RICR-60-05-6; CCC adopts those regulations and may enforce them (560-RICR-10-10-1 § 1.13, Cornell mirror). Labs are licensed only for tests they demonstrate, renew annually, undergo annual proficiency testing and RIDOH on-site inspection; samples are taken by the laboratory, not producers (CCC Sept 2026 lab presentation — CCC describes RI contaminant standards as “some of the strictest in the nation”). Statute § 21-28.11-11 Index: a lab must report contamination results to CCC, RIDOH and DEM within 48 hours; no lab interest-holder/employee (or immediate family) may hold an interest in or be employed by a cultivator/manufacturer/retailer, and vice versa; lab licence qualifications include 21+, RI residency, NCIC check, tax compliance.
  • QA programme (§ 2.28): Commission/RIDOH random sampling a minimum of 4× per calendar year; no establishment sampled more than 1×/year; no remuneration to licensees/labs.
  • Recalls (§ 2.27): CCC/RIDOH may require a recall on a public health/safety/welfare risk (incl. contaminants or non-compliance with RIDOH testing regulations); the licensee initiates immediately under its approved recall plan; CCC may require destruction of recalled product.

11.5 Security and transport

  • Security (§ 2.13): systems to deter/prevent unauthorised entry and theft; firearms prohibited except AG-licensed guards (Ch. 5-5.1) under written contract and on-duty law enforcement; retail perimeter well-lit at all times (cultivation/manufacturing: adequate lighting, motion-activated allowed); visitors without a commercial card escorted at all times + visitor log; cannabis stored in a locked area with commercial-grade non-residential locks; alarm at all perimeter entry points/windows with automatic notification to law enforcement, tested/inspected at least every 30 days — if failure is expected >8 hours, close the premises unless CCC approves an alternative; video: digital/network recorders, date/time embedded (EST), failure notification to designated personnel + the Commission, ≥4 hours battery backup, continuous live feed access for the Commission, coverage of all grow/store/weigh/package/process/manufacture/sale areas, ingress/egress, POS, security room and tracking-system access points, 24/7 recording, activity identifiable within 20 ft of ingress/egress, surveillance recordings retained a minimum of 60 calendar days (§ 2.13(H)(1)(e) — audit-confirmed, Q42a); surveillance equipment in a locked security room; security records kept on-site + digital backup ≥24 months; emergency plan including cybersecurity.

11.6 Waste, environment, energy

  • Waste (§ 2.25): store/secure/manage per all applicable federal/state/local law + DEM rules; before disposal render unusable and plant material indistinguishable — grind + incorporate with non-consumable waste so the mixture is ≥50% non-cannabis by volume (other methods need prior CCC approval); then to a licensed RI solid waste facility or CCC-approved alternative; whole-plant/contaminated-batch destruction in a CCC-acceptable manner (CCC may consult law enforcement); comprehensive waste records kept (may be via the tracking system).
  • Odour (§ 2.23): ventilation/filtration preventing odour significantly altering the outside environment and preventing mould; CCC-approved odour plan; municipal permits/inspection.
  • Sanitation (§ 2.22): potable hot/cold water, non-porous surfaces, pest screening, OSHA compliance; all waste — including liquid, chemical, hazardous, pesticide and solvent waste — per DEM + federal/state/local law.
  • Pesticides — cannabis (§ 2.24): only “minimum risk” pesticides under 40 CFR § 152.25(f), labelled for all/other/bedding/unspecified plants/crops, label must not prohibit indoor/greenhouse use, active + inert ingredients EPA food-use eligible, DEM-registered for sale in RI, used per label; no application after the vegetative stage (visual buds/flower or <18 hrs light per 24 hrs); detailed records + video recording during applications.

12.2 Drug testing — § 28-6.5-1 resolved (official full text)

§ 28-6.5-1 Verified. History through P.L. 2022, ch. 234, art. 1, § 5, eff. 31 Dec 2022. Title: “Testing permitted only in accordance with this section.”

An employer may require a drug test only if ALL of the following are met: (1) reasonable grounds from specific job-performance aspects plus specific contemporaneous documented observations of appearance/behaviour/speech that the employee may be under the influence impairing job performance; (2) sample given in private; (3) a positive result leads to referral to an RI-licensed substance abuse professional, not termination on that basis (termination allowed for continued use despite treatment; further testing per the referral allowed); (4) positives confirmed by a federally certified lab via GC/MS or equally accurate technology; (5) employer pays and offers an independent retest opportunity; (6) reasonable opportunity to rebut/explain; (7) employer has promulgated a compliant drug-abuse-prevention policy; (8) results confidential (positives disclosed only on a job-related need-to-know basis + legal defence). Violation = misdemeanour, ≤$1,000 fine / ≤1 year; civil punitive damages + fees + injunction available. Exceptions: federally regulated drivers (49 CFR Part 40/382), federally mandated public utility/mass transit and highway maintenance testing, the IMPACT iron workers programme. 12.3 Driving — § 31-27-2 (official text)

§ 31-27-2 Verified (first 325 of 662 lines read).

  • (b)(1): 0.08% BAC per se applies to alcohol only. Guilt may also rest on DRE testimony or proof of being under the influence “to a degree that rendered the person incapable of safely operating a vehicle”; legal entitlement to use the drug is no defence.

12.7 Medical cannabis in schools — 2018 Act (H8011) & RIDOH rule

12.9 Pets / veterinary

The hemp rule defines “consumable” to include ingestion “by a human or animal” and covers “hemp-derived consumable CBD products” for ingestion by a human or animal (560-RICR-10-20-1 § 1.5(H), (P)) — so animal hemp/CBD products fall within the hemp consumable framework (the 21+ retail rule is drafted for persons). No separate RI veterinary or pet-product rules, dosing standards, or veterinary prescribing provisions were found in Ch. 2-26 or 560-RICR-10-20-1 as read, and no RI veterinary cannabis rules were found elsewhere.
