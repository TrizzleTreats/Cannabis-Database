What Run 1 established (Parts 0–6)

  • Both adult-use (21+) and medical cannabis are legal. Adult-use retail sales are reported to have begun 1 October 2022 — secondary sources only; the Parts 0–6 audit found no primary State source for the date (Part 14, A2). The CCB, created by Act 164 (2020), has administered the Medical Cannabis Registry and medical dispensaries since 1 March 2022 (Part 0, Part 1).
  • Two major 2026 laws. Act 176 (S.278) doubled the possession and retail transaction limits to 2 ounces, created the §912 event-permit pilot, repealed the integrated licence, and halves outdoor cultivator fees Tiers 1–5 from 1 July 2027. Act 178 (S.323) moved hemp from the Agency of Agriculture to the CCB (new 7 V.S.A. §§851–858) (Part 1.9).
  • Taxes: 14% excise + 6% state sales tax + 1% local option where adopted; medical patient sales exempt from the excise; Vermont decouples from IRC §280E. FY2026 cannabis tax revenue was $30,758,285 (JFO); FY2024 is now settled at excise $17,380,977 / total $24,671,141 (JFO, Part 14, B8) (Part 2).
  • Local control is municipal, not county — retail requires an Australian-ballot opt-in vote under 7 V.S.A. §863 (Part 3).
  • Six licence types; cultivation and retail applications are closed to new applicants while manufacturing, wholesale, propagator and testing-lab applications are open. Licence counts from secondary compilations conflict (541 vs 539) — the CCB's daily portal is authoritative (Part 4, Part 5).
  • Potency caps exist: flower ≤30% THC; concentrates >60% THC generally not sold to the public (packaged-vape exception); edibles ≤100 mg THC per package, ≤5 mg per serving (Part 1.3).
  • Corrections to the research brief's assumed citations: hemp was formerly 6 V.S.A. Chapter 34 (not Ch. 204), repealed by Act 178; the medical programme sits in 7 V.S.A. Chapters 35 and 37 (not 18 V.S.A. Ch. 86); manufacturer tiers are 1–3 (not 1–2); the integrated licence no longer exists.

What Run 2 established

  • Hemp is now a CCB programme on paper. Act 178 (S.323, eff. 18 June 2026) moved hemp from the Agency of Agriculture to the CCB under new 7 V.S.A. §§851–858 — but growers must register with both the Board and USDA, because Vermont has no USDA-approved state plan; USDA licenses producers directly. The audit confirmed the CCB's §854 registry is not live as at 24 Oct 2026 — the CCB hemp page still describes the pre-Act 178 regime (Part 13, Group A).
  • Two numbering questions from Run 1 are resolved. §834 is Act 178's sales restriction on unregistered hemp/cannabis shipments; §835 is Act 176's commercial cannabis compact, which cannot take effect until one of four federal triggers in §835(l) occurs.
  • Three history corrections. Decriminalisation was Act 76 (2013), not Act 39 (the end-of-life act). Medical cannabis was Act 135 (2004), bill S.76. Automatic expungement is S.234 (2020), not Act 86/88.
  • No federally recognised tribes — the BIA denied the St. Francis/Sokoki petition (No. 068). State-recognised Abenaki tribes under 1 V.S.A. ch. 23 have no tribal cannabis or hemp programme.
  • No per se THC driving limit (23 V.S.A. §1201 is impairment-based), no consumption lounges, and Act 176's §912 event permits are retail-only with consumption expressly banned.
  • Property search found five Vermont listings on 420Property plus one on LoopNet — including a price conflict on the Waterbury–Stowe Road property ($1,950,000 vs $500,000) flagged VERIFY. No Vermont cannabis listings were found on CREXi or LandWatch.

1.3 Possession, home grow, public consumption, product types and potency

Possession (adults 21+) — 18 V.S.A. §4230a(a)(1), current text

Verbatim: a person 21 years of age or older who "possesses two ounces or less of cannabis or 10 grams or less of hashish and two mature cannabis plants or fewer or four immature cannabis plants or fewer… shall not be penalized or sanctioned in any manner by the State or any of its political subdivisions." The limit "shall not include cannabis cultivated, harvested, and stored in accordance with section 4230e of this title." Last amended by Act 176 §§4, 31, eff. 1 July 2026 (limits doubled from 1 oz / 5 g). Source: legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/084/04230a, accessed 23 Oct 2026.

Home cultivation — 18 V.S.A. §4230e

Per dwelling unit: maximum 2 mature + 4 immature plants regardless of the number of adults in the unit. Cultivation only on property lawfully possessed or with written consent of the owner; plants must be screened from public view; access limited to persons 21+ with permission. Civil penalties $100 / $200 / $500 (first/second/subsequent). Drafting flag (audit-confirmed still stale): §4230e(a)(3) as printed still refers to a "one-ounce possession limit in section 4230a" — a stale cross-reference after Act 176 doubled the limit (Research Notes 12a). Source: Vermont Statutes Online, Ch. 084, accessed 23 Oct 2026.

Public consumption — 18 V.S.A. §4230a(a)(2); 7 V.S.A. §833

"A person shall not consume cannabis in a public place" ("public place" has the meaning in 7 V.S.A. §831). Civil penalty: "not more than $100.00 for a first offense; not more than $200.00 for a second offense; and not more than $500.00 for a third or subsequent offense." Municipalities may add civil ordinances. The section does not exempt a person from DUI laws, school discipline on school property, or corrections rules; a landlord may ban possession/use of lighted inhaled cannabis in a lease. Sources: §4230a; §833 (Ch. 31).

Retail transaction limit — 7 V.S.A. §907(b)

As amended by Act 176 Sec. 2 (eff. 1 July 2026), a retailer may provide two ounces or equivalent per single transaction to a person 21+ showing photo ID (previously one ounce). Source: Act 176 As Enacted PDF; Ch. 33 text. (Audit-confirmed — Part 12.5.)

Prohibited products and potency caps — 7 V.S.A. §868

Last amended Act 166 (2024), eff. 10 June 2024. Verbatim operative list — the following are prohibited:

"(1) cannabis flower with greater than 30 percent tetrahydrocannabinol; (2) flavored oil cannabis products sold prepackaged for use with battery-powered devices and any cannabis flower that contains characterizing flavor that is not naturally occurring…; (3) cannabis products that contain delta-9 THC and nicotine or alcoholic beverages; (4) any cannabis… designed to make the product more appealing to persons under 21."

§868(b): solid/liquid concentrates >60% THC may be produced and sold licensee-to-licensee but not sold to the public, except liquid concentrates >60% THC prepackaged for battery-powered devices, which may be sold to the public. Exceptions apply for retailers with a medical endorsement under §907. Note: Act 176 as introduced/passed by the Senate would have removed potency caps; that provision was not enacted — the caps above remain current law. Source: Vermont Statutes Online, §868, accessed 23 Oct 2026.

Edibles — 7 V.S.A. §881(a)(3)

  • Single package: ≤100 mg THC (exceptions: non-consumables/topicals, solid concentrates/oils/tinctures, and dispensary sales).
  • Servings: ≤5 mg THC per serving.
  • Labelling must include servings, dates, ingredients, onset information, warnings, and the standard symbol; opaque, child-resistant packaging required.
  • Note: Act 176 Sec. 1, which would have raised the package limit (a 200 mg figure appeared in earlier drafts), appears as [Deleted] in the enacted act — 100 mg remains the limit. Sources: §881; Act 176 As Enacted PDF.

Delivery

No general adult-use delivery permit exists in current law: in Act 176 as enacted the delivery-permit section is "Sec. 6. [Deleted.]". 7 V.S.A. §907(e): "Delivery of cannabis to customers is prohibited, except as provided in subsection (f)" — delivery is allowed only by a retailer holding a medical-use endorsement, to registered patients/caregivers (§907(f)(2)), and by medical dispensaries under Ch. 37 (§971(b)(3) — §1.4). A delivery-permit scheme appeared in S.278 as passed by the Senate but was not enacted in Act 176. (Audit-confirmed, Part 14 A14.)

1.4 Medical programme — 7 V.S.A. Chapters 35 and 37

The CCB administers the Medical Cannabis Registry and medical dispensaries (since 1 March 2022, per §843(b)(3)–(4)); patients are no longer required to designate a dispensary (CCB med-dispensaries page). Full Ch. 35 and Ch. 37 texts opened directly, accessed 23 Oct 2026.

Qualifying conditions — 7 V.S.A. §951(8)

Verbatim: "'Qualifying medical condition' means: (A) cancer, multiple sclerosis, positive status for HIV, AIDS, glaucoma, Crohn's disease, Parkinson's disease, post-traumatic stress disorder, ulcerative colitis, or treatment of these, if the disease or treatment results in severe, persistent, and intractable symptoms; or (B) a disease or condition or its treatment that is chronic and debilitating and produces cachexia/wasting syndrome, chronic pain, severe nausea, or seizures." (§951 last amended Act 166 §7, eff. 10 June 2024.)

Registry — §952

  • Registered patient/caregiver may cultivate ≤6 mature and 12 immature plants (harvest stored indoors does not count toward the limit), possess ≤2 oz, and purchase at a licensed dispensary.
  • The Board must approve or deny an application within 30 days; a Health Care Professional Verification Form is required.
  • Applicants under 21 require a treating relationship of at least 3 months with the verifying health care professional (exceptions include terminal illness, cancer, AIDS, hospice care, recent onset, etc.).
  • §953: an applicant under 18 must have the application signed by a parent/guardian.
  • §955: "(a) A registration card shall expire three years after issuance… (b) $50.00 registration and renewal fee for patients and caregivers" (last amended Act 166 §8, eff. 10 June 2024).

Caregivers — §954

Background checks required — a name-and-date-of-birth Vermont criminal conviction record check plus the Child Protection Registry (DCF) and the Vulnerable Adult Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation Registry (DAIL); the current text is not fingerprint-based (audit, Part 14 A15). Verbatim: "(d)(1)… a caregiver may serve not more than two patients at a time, and a patient shall have only one registered caregiver at a time. A patient may serve as a caregiver for one other patient." A patient under 18 may have two caregivers ((d)(2)).

Medical dispensaries — Ch. 37

§971(b): a dispensary may "(1) be vertically integrated under one license; (2) sell tax-free…; (3) deliver…; (4) allow patients/caregivers to purchase without leaving their vehicles; (5) produce and sell products with higher THC than permitted for a cannabis establishment; (6) produce/sell products not otherwise permitted… as determined by Board rulemaking; (7) sell larger quantities…" §973 covers dispensary activities. §977 fees: RESOLVED (audit) — one-time $1,000 application fee; $5,000 annual renewal; $50 annual registry card fee per owner/principal/financier/employee; no $20,000 fee appears in the current text (Research Notes 7a; source: legislature.vermont.gov — 7 V.S.A. ch. 37 full text). The CCB states it has voted to pause new applications for medical-only dispensaries (CCB med-dispensaries page, ccb.vermont.gov/ccb/med-dispensaries, accessed 23 Oct 2026). As of the CCB list dated 7/1/2026, exactly one business is listed with type "Medical Dispensary" — Vermont Patients Alliance, Inc, 188 River St, Montpelier — alongside a long list of retailers holding a "Medical-use endorsement" (see Part 5).

Medical vs adult-use distinction (post-2022)

Medical patients buy through (a) Ch. 37 dispensaries or (b) adult-use retailers holding a medical-use endorsement (effective 1 July 2025 for qualified retailers; +$250 fee — Part 4). Dispensary/endorsed-retailer sales to registered patients directly or through registered caregivers are exempt from the 14% excise tax (32 V.S.A. §7902(d)(2) — Part 2). Dispensaries may sell higher-THC and larger-quantity products than adult-use establishments (§971(b)).

Out-of-state reciprocity

RESOLVED (audit) — Vermont is not reciprocal: out-of-state medical cannabis cards are not valid in Vermont (UVM official policy, verbatim, Part 12.9; Research Notes 1). Caveat: no CCB page states it in terms.

1.5 Hemp

New law — Act 178 (S.323), signed/effective 18 June 2026

Official Act Summary (legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT178/ACT178 Act Summary.pdf, accessed 23 Oct 2026): Act 178 "transitions oversight of hemp growing and processing, hemp products, and enforcement from AAFM to the Cannabis Control Board… conform[s] to evolving federal law that would redefine THC concentration limits for hemp. The CCB will require annual licensure and fees for hemp producers, processors, and certain hemp products." Full detail in Part 7.

New statute — 7 V.S.A. §§851–858 (opened directly)

  • §851 Findings (verbatim): "the federal legal status of most hemp products will be contingent upon an amendment to 7 U.S.C. §1639o, to take effect in November 2026, pursuant to… Pub. L. No. 119-37."
  • §852 Definitions: "Hemp" = Cannabis sativa L. "with the federally defined tetrahydrocannabinol concentration level of hemp." Products prohibited by Board rule, or not lawful in interstate commerce, are cannabis products, not hemp products.
  • §853: hemp is an agricultural product, subject to the Required Agricultural Practices (6 V.S.A. §4810).
  • §854: hemp producers must register with the Board and with USDA; hemp processors must be licensed. Verbatim: "All hemp-derived products containing or reasonably expected to contain more than 0.4 mg THC shall be registered with the Board prior to sale."
  • §§855–857: rulemaking, testing, enforcement and penalties. (The §857 history line in the online text cites "Act 158" — the audit confirmed this anomaly as printed; Research Notes 12c.)
  • §858 Fees (verbatim): "Producer: $50.00. Processor: $500.00. Product: $75.00."

Repeal of the old hemp law

6 V.S.A. §562 page (Vermont Statutes Online, opened directly): "Repealed. 2025, No. 178 (Adj. Sess.), §15, eff. June 18, 2026"; 6 V.S.A. Chapter 034 header reads "[Repealed.]". The research brief's references to "6 V.S.A. Ch. 204 / hemp" do not match current official text.

CCB hemp guidance (partly pre-Act 178 — staleness flag)

CCB, "Understanding Hemp in Vermont," ccb.vermont.gov/hemp, accessed 23 Oct 2026. The page still reflects the pre-Act 178 framework in part: VAAFM ceased its State Hemp Program in 2022 and cultivation oversight returned to USDA (apply via the USDA HeMP platform). Under CCB guidance/rule, a product is treated as "cannabis" (not hemp) if it has: >0.3% THC dry weight — potency measured as total theoretical THC = ([delta-9 THC] + ([THCA] × 0.877)) (CCB Rule 2.9.2) — or >1.5 mg THC/serving, or >10 mg THC/package (exception where CBD:THC ratio >20:1), or contains beverage alcohol, or has a dominant market appeal of mimicking intoxicating effects. Synthetic cannabinoids are categorically prohibited (CCB Rule 2.17); CB1/CB2 agonists are controlled drugs (CVR 13-140-11). Implementation of the new §§851–858 registration regime: RESOLVED (audit) — NOT live as at 24 Oct 2026; the CCB hemp page still describes the pre-Act 178 regime and no §854 forms or §858 fees appear on ccb.vermont.gov (Part 7.4–7.5; Research Notes R2-1).

Delta-8 / synthetic cannabinoids — VAAFM statement

Federal hemp definition change / PL 119-103 delay

1.6 Packaging, labelling, advertising, zoning/buffers

1.8 Employment and drug testing

  • 18 V.S.A. §4230a(e): nothing in the cannabis laws requires an employer to permit or accommodate cannabis use in the workplace, and no cause of action arises for discharge under an employer policy.

3.2 Opt-in and limits on municipal power — 7 V.S.A. §863

Last amended Act 176 §11, eff. 1 July 2026; opened directly.

  • §863(a)(1) (verbatim, operative): "Prior to a cannabis retailer operating within a municipality, the municipality shall affirmatively permit… by majority vote… by Australian ballot at an annual or special meeting warned for that purpose." The opt-in vote applies only to retail; other licence types do not require an opt-in vote (CCB Municipal Guidance and CCB municipal FAQ agree).
  • Rescission: a municipality may vote to opt back out by the same process, but the rescission does not apply to retailers already operating.
  • §863(b): a host municipality may establish a local cannabis control commission, which may issue local control licences conditioned only on zoning bylaws (24 V.S.A. §4414) or sign/public-nuisance ordinances (24 V.S.A. §2291), and "shall not assess a fee for a local control license."
  • §863(d): a municipality shall not prohibit a cannabis establishment by ordinance/bylaw, regulate it so as to prohibit it, condition it on bases other than §863(b), or exceed its authority. The CCB's municipal FAQ adds that towns may not limit licence types other than retail opt-in, that local commission approval is required before the CCB issues a licence only if the town has a commission, and that indefinite local delay exceeds §863 authority.
  • State-collected local fee: a $100 local licensing fee (§910(10)) is collected by the CCB and distributed to the municipality annually (§846(c) as amended by Act 176).
  • CCB guidance documents (ccb.vermont.gov/guidance) include "Local Cannabis Commission Guidance" (Jan 2026 — opt-in, commissions, cannabis cultivation districts and setbacks) and "Municipal Guidance" (Sept 2026).

PART 7

Hemp Licences (after Act 178)

7.1 Legal framework

  • Hemp oversight moved from the Agency of Agriculture, Food and Markets (VAAFM) to the Cannabis Control Board (CCB). The official Act 178 summary (Office of Legislative Counsel): "This act transitions oversight of hemp growing and processing, hemp products, and enforcement from the Agency of Agriculture, Food and Markets to the Cannabis Control Board… The Cannabis Control Board will require annual licensure and fees for hemp producers, processors, and certain hemp products." Multiple effective dates beginning 18 June 2026. Source: Act 178 Act Summary (PDF)
  • New law: 7 V.S.A. chapter 31, subchapter 3 (§§851–858), each section history-lined "(Added 2025, No. 178 (Adj. Sess.), § 16, eff. June 18, 2026)" — except §857 (see 7.6). The former 6 V.S.A. chapter 34 was repealed. Full text: legislature.vermont.gov — 7 V.S.A. ch. 31
  • §851 Findings; purpose (verbatim, in part): "the federal legal status of most hemp products will be contingent upon an amendment to 7 U.S.C. § 1639o, to take effect in November 2026, pursuant to the Continuing Appropriations, Agriculture, Legislative Branch, Military Construction and Veterans Affairs, and Extensions Act of 2026, Pub. L. No. 119-37." Purpose: "to unify oversight of cannabis and hemp-derived cannabinoids under the Cannabis Control Board… [and] to support small-business hemp producers and processors in taking advantage of opportunities for the cultivation and sale of hemp and hemp products."
  • §852 Definitions (verbatim key points): "'Grower' means a person who is registered with the Board and the U.S. Department of Agriculture to produce hemp. 'Grower' also means producer." "'Hemp' means the plant Cannabis sativa L. … with the federally defined tetrahydrocannabinol concentration level of hemp. Hemp is considered an agricultural commodity." A hemp product does not include a substance/product that "(i) is prohibited or deemed a regulated cannabis product by administrative rule of the Board; or (ii) is not lawful in interstate commerce"; an excluded product "is considered a cannabis product as defined by subdivision 831(3)… provided, however, that a person duly licensed or registered by the Board lawfully may possess such products in conformity with the person's active hemp processor license." "'Processor' means a person who is licensed by the Board to process hemp. A retail establishment selling hemp products or hemp-infused products is not a processor."
  • §853: "Hemp is an agricultural product that may be grown as a crop produced, possessed, marketed, and commercially traded in Vermont pursuant to the provisions of this chapter and administrative rules of the Cannabis Control Board"; cultivation must comply with the Required Agricultural Practices under 6 V.S.A. §4810. (Contrast cannabis, §869 — Part 11.6.)

7.3 Requirements, denial, enforcement (§§854(d), 855, 856, 857)

  • Denial (§854(d)): Board may deny licensure/registration/renewal if the applicant (1) "fails to establish that its activities comply with State and federal law"; (2) "refuses the Board or its lawful designees entry upon its premises to inspect and confirm compliance, including by sampling hemp and hemp products for potency testing"; (3) fails to submit requested information; or (4) fails to submit the §858 fee.
  • Rulemaking (§855(a)): Board may adopt rules to require testing of hemp during growth for THC levels; specify testing methods (including CBD:THC ratio or genetic/taxonomic testing); require inspection/supervision during sowing, growing, harvest, storage, processing, distribution; require labels free of false/misleading claims and claims contrary to the federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act; establish sanitary requirements for licensed processing facilities; establish product-registration requirements "including requirements that each product be sampled and tested by a laboratory recognized by the Board"; require cannabinoid-content disclosure/labelling; require licensees/registrants (including out-of-state purveyors of registered products) to "obtain and maintain commercially reasonable insurance, which for producers of consumer products in final form shall include product liability insurance"; prohibit hazardous additives; define craft processors; exempt product categories from registration; establish consumer-sale requirements for THC/cannabinoid products; prohibit false/misleading/unsubstantiated claims. §855(b): Board shall adopt rules for processor licensure and to "regulate the use of processing facilities and equipment to permit processors to use the same equipment for hemp and cannabis processing and to prevent cross contamination between hemp and cannabis."
  • Testing failure / enforcement (§856): On notice that THC exceeds "the applicable federally defined tetrahydrocannabinol concentration level of hemp," the grower/processor "shall arrange for disposal, remediation, or destruction… in a manner consistent with applicable State and federal law." Board may enter/inspect grow/process premises and retail locations, take samples, inspect records/equipment/vehicles, and issue "stop sale" orders (appeal to the Board within 15 days after receipt).
  • Penalties (§857): Board may assess violations/administrative penalties against licensees/registrants and persons required to be licensed/registered who fail to obtain credentials; "The compliance and enforcement authorities and procedures applicable to cannabis establishments shall apply"; final penalties enforceable by civil collection action in Superior Court.
  • Related new offence — §834 (Sales restrictions), added by Act 178 §20, eff. 18 June 2026 (same chapter): "No person shall cause unregistered hemp or unregistered cannabis purchased by mail or through a computer network… to be shipped to anyone other than a licensed cannabis laboratory in this State." Knowing/intentional violation: imprisonment ≤5 years or fine ≤$5,000 or both; Attorney General civil penalty ≤$5,000 per violation (each shipment a separate violation), injunctive relief, disgorgement, and a Consumer Protection Act (9 V.S.A. §§2451 et seq.) violation.

RESOLVED — the compact is §835; §834 is the sales restriction

Run 1 gap 7, this item: the commercial cannabis compact is 7 V.S.A. §835; §834 is the Act 178 unregistered-product sales restriction (Part 7.3). §835 history line: "(Added 2025, No. 176 (Adj. Sess.), § 27b, eff. July 1, 2026.)" Audit note (codification): Act 176 as enacted numbered the new section "§ 834" in its Sec. 27b; it is codified as §835 because Act 178 §20 separately added §834 (sales restrictions). Act 176 Sec. 27a recites the legislative intent, citing Executive Order 14370, 90 F.R. 60541. Full text: legislature.vermont.gov — 7 V.S.A. ch. 31; Act 176 as enacted — audit 24 Oct 2026.

  • §835(a)–(b) (verbatim substance): "'Agreement' means an agreement relating to commercial cannabis authorized pursuant to this section… 'Foreign licensee' means the holder of a cannabis license issued pursuant to the laws of another state… The Governor is authorized to enter into an agreement with another state or states authorizing medical or commercial cannabis activity, or both, between entities licensed under the laws of the contracting state and entities operating with a Vermont license, provided the activities are lawful and subject to licensure in the contracting state, and the agreement prohibits interstate transportation by unauthorised means or through any jurisdiction that does not authorise that transportation."
  • §835(c)–(j) conditions: a foreign licensee may deal with a Vermont licensee (and vice versa) subject to the section; a foreign licensee "shall not engage in commercial cannabis activity within the boundaries of this State without a Vermont license" or without local authorisation; the agreement must require the contracting state to impose standards meeting or exceeding Vermont's on public health/safety, mandatory participation in Vermont's seed-to-sale tracking system, testing, packaging/labelling, quality assurance/inspection, marketing restrictions, and adulterated/misbranded product identification and destruction; must require equivalent advertising/marketing/labelling/sale restrictions in the contracting state; must provide for collection of all applicable taxes; must include public-health emergency/recall/embargo cooperation, mutual investigation cooperation, and provisions reflecting CCB programmes for inclusion of communities disproportionately impacted by cannabis criminalisation.
  • §835(k) pre-execution process: Governor must submit the proposed agreement/amendment to the Board and the Joint Fiscal Committee for 60 days' review and comment (and state in writing reasons for not incorporating recommendations) and post it on the Governor's and Board's websites for 30 days' public comment.
  • §835(l) — federal trigger (verbatim):

"An agreement entered into pursuant to this section shall not take effect unless one of the following occurs: (1) federal law is amended to allow for the interstate transfer of cannabis or cannabis products between authorized commercial cannabis businesses; (2) federal law is enacted that specifically prohibits the expenditure of federal funds to prevent the interstate transfer of cannabis or cannabis products between authorized commercial cannabis businesses; (3) the U.S. Department of Justice issues an opinion or memorandum allowing or tolerating the interstate transfer of cannabis products between authorized commercial cannabis businesses; or (4) the Attorney General issues a written opinion that implementation of agreements entered into under this section will not result in significant legal risk to this State based on review of federal judicial decisions and administrative action." (Full text confirmed verbatim by the audit, 24 Oct 2026.)

11.2 Licence rules (Rule 1; Rule 2 §§2.11, 2.13; 7 V.S.A. §901)

  • Term/renewal: All licences are valid one year, expiring "at midnight on the eve of the anniversary of the date the license was issued"; annual renewal on Board forms with §910 fees (7 V.S.A. §901(b)–(c), as reproduced at LawServer — pre-Act 176 text; the one-year term is consistent with Run 1). Rule 1 renewal mechanics (per LCAR filing text and CCB "License Renewal Requirements (one-pager)," July 2025): renewal applications may be filed up to 90 days before expiration and must be complete at least 30 days before expiration (90/30-day figures as summarised from Rule 1 in secondary guidance — VERIFY against current Rule 1 text); a timely-filed renewal keeps the licence/card valid until the Board responds; a licensee who does not file before expiration "is no longer a licensee upon the date their license expires" and may no longer operate. ID cards follow the same pattern (Rule 1.16/1.17 filing text). Indoor/mixed licensees must also file the annual energy report as a condition of renewal (Rule 2.5.6(a), Part 11.6). Employee cards change 1 July 2027 (Act 176 §13, §910 as amended — audit-confirmed): a biennial $100 fee and two-year employee licences, replacing the $50 annual card.
  • Transfers / ownership & control changes: There is no free-standing transfer; a change of control or location requires a licence renewal application — Rule 1.17 (current numbering; §1.15.2 in the original 2021 proposed text): required "(i) When an interest holder who has control of a licensee will be changed, including by adding a person who will be an interest holder and will have control, removing a person who is an interest holder and has control, or transferring control from one person… to another"; and "(ii) When a licensee wishes to change the physical site of operations." Exception: death of a controlling interest holder is notified at renewal. Rule 2.11.1: "mere disclosure may be insufficient… It must seek a license renewal prior to any change in control, to the extent required by Board Rule 1.17." CCB guidance: a licence that changes location or ownership without authorisation "shall be regarded as having expired" (Rule 1.17(g)), and CCB published dedicated "Ownership Changes Guidance" (March 2026) and "Retail Relocation Guidance" (Jan 2026 — noting the Board voted in May 2026 to pause consideration of retail relocations). Ongoing duty to disclose/update application information: Rule 2.11; background/financial re-disclosure may be required (Rule 2.2.3).
  • Ownership caps: 7 V.S.A. §901(d)(3)(A): "an applicant and its affiliates may obtain a maximum of one [licence] of each type of license" — audit-corrected §901(d)(1): "There shall be six types of licenses available: (A) cultivator; (B) propagator; (C) wholesaler; (D) product manufacturer; (E) retailer; (F) testing laboratory" (the integrated licence is removed; Act 176 also repeals §909 eff. 1 July 2026 and strikes §901(d)(3)(B); sources showing seven types including "integrated" are pre-Act 176) — and §901(d)(3)(C) permits multiple testing-laboratory licences. 11.3 Cultivation rules

  • Canopy tiers are as in Run 1 Part 4 (Rule 1.3.1 tiers 1–6; indoor 1,000–25,000 sq ft and outdoor 1,000–37,500 sq ft per secondary summary of adopted Rule 1; mixed Tier 1 = 1,000 sq ft indoor + 125 plants outdoor). Plant counts as such apply to (i) outdoor Tier 1 alternative measure ("fewer than 125 cannabis plants," §910 as amended) and (ii) breeding-stock tiers under §901(d)(2)(A)(i) ("plant canopy size… or plant count for breeding stock"). Co-located cultivators are capped at the combined Tier 6 canopy limit unless the Board waives it (Rule 2.2.18(c)).
  • Indoor/outdoor/greenhouse definitions (Rule 2.1.3): "Indoor cultivation" = growing using artificial lighting; "Outdoor cultivation" = growing without artificial lighting, except "the minimum amount of artificial lighting necessary to keep photoperiod plants in a vegetative state," which "must not extend beyond May 1st in a calendar [year] or past when the specific cultivar can sustain vegetative growth under natural sunlight, whichever comes first"; "Greenhouse" = a thermally isolated sunlit structure/area used for cultivation ≥180 days per calendar year. Outdoor licensees may keep mother plants, seeds and clones in propagation/vegetation indoors during winter months but "may not cultivate Cannabis indoors" (Rule 2.4.7).
  • Cultivator operating duties: submit cultivation schedule, waste management plan and integrated pest management plan within 60 days of licensure (Rule 2.3.8); maintain pesticide-usage records accessible on site (Rule 2.3.1); sanitation duties (Rule 2.3.7); unannounced inspections including sampling, photography, records/equipment/vehicle inspection, and Board use of samples for genetic/taxonomic research (Rule 2.3.6); personal-use grow under 18 V.S.A. §4230e must be physically separated, unambiguously labelled, never enter the regulated market or the Inventory Tracking System (Rule 2.3.10). §4230e itself — audit-confirmed: limits are 2 mature / 4 immature plants per person and per dwelling unit; civil penalties (b)(2) are $100 / $200 / $500; and §4230e(a)(3) still provides that plants "shall not count toward the one-ounce possession limit in section 4230a" — a stale cross-reference, confirmed still present: it was not updated to two ounces despite Act 176's amendment of §4230a (history: added 2017, No. 86, §7; last amended 2021, No. 158, §8, eff. May 31, 2022; no 2026 amendment).
  • Pesticides/seasons: Rule 2.3.1/2.9.6 incorporate VAAFM pesticide rules and guidelines; CCB "Pesticide Use Guidance" (Oct 2025) "explains cannabis cultivators' obligations for the use of pesticides and lists approved active ingredients" (CCB guidance index). Use of unauthorised pesticides is a serious (Category II) Rule 4 violation per the Rule 4 text noted in Run 2 searching, and willful pesticide misuse makes product adulterated/destructible (Rule 2.2.19(b)). No separate CCB "season" rule beyond the outdoor photoperiod-lighting May 1 cut-off and the winter indoor-storage allowance (above) was located. Outdoor security must be operating "for no less than the three-week period preceding a harvest, as well as while drying, curing, or storing a harvested crop" (Rule 2.4.2(b)).

11.4 Security, storage, transport

  • Outdoor (Rule 2.4): "Outdoor Security Management Practices" = fencing; video surveillance with unobscured views; alarm system; motion-activated flood-light; security services/guard; controlled point of access. Minimums scale by tier: T1 ≥1 practice; T2 ≥2 (incl. fencing); T3 ≥3 (incl. fencing); T4 ≥4 (incl. fencing); T5 ≥5 (incl. fencing); T6 all 6. Fencing must prevent unauthorised entry (variance possible case-by-case). Video standards: retain footage ≥30 days, date/time stamps, usable images in situ lighting, clear identification of persons/activities, exportable, ≥720p. Crops visible from a public road need a concealment barrier (fence/hedge/structure). Board may impose more after inspection or after >1 theft in a year. Drying/curing/storage security = indoor standard (2.4.6).
  • Indoor/mixed (Rule 2.5.1): all perimeter doors/windows locked, keys only to ID-card holders; operational security alarms on perimeter doors/windows (Tier 1/mixed exempt unless Board requires case-by-case); video surveillance with continuous monitoring of any space containing cannabis/harvested cannabis/products, meeting the 2.4.2(c) standards. Manufacturers and wholesalers must meet §2.5.1 (Rules 2.6.1, 2.7.1); manufactured product (not flower) at wholesalers must be in a "reasonably secure locked space."
  • Retail (Rule 2.8.2): §2.5.1 plus commercially-installed alarm system, video covering point-of-sale areas, all entrances/exits and all storage/handling areas, footage kept ≥30 days with devices continuously recording, strict access controls to storage/handling areas, employee ID badges, full security information to the Board on request, and customer numbers in the retail area limited to what staff present can easily monitor. Retail buffer: a retailer "shall not be located at a place where the sale of a regulated drug would constitute a violation of 18 V.S.A. §4237(d)" (Rule 2.8.1) — i.e. the Rule incorporates the drug-sale school-zone statute by reference; Rule 1/2 state no separate numeric buffer in the text read. Audit — RESOLVED (Run 1 gap 7c): §4237(d) prohibits the conduct within 500 feet of school property ("abutting" = shares a boundary, or adjacent separated only by a river, stream or public highway), and §4237(e) adds, for (c) violations, "in addition to any other penalty," imprisonment of not more than 10 years. (1,000-foot claims are wrong for Vermont.)
  • General: visitors restricted/escorted/logged, no under-21s where cannabis is located (Rules 2.2.13, 2.3.2); theft/loss must be reported to the Board immediately on discovery and entered in the Inventory Tracking System (Rule 2.2.17); all workers must hold and carry a Board ID card (Rule 2.2.20).
  • Transport/manifests (Rule 2.2.7): only ID-card holders may transport between establishments; transport by vehicle (exceptions for same/contiguous property or line-of-sight); in a vehicle, product not visible from outside, not accessible from the driver's seat, vehicle unmarked; vehicle registration/inspection/insurance current. Before departure the transporter must enter the load in the Inventory Tracking System and generate a transport manifest stating departure date/location/time, destination establishment(s), transporter name + ID card number, product names/quantities (weight and unit) per destination, estimated arrival, vehicle make/model/plate, and a signature/time line for the receiver; manifest transmitted to receivers before departure; stops logged contemporaneously with reasons; vehicle locked/secured and attended where possible; >20 lb dry-weight cannabis requires a secure locked storage compartment; accidents/diversions/losses reported to the Board within 24 hours; receivers log receipt time and reconcile inventory in the system within 24 hours, noting discrepancies. No transport may cross state borders (2.2.7(k)).

11.5 Waste disposal

Rule 2.2.8: all applicable federal/state/local waste law applies, including chemical/dangerous/hazardous waste and pesticide/agricultural-chemical rules; "Cannabis or Cannabis Products must be rendered unusable and unrecognizable before disposal," by Board-enumerated methods in a public policy; organic material with no THC or <0.3% THC need not be rendered unusable and may be composted on site or otherwise lawfully disposed of; disposal must be tracked in the Inventory Tracking System (Rule 2.2.6). A waste log is a required on-site record (Rule 2.2.1(h)). CCB "Waste and Waste Disposal Guidance" (July 2025) "explains proper cannabis waste disposal procedures" (CCB guidance index). Retailers may collect/reuse/recycle consumer packaging waste under Rule 2.8.5 (receptacles inside, labelled "consumer packaging waste," supervised; residual cannabis removed/destroyed; packaging sanitised/disinfected before reuse; child-resistant containers reusable only while still meeting 7 V.S.A. §861(16)). Consumer packaging generally must be reusable and not plastic (Rule 2.2.9(b)).

11.6 Environmental, water, energy

  • Statute — 7 V.S.A. §869 (text via Justia 2022 version and 2024–2026 court filings quoting the current section): "(a) A cannabis establishment shall not be regulated as 'farming' under the Required Agricultural Practices, 6 V.S.A. chapter 215, or other State law, and cannabis… shall not be considered an agricultural product, farm crop, or agricultural crop for the purposes of 32 V.S.A. chapter 124, 32 V.S.A. §9741, or other relevant State law" (with a narrow current-use exception for small cultivators ≤1,000 sq ft on enrolled agricultural land). "(b) The cultivation, processing, and manufacturing of cannabis… shall comply with all applicable State, federal, and local environmental, energy, or public health law…" All cultivators must comply with RAP §6 (conditions/restrictions/operating standards), §8 (groundwater quality and investigations) and §12 (subsurface tile drainage), as administered/enforced by the Board (§869(d)(1) in current numbering). Outdoor cultivators under §869(f) get RAP-style protection from municipal bylaw regulation except minimum canopy setbacks from property boundaries/highway edges — the scope of §869(f) was litigated in In re Taft St. DRB & NOV Appeal, 2025 VT 27 (Vt. Supreme Court), per court documents hosted at legislature.vermont.gov/vtcourts.gov. Contrast hemp: hemp cultivation is subject to the full RAPs (7 V.S.A. §853(b)).
  • Water/wastewater (Rule 1.5.2–1.5.3, Cornell text): cultivators on municipal water must file the utility's letter certifying capacity to supply sufficient water; users of municipal/off-site wastewater must file a letter certifying capacity to accept the quantity and anticipated strength of wastewater. Tier 5–6 applicants must additionally state, under DEC's Wastewater System and Potable Water Supply Rule: where/by what means water will be withdrawn and stored, on-site potable/sanitary use and daily headcount, anticipated wastewater volume/strength and how it will be treated/disposed of, whether DEC Indirect Discharge Rules and Underground Injection Control Rules apply, and the means of collecting/storing/treating/discharging wastewater. Indoor cultivators must identify any applicable DEC Drinking Water and Groundwater Protection Division rules. CCB Municipal FAQ adds: establishments "must abide by all generally applicable environmental regulations, whether state or municipal," municipalities may not impose cannabis-specific environmental rules beyond general zoning authority, and the Board imposes additional environmental standards via Rule 2 (ccb.vermont.gov/municipal-faq).
  • Energy (Rule 2.5.3–2.5.6): Vermont Commercial Building Energy Standards (CBES) apply to indoor facilities for envelope insulation, non-cultivation lighting, ventilation and HVAC efficiency (cultivation HVAC exempt from economiser/heat-recapture requirements); greenhouses must meet CBES for HVAC efficiency. Fans/clean-water pumps should meet current federal DOE efficiency standards at purchase. Lighting: indoor cultivation lighting minimum 1.9 Photosynthetic Photon Efficacy (PPE); greenhouse envelope u-factor ≤0.7 and supplemental lighting minimum 1.7 PPE (exempt if total connected lighting load <40 kW); compliance within one year of licensure. Dehumidification (within one year): standalone units minimum integrated energy factor 1.77 L/kWh (≤8.0 cu ft) or 2.41 L/kWh (>8.0 cu ft), or integrated HVAC/chilled-water with on-site heat recovery meeting ≥75% of annual dehumidification reheat, or desiccant systems for ≤50°F dewpoint designs. Reporting (Rule 2.5.6): licensees "must report energy efficiency benchmarks annually to the Board as a condition of license renewal," annually update equipment-maintenance/calibration SOPs for major energy equipment, and annually assess/report energy-reduction opportunities including renewables assessment, demand-reduction strategies, and "engagement with energy efficiency programs offered by Efficiency Vermont, Burlington Electric Department, or Vermont Gas Systems." CCB "Energy Efficiency Guidance for License Renewals" (July 2025) "outlines energy standards required for cultivation licensees." Tier 1 home-occupancy cultivators are exempt from §§2.5.3/2.5.6(b)–(c) (Rule 2.12). Act 176 energy provisions: no new cannabis energy standard was identified in the Act 176 text read in this run — any Act 176 energy-reporting change is VERIFY; the operative requirements remain Rule 2.5.3–2.5.6 above.

12.1 Paraphernalia / smoke shops

  • Possession is expressly protected: 18 V.S.A. §4230a(a)(1) (as amended by Act 176): a person 21+ "who possesses… paraphernalia for cannabis use shall not be penalized or sanctioned in any manner by the State or any of its political subdivisions or denied any right or privilege under State law." Source: Act 176 as enacted (PDF) — verified live.
  • What "cannabis product" excludes: 7 V.S.A. §831(3) provides a cannabis product "does not mean a 'tobacco product' as defined in 32 V.S.A. §7702, a 'tobacco substitute' as defined in section 1001 of this title, or 'tobacco paraphernalia' as defined in section 1001 of this title" — i.e. pipes/devices sold as tobacco paraphernalia sit in the Title 7 tobacco framework (7 V.S.A. §1001 et seq.), not the cannabis-establishment framework, and no CCB licence is required to sell paraphernalia alone; conversely, a "vaporizer cartridge containing cannabis oil… and any device designed to deliver cannabis into the body through inhalation of vapor that is sold at a cannabis establishment" is a cannabis product (§831(3)). Disposable (non-rechargeable, non-refillable) vape pens are prohibited as products (Rule 2.6.3(b)(vi), subject to CCB disposable-vape guidance, Aug 2025). Sale of tobacco paraphernalia to minors and tobacco-substitute rules under Title 7 ch. 10 were not re-read in full in this run — VERIFY specific smoke-shop age/licensing provisions (tobacco retailer licensing is via the Department of Liquor and Lottery / Division of Liquor Control for tobacco products) before advising a smoke shop.

12.2 THC driving limits and impairment testing

  • No per se THC limit — verified from the statute text. 23 V.S.A. §1201(a) provides a person shall not operate/attempt to operate/be in actual physical control of a vehicle on a highway: "(1) when the person's alcohol concentration is: (A) 0.08 or more; (B) 0.02 or more if… operating a school bus…; (C) 0.04 or more if… operating a commercial vehicle…; or (2) when the person is under the influence of alcohol; or (3) when the person is under the influence of any other drug or under the combined influence of alcohol and any other drug." The only numeric thresholds in §1201 are for alcohol; for drugs (including THC) the standard is impairment ("under the influence"). §1201(e): entitlement to use a drug under Vermont law "shall not constitute a defense." Source: legislature.vermont.gov — 23 V.S.A. §1201, accessed 23 Oct 2026. (Secondary compilations agree: "Per Se Law? No.")
  • Testing — 23 V.S.A. §1202 (implied consent / evidentiary tests): an evidentiary test is required when an officer has reasonable grounds to believe the person violated §1201, and also for a surviving operator in a fatal/serious-injury collision where the officer has reasonable grounds to believe the person has any alcohol/drugs in their system; refusal consequences include licence suspension of at least six months (as summarised in §1202(d) warnings); the person has a right to consult an attorney and must decide within 30 minutes of the initial attempt to contact counsel. Saliva: §1202 expressly contemplates saliva testing — "Any saliva test administered under this section shall be used only for the limited purpose of detecting the presence of a drug in the person's body and shall not be used to extract DNA information." Source: legislature.vermont.gov — 23 V.S.A. §1202, accessed 23 Oct 2026. The 2019 authorisation of roadside oral-fluid screening (preliminary, presence-only; evidentiary confirmation is by blood test under §1202's scheme) is consistent with this text. Audit correction: the attribution "Act 83 (2019) added oral fluid screening" is not supported — §1202's history line contains no Act 83 (2019's Act 83 was S.160, agricultural development, unrelated); the history line ends "…2017, No. 62, § 9; 2017, No. 132 (Adj. Sess.), § 14; 2019, No. 164 (Adj. Sess.), § 23; 2025, No. 41, § 2, eff. July 1, 2025; 2025, No. 91 (Adj. Sess.), § 123, eff. April 30, 2026." Roadside oral-fluid screening and evidentiary saliva testing are distinct concepts. A saliva result shows presence, not impairment or a numeric THC level, and cannot itself establish a §1201(a)(3) violation.
  • Related: consumption in a public place is prohibited (7 V.S.A. §833, violations punished under 18 V.S.A. §4230a); the mandatory product warning states "It is against the law to drive or operate machinery when under the influence of this product" (Rule 2.2.10(a)).

Sec. 34: the act is effective on passage except Sec. 32 (marijuana penalties), eff. Jan. 1, 2021. The court notifies VCIC (which notifies the FBI's NCIC), the arresting agency and other record holders; custodians must respond "NO RECORD EXISTS" to inquiries. Primary source (audit): Act 167 as enacted (PDF), Secs. 31–34; bill status legislature.vermont.gov — S.234. 12.4 Consumption lounges and events

  • No consumption lounges: no licence type in 7 V.S.A. §901(d) authorises on-premises consumption, and 7 V.S.A. §833 prohibits consumption in a public place "unless specifically authorized by law." The earlier Senate-passed version of S.278 that would have allowed consumption at events ("Notwithstanding section 833… persons 21 years of age or older may consume cannabis… at an event") was replaced in the enacted Act 176 — do not cite the draft.
  • Act 176 event permits — retail-only, NO consumption (verified from the act as enacted): 7 V.S.A. §912(a): "The Board may grant event permits to licensed cannabis retail establishments in good standing… No cannabis or cannabis products shall be consumed at an event authorized by this section." §912(b) eligibility: written approval of the local cannabis control commission (or municipal legislative body if none), which may impose public-protection/traffic/nuisance conditions; security plan (no access by intoxicated persons or under-21s; premises secured against diversion/inversion; lawful use); product sale plan (quantities/types; transport/monitoring/security/display/sale in conformity with law and Board rule); capacity to administer/enforce the plans, including county law-enforcement or licensed private security (26 V.S.A. ch. 59) if the Board requires; proof of commercially reasonable insurance; other Board health/safety requirements including attendee limits. §912(c): "Annually, the Board shall issue not more than ten permits for public events"; single event, ≤24 hours, single access-controlled location, only in a municipality that has voted affirmatively to permit retail; not at a location where alcohol is sold/furnished for on-premises consumption; the retailer may not sell simultaneously at its store and the event (except a contiguous event location); only registered adult-use products may be sold. §912(d): deviations are licensing violations. §912(e): fee $500, split 50% to the host municipality / 50% to the Cannabis Regulation Fund. §912(f): Board procedures adopted under 3 V.S.A. §835 (public website posting + ≥2 public hearings; no APA rulemaking required; procedures have force of law). Act 176 §10: CCB must initiate rulemaking by 1 July 2027 for event permits and report to the legislature by 15 November 2027 (including whether §912 should be repealed as scheduled). §912 is repealed 1 July 2028 (Act 176 §29(c)). Event sales are taxed: Act 176 amended 32 V.S.A. §§7902/7904/7906 so event-permit holders collect/file/pay the cannabis excise tax like retailers. Sources: Act 176 as enacted, §§5, 7–10, 29, 32 — verified live. This matches Run 1.

12.5 Cannabis tourism and visitor purchase limits

  • No residency requirement; same transaction limit for everyone: 7 V.S.A. §907(b) as amended by Act 176 §2 (verbatim):

"In a single transaction, a retailer may provide two ounces of cannabis or the equivalent in cannabis products, or a combination thereof, to a person 21 years of age or older upon verification of a valid government-issued photograph identification card."

  • Nothing in §907 or Rule 2.8.3 requires Vermont residency — Rule 2.8.3 requires inspection of a current, valid government-issued photo ID at entry and again before purchase, refusal to sell without it, and prohibits recording/retaining visitor personal information beyond ordinary retail-transaction/age information without permission. Visitors are therefore subject to exactly the resident limits: 2 oz / 10 g hashish possession (§4230a), the 2 oz single-transaction cap, no public consumption (§833), no transport across state lines (Rule 2.2.7(k); product warning), and no consumption lounges (12.4). CCB's pre-Act 176 "Purchase Limits Guidance" was withdrawn as of July 2026 (CCB guidance index: "Purchase Limits Guidance (withdrawn). As of July 2026, this guidance has been withdrawn. Please read this statement on purchase limits…") — the operative limit is the amended §907(b) text above. Medical purchases by visitors: see 12.9 — out-of-state medical cards are not valid in Vermont, so visiting patients must use the adult-use market (21+).

12.6 Landlord/tenant

New 9 V.S.A. §4468b (Act 176 §30) — verbatim:

"§ 4468b. RENTAL AGREEMENTS; CANNABIS RESTRICTIONS PROHIBITED. A rental agreement shall not contain a provision that prohibits a tenant from possessing cannabis or cannabis products within the rental premises or using cannabis or cannabis products within a dwelling unit, except that a rental agreement may prohibit the use of lighted cannabis or cannabis products intended for inhalation within the rental premises. This section shall not apply to any rental agreements that are required by federal law to prohibit the possession or use of cannabis within the rental premises."

Source: Act 176 as enacted, §30 — verified live. Act 176 §31 also amended 18 V.S.A. §4230a(b)(2)(E) to preserve that §4230a does not "prohibit a landlord from banning possession or use of lighted cannabis or cannabis products intended for inhalation in a lease agreement." Net position (matches Run 1): landlords may not ban tenant possession or (non-inhaled) use inside the dwelling unit; landlords may ban smoking/vaping/lighted inhaled cannabis; federally-mandated prohibitions (e.g. in federally subsidised housing) are carved out. Note §4468b's inhalation exception is drafted as to use of lighted/inhaled products "within the rental premises," while §4230a(b)(2)(E) speaks of banning possession or use of lighted products — a minor drafting asymmetry [note, not resolved].

12.8 Medical cannabis in schools

  • No Vermont statute authorising school administration of medical cannabis was located. The controlling official guidance found is the Vermont Department of Health, "Medication Training Guide for School Nurses" (2019), Section 11, "Medical Marijuana (Cannabis)" (verbatim substance): "Marijuana remains a Schedule I (illegal) substance under federal law, potentially jeopardizing federal funding for agencies or school districts that accommodate this law… Licensed clinicians (doctors and nurses) are unable to prescribe or administer Schedule 1 drugs. Most states in the U.S. do not support the administration of these products in schools (NASN, Nov. 2018)… Until the conflict between federal and state laws is resolved, each case will be assessed on an individual basis with local LEA's and the [school nurse] to ensure an optimal outcome for the student." The guide treats FDA-approved Epidiolex (cannabidiol, approved 25 June 2018) as an ordinary prescription drug a school nurse may administer with nursing judgment. Source: healthvermont.org — Medication Training Guide for School Nurses (PDF), accessed 23 Oct 2026.
  • Separately, smoking cannabis on school grounds/buses was expressly prohibited under the former medical statute (18 V.S.A. §4474c(a)(3), 2021 text), and a 2013 Oversight Committee recommendation had proposed (but the located sources do not show enacted) "exemption from criminal and civil penalties; seizure of property for hospitals and school administering cannabis oil" — do not treat that recommendation as law. Conclusion: school-nurse administration of (non-FDA-approved) medical cannabis in Vermont schools = UNAVAILABLE / no authorising law located; case-by-case LEA assessment per VDH guidance; Epidiolex excepted as an FDA-approved prescription.
