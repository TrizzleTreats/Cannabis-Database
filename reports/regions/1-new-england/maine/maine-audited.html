Cannabis & hemp in Maine: laws, taxes, licences, holders and market

Both medical and adult-use cannabis are legal in Maine, regulated by the Office of Cannabis Policy (OCP). Hemp runs under a Maine state plan approved by USDA and administered by DACF. This dossier covers the statutes, the tax changes taking effect 1 January 2026, municipal opt-in and local fees, every state licence type, the complete official hemp grower registry, market data, and the properties publicly marketed for cannabis use (Parts 1–10) — plus the regulators' contact details, expanded history, tribal and interstate position, testing rules and labs, licence transfer and renewal rules, environmental rules, consumer and social rules, research licences, staff drug testing, cultivation and security rules, waste, official price data, sampling and promotion rules, and ballot history (Parts 11–26) — with every uncertain, conflicting or unavailable item flagged rather than filled in. Parts 27–28 (added 23 October 2026) re-verify every law category against current official Maine statutes and OCP regulations only, with verbatim quotes and last-amended dates, and inventory every state and municipal source of licence-holder data.

Last audited: 24 October 2026 — independent primary-source audit (Part 29) checked every law, tax, bill-status and count claim in Parts 1–28 against legislature.maine.gov, maine.gov (OCP, DACF, Maine Revenue Services) and Federal Register / govinfo only, with corrections applied in place. Maine statutes are as published current through 1 October 2026 (132nd Legislature). All other sources accessed 23 October 2026 unless a document or registry snapshot date is stated separately.

Overview

Summary of findings

  • Legal status: Medical cannabis legal since 1999; adult use legal since the 2016 ballot (retail from 9 Oct 2020). Hemp is legal under a Maine-run, USDA-approved state plan (DACF) — not the direct USDA licensing programme.
  • Tax headline (changed 1 Jan 2026): Adult-use sales tax rises 10% → 14%; the cultivation excise is cut from $335 → $223 per lb of flower and $94 → $63 per lb of trim. Medical cannabis carries 5.5% sales tax (8% on edibles as prepared food) and no excise. Maine decouples from IRS §280E at state level. There is no county or local cannabis sales tax — municipalities charge licence fees instead (Part 9).
  • Local control is decisive — at municipal level, not county level: Adult-use establishments may operate only in municipalities that have affirmatively opted in (28-B MRS §§401–403; operative opt-in and buffer provision §402). Counties have no role in §§401–403. A commonly cited total of ~121 opt-in municipalities could not be verified from a primary source — OCP publishes opt-in status by community and by activity, not a single deduplicated total Unverified — count from OCP's opt-in list (OCP Adult Use Opt-in Communities). Roughly 83% of Maine's 433 municipalities were estimated to prohibit adult-use retail (secondary estimate).
  • Medical sales total is not published by OCP Unavailable — Maine Revenue Services taxable-sales data is the proxy.
  • Hemp collapse in numbers: DACF's currently-licensed grower list contains only 8 growers (licences 1600–1607), versus 110 outdoor + 7 indoor licences reported at USDA plan approval in 2020/21. Maine offers one hemp licence type in practice — the grower licence. DACF does not license processors and keeps no processor list — confirmed on DACF's own pages; the 2022 proposal to create processor licensing was never implemented (see Part 29, item 13).
  • Local fees range from $300 (South Portland manufacturing; Augusta testing) to $5,000/yr (Auburn and Waterville retail; Penobscot County unorganised-territory retail) — plus a proposed, unconfirmed $22,500 in Sanford Verify. Portland's current schedule (10 Jul 2026) is $596 application + $3,969 licence for most types.
  • Zoned properties: Only a handful of Maine properties are publicly marketed specifically for cannabis. The strongest current example is 27 Sandy Creek Rd, Bridgton — $599,000, a 3,982 sq ft cultivation facility on 4.2 acres (420Property). All listings are flagged Verify — listing status unconfirmed except where the listing itself shows ACTIVE with a recent date.
  • Law accuracy audit (Part 27, 23 Oct 2026): Every law category was re-checked against Revisor of Statutes texts current through 1 Oct 2026 and OCP/DACF/MRS primary sources only. Headline corrections: the edible package limit is 200 mg THC (not 100 mg); caregiver limits were rewritten by PL 2025 c. 514; the medical anti-discrimination provision is 22 MRS §2423-E(2), not §2426; LD 2110 is enacted law — PL 2025 c. 666; and the preroll excise and 14% intoxicating-hemp tax in earlier parts are an LD 1942 committee amendment only — not enacted law. Possession (2½ oz / ≤10 g concentrate) and home grow (6 mature / 12 immature) are confirmed from the statute text.
  • Licence data sources (Part 28): 13 state-level sources are inventoried with format, access steps, update frequency and latest-data date (OCP registries and downloads last updated 8 Oct 2026; compliance data 20 Oct 2026; DACF hemp PDF dated 13 May 2026 against a page claiming 3 Aug 2026 — conflict flagged). Local records are verified for 8 municipalities from official municipal sources; Biddeford, Augusta, Waterville and all other opt-in towns are marked Unavailable because the OCP opt-in list table could not be retrieved — no portals were invented.
  • Independent audit (Part 29, 24 Oct 2026): All 25 numbered claim-groups plus 15 bills were re-checked against primary sources only. Most headline figures were confirmed. Material corrections applied in place: the Fund revenue split is now 9% of sales tax + 9% of excise tax from 1 Jan 2026 (fund at 28-B §1101, not §1051); LD 1609 was enacted only in narrowed form as PL 2025 c. 611 (background checks — the organised-crime bar was not enacted); LD 1942 and LD 1820 were never enacted; a narrow federal final rule does exist — 91 FR 22714 (28 Apr 2026) placing FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana in Schedule III (adult use remains Schedule I); and OCP's medical figure is 112,547 printed patient certifications in 2025, not a count of 110,000+ patients. Eight items remain unverified from primary sources — listed in Part 29.

How to read the flags. Verify marks a fact that is uncertain, conflicting between sources, or not confirmed against the primary source. Unavailable marks data that does not exist in the official sources or could not be retrieved. Nothing flagged has been guessed or filled in.

1.3 Possession, home grow, public consumption, product types

Primary-source correction — controlling over any conflicting figure elsewhere. The possession and home-grow limits in §1.3 are stated from the current official statute text (legislature.maine.gov, current through 1 Oct 2026, retrieved 23 Oct 2026) and quoted verbatim there: adult-use possession 2½ oz including no more than 10 g of concentrate (28-B MRS §1501(1)(B), last amended PL 2023, c. 396); home grow 6 mature / 12 immature / unlimited seedlings per person (28-B MRS §1502, last amended PL 2023, c. 220); medical patients may possess up to 8 lb of harvested cannabis and cultivate 6 mature / 12 immature (22 MRS §2423-A, last amended 2025). Lower figures in older secondary summaries reflect the superseded pre-2023 law and are not current.

  • Adult-use possession — 28-B §1501(1)(B), verbatim: a person 21 years of age or older may "use, possess or transport at any one time up to 2 1/2 ounces of cannabis or 2 1/2 ounces of a combination of cannabis and cannabis concentrate that includes no more than 10 grams of cannabis concentrate." The same 2½ oz / 10 g limits apply to transfers without remuneration (§1501(1)(C)) and to purchases from a cannabis store (§1501(1)(F)). Last amended: PL 2023, c. 396, §§20–22 (the amendment that set the concentrate figure at 10 g).
  • Home grow — adult use, 28-B §1502(1), verbatim: "a person 21 years of age or older may cultivate up to 6 mature cannabis plants, up to 12 immature cannabis plants and an unlimited number of seedlings for personal adult use" — per person, not per household, on land the person is domiciled on, owns, or has the owner's written permission to use. Under §1502(2) the grower must ensure the cannabis "is not visible from a public way without the use of aircraft or binoculars or other optical aids", take reasonable precautions against under-21 access, and attach to each mature and each immature plant a legible tag with the grower's name, driver's licence or ID number, a notation that the plant is grown for personal adult use, and the landowner's name where the land is another's. Under §1501(1)(E) the grower may also "possess all the cannabis produced by such plants at the person's place of residence or at the location where the cannabis was cultivated" — i.e. the harvest is not capped by the 2½ oz carry limit at home. Under §1502(3)–(4), a municipality (or LUPC in unorganised areas) may cap the total plants per parcel only so long as it still allows 6 mature, 12 immature and unlimited seedlings per domiciled person 21+, and may not generally prohibit home cultivation, restrict the areas where it is allowed, or charge a licence or other fee for it. Last amended: PL 2023, c. 220, §§1–4.
  • Medical possession and cultivation — 22 MRS §2423-A(1), verbatim: a qualifying patient may "possess up to 8 pounds of harvested cannabis" (§2423-A(1)(A)) and "cultivate … up to a total of 6 mature cannabis plants, 12 immature cannabis plants and unlimited seedlings for that qualifying patient" (§2423-A(1)(B); the per-patient totals apply whether the patient or a designated caregiver cultivates). Dispensing is capped per transaction: a caregiver may "transfer up to 2 1/2 ounces of harvested cannabis to a qualifying patient in one transaction" (§2423-A(2)(A-1)), and a dispensary likewise may not dispense more than 2½ oz in one transaction; for a visiting qualifying patient the cap is 2½ oz in a 15-day period. Source: current text of 22 MRS Ch. 558-C (legislature.maine.gov, current through 1 Oct 2026), retrieved 23 Oct 2026. Attribution corrected in Part 27: the 8 lb figure in §2423-A(1)(A) traces to PL 2017 c. 452 §4 and PL 2021 c. 669 §5 — not PL 2025; the PL 2025 changes to §2423-A are the caregiver provisions (PL 2025 cc. 512, 514, 611).
  • Public consumption — 28-B §1501(2): A person 21+ may consume cannabis "only if that person is: (1) In a private residence, including curtilage; or (2) On private property, not generally accessible by the public, and the person is explicitly permitted to consume cannabis or cannabis products on the property by the owner of the property." Consumption as an operator or passenger in a vehicle on a public way, in a residence or property operating as a day care during its hours, or by smoking in a public place is prohibited; a violation is a civil violation with "a fine of not more than $100" (§1501(2)(C)). No licensed social-consumption lounges are in operation as of Oct 2026 — the LD 1365 consumption-lounge bill was rejected by the House in April 2026 (see §1.9 and Part 18).
  • Product types (both programmes, via licensed/medical channels): Flower, pre-rolls, edibles, vapes, concentrates, tinctures, topicals, capsules, beverages. Adult-use edible limits — confirmed in statute: an edible cannabis product may not contain more than 10 mg of THC per serving and not more than 200 mg of THC per package (28-B MRS §703(1)(F), as amended by PL 2023, c. 396, §19 — package limit raised 100 mg → 200 mg; §703 official text). The pinpoint citation to 18-691 CMR ch. 30 for these limits could not be verified against a maine.gov rule text Unverified pinpoint — operative authority is §703. THC beverages in the adult-use/medical system are distinct from hemp THC drinks in general retail (Part 1.5 / Part 7). Home extraction with inherently hazardous substances is prohibited (§1503) except as licensed.

1.4 Medical programme

  • Qualifying conditions — physician-discretion model: yes. Since the 2018 amendments removed the statutory condition list, there is no list of qualifying conditions; a medical provider certifies, in their professional opinion, that the patient is likely to benefit. (18-691 CMR ch. 2 §5 — OCP Medical Use rules page.)
  • Card / registry identification: Registration is voluntary for patients — legal protection flows from holding a valid written certification (tamper-resistant, 1-year validity, no condition disclosure required) plus photo ID. A patient may optionally apply to OCP for a registry identification card (RIC) by mail or online portal (OCP, 162 State House Station, Augusta ME 04333-0162). Providers, caregivers (non-family) and dispensary staff must register. RIC replacement and staff card fees sit in 22 MRS §2425-A (officer/director/assistant $20–$50 range in statute).
  • Certification process: A bona-fide provider–patient relationship and an assessment of history, then written certification. Minor patients: a parent or legal guardian acts as caregiver.
  • Caregiver system: 21+, no disqualifying drug offence, criminal-history check ($31). May serve up to 5 patients (family/household-only caregivers need not register). Cultivating caregivers register by plant count — $240 per group of up to 6 mature plants (to 30 mature = $1,200) — or by canopy: up to 500 sq ft, up to $1,500 (22 MRS §2425-A; OCP testimony). Caregivers may operate a caregiver retail store, wholesale unlimited amounts to and from other registrants, and deliver. 286 caregivers reported operating a retail store in 2025; 90 active dispensary registration certificates in 2025 (70 in 2024) — OCP 2025 MMCP Annual Report (OCP 2025 MMCP Annual Report). Corrected (Part 29, item 21): OCP does not publish a unique-patient count. Its metric is printed patient certifications — 112,547 in 2025 (110,484 in 2024) — which include reprints and are not unique patients. Registered caregivers numbered 1,539 at 31 Dec 2025 (down from 1,677).
  • Reciprocity / visiting patients: yes — PL 2019 c.209 (LD 538): visiting patients need only their home-state registry card or certificate; no Maine provider certification or caregiver designation is required, provided their home state authorises out-of-state use of its credential. OCP maintains the approved-states list: maine.gov/dafs/ocp/medical-use/visiting-patients.

1.5 Hemp (summary — full detail in Parts 7–8)

  • Definition gap — quote both sources: the statute text, 7 MRS §2231(1-A)(D), defines hemp as Cannabis sativa L. with a delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol concentration of not more than 0.3% on a dry weight basis, "or as otherwise defined in federal law" (§2231 official text); DACF operates its USDA-approved plan on Total THC (dry weight) at or below 0.3% (DACF hemp page). Both quotations are accurate — the gap is statute text vs USDA-plan operation, and federal law moves to a total-THC basis in late 2026 (below). 7 MRS §2231(1-A), as amended by PL 2025 c.416 (LD 1920), now defines nonintoxicating cannabinoids (9 listed: CBD, THCV, CBC, CBTC, CBL, CBE, CBG, CBDV, CBN), potentially intoxicating cannabinoids (12 categories including delta-10/9/8/7-THC, delta-6a,10a-THC, exo-THC, metabolites, hydrogenated forms incl. HHC, synthetic forms incl. dronabinol, ester forms incl. THC-O-acetates, varin forms excl. delta-9-THCV, and analogs) and potentially intoxicating hemp product — defined by negation (§2231(1-A)(H)): a product falls outside that category only if its final form contains both (1) a concentration of less than 0.3% potentially intoxicating cannabinoids and (2) a ratio of more than 10:1 of nonintoxicating to potentially intoxicating cannabinoids. Do not present "<0.3% AND >10:1" as what such a product contains.
  • LD 1920 (emergency legislation, law without the Governor's signature 24 Jun 2025, PL 2025 c.416) prohibits sale of potentially intoxicating hemp products to under-21s (§2231(12)), requires that such a product that is not a beverage, salve or topical be in child-resistant, tamper-evident packaging (§2231(13)(A)), and bans packaging/labels likely to be confused with trademarked products (§2231(13)(B)).
  • Delta-8 / intoxicating hemp: Not a scheduled substance if within the hemp definition (17-A §1102 carve-out), but post-LD 1920 it is age-gated and packaging-regulated. A claim in secondary sources that delta-8 is "effectively prohibited outside dispensaries" is Verify — no such general-retail ban was found in the LD 1920 text retrieved.
  • THC drinks: Hemp THC beverages were the subject of the DACF working group cited in LD 1920 testimony (a proposed 5 mg cap appears in testimony — not confirmed as enacted Verify). A proposed 14% tax on potentially intoxicating hemp products from 1 Jan 2027 appears only in an LD 1942 committee amendment — not enacted law as of statutes current through 1 Oct 2026 (see Parts 2 and 27).
  • Federal redefinition (2025) — both laws confirmed in primary sources (Part 29, item 17): PL 119-37 §781 (enacted 12 Nov 2025; effective 365 days after enactment = 12 Nov 2026) redefines hemp at 7 USC §1639o on a total tetrahydrocannabinols (including THCA) ≤0.3% dry-weight basis, excludes intermediate/final products containing cannabinoids not capable of being naturally produced by the plant or synthesised/manufactured outside it, and caps final products at >0.4 mg combined total THC per container (PL 119-37 (govinfo)). HR 6500 §2019 is real: Public Law 119-103, 140 Stat. 987 (2 Sep 2026), §2019 (140 Stat. 1003) provides that until 11 December 2026, §781 applies only to the two "not capable of being naturally produced" clauses — so those clauses take effect 12 Nov 2026 and the remainder applies from 11 Dec 2026. §2019 limits §781's application; it does not amend §781's effective date (PL 119-103 (govinfo)). Maine-specific DACF/OCP guidance on the federal cliff was not available in the sources retrieved — a major gap (see Research Notes).

1.6 Packaging, labelling, advertising, delivery, zoning & buffers (state)

  • Packaging/labelling (adult use, 28-B §701 & 18-691 CMR ch. 30): Opaque, child-resistant, tamper-evident; health and safety warnings; THC potency and cannabinoid profile; solvents/chemicals used; usage instructions; for edibles — ingredients, allergens, use-by/expiration, nutrition panel; licence numbers of the cultivator, manufacturer and store, plus batch number. No packaging appealing to under-21s, no confusion with trademarked products.
  • Advertising (28-B §702, ch. 30): No false or deceptive claims, no health-benefit claims, no targeting or appeal to under-21s, no unsolicited internet banner ads, no location-based device marketing (except an opt-in app with an easy permanent opt-out). Advertising/signs buffer — §702(1)(C): no advertising within 1,000 ft of the property line of a preexisting school, except that the municipality (or LUPC) may set a greater or lesser distance, not less than 500 ft. Note the difference from the establishment buffer in §402, where the municipality may only reduce (see below). A billboard ban on interstate/border highways (from Jan 2021) comes from a secondary summary Verify in §702 text.
  • Delivery: Cannabis stores may deliver (PL 2021 c.667, from 2023), as may cultivation and manufacturing licensees (PL 2023 c.679, from 2024), with OCP authorisation; ID verification (21+) before delivery or curbside sale (§504); no third-party delivery services. Corrected against the primary text of 28-B §504(9): delivery is not limited to residential addresses — a licensee operating a limited delivery service "may deliver to any location in a municipality, except locations within a safe zone designated by a municipality under Title 30-A, section 3253" (§504(9)(B) — the municipal drug-free safe zones), and may deliver to a hotel or business where it has received the written consent of an authorised employee of the hotel or business, retained and open to inspection (§504(9)(C); §511) (§504 official text). Per OCP's 2024 Annual Report, 11 stores and 1 manufacturer were authorised for delivery by 31 Dec 2024, only 5 stores were actually delivering, and 2024 delivery sales were $67,047.
  • Zoning/buffer (state minimum) — confirmed with a wording correction (Part 29, item 7): Under 28-B §402(2)(A), a municipality may not authorise a cannabis establishment within 1,000 ft of the property line of a preexisting public or private school, except that it may by ordinance allow one at a lesser distance not less than 500 ft — for establishments the municipality may only reduce the buffer. §401 is the general local-regulation provision; the operative buffer is §402 (§402 official text). The advertising buffer under §702(1)(C) differs: there the municipality may set a greater or lesser distance, not less than 500 ft. Municipal detail is in Parts 3, 9 and 10.

1.7 Seed-to-sale tracking

  • Adult use: Metrc — mandatory, from immature plant to point of sale (28-B §105). History: BioTrackTHC was awarded the contract in May 2019; the contract was mutually terminated (failure to deliver); Metrc was negotiated in 2019/20. OCP finalised a new 5-year Metrc agreement on 29 Jan 2026. Metrc charges cited historically: $40/month per licence, plant tags $0.45, package tags $0.25 Verify current pricing in the 2026 contract.
  • Medical: no Metrc; not subject to the same mandatory tracking or testing as adult use; no OCP recall authority. Registrants must use trip tickets and transaction logs for transfers — use OCP's comparative wording, not an absence of all recordkeeping. OCP patient advisory, verbatim: "While OCP has the authority to issue recalls in the Adult Use Cannabis Program, Maine law does not give OCP that same authority in the Maine Medical Use of Cannabis Program. The medical program is also not subject to the same mandatory inventory tracking or contaminant testing requirements as the adult use program." (OCP medical patient advisory.) The gap was illustrated in Oct 2026: OCP could only issue a patient advisory (not a recall) for The B Maine (Lebanon) flower and pre-rolls found with unsafe yeast, mould and microbials after a Sept 2026 complaint.

1.8 Employment & drug testing

  • Off-duty use: Under the 28-B §112 / Marijuana Legalization Act framework and 26 MRS Ch. 7, employers may not penalise or refuse to hire solely for off-duty, off-premises cannabis use; employers may prohibit use, possession and impairment at work and in safety-sensitive or federally mandated roles (26 MRS §681(8) exemption).
  • Drug-testing law overhaul — LD 2110 (signed 13 Apr 2026, most provisions effective 29 Jul 2026): Replaces "probable cause" with "reasonable suspicion" based on observable appearance, behaviour, speech or odour; ends "arbitrary" testing of general employees while allowing random, reasonable-suspicion and new "criteria-based" testing; creates a right to contest a non-negative result with a Medical Review Officer; shortens the rehabilitation period from 6 months to 12 weeks (cost now on the employee); a written policy approved by the Maine Department of Labor is still required; and medical cannabis use is a "legitimate medical explanation" for a positive test — patients are treated like prescription-medication users.

Local rules

Maine's adult-use system is opt-in: no municipality, no market. Local licence fees are in Part 9 and zoning districts in Part 10.

The opt-in rule

An adult-use establishment may operate only if "the legislative body of the town or plantation has voted to allow some or all types of cannabis establishments within the town or plantation, including that type" — and the applicant holds all local approvals, permits and licences, land-use approvals, and an active OCP licence. — 28-B MRS §§402–403

For unorganised or deorganised townships, a county commissioners' vote plus Maine Land Use Planning Commission (LUPC) land-use approval substitutes for the municipal vote. Medical is different: municipalities may regulate medical establishments under 22 MRS §2429-D, but medical does not use the adult-use opt-in vote in the same way Verify town by town.

Hemp licences

Every Maine hemp licence type, what each allows, fees, requirements, and the state-plan question — plus the unresolved processor-licence conflict.

7.1 Maine runs its own programme — not the USDA's

Maine licenses hemp growers itself under a state plan approved by USDA under 7 USC §1639p / 7 CFR Part 990 — the "Approved Plan" model. It does not use the direct USDA Hemp Producer License (that route applies in states such as NH, VT, IA and KS, per the USDA list, where Maine appears as "ME Approved Plan", list updated 17 Feb 2026).

"DACF is the licensing authority for growing hemp in Maine… DACF is operating Maine's hemp production plan approved by USDA. All hemp licensed in Maine meets USDA's definition for hemp (Total THC (dry weight) is at or below 0.3%) and can be marketed nationwide." — DACF Hemp Program page (updated 3 Aug 2026)
  • Statute: 7 MRS Chapter 406-A (§2231 is the operative section). §2231(6) (PL 2021 c.761) requires DACF rules to align with 7 USC §1639p(a)(2)(A) and 7 CFR Part 990, and to establish application, licence, per-acre and per-sq-ft fees, monitoring/sampling/testing guidelines, and criminal background checks for grower licensees and all "key participants" (owners/partners; corporate executives CEO/COO/CFO — not farm, field or shift managers).
  • Rule: 01-001 CMR Chapter 274 — Rules for Growing Hemp (DACF).
  • Other hemp law: 7 MRS §2231(10) — hemp and hemp-derived products may not be tracked as part of the medical (22 MRS 558-C) or adult-use (28-B) programmes. Food law: 22 MRS §2158-A — food, food additives and products containing hemp/CBD are not adulterated solely for that reason (LD 630, 2019). Makers of ingestible hemp/CBD products must separately be licensed under food laws (22 MRS §2901-C / Ch. 551, 562, 562-A or 28-A) — that is a food licence, not a hemp licence.

7.3 Grower requirements, THC testing, acreage, renewal

  1. Apply ≥30 days before planting, with the $100 application fee.
  2. DACF reviews and emails a draft licence agreement.
  3. Sign the agreement and pay the licence/acreage fees.
  4. Register grow sites with the local USDA Farm Service Agency (FSA).
  5. Plant in discrete lots by variety.
  6. Planting report within 14 days of planting (locations, plus confirmation that stock was ≤0.3% total THC, with third-party COA documentation: tester identity, variety/lot, test date, seed supplier and origin).
  7. DACF inspects.
  8. Report changes, crop failure and harvest dates.
  9. Notify DACF 30 days before harvest if not yet sampled.
  10. An inspector samples each lot → Certificate of Analysis.
  11. Post-harvest report. Harvest must be completed within 30 days after sampling (2025 instructions).
  • Criminal history: An FBI Identity History Summary Check is required; the licence cannot issue without it; background checks also apply to key participants. Landowner consent/affidavit is required if leasing. GPS coordinates, legal description and maps of sites are required — and are confidential by statute (shared only with government agencies for administration/enforcement; public reports must be aggregate).
  • THC testing: State sampling of every lot, random plant sampling, lab COA for total THC. Limit: ≤0.3% total THC dry weight. Crops over the limit must be destroyed (in a DACF-approved manner, at the licensee's cost) if remediation is not feasible. DACF does not certify seed and maintains no approved variety list.
  • Acreage/site rules: Acreage or sq-ft in the licence cannot be increased once finalised; non-contiguous areas or facilities more than 50 miles apart require a separate licence application and fees. The licence is site-specific and non-transferable.
  • Renewal / term: Annual — a new licence application is required every year ("If you grow hemp again next year, you must complete a new license application" — 2025 instructions). Secondary sources state licences expire 30 April each year Verify the expiry date in Ch. 274 / the licence agreement; it is not stated on the DACF page retrieved. 7.4 The processor-licence conflict — flagged, not resolved

DACF's 28 Nov 2022 Chapter 274 rulemaking notice states the statute has allowed DACF to license hemp processors since 2015 but the Department had not implemented it, and the proposed rule "incorporates licensing for hemp processors and product producers" with inspection and sampling of concentrate and products. DACF's current hemp page (updated Aug 2026) still says it does not regulate processing and keeps no processor list, and Hemp Handbook coverage states: "The hemp growers license is the only license specifically pertaining to hemp that the State of Maine offers. Maine does not yet have licensing or registration programs for processing, handling, transporting or selling hemp or CBD products."

Conclusion: processor licensing was proposed in 2022; no evidence was found that it was implemented by Oct 2026. Status = Verify — ask the DACF Hemp Program directly (hemp@maine.gov / (207) 287-7545, as published in DACF instructions).

History — expanded chronology

New detail beyond the §1.1 timeline: implementation delays, the hemp programme's federal approval, the residency-requirement litigation, and the 2026 tax change. The Legislature/OCP's official one-page chronology is at legislature.maine.gov/doc/9586.

  • Implementation delay: Gov. LePage vetoed the 2017 implementation bill; the Legislature overrode the veto of LD 1719 (2018), which established licensing, tax and local control. The Office of Marijuana Policy was created in 2019 and later renamed OCP. (Bill identity official via the Part 26 table note "Implementation delayed pursuant to PL 2017, c. 1"; narrative from secondary accounts.)
  • 2016 recount: Opponents of Question 1 requested a recount and withdrew/conceded on 17 Dec 2016 (see Part 26 for the vote-count discrepancy).
  • Hemp: Maine legalised hemp in 2015 (LD 4); the programme has run since 2016, beginning with one grower harvesting seed from less than an acre. USDA approved Maine's hemp production plan on 3 Sep 2020; DACF assumed primary regulatory responsibility under the USDA Domestic Hemp Production Program. At that date Maine had 110 outdoor licences / 362 acres and 7 indoor / 22,700+ sq ft, across all 16 counties. (Compare Part 8: 8 growers in the 2026 list.)
  • LD 630 (2019) clarified that hemp-derived CBD food produced or sold in-state is not adulterated solely for containing hemp.
  • Residency-requirement litigation: The adult-use residency requirement was challenged on 20 Mar 2020 (dormant Commerce Clause); the State ceased enforcing it in 2020 on Attorney General advice. The medical case Northeast Patients Group v. Maine DAFS, 554 F. Supp. 3d 177 (D. Me. 11 Aug 2021), struck the requirement; the 1st Circuit affirmed on 17 Aug 2022 (2–1) — the requirement was facially protectionist, and the dormant Commerce Clause applies despite federal prohibition (2022 WL 3442203).
  • Tax change 2026: The adult-use sales tax under 36 MRS §1811 rose from 10% to 14% for sales on or after 1 Jan 2026, per the enacted text: "Before January 1, 2026, 10%… For sales occurring on or after January 1, 2026, the applicable rate… is 14%." LD 1942 is a separate later proposal (pre-roll excise / intoxicating-hemp tax from 1 Jan 2027) and should not be conflated with the 2026 rate change. Part 15

Testing

Required product testing, sampling rules, failed-test and recall rules, and the certified laboratories — including an unresolved conflict in the official lab count.

Adult use — mandatory testing

Under the Cannabis Legalization Act (subchapter 6), a licensee may not sell or distribute adult-use cannabis to a consumer unless it has been tested and passed. OCP's official testing-data page groups mandatory testing into six categories: (1) residual solvents, poisons and toxins; (2) harmful chemicals ("metals"); (3) dangerous yeasts, molds and mildew (including mycotoxins); (4) harmful microbes including E. coli and salmonella; (5) pesticides, fungicides and insecticides; (6) THC potency, homogeneity and cannabinoid profiles. Results must be reported to the licensee and OCP within 2 business days (COA plus electronic data deliverables).

At rule level (18-691 CMR ch. 5, §6) there are eight categories: (1) filth and foreign material; (2) residual solvents (a named list including acetone, butane, ethanol, hexane, isopropyl alcohol, methanol, propane, toluene, xylenes, benzene, chloroform, ethylene oxide, methylene chloride, trichloroethylene; with exemptions for dried flower/kief/hashish made without solvents, ethanol in alcohol tinctures, and concentrate that already passed); (3) pesticides per Table 6.8-A / §6(8)-A (flower/trim must be dried and cured; products may not be made from untested cannabis); (4) metals — cadmium, lead, arsenic, mercury (rule limits, e.g. inhalation: Cd 200 ppb, Pb 500 ppb, As 200 ppb, Hg 100 ppb, via a Justia rule mirror); (5) dangerous molds/mildew — total yeast and mold, and on an initial TY&M failure, mycotoxins aflatoxins B1, B2, G1, G2 and ochratoxin A; (6) harmful microbes — total viable aerobic bacteria, total coliforms, Enterobacteriaceae, Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC), Salmonella spp.; (7) THC potency, homogeneity, cannabinoid profiles; (8) water activity (mandatory for solid/semi-solid edibles not requiring refrigeration and dried plant material in final form; moisture content also required for flower/trim).

Medical contrast

Testing is not mandatory in the medical programme; no testing facilities are registered specifically in the MMCP; the adult-use licensed certified testing facilities are authorised to test medical cannabis; voluntary medical testing is "extremely limited and inconsistent" (OCP official reports). OCP's Aug 2023 audit of 120 medical samples found 42% contained at least one contaminant that would fail adult-use standards (yeast & mold 25.0%; pesticides 17.5%; heavy metals 2.5%; filth/foreign material 0.8%).

  • Remediation (OCP testing-data page): "For… metals and pesticides, remediation is not permitted but licensees may submit two samples for retesting if an initial sample fails. For other analyte categories, such as yeast, mold, microbials, or residual solvents, remediation is permitted." After remediation, two samples must pass all failed categories; product failing retest must be destroyed; OCP issues an Order of Destruction for batches not retested, remediated or destroyed (detail via a secondary summary of the rule — Verify rule text).

Part 16

Licence rules — transfers, renewals, caps, insurance

Transfer of ownership & changes of principals

  • Prior approval (OCP official instructions): "A licensee may transfer ownership interests or change principals, only after the Application for Transfer of Ownership Interests has been approved by the Office." Exception: if the transfer does not result in a new person obtaining, or an existing person increasing, an ownership interest equal to or greater than 5%, prior approval is not required and the transfer may be reported at licence renewal. The municipality/LUPC must submit a new local authorization reflecting the new ownership. Enforcement example (OCP 2024 Annual Report): 6 licensees were fined $2,500–$15,000 in 2024 for transfer of ownership without prior approval.
  • Death, disability, bankruptcy (older AUCP rule text): a court appointee may take possession and operate; must be 21+, have no disqualifying drug offences, hold an IIC to enter limited-access areas or sell, and file a transfer application as soon as possible and no later than 45 days after the qualifying event; the Department may revoke or void a licence immediately on discovering a non-compliant transfer attempt; appeal under the Maine APA, 5 MRS ch. 375. Verify against current ch. 20 text.
  • New owners, officers, managers, employees: the licensee must notify the Department in writing (name, address, DOB); the person must submit to a criminal history check (28-B §204), obtain an IIC (§106) and, for equity ownership, obtain transfer approval (§210) — statute structure per the LD 1719 text; Verify line-by-line against current section text.

Renewals

  • Adult use (28-B MRS §209): the active licence term is 1 year; the Department shall notify the licensee 90 days before expiration; the licensee must file renewal not less than 30 days before expiration. Non-renewal grounds (rule): outstanding fines/penalties (unless on an approved payment plan), no licensed activity for a year or more without reasonable justification, or renewal that would breach caps/conflict rules. IIC renewal: the cardholder is responsible; the Department does not mail renewal applications; submit at least 30 days before expiry (OCP IIC form).
  • Hemp (DACF official): licences expire 365 days after issuance and do not automatically renew; apply at least 30 days before intended planting; a new application is required each year grown; the licence is non-transferable and the application fee non-refundable; DACF does not licence processing. (See also Part 7.3.)
  • Medical: caregiver registry ID cards are valid 1 year; the "apply at least 45 days before expiry" figure is secondary advice — Verify official.

Ownership caps & conflicts (28-B MRS §205)

  • Issuance or renewal may not result in a person holding, or having a direct or indirect financial interest in, more than 3 cultivation facility licences, or multiple cultivation licences with combined canopy exceeding 30,000 sq ft (except solely from approved Tier 4 increases under §304).
  • Testing conflict: a testing facility or sample collector applicant may not be a caregiver/registered caregiver or hold an interest in a registered dispensary, cultivation, products manufacturing or cannabis store licence — and vice versa. "Interest" includes equity, partial equity, or any financial interest including investor or management positions. One qualified applicant may hold multiple testing facility licences.
  • The former "more than 4 marijuana stores" limit in the old rule "does not apply after December 31, 2021" — treat as expired; no current statewide store cap was found.

Insurance & bonding

No OCP state insurance minimum was found in the official rules searched. One secondary source (Insureon) states both that Maine law does not require dispensary business insurance and that dispensaries "must carry a surety bond" — internally ambiguous, Verify; the same source reports that Portland requires $1M per-occurrence commercial general liability — Verify Portland ordinance. The transport-vehicle insurance requirement in ch. 30 (Part 14) is official.

Change of location, surrender, revocation

  • OCP's post-active instructions list changes requiring prior OCP approval (which may require a new Local Authorization Form): change of location, change of ownership, change of principals, changes to financial instruments, new or no-longer-valid financial interests, and changes to the Official Plan of Record.
  • Relocation — 28-B MRS §211: apply to OCP; local authorization is required (municipality under §402 or LUPC under §403); OCP shall, within 10 days of receiving the local certification, notify the licensee and issue an updated licence with the new address; relocation does not extend or modify the licence term. Older rule detail (decision within 30 days; concurrent operation at both sites for up to 90 days) is Verify current rule.
  • Suspension, revocation, restriction and voluntary surrender are addressed in 18-691 CMR ch. 1, §10.3; final orders are appealable to Superior Court under Rule 80C. The Department may revoke or refuse renewal if premises are inactive without good cause for a year or more.

Environmental rules

Water, pesticides, energy, odour and packaging sustainability for growers.

  • DEP cannabis-sector guidance (official): Maine DEP regulates waste from licensed cultivation, manufacturing, sample collectors, testing facilities and stores under the Solid Waste Management Rules; cannabis waste over 0.3% THC must be transported by an authorised entity (licensed individuals, DEA/law enforcement) or rendered unusable before leaving the site; composting is encouraged if the waste contains no solvents/chemical extraction agents, the operation meets regulatory exemptions, and THC is adequately degraded (see Part 23). OCP's other-agency guidance page lists DEP permitting triggers for cannabis operations under Waste (handling/storage/treatment/disposal), Air (drying and processing), Water (wastewater and stormwater) and Land (construction).
  • Water: No cannabis-specific water right or withdrawal rule was found. General Maine law: the Water Use Reporting law (2002), 38 MRS art. 4-B (e.g., users of 20,000+ gal/day on small streams report annually; sliding scales; reports under §470-D filed annually by 1 Dec to DEP, DACF or DHHS depending on user class; individual reports are confidential). NRPA Significant Groundwater Well thresholds (38 MRS §480-B definition): 75,000+ gal/week or 50,000+ gal/day within 500 ft of a wetland, great pond, vernal pool, water-supply well or river/stream/brook, or 216,000+ gal/week or 144,000+ gal/day beyond 500 ft; exemptions include agricultural use — applicability to cannabis cultivation Verify.
  • Pesticides: Medical statute 22 MRS §2423-A(2)(J) (current official text): a caregiver may use a pesticide only if it is "used consistent with federal labeling requirements, is registered with the Department of Agriculture, Conservation and Forestry, Board of Pesticides Control pursuant to Title 7, section 607 and is used consistent with best management practices for pest management approved by the Commissioner"; the applicator must be certified under 7 MRS §1471-D; assistants with direct contact with treated plants need safety training under 40 CFR §170.130, and non-certified handlers need §170.230 training first. For adult use, pesticide residues are controlled through mandatory testing (Part 15); no separate OCP approved-pesticide list was found Verify — 2021 correspondence shows growers and BPC discussing developing an allowed list; none located. Chlorfenapyr is prohibited in effect (the 2025 NorCO recall, Part 15).
  • Energy: No cannabis-specific energy, lighting-efficiency, energy-reporting, carbon or renewable requirement was found in OCP rules or Efficiency Maine sources — Unavailable. (LD 1535, 132nd Leg., a bill aimed at illegal grows via high-amperage residential service permits, died in Legislative Files on 9 Jun 2025 — secondary.)
  • Odour: LUPC's cannabis-specific land-use standards address odour and lighting (Ch. 10 §10.27,S,4 for commercial operations; by reference §10.27,N for home-based). Cultivation operating plans must include "plans for ventilation and filtration systems that prevent marijuana plant odors from significantly altering the environmental odor outside" (OCP rule plan-of-record requirement). Municipal odour rules vary (see Bangor in Part 10).
  • Packaging sustainability: No cannabis-specific packaging-sustainability or recycling mandate was found — Unavailable; but see the ch. 30 packaging-waste-reduction provision in Part 23.
Part 18

Consumer & social rules

Paraphernalia, driving, record sealing, lounges and events, visitors, landlords, firearms, pets, and medical cannabis in schools — each with its Maine rule and source.

Paraphernalia & smoke shops

28-B MRS §1501(1)(A) (current text): a person 21+ may "use, possess or transport cannabis paraphernalia." Under 17-A MRS §1111-A, the paraphernalia offences do not apply to persons authorised under 22 MRS ch. 558-C or 28-B, or to a 28-B-licensed cannabis store regarding sale or offering; an affirmative defence exists for hemp propagation, cultivation and processing (PL 2019, c. 12). Otherwise, trafficking or furnishing drug paraphernalia is a Class E crime (to a person 16+) or Class D (under 16), advertising is Class E, and personal use is a civil violation (sub-§4-B). No separate Maine smoke-shop/head-shop licence was found — Unavailable; whether the §1111-A exception covers general (non-cannabis-store) retailers selling paraphernalia for lawful cannabis use is Verify — the exception's wording names licensed cannabis stores.

THC driving & OUI

Maine has no per se THC blood limit for adults (secondary reporting; no per se limit appears in the statute sources searched — Verify against 29-A §2411 text). OUI under 29-A MRS §2411 is impairment-based. The under-21 THC provision (29-A §2472) and the metabolite-based suspension provision (29-A §2453-A) were identified only in secondary sources — Verify statute text before reliance.

Expungement, sealing & pardons

LD 2236 (131st Leg., signed 2024) created petition-based sealing (the statute does not use "expungement"): it added to "eligible criminal conviction" in 15 MRS §2261(6) certain pre-30 January 2017 offences where the drug was marijuana — Class D aggravated trafficking/furnishing/cultivation (former §1105), aggravated cultivating marijuana §1105-D(1)(A)(4), (B-1)(4), (D)(4), and Class D unlawful possession (former §1107). The companion automatic-sealing bill LD 2269 was rejected (secondary). 2026 proposals LD 1916 (automatic sealing), LD 1917, LD 1911, LD 1919 — status Verify. A cannabis-specific pardon programme was not found — Unavailable.

Consumption lounges & events

Current law (28-B §1501(2), quoted in §1.3): consumption is lawful only in a private residence/curtilage or on private property with the owner's explicit permission; a violation is a civil violation, fine not more than $100. LD 1365 (132nd Leg., Rep. Boyer) as introduced would have let municipalities/LUPC-area entities license "cannabis hospitality lounges" that are not cannabis establishments, need no additional state licensing, and are not subject to OCP oversight. A committee amendment replaced that model with an OCP endorsement model effective 1 Jan 2028 (application fee $250; endorsement fee up to $2,500; a cannabis server education course; a Maine Cannabis Liability Act; a $250,000 public-service campaign). Outcome — DEAD, confirmed on the official status page: Final Disposition: "Accepted Majority (ONTP) Report, Apr 6, 2026" (House 108–35; Senate concurred); placed in Legislative Files (DEAD) (LD 1365 status page). The committee amendment's lounge-endorsement scheme was never enacted, and no licensed social-consumption lounges are in operation.

Events: under 28-B MRS §504-A, a cannabis store with an OCP specified-event permit may sell only during the event and only on the permitted premises; the permit may not authorise consumption on the permitted premises; conditions include a tent or similar structure with a single entry, verifying entrants are 21+, signs that consumption is prohibited, and secure storage of products; a specified event is outside the licensed premises, not primarily for under-21s, and lasts no more than 10 consecutive days or 10 occurrences.

Cannabis tourism & visitor purchase limits

Adult use — any adult 21+ with government-issued photo ID may purchase; no Maine residency is required, and the §1501 purchase/possession limits (2½ oz; ≤10 g concentrate) apply identically to visitors (statute text, §1.3). Visitors remain subject to the same consumption, vehicle and state-line rules (Part 14). Medical visitors — Maine recognises visiting qualifying patients from an OCP-approved jurisdiction list Verify the full current list on OCP's visiting-patients page; the visiting-patient purchase limit of 2½ oz per 15 days is in current statute, 22 MRS §2423-A(2)(A-1): a caregiver "may not dispense more than 2 1/2 ounces of harvested cannabis to a visiting qualifying patient during a 15-day period."

Landlord & tenant rules

Home cultivation on another's land requires the owner's written agreement (28-B §1502(1)(C), quoted in §1.3). Medical anti-discrimination (22 MRS ch. 558-C): "A school, employer or landlord may not refuse to enroll or employ or lease to or otherwise penalize a person solely for that person's status as a qualifying patient or a caregiver unless failing to do so would put the school, employer or landlord in violation of federal law or cause it to lose a federal contract or funding." The same provision does not prohibit restricting administration or cultivation on premises inconsistent with the premises' general use, and a landlord may prohibit smoking of medical cannabis on the premises. No general statutory tenant right to smoke or grow overriding a lease was found for adult use. Public housing: federal law prohibits controlled substances in federally subsidised housing; the current MaineHousing (Maine State Housing Authority) cannabis policy was Unavailable from an official source.

Gun ownership

State — LD 1103 (131st Leg.), PL 2023, c. 381 (enacted without signature 5 Jul 2023) amended 15 MRS §393 so that, notwithstanding 18 USC §922(g)(3), use, possession or transport of cannabis in accordance with 22 MRS ch. 558-C or 28-B may not serve as the basis for a prohibition on owning, possessing or controlling a firearm under Maine law. Federal — 18 USC §922(g)(3) continues to bar firearm possession by an "unlawful user" of a controlled substance, and the federal Form 4473 conflict remains while cannabis is federally illegal. Federal rescheduling — corrected (Part 29, item 18): a narrow final rule exists — 91 FR 22714 (28 Apr 2026) places marijuana in Schedule III only to the extent it is in an FDA-approved drug product or subject to a state-issued medical marijuana licence; adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. See Part 2 (§280E decoupling) for the full statement and source.

Pets & veterinary products

DACF bulletin: "it is officially not legal to feed anything arising from hemp to animals. FDA approval is required… through AAFCO and/or FDA-Center for Veterinary Medicine." A Hemp Feed Coalition petition for hemp seed meal/cake for laying hens was accepted by AAFCO/FDA-CVM in early 2021 (decision then pending). LD 630 (2019) was prompted in part by DACF/DHHS directing pet stores to remove hemp-CBD pet food. FDA's position (secondary): CBD pet products are unapproved new animal drugs. A Maine-specific veterinarian rule (e.g., Board of Veterinary Medicine guidance on recommending CBD) was not found — Unavailable.

Medical cannabis in schools

LD 557 (127th Leg.), PL 2015, c. 369, enacted 20-A MRS §6306 and 22 MRS §2426(1-A): a child with written certification under 22 MRS §2423-B may not be denied eligibility to attend school solely because the child requires medical cannabis in nonsmokeable form as a reasonable accommodation; a parent, guardian or designated primary caregiver may possess and administer it in nonsmokeable form on school grounds or in a school bus (see also §2423-A(1)(F-1)(4), quoted in §1.3's source text). School-district policies (Portland Public Schools JLCD; RSU 60 and Cape Elizabeth JLCDA — district sources, not State) uniformly provide that only the designated/registered caregiver may bring, hold and administer it; it may not be held, possessed or administered by any school employee (including the school nurse) or by the student except during caregiver-supervised administration; the caregiver signs in and out and removes any remaining cannabis; vaporisers are not permitted (Cape Elizabeth).

Part 20

Drug testing for licence holders & staff

Testing rules specific to cannabis businesses and their staff — distinct from the general employment protections in §1.8.

  • MDOL official guide (2026): employers may prohibit any use or possession of cannabis in the workplace and may discipline employees under the influence at work under a workplace policy. "In Maine, cannabis is still on the list of substances for which an employer may test. Testing is only allowed if a company has a drug testing policy that has been approved by the Maine Department of Labor (MDOL) or they are testing following federal testing requirements."   • 2026 revision — confirmed as enacted law (Parts 27 and 29): LD 2110 is PL 2025 c. 666 (HP 1425), signed 13 Apr 2026, amending 26 MRS §682(3-B) so that "Legal use of cannabis pursuant to the Maine Medical Use of Cannabis Act" is a "legitimate medical explanation." Effective date 29 Jul 2026 — confirmed (non-emergency 2nd Regular Session law, general effective date). Remaining nuance: the cannabis-specific application of the "legitimate medical explanation" mechanism is described in secondary commentary; the mechanism itself is confirmed in the chapter text and §685. See also §1.8.
  • Federally mandated testing (e.g., DOT safety-sensitive transport, law enforcement) follows federal rules instead of Maine law.
  • OCP-specific staff rules: no OCP rule requiring drug testing (pre-employment or otherwise) of IIC holders or cannabis-establishment employees was found — Verify absence. OCP instead controls staff through the Individual Identification Card (IIC) regime (18-691 CMR ch. 20, §1): an IIC is required for anyone who possesses, cultivates, manufactures, packages, tests, dispenses, transfers, serves, handles, transports or delivers cannabis, and for anyone with inventory-tracking/POS access; licensees must verify each required person holds a valid IIC and report within 24 hours any attempt to use a falsified, altered or another person's IIC; contractors and visitors not handling cannabis do not need an IIC. IIC issuance involves a criminal-history/background check (Part 16), not a drug test, in the sources found. Under ch. 1, §10, operating without an IIC holder on premises able to cooperate with a Department inspection is a violation.

Personal home grow & medical cultivation

  • Adult use — 28-B MRS §1502: up to 6 mature + 12 immature + unlimited seedlings per person 21+ (quoted verbatim in §1.3). The §1502(2) duties — invisibility from a public way without optical aids, reasonable precautions against under-21 access, and a legible tag on each mature and each immature plant (grower's name, licence/ID number, personal-use notation, and the landowner's name where applicable) — apply to both indoor and outdoor home grow; the statute does not distinguish them. Municipalities/LUPC may limit the per-parcel total but may not go below 6 mature / 12 immature / unlimited seedlings per domiciled person, may not generally prohibit home cultivation, restrict where it is allowed, or charge a licence or fee (§1502(3)–(4), as amended PL 2023, c. 220, §§3–4).
  • Medical patients — 22 MRS §2423-A(1)(B): up to 6 mature + 12 immature + unlimited seedlings per qualifying patient, cultivated by the patient or a designated caregiver; household members who are qualifying patients may share not more than 2 cultivation areas.
  • Medical caregivers — 22 MRS §2423-A(2)(B) and (B-1) (as amended PL 2025, c. 514, §§2–3), verbatim:
    "B. Cultivate up to 30 mature cannabis plants, up to 60 immature cannabis plants and unlimited seedlings or cultivate up to 500 square feet of mature plant canopy, up to 1,000 square feet of immature plant canopy and unlimited seedlings. A caregiver may not cultivate immature plants by canopy if cultivating mature plants by plant count and may not cultivate immature plants by plant count if cultivating mature plants by canopy. A caregiver cultivating cannabis under this paragraph may not cultivate cannabis under paragraph B-1…"
    "B-1. Notwithstanding paragraph B, for a caregiver who cultivates mature cannabis plants only in an outdoor cultivation area, cultivate between the months of March and December up to 150 mature cannabis plants, up to 300 immature cannabis plants and unlimited seedlings or cultivate between the months of March and December up to 2,500 square feet of mature plant canopy, up to 5,000 square feet of immature plant canopy and unlimited seedlings… 'outdoor cultivation area' means a cultivation area that primarily uses sunlight for the cultivation of mature cannabis plants…"
    Caregivers must be 21+ with no disqualifying drug offence conviction; they may possess all harvested cannabis produced by their cultivation (§2423-A(2)(A), as amended PL 2025, c. 514, §1) and transfer up to 2½ oz of harvested cannabis to a qualifying patient in one transaction (§2423-A(2)(A-1)).

Verify the current §304 increase increment — older rule text referenced +7,000 sq ft every 2 years and an 85%-sold test.
  • Operation — 28-B MRS §501: §501(1) cultivation is only for sale/distribution to products manufacturing facilities, cannabis stores or other cultivation facilities. §501(2) (as amended PL 2023, c. 679, Pt. B, §61): no retail sales to consumers without a separate cannabis store licence; "A cultivation facility may not give away adult use cannabis, adult use cannabis products or cannabis plants to a consumer." (See Part 25.) §501(3) Nursery: may cultivate immature plants, seedlings and seeds for sale to stores and other cultivation facilities and — within a dedicated consumer-sales portion of the premises complying with store rules — to consumers; mature plants only for propagation/seed production, in an area physically separated from immature plants/seedlings; may not sell or transfer mature plants, flower or trim. §501(5): co-location of adult-use and medical cultivation in one facility is allowed with a visually conspicuous physical barrier (wall or fencing) between the areas, per-plant tagging as adult-use or medical, and an OCP colocation plan if required. §501(10): plants are tracked from immature plant to delivery/transfer, disposal or destruction.
  • Outdoor cultivation — §501(7), verbatim in substance: "A. B.   • Indoor / outdoor / greenhouse — rule level (18-691 CMR ch. 30, §1): cultivation areas in outdoor areas, greenhouses, or other structures that do not meet all building security requirements must be secured with fencing and lighting: fencing and gates must be secure, at least 6 feet high, obscure (or have a cover obscuring) the limited-access area from outside view, and be commercial or security grade (not agricultural or residential grade); lighting must illuminate a perimeter of at least 10 feet around any entry point (motion-sensor lighting permitted). Part 22

Security requirements

Adult use: 18-691 CMR ch. 30, §1 (official OCP rule PDF). Medical: 18-691 CMR ch. 2 dispensary rule, via OCP's proposed-rule PDF — flagged where that matters.

  • Written security plan (adult use): before cultivating, manufacturing, testing, selling, storing or transporting, each licensee must obtain Department approval of a written security plan (part of the facility plan of record) showing: lighting adequately illuminating entry/exit points; all doors and windows lockable; fences (if present) meeting height/requirements; alarm sensors on all entry points and windows, remotely monitored; video cameras in all required locations meeting specifications; and, in retail areas, lockable secure display cases or counters preventing public handling without supervision.
  • Limited-access areas (adult use): nurseries and cannabis stores selling to 21+ consumers must use "identification checks, locked doors, video surveillance, counters, and locked displays," per their approved security plan, to prevent unauthorised entry to limited-access areas; other licensees must use identification checks, locked doors and video surveillance. Any security breach must be reported within 24 hours, in writing, to the Department. All ingress/egress points to limited-access areas must be signed (sign at least 8.5" × 11", letters at least ½ inch): "Pursuant to State Law: Do Not Enter – Authorized Persons Only." Department staff and state/local regulators with authority must show ID but are not visitors and may not be denied entry to any area.
  • Video surveillance (adult use) — minimum specifications: camera resolution at least 720p; IP-compatible system/storage; cameras record continuously 24 hours/day or are motion-activated at at least 15 frames per second; motion-activated storage must capture and store footage from 120 seconds before motion activation to 120 seconds after motion ceases; images must display accurate time/date (NIST standard); the storage device must be secured on-premises in a lockbox, cabinet or closet, or held on a third-party server, secured against tampering or theft; delivery/specified-event recordings must be uploaded to the system server within 24 hours; facial-surveillance software is not part of the requirement. Retention: "All surveillance recordings… must be kept for a minimum of 45 days on the licensee's recording device." Videos are subject to inspection and must be copied/provided to the Department on request; licensees must keep a list of all persons with access to recordings, and access-control procedures.
  • Visitors & contractors (adult use): the active-licence application must describe the licensee's visitor identification badge verification process and visitor entry log in compliance with ch. 30, §1. Security guards are permitted but not required; guards must meet 32 MRS ch. 93 qualifications, be 21+, hold and display an IIC if in limited-access areas or transport vehicles, and must not consume cannabis or be intoxicated on duty.
  • Testing facilities: must install keycard doors, alarms or other means of detecting entrance/exit to limited-access areas outside operating hours, and maintain written security protocols to prevent diversion, theft and loss of samples (older ch. 5 rule text — Verify current ch. 5 text).
  • Medical — registered dispensaries (18-691 CMR ch. 2; text from OCP's proposed-rule PDF — Verify final adopted text): dispensaries "must implement appropriate security measures to deter and prevent unauthorized entrance into areas containing cannabis and the theft of cannabis," including at minimum: onsite parking; exterior lighting sufficient to deter nuisance activity and facilitate surveillance of the perimeter and all ingress/egress (without disturbing neighbours); a burglar alarm monitored by a licensed alarm company; video surveillance of any area where cannabis is stored, displayed or sold, and of cultivation areas where harvested cannabis is stored, processed and cured (cameras should also cover manufacturing areas); interior electronic monitoring, video cameras and panic buttons. "Video recordings must be maintained for 14 days" (motion-activated recording must capture all motion for 60 seconds before the triggering event) — i.e., the medical retention period (14 days) is shorter than the adult-use ch. 30 minimum (45 days).
  • Storage / vault: adult-use cannabis must be kept within limited-access areas behind locked doors, with locked displays/counters in retail areas, per the approved security plan; a specific vault specification (e.g., safe class or anchoring) was not found in the official rule excerpts reviewed — Verify (secondary self-assessment guides citing "ch. 3" vault sections could not be matched to a current OCP rule and were not relied on).

Waste

  • Adult-use establishments — 18-691 CMR ch. 30, §4: "All wastes must be managed in accordance with federal, state and local requirements." Hazardous waste: discharges of hazardous waste or other matter "in any quantity and under any circumstances must be reported to the Department"; licensees must immediately report discharges to the Department of Public Safety (State Police) at 1-800-452-4664 or 207-624-7000, unless exempted under DEP rules chs. 800/850 (licensees are not required to notify DEP under that provision), and must report discharges exceeding federal reportable quantities as specified in those chapters.
  • Rendering unusable / composting (OCP rule + DEP guidance): cannabis waste must be rendered unusable before disposal or transport off-site unless transported by an authorised entity (Part 17: waste over 0.3% THC). Testing-facility rule (18-691 CMR ch. 5, §8): written waste SOPs; solid waste handled under 38 MRS §1303-C(29) and the Solid Waste Rules (06-096 CMR ch. 400–425); cannabis rendered into waste by mixing with at least 50% non-cannabis material (e.g., compostable material/manure), one batch at a time, tracked in the statewide inventory tracking system; no disposal in trash or a dumpster unless composted or rendered unusable as described; waste quarantined on premises for at least 3 business days for possible OCP investigation or witness. Consumer-returned cannabis must be destroyed, never resold (ch. 30).
  • Waste exceptions (ch. 30, §4): the following are not cannabis waste requiring rendering, if completely free of all cannabis flowers and leaves with any visible trichomes and nonhazardous: (A) root balls, soil or growing media; (B) stalks; (C) leaves and branches removed from clones, seedlings and plants. Wastewater from cultivation/manufacturing must be disposed of in compliance with all applicable state and local laws.
  • Composting (Maine DEP, "Maine Environmental Guidance for the Cannabis Sector"): DEP "highly encourages" composting and beneficial reuse. Cannabis waste taken off the farm where grown is a Type IA Residual: agricultural composting operations may accept any amount if at least 70% of the finished compost from any volume of cannabis waste is used at appropriate agronomic rates on the farm that produced the compost within two years, and the operation follows a Compost Management Plan (CMP) approved by DACF; any farm may accept up to 10 cubic yards of cannabis waste in any 30-day period without a CMP; otherwise a DEP compost facility licence is required (see also 06-096 CMR ch. 410). Other lawful disposal: solid-waste landfills or incinerators licensed by DEP to accept municipal solid waste, or a licensed aerobic digestion facility. Waste from extraction containing chemical extraction agents or solvents may be hazardous waste. Grow/extraction wastewater must be characterised before operational startup to confirm it meets disposal criteria.
  • Packaging waste (ch. 30, §4(5), "Reducing Packaging Waste"): "A licensee may reuse containers and exit packaging and may permit a consumer to use their own reusable exit packaging…"; a licensee may charge a reasonable fee for reusable containers/exit packaging and may refund fees on return; reused packaging must be clean and not impart deleterious substances.
  • Sample collectors: a sample collector in possession of samples to be wasted must return them to the licensee from which they were collected (ch. 30).
  • Hemp waste / crop destruction (DACF / USDA framework): Maine's hemp programme operates under the USDA-approved state plan (Part 12). DACF's hemp programme newsletter summarising the USDA Final Rule (effective 22 Mar 2021) notes the rule allows on-farm disposal of non-compliant hemp, sets the negligent-violation threshold at 1.0% total THC (crops above 0.3% total THC are non-compliant and must be disposed of or remediated; above 1.0% is a negligent violation), and allows performance-based sampling. Maine's own hemp rule, 01-001 CMR ch. 274, requires licensees to allow Department inspection and sampling of the crop at any time and to file planting reports within 14 days. The Maine-specific prescribed destruction method (e.g., plowing under, composting, burning per USDA disposal options) in ch. 274's disposal section was Verify — disposal section text not retrieved.

Sampling, giveaways & promotions

All rule text from 18-691 CMR ch. 30 (official OCP rule PDF) and OCP guidance.

Four authorised sample types (OCP guidance, 29 May 2025)

  • Consumer Sample: "Provided by a cannabis store to a consumer who is at least 21 years of age for no remuneration."
  • Employee Sample: "Provided by a cannabis establishment to its employees for the purposes of employee education."
  • Trade Sample: "Provided by a cultivation or manufacturing facility to another Maine adult use cannabis licensee for business or marketing purposes only."
  • R&D Sample: "Provided to a testing facility by a cannabis establishment for testing for research and development purposes only."

Mandatory-testing samples are separate and distinct (Part 15). Amounts, recipients and inventory-tracking workflows differ by type.

Trade samples (ch. 30)

May be provided only by cultivation facilities (except nurseries) and products manufacturing facilities; solely for business-to-business marketing; "May not be sold or otherwise provided for payment or consideration, including swapping samples among licensees"; must move by authorised transfer with full tracking; packaged and labelled per §5; may not be consumed on the premises of the providing or receiving licensee; may not be sold or conveyed to another licensee or consumer. Cultivators may give trade samples of their own-grown cannabis to licensed manufacturers or stores; manufacturers may give trade samples of their products to licensed stores (or delivery retailers). Monthly limits per recipient licensee (aggregate): (a) edible cannabis products containing less than 5 grams of THC, divisible into servings of ≤10 mg THC; (b) cannabis concentrate containing 10 grams of THC; and (c) 2.5 ounces of cannabis. (Statutory basis: 28-B §501(8) — samples to another licensee "for business or marketing purposes only," not to be consumed on the cultivator's premises.)

Employee samples (ch. 30)

A licensee may provide samples to its employees for R&D (cultivation/manufacturing) or employee education (stores). Conditions: an employee may never be required to accept or consume samples; samples must have passed all mandatory testing; must be tracked in the inventory tracking system and packaged/labelled per §5; may not be consumed on the employer's premises; may not be sold or conveyed to anyone other than the employee identified in the tracking system. Limits: no more than 2.5 ounces of a combination of cannabis and cannabis concentrate, including no more than 10 grams of concentrate, per employee per 90-day period; no edible package containing more than 200 mg THC (divisible into servings of ≤10 mg THC).

Consumer samples (ch. 30, §1(L))

A cannabis store may provide samples to a consumer 21+ for no remuneration. One consumer sample = (a) ≤0.5 g cannabis flower (including an uninfused raw pre-roll of flower/trim only); (b) one serving of an edible containing ≤10 mg THC; or (c) one vape cartridge containing ≤0.5 g concentrate. Maximum two consumer samples per consumer per day; none to a visibly intoxicated person; samples plus purchases combined may not exceed the §1(8)(B) sales limits; samples must have passed mandatory testing, be tracked, and be packaged/labelled per §5.

Giveaways generally

Outside the four sample types above, giveaways are prohibited — 28-B §501(2) bars a cultivation facility from giving cannabis, products or plants to a consumer, and 18-691 CMR ch. 1, §10 lists as a violation: "Except by way of authorized transfer of trade samples or testing samples, giving away or otherwise transferring cannabis in exchange for a monetary sum less than the licensee has paid for the cannabis by way of authorized transfer or less than the value the licensee has invested, in labor and materials, in the cannabis." The same section makes improper handling, storage, transport or transfer of samples a basis for seizure.

Discounts, coupons, loyalty, "happy hour"

No provision addressing retail discounts, coupons, loyalty programmes, or time-of-day ("happy hour") promotions was found in 28-B MRS or 18-691 CMR ch. 1/30 in the texts reviewed — Unavailable / Verify. The operative constraints located are the below-cost giveaway prohibition above and the sample regimes; no express happy-hour ban (an alcohol-law concept) was located for cannabis. This absence should be confirmed against the full current ch. 30 marketing/advertising section before reliance.

29C. Internal contradictions — resolved

  1. Edible package limit (100 mg vs 200 mg): 200 mg/package, 10 mg/serving — 28-B §703(1)(F), PL 2023 c.396. Any 100 mg statement is pre-2023 law.
  2. Hemp THC test (delta-9 vs total): both quotations genuine — statute §2231(1-A)(D) delta-9; DACF/USDA plan Total THC. A statute-vs-plan gap, closing federally in late 2026.
  3. Hemp registry dates (page 3 Aug 2026 vs PDF 13 May 2026): real, in the primary sources; the linked PDF is the 13 May 2026 file listing 8 growers.
  4. Fund split (12%+12% vs 9%): both were law at different times — 12% + 12% before 1 Jan 2026; 9% + 9% from 1 Jan 2026.
  5. §4923(7) two enacted texts: the Revisor prints the section as (CONFLICT); neither the c.388 nor the c.504 version may be quoted as the sole text.
  6. "110,000+ patients": OCP's metric is printed certifications (112,547 in 2025), not unique patients.
  7. 2025 transactions (4,835,682 vs 4,835,678): annual-report figure vs preliminary-release figure; flagged, unresolved from PDF text.
  8. LD 1063 year (2023 vs 2024): primary text — taxable years beginning on or after 1 Jan 2023.
  9. Delivery numbers by year: 2024 (11 + 1 authorised, $67,047) and 2025 (6 authorised, $231,298) must not be merged.
  10. c.416 shorthand: "<0.3% AND >10:1" describes falling outside the potentially-intoxicating category — the category is defined by negation.
  11. LD 1942 taxes described as law: committee amendment only; died unenacted; absent from current §§1811/4923.
  12. Federal rescheduling flagged "Unverified": superseded — a narrow final rule exists (91 FR 22714) for FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana only.
