New Hampshire Cannabis & Hemp

New Hampshire is medical-only, with small-amount decriminalisation, and no adult-use programme. Therapeutic cannabis is lawful only under RSA 126-X through nonprofit Alternative Treatment Centers — a statutory cap of 4 ATC registration certificates, operating 7 dispensary locations run by 3 organisations. Hemp growers are licensed by the USDA, not the State, and an enacted 2026 law rewrites the hemp definition to total THC including THCA, effective 1 January 2027. There is no general sales tax and no cannabis-specific excise tax.

Read the verbatim text of RSA 318-B:2-c, I–VI

318-B:2-c, I(a): “‘Marijuana’ includes the leaves, stems, flowers, and seeds of all species of the plant genus cannabis, but shall not include the resin extracted from any part of such plant … including hashish … [or] the mature stalks … fiber … oil or cake … or the sterilized seed … Marijuana shall not include hemp grown, processed, marketed, or sold under RSA 439-A.”

I(b): “‘Personal-use amount of a regulated marijuana-infused product’ means one or more products that is comprised of marijuana, marijuana extracts, or resins and other ingredients and is intended for use or consumption, such as, but not limited to, edible products, ointments, and tinctures, which was obtained from a state where marijuana sales to adults are legal and regulated under state law, and which is in its original, child-resistant, labeled packaging when it is being stored, and which contains a total of no more than 300 milligrams of tetrahydrocannabinol.”

II: “Except as provided in RSA 126-X, any person who knowingly possesses 3/4 of an ounce or less of marijuana … shall be guilty of a violation…”

III: “…any person who knowingly possesses 5 grams or less of hashish … shall be guilty of a violation…”

IV: “…any person 21 years of age or older possessing a personal-use amount of a regulated marijuana-infused product shall be guilty of a violation… Persons 18 years of age or older and under 21 years of age who knowingly possess marijuana-infused products shall be guilty of a misdemeanor.”

V(a): “…a fine of $100 for a first or second offense … or a fine of up to $300 for any subsequent offense within any 3-year period; however, any person convicted based upon a complaint which alleged that the person had 3 or more prior convictions … within a 3-year period preceding the fourth offense shall be guilty of a class B misdemeanor. The offender shall forfeit the marijuana, regulated marijuana-infused products, or hashish to the state. A court shall waive the fine for a single conviction within a 3-year period upon proof that person has completed a substance abuse assessment by a licensed drug and alcohol counselor within 60 days of the conviction…”

V(b): Under-18s: forfeiture + delinquency petition under RSA 169-B:6.

VI(a): “no person shall be subject to arrest for a violation of paragraph II, III, or IV…” (absent lawful grounds for arrest for a different offence).

Implications, strictly from that text: (i) there is no lawful purchase route for non-patients in NH — the infused-product paragraph presupposes a product obtained from a state where adult sales are legal; (ii) amounts above ¾ oz / 5 g / 300 mg fall outside 2-c and back under the general Controlled Drug Act — penalties by amount are summarised at §1.2 from RSA 318-B:26; (iii) home grow is not decriminalised by 2-c; (iv) public-consumption penalties for non-patients: no standalone non-patient public-consumption offence was found in the current RSA 318-B text in the original research or in the 24 Oct 2026 audit (stated as “none found in the RSA text searched,” not as absolute proof of absence) — the public-use prohibitions quoted above are in RSA 126-X:3 (patients); HB 198 (2025) would have created public smoking/vaping penalties and failed (see §1.9).

1.5 Hemp

Definitions and THC measurement — verbatim

CURRENT — UNTIL 1 JAN 2027 · RSA 439-A:2, V

“‘Hemp’ means the plant Cannabis sativa L. and any part of the plant, whether growing or not, with a delta-9 tetrahydrocannabinol concentration (THC) of not more than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis.”

FROM 1 JAN 2027 · 2026, 275:1 · RSA 439-A:2, V

“‘Hemp’ means the plant Cannabis sativa L. and any part of the plant, including the seeds thereof and all derivatives, extracts, cannabinoids, isomers, acids, salts, and salts of isomers, whether growing or not, with a total tetrahydrocannabinols concentration (including tetrahydrocannabinolic acid) of not more than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis.”

RSA 439-A:3: “Hemp is an agricultural product which may be grown as a crop.” (The current merged text is a single sentence; the pre-2021 licensing/committee language was removed — growers are licensed by USDA, see below.)

CURRENT · RSA 439-A:4 (2023, 237:6)

“Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to authorize the sale of products that are derived from hemp which contain natural or synthetic tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) greater than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis, which appear in any formulation, including delta-8 THC, delta-9 THC, or any other THC isomer variant.”

FROM 1 JAN 2027 · RSA 439-A:4 (2026, 275:2)

Same text plus “tetrahydrocannabinolic acid (THCA)” in the isomer list and a second sentence: “Such products are prohibited.”

THC measurement — NH vs USDA/federal

  • NH statute (current): delta-9 THC only, 0.3% dry weight, for the plant definition; for products, 439-A:4 uses THC “in any formulation, including delta-8, delta-9, or any other THC isomer variant” >0.3% — i.e. NH product law already reaches delta-8 etc. even though the plant definition says delta-9. Claims in secondary sources that NH “classifies delta-8 >0.3% as a Schedule I controlled substance” were not verified in the RSA 318-B text opened — treat as Verify, do not repeat as law.
  • NH statute (from 1 Jan 2027): total tetrahydrocannabinols including THCA, 0.3% dry weight — a total-THC standard in the definition itself.
  • USDA hemp production rule (7 CFR Part 990, federal — NH producers are under the USDA plan): THC is measured as total THC on a post-decarboxylation basis — delta-9 THC + (THCA × 0.877) — with a 0.3% threshold and measurement-uncertainty / negligent-violation framework. The NH RSA text does not itself contain the 0.877 formula or “post-decarboxylation” language — the formula is federal rule, not NH statute; cite the USDA rule for it, not RSA 439-A.
  • Federal statutory definition (Pub. L. 119-37 §781, as narrowed by Pub. L. 119-103 §2019): total THC incl. THCA ≤0.3% dry weight; synthesised/manufactured cannabinoids excluded; final products >0.4 mg combined total THC per container excluded from “hemp” (CRS, Part 0 #9). Under §2019, until 11 Dec 2026 §781 applies only to the not-naturally-producible-cannabinoid exclusions (from 12 Nov 2026); the balance applies from 11 Dec 2026. NH's 2027 total-THC definition aligns with the first element. NH has no 0.4 mg/container cap (resolved by the 24 Oct 2026 audit): the Senate-amended draft of SB 624 did contain a “Container” definition and a draft 439-A:4 II(a) capping final products at 0.4 mg total THC per container, but none of that survived into the chaptered law — the merged RSA 439-A:2 contains no “Container” paragraph and the 2027 RSA 439-A:4 is the single paragraph quoted above. The 0.4 mg/container cap is federal only; do not state it as NH law.

PART 3

Local Rules

Which level of government sets the rules, opt-in/opt-out, the official list of jurisdictions hosting cannabis businesses, local fees, zoning, and where businesses cluster.

3.1 Which level sets the rules?

Municipal (city/town) level, not county level — for land use. In NH, zoning and land-use regulation are municipal functions (RSA 674 framework; ATC applications must show “compliance with any local zoning laws” and DHHS must solicit input “in partnership with the local governing body of the town or city” — RSA 126-X:7, IV(b)–(c)). Counties in NH are a limited layer of government (county courts/jails, nursing homes, registry of deeds, sheriff) and do not enact zoning or cannabis-business licensing ordinances — no county cannabis ordinance was found for any NH county. DHHS reports patient data by county (Part 6), which is a statistical geography, not county regulation. A precise RSA citation for county powers was not opened — the municipal conclusion above rests on RSA 126-X itself, which refers throughout to towns/cities and never to counties as regulators.

Source: RSA 126-X:7 (2026-10-23)

3.2 Opt-in / opt-out, moratoriums, bans, caps

  • Adult-use opt-in/opt-out: does not exist, because there is no adult-use law. The opt-in municipal-vote mechanisms in HB 1633 / HB 186 (proposed RSA 318-F) died with those bills.
  • Medical ATCs: no municipal opt-in/opt-out statute. A town/city cannot enact a blanket opt-out in RSA 126-X; instead, siting is controlled by (i) the state bars (no residential district, no pre-existing drug-free school zone), (ii) ordinary local zoning (the ATC must comply; DHHS weighs zoning compliance + geographic convenience), and (iii) the DHHS competitive process with mandatory local-government/public input. He-C 402.05 (as summarised in the rule materials) requires the selected ATC to file local certifications of compliance from the municipal health officer, building official, and zoning official, plus fire approvals — Verify rule number/text in full He-C 402.
  • Local caps / moratoriums for ATCs or hemp/smoke shops: No compiled official list exists and none was retrieved town-by-town — Unavailable. No ATC-town moratorium was identified in sources opened. Hemp retail / smoke shops are subject only to general local zoning/business rules town-by-town; no NH-wide local-cap data exists.
  • Curbside pickup is the one expressly localised medical rule found: municipalities may restrict it (He-C 402.20(k)(4)).

PART 4

Licence Types

Every state cannabis licence type. New Hampshire has exactly one cannabis business licence — the vertically integrated, nonprofit ATC registration certificate.

4.1 Cannabis (medical) — the only state cannabis licences

ATC Registration Certificate (RSA 126-X:7)The sole cannabis business licence type

What it allows: Vertically integrated by definition — one nonprofit entity acquires, possesses, cultivates, manufactures, delivers, transfers, transports, sells, supplies, and dispenses (RSA 126-X:1, I). There are no separate NH cultivation-only, manufacturing-only, retail-only, transporter, or testing-lab cannabis licences (testing labs are licensed by DHHS under RSA 151 for health-facility purposes, not under a cannabis-lab licence type).

Key limits: Cap: max 4 certificates statewide (126-X:7, III). One primary dispensary per geographic area + a DHHS-authorised second dispensary (dispensing/education only) (126-X:7, X); a second cultivation location only as authorised — incl. a greenhouse location from Aug 2026 (126-X:7, XI). Possession/cultivation caps: 126-X:8, XV(a) (see §1.2). Agents 21+, no felonies.

Fees — Unverified: the adopted He-C 402.04 text could not be obtained from the Secretary of State or DHHS in the original research or in the 24 Oct 2026 audit, and two conflicting accounts exist — neither may be stated as current: (i) a 2014 proposed-rule fiscal note: RFP submission $1,500; initial registration $80,000; annual renewal $80,000; name change $250; administrator change $250; plan-of-correction monitoring $1,000; post-construction inspection $1,200; ownership/location change charged as a new registration; (ii) secondary summaries claiming a current schedule of $3,000 application (RFA) fee, $20,000 selection fee (due within 10 days, credited toward programme expenses), $30,000 location change, $250 name change, $250 administrator change, plus ongoing allocated programme-expense assessments. Ongoing: DHHS allocates TCP programme expenses among ATCs (by usable cannabis dispensed / projected activity / prior-year adjustments), payable within 30 days of notice — there is no simple flat “annual licence fee” figure in either account. Registry/ATC fees must in aggregate cover DHHS's costs (RSA 126-X:6). Confirming source: the adopted He-C 402 text via the NH Secretary of State administrative rules / DHHS adopted-rule postings.

Requirements: Nonprofit; articles/bylaws; board incl. a NH-licensed physician/APRN/pharmacist + a qualifying patient, majority NH residents (the medical board member may not hold an ownership interest); proposed address (+ possible 2nd cultivation location); enclosed locked cultivation description; security/safety plan; distance from any pre-existing school; affordability policy for patients who cannot afford cannabis + plan for Medicaid/SSI/SSDI patients; organic-growing knowledge; quality/purity/dose-consistency steps; start-up timetable; nonprofit/business experience; recordkeeping/inventory plan. Competitive numerically-scored process, minimum score, local public input (RSA 126-X:7, IV).

Open / capped? Capped and currently closed in practice: DHHS must accept applications only if a certificate is revoked/relinquished/expires unrenewed, or fewer than 4 ATCs hold valid certificates (126-X:7, II–III). No open application window was announced in sources opened — [no RFA currently open, Verify with DHHS]. No upcoming window date found — Unavailable.

7.3 Sampling and testing under the USDA plan (NH growers)

  • Timing (7 CFR §990.24): “No more than 30 days prior to the anticipated harvest of cannabis plants, have a sampling agent collect samples from the cannabis plant for total delta-9 tetrahydrocannabinol concentration level testing.” The licensee may not harvest before samples are taken (§990.24(e)); a second sample is required if harvest is not completed within the regulatory window — Verify exact re-sample trigger in current §990.24/990.26 text.
  • Who samples: A USDA-certified sampling agent — the licensee may not collect their own compliance samples. USDA maintains a directory of certified sampling agents. The licensee must give the agent “complete and unrestricted access during business hours” to all hemp/other cannabis plants, production and storage areas, land, buildings and all locations listed in the licence (§990.24(d)); an authorised representative should be present at collection if possible (§990.24(c)).
  • What is sampled: Flowering tops — “cutting the top five to eight inches from the ‘main stem’… ‘terminal bud’… or ‘central cola’” — per lot (a contiguous area planted with the same variety/strain; each lot is sampled and tested separately). The method must be sufficient at a 95% confidence level that no more than 1% of plants in the lot would exceed the acceptable hemp THC level (§990.24(b), §990.25).
  • Testing: By a laboratory using post-decarboxylation or other similarly reliable method for total THC; labs report through HeMP. USDA has delayed enforcement of the DEA-registered-laboratory requirement until December 31, 2026 (USDA AMS Hemp Production page, accessed 2026-10-23). Secondary summaries conflict (Dec 31, 2025 vs Dec 31, 2026) — the current AMS page states 31 Dec 2026; treat the 2025 date as superseded. Verify against the AMS “Information for Hemp Testing Laboratories” page before acting Source: ams.usda.gov — hemp
  • Costs: Sampling and testing fees are paid by the licensee to the sampling agent/lab, not to USDA; amounts vary by provider — no official NH schedule exists. Unavailable
  • Non-compliant lots: Lots exceeding the acceptable THC level must be disposed of or remediated under 7 CFR Part 990 (reported via AMS-27 in HeMP); negligent/culpable violations carry programme consequences under Part 990. Source: ecfr.gov — Part 990 (accessed 2026-10-23)

10.2 Interstate — therapeutic cannabis

Visiting qualifying patients (reciprocity) — current statute

RSA 126-X:2 V: “A valid registry identification card, or its equivalent, that is issued under the laws of another state, district, territory, commonwealth, or insular possession of the United States, or under the laws of Canada that allows, in the jurisdiction of issuance, a visiting qualifying patient to possess cannabis for therapeutic purposes, shall have the same force and effect and be subject to the same restrictions as a valid registry identification card issued by the department in this state, provided that: (a) [Repealed.] (b) A visiting qualifying patient shall not cultivate cannabis in New Hampshire or obtain or purchase cannabis from a New Hampshire qualifying patient or designated caregiver.”

Source: gc.nh.gov — RSA 126-X (accessed 2026-10-23). “Visiting qualifying patient” is defined in RSA 126-X:1 XVI (non-resident, or resident <90 days, holding such a card/equivalent).

What visitors may do: Buy at an ATC (ATCs may dispense to visiting qualifying patients — RSA 126-X:8 XV(b)(4); He-C 402.20 dispensing/ID rules, Part 11), subject to the same 2 oz / 10-day limit — He-C 402.20(e) expressly applies the limit to “a qualifying patient or a visiting qualifying patient” (adopted rule text, confirmed in the 24 Oct 2026 audit).

Purchase-count cap — resolved (audit, 24 Oct 2026): there is none in current law. RSA 126-X:2, V(a) is “[Repealed.]” in the current statute, and the final adopted He-C 402.20(j) (eff. 1 Oct 2025) contains no purchase-count cap. Transporting cannabis across state lines remains a federal offence regardless of the other state's law (no NH official source stating a NH-specific border rule was found — Unavailable). 11.3 Cultivation rules

RSA 126-X:8 XV(a), verbatim: “An alternative treatment center shall not possess or cultivate cannabis in excess of the following quantities: (1) Eighty cannabis plants, 160 seedlings, and 80 ounces of usable cannabis, or 6 ounces of usable cannabis per qualifying patient; and (2) Three mature cannabis plants, 12 seedlings, and 6 ounces for each qualifying patient registered as a qualifying patient under this chapter.”

12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke shops

Definition (RSA 318-B:1 X-a, as quoted in ACLU-NH legislative materials — Verify paragraph number in current gc.nh.gov text): “‘Drug paraphernalia’ means all equipment, products and materials of any kind which are used or intended for use or customarily intended for use in planting, propagating, cultivating, growing, harvesting, manufacturing, compounding, converting, producing, processing, preparing, testing, analyzing, packaging, repackaging, storing, containing, concealing, ingesting, inhaling, or otherwise introducing into the human body a controlled substance in violation of this chapter.” Source: aclu-nh.org — HB 287 fact sheet (accessed 2026-10-23)

Offence (official text) — RSA 318-B:26 III: “A person shall be guilty of a misdemeanor who: … (c) Manufactures with the intent to deliver, delivers or possesses with the intent to deliver any drug paraphernalia when such paraphernalia is knowingly manufactured, delivered or possessed for one or more of the uses set forth in RSA 318-B:2, II.” Source: gc.nh.gov — RSA 318-B:26 (accessed 2026-10-23)

The offence as codified targets manufacture/delivery/possession-with-intent-to-deliver, not mere personal possession of paraphernalia; therapeutic-use paraphernalia is expressly within “therapeutic use” for registered patients (RSA 126-X:1 XIII). Note on paragraph numbers: RSA 318-B:2-f is, from 1 Jan 2027, the criminal-adulteration section enacted by SB 624 (2026, 275:6) — see §1.5 — so bill-draft references to “318-B:2-f” in a drug-checking context are superseded; whether any drug-checking-equipment amendment (e.g. to RSA 318-B:1) was enacted remains Verify — do not state any draft as law. Smoke shops: No NH “smoke shop” / head-shop licence category was found; such retailers operate under ordinary business/local rules + RSA 318-B:26 III(c) + RSA 439-A:4 for any hemp products sold (Part 7). Municipal paraphernalia ordinances exist in some towns (e.g. Town of Northumberland Drug Paraphernalia Ordinance, adopted 2 May 2022: use/possession-with-intent-to-use = violation, $124 fine — (secondary/local) northumberlandnh.gov) — municipal rules vary; do not generalise one town's ordinance statewide.

12.4 Consumption lounges and events

None authorised — none enacted. RSA 126-X permits dispensing only at registered ATC dispensaries and therapeutic use subject to RSA 126-X:3: “The smoking or vaporization of cannabis in any public place” (incl. public vehicles, parks, beaches, fields) exempts no one (RSA 126-X:3 II(c)); possession is barred in schools (drug-free zones), workplaces (without employer permission), correctional facilities, public recreation/youth centres, and law-enforcement facilities (RSA 126-X:3 II(d)). 12.5 Cannabis tourism and visitor purchase limits

Recap of Part 10: visitors with US state/territory or Canadian therapeutic cards may buy at ATCs; may not cultivate; may not buy from NH patients/caregivers; same 2 oz possession (RSA 126-X:2 I) and 2 oz/10-day dispensing framework. There is no adult-use tourism market in NH — a non-patient tourist may possess only within RSA 318-B:2-c decriminalised amounts and commits a violation-level offence in doing so; purchase from any NH seller other than an ATC dispensing to a registered/visiting patient is an unlawful sale (RSA 318-B). There is no purchase-count cap for visitors in current law — RSA 126-X:2, V(a) is repealed and the adopted He-C 402.20(j) contains none (audit-resolved; see §10.2). DHHS requires ATCs to retain copies of visitor ID/card documents (He-C 402.20(c)(4)).

12.6 Landlord and tenant rules

RSA 126-X:3 I, verbatim: “A qualifying patient may use cannabis on privately-owned real property only with written permission of the property owner or, in the case of leased property, with the permission of the tenant in possession of the property, except that a tenant shall not allow a qualifying patient to smoke cannabis on rented property if smoking on the property violates the lease or the lessor's rental policies that apply to all tenants at the property. However, a tenant may permit a qualifying patient to use cannabis on leased property by ingestion or inhalation through vaporization even if smoking is prohibited by the lease or rental policies. For purposes of this chapter, vaporization shall mean the inhalation of cannabis without the combustion of the cannabis.” Source: gc.nh.gov — RSA 126-X (accessed 2026-10-23)

Practical effect: a lease may effectively ban smoking (if the ban applies to all tenants), but it may not bar a tenant permitting a qualifying patient to ingest or vaporise cannabis on the leased property. Cultivation is moot — patients/caregivers may not cultivate at all (RSA 126-X:1 XIII(c)), so no tenancy cultivation right exists. (Adult-use bill drafts containing broader landlord language, e.g. SB 263 amendments, are not law.) Property owners generally need not allow guests/visitors to use cannabis (RSA 126-X:3 III(b)).

12.7 Gun ownership conflicts — federal vs state (labelled)

  • Federal (18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) / (d)(3)): It is unlawful for a person “who is an unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance” to possess/receive firearms or ammunition, and ATF has treated state medical-cannabis users as prohibited persons (ATF 2011 Open Letter to FFLs; ATF Form 4473). Two 2026 developments narrow — but do not remove — that bar: (1) ATF's interim final rule (eff. 22 Jan 2026) rewrote 27 CFR 478.11 to require regular use continuing into the present; (2) in United States v. Hemani, No. 24-1234 (decided 18 June 2026, Gorsuch, J.), the Supreme Court held a status-only §922(g)(3) prosecution of a regular marijuana user for in-home possession, with no showing of danger, inconsistent with the Second Amendment — an as-applied, narrow holding. Note also that the April 2026 Schedule III rule (Part 2) does not by itself remove medical users from §922(g)(3)'s text, and no ATF safe harbour for them was located — Verify current ATF position before acting. Sources: 18 U.S.C. §922; 27 CFR 478.11 (as amended Jan 2026); US v. Hemani, No. 24-1234 (2026) (accessed 2026-10-24)
  • NH's own warning (official): DHHS must give every patient/caregiver a statement explaining federal law, warning that using cannabis “may be subject to the denial of rights and privileges by federal agencies including, but not limited to… the right to own, possess, or purchase a firearm and/or ammunition” and that a state card “does not protect a person from federal criminal penalties” (RSA 126-X:4 VI).
  • State bar: No NH statute bars a therapeutic-cannabis patient from owning/possessing a firearm under state law (none found in RSA 126-X / RSA 159 searches in Run 2 — Verify RSA 159:3 felon-in-possession scope, which turns on felony/drug-trafficking convictions, not patient status). The conflict is federal-only on current findings.

12.8 Pet and veterinary products

  • NH law: No NH statute authorising veterinarians to certify, recommend, prescribe or dispense cannabis for animals was found — none enacted/found. RSA 126-X is limited to human qualifying patients (“provider” = licensed to prescribe drugs to humans, RSA 126-X:1 VII). A veterinarian therefore has no NH therapeutic-cannabis authority for pets.
  • Federal / professional position: FDA has approved no animal drug containing cannabis/cannabis-derived compounds; products marketed for pets with therapeutic claims are unapproved animal drugs considered unsafe under the FDCA; AVMA guidance is that veterinarians should not administer/prescribe/dispense/recommend such products, while they may discuss risks/quality with clients. Sources (secondary, reproducing AVMA/FDA FAQ): gvma.net · todaysveterinarypractice.com (accessed 2026-10-23)
  • Hemp pet products sold at retail are ordinary consumer products subject to RSA 439-A:4 (>0.3% THC prohibition) and general consumer law; there is no NH hemp-pet-product licence or approval (none found). Owners giving human CBD/hemp products to pets do so outside any NH veterinary framework.

12.9 Medical cannabis in schools and facilities

  • Schools — prohibition (official): Possession of cannabis in “The building and grounds of any preschool, elementary, or secondary school, which are located in an area designated as a drug free zone” is not exempted by RSA 126-X (RSA 126-X:3 II(d)(1)) — therapeutic possession on school grounds remains exposed to arrest/prosecution. No school-nurse administration rule for therapeutic cannabis was found in RSA 126-X or He-C 401/402 excerpts. Unavailable / none found — Verify He-C 401 minor-patient sections + Dept. of Education rules before stating absolutely Minor patients (7 in NH at 30 June 2024 — Run 1) obtain cannabis only through a parent/guardian designated caregiver (two providers incl. a paediatric provider must certify — RSA 126-X:4 V; ATCs dispense to minors only via the caregiver — He-C 402.20(d)).
  • Care facilities — the “facility caregiver” pathway (official): RSA 126-X:1 VI-a — a “residential care facility, nursing home, hospital, or hospice house licensed under RSA 151” (plus community living facilities certified under RSA 126-A:19/20) may agree to allow resident qualifying patients to use therapeutic cannabis obtained from an ATC by the resident or their designated caregiver, assisting “in accordance with a policy developed by the facility” and DHHS rules. Facility caregivers/designated employees are protected for possessing cannabis on facility premises to assist (RSA 126-X:2 XVI), and “A facility caregiver shall treat cannabis in a manner similar to controlled prescription medications with respect to its storage, security, and administration” (RSA 126-X:3 VIII). But no facility is required to accommodate therapeutic use: RSA 126-X:3 III(c) expressly preserves non-accommodation on the premises of “any… residential care facility, nursing home, hospital or hospice house, jail, correctional facility, or other… penal institution” and preserves employer discipline rights.
  • Employment (cross-cutting): Being under the influence at work without the employer's written permission is unprotected (RSA 126-X:3 II(a)(2)); possession at a place of employment without written employer permission is unprotected (RSA 126-X:3 II(d)(2)).
