How the federal baseline interacts with Iowa

The federal baseline below was supplied as already verified and was not re-researched in this run. Anything found that contradicts it is flagged.

  • Split scheduling: state-licensed medical marijuana and FDA-approved products moved to Schedule III effective 28 April 2026 (91 FR 22714). All adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. The broader 2024 rescheduling proposal is not final.
  • IRS §280E is generally lifted for Schedule III medical activity; adult-use remains fully subject to §280E.
  • Hemp: PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026. After 11 December 2026, the full total-THC hemp definition and the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply.
  • USDA enforcement of DEA-registered lab testing for hemp is delayed to 31 December 2026. The federal medical marijuana appropriations rider runs through 11 December 2026. There is no federal cannabis banking law.

Iowa application — Verify in part

Iowa's programme is state-licensed (Iowa Code ch. 124E, licences issued by Iowa HHS), so on the baseline above its manufacturers and dispensaries fall in the category described as moving to Schedule III, and §280E relief would in principle follow for that medical activity. However: whether a THC-capped cannabidiol programme — not called “medical marijuana” in Iowa statute — counts as “state-licensed medical marijuana” under the federal rule's exact text was not confirmed from the rule text in this run, and no Iowa guidance on the point was found. Non-medical possession in Iowa remains Schedule I federally and criminal under Iowa ch. 124.

Iowa does not automatically follow federal scheduling. Under Iowa Code §124.201 the Board of Pharmacy only recommends schedule changes to the General Assembly (annual recommendation, eight statutory factors); the legislature is not bound to follow federal scheduling. The only automatic federal-following provision in §124.201(4) concerns a new substance newly designated federally, with a board objection procedure — it does not automatically move an already-listed substance such as marijuana.

Source: Iowa Code §124.201 (legis.iowa.gov) — Accessed 30 October 2026.

Iowa hemp caps vs. the federal dates

Iowa's own caps — 0.3% dry weight and 4 mg per serving / 10 mg per container — are far above the coming federal cap. A compliant Iowa 4 mg serving / 10 mg container product would exceed the federal 0.4 mg per container cap after 11 December 2026 and would therefore cease to be federal “hemp” even if it still satisfied Iowa law, unless Iowa amends ch. 204 or a federal delay or repeal intervenes. The 12 November 2026 first stage (cannabinoids the plant cannot naturally produce) already overlaps Iowa's existing ban on synthetic consumable hemp products (§204.14A(3)).

What is legal

  • For non-patients: any marijuana (cannabis above the hemp threshold, or any THC product outside ch. 204 / 124E).

1.5 Consumable hemp

Legal definition and how THC is measured

“Consumable hemp product” (§204.2(2), as amended by HF 2605) is a hemp product that includes a substance metabolised or otherwise subject to a biotransformative process when introduced into the human body; it may be introduced by ingestion or absorption by any device including an electronic device (historical text), may be solid or liquid, and is deemed consumable if designed/advertised/distributed etc. to be introduced into the human body and it meets the THC test below. It includes noncombustible digestible food, internally absorbed chew/snuff, topical application, and hemp processed/marketed/sold/distributed as food, food additive, dietary supplement, or drug. It excludes any product whose intended use is introduction by inhalation (§204.14A).

Total THC = delta-9 THC + 0.877 × THCA (§124E.2(12) formula; 641 IAC 156.1 uses the same formula, per the HHS manufacturer guide). Hemp itself (ch. 204A.2, incorporating 7 CFR 990.1) is cannabis sativa L. with delta-9 THC ≤0.3% dry weight — the federal definition as incorporated; the coming federal total-THC definition is addressed in the federal box above.

1.8 Employment and drug-testing protections for patients

No employment protection — express non-protection

§124E.21: an employer need not permit or accommodate use/possession etc. in the workplace; may restrict use for health/safety; may contractually prohibit use; may enforce zero-tolerance / drug-free workplace policies via drug testing under §730.5 or federal law. §124E.22: no duty for Medicaid, private insurers, workers'-comp carriers or self-insured employers to reimburse medical-marijuana costs. §124E.23: property owners need not allow use/possession etc. on their property. §124E.24: no discrimination or civil-liability claim under ch. 216 or other law based on an act/policy permissible under ch. 124E — including refusing to hire, discharging, disciplining, or other adverse employment action.

In short: a patient has no statutory protection against adverse employment action for lawful programme use, and drug testing under §730.5 is expressly preserved.

Non-employment protections that do exist: §124E.12 affirmative defences (patients/caregivers, incl. dismissal if a valid card is produced at/before trial); §124E.12(5) (the state/political subdivision, incl. law enforcement, shall not remove or initiate removal of a minor from a parent's home solely because of authorised possession/use); §124E.12(6)–(9) (no civil/disciplinary penalty for HHS/practitioners solely for programme activities; no attorney discipline for assisting patients/caregivers; possession of a card/application is not probable cause or reasonable suspicion and shall not support a search/inspection).

How licences were awarded & headline requirements

Manufacturers: HHS request-for-proposals / competitive selection (pre-HF 990 wording); factors include technical expertise, employee qualifications, long-term financial stability, security, and ability to meet production needs (dosage range, chemical compositions, forms), plus fee projections/assessments. Application information is confidential until licensure. Dispensaries: HHS applications (HF 990 changed “request for proposals” to “accept applications”); factors include technical expertise, employee qualifications, financial stability, security and fee projections; locations are “based on geographical need.”

Requirements (both): no employee under 18; no employee/owner with a disqualifying felony (a federal/state felony with possession/use/distribution of a controlled substance as an element, 21 USC §802(6)); DCI + FBI national background checks (§124E.19); operating documents (oversight/recordkeeping, security); alarm/access/perimeter/personnel-ID security; no office-sharing, referral or financial relationship with a practitioner; 1,000-ft school buffer; department signage/marketing/display/advertising restrictions. Manufacturers must additionally contract with a laboratory for spot-check testing (below) and provide a reliable ongoing supply to dispensaries.

4.2 Testing laboratories

  • Statutory definition (§124E.2(8)): “‘Laboratory’ means the state hygienic laboratory or any other independent medical cannabidiol testing facility accredited to standard ISO/IEC 17025 by an [international standards-approved accrediting body], with a controlled substance registration certificate from the [DEA] and a certificate of registration from the board of pharmacy.” Independent = no equity ownership in a manufacturer.
  • Manufacturer duty (§124E.7(1)): contract with a laboratory for spot-check testing (content, contamination, consistency); the manufacturer pays; if the lab is not the State Hygienic Laboratory, HHS must approve it.
  • State Hygienic Laboratory's role: a 2022 report summary states “All products are tested by the University of Iowa's State Hygienic Laboratory (SHL)” (secondary report of an official report — Verify current practice; the 2025 report notes a Bureau priority project on “independent laboratory pathways,” suggesting SHL is not the sole pathway in future).

7.1 The production / product split — the organising fact

Iowa no longer has one hemp licence. Production (growing, harvesting, storing raw hemp) is federal / USDA; product manufacture and sale (especially consumable hemp) is state / HHS. 7.5 Pre-harvest sampling, lot testing, and the DEA-lab issue

  • Federal sampling (current USDA programme): a compliance sample from each lot within 30 days of anticipated harvest (7 CFR 990.24–990.26), collected by a certified sampling agent, not the licensee; labs prepare (dry/grind) samples — sampling agents do not. Testing must determine total THC (post-decarboxylation, THCA conversion) and report Measurement of Uncertainty; a lot is compliant if THC + MU ≤ 0.3% dry weight. USDA hemp FAQ — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Historical Iowa sampling (defunct, for contrast): an IDALS inspector verified geospatial coordinates, took a separate official sample per lot/sub-lot, and harvest had to follow within 15 days of sampling (day 0 = sampling day) or supplemental sampling was required; a non-compliant crop was ordered destroyed by a method agreed by licensee, IDALS and law enforcement. ARC 4842C (21 IAC 96) — Accessed 30 Oct 2026 Do not apply these timelines to USDA licensees.
  • DEA-lab delay: “The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Agricultural Marketing Service is delaying until December 31, 2026, the enforcement of a requirement that all hemp must be tested by a Drug Enforcement Agency (DEA)-registered laboratory.” The USDA FAQ page still shows the earlier 31 December 2025 date with a 14 November 2024 extension note — stale text on that page, superseded by the 31 December 2026 delay on the main USDA hemp page. USDA AMS hemp — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Consumable-hemp testing is separate from production testing: under ch. 204 / 156, products need a COA, maintained and updated in the HHS portal; testing requirements and COA issuance for hemp are governed by ch. 204A per §204.8 as amended by HF 2641. The approved-lab list for consumable products, the analyte panel and action levels in current 156 are Verify — not extracted in Run 2. Do not conflate USDA pre-harvest testing with HHS product COAs.

7.6 Interaction with the 12 November / 11 December 2026 federal dates

The federal baseline is carried forward unchanged (see the fixed-context section at the top): PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 November 2026 but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026, after which the full total-THC definition and the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply; USDA DEA-lab enforcement is delayed to 31 December 2026 (verified live in Run 2, above); the medical rider runs through 11 December 2026; there is no federal banking law.

  • Iowa consequence if the federal cap lands as described: Iowa's 10 mg/container cap would exceed a 0.4 mg federal per-container cap by 25×, and Iowa's total-THC formula (THC + 0.877 × THCA) would need to be read against any new federal total-THC definition. 10.2 Interstate issues

The general rule

A purchase lawful in another state does not make possession lawful in Iowa. Non-patient marijuana is illegal under ch. 124; medical possession is lawful only in the forms and quantities, and with the card/defence, set out in ch. 124E. Interstate transport of marijuana is also federally unlawful. Secondary guides agree: “Do not take Illinois cannabis into Iowa.” Enforcement specifics asserted in secondary guides (“Law enforcement actively patrols I-80 and I-29”) are unverified enforcement claims, not official policy.

  • Illinois (adult-use, 21+): Illinois sells to nonresidents; nonresident possession limits are cited as 30 g flower / 5 g concentrate / 500 mg THC in infused products (Secondary, post-12 June 2026 change — Verify in Illinois law). An Iowa resident may lawfully buy and possess in Illinois within Illinois limits, but bringing it into Iowa exposes them to Iowa possession prosecution unless the product and person fall within ch. 124E (Iowa-legal form + Iowa card or §124E.18 defence). Flower and edibles bought in Illinois are not Iowa-legal forms even for a cardholder (see reciprocity, below).
  • Minnesota (adult-use 21+; medical visiting patients): Minnesota sells to visitors 21+ (Secondary 2026 guide: 2 oz flower public possession, etc. — Verify in Minnesota law). For medical patients, Iowa Code §124E.15 provides:
    “A patient or a primary caregiver with a valid medical cannabidiol registration card issued pursuant to this chapter may register in the state of Minnesota as a visiting qualified patient or primary caregiver and may register with one or more medical cannabis manufacturers registered under the laws of Minnesota.”Iowa Code §124E.15 (2026), 2017 Acts ch. 162 §18 — legis.iowa.gov — Accessed 30 October 2026
    Note: this is an Iowa permission to register in Minnesota; Minnesota's current visiting-patient / adult-use rules govern actual purchase there, and return to Iowa is still governed by Iowa law — Verify Minnesota's current visiting-patient registration (Minnesota's adult-use rollout may have superseded the medical visiting route).
  • Missouri (adult-use 21+): the closest Missouri recreational dispensaries to Omaha/Council Bluffs are cited as Rock Port, ~70–75 miles (Secondary). The same bring-back rule as Illinois applies. No Iowa–Missouri agreement was found.
  • Nebraska: Nebraska voters approved limited medical possession (Nov 2024, up to 5 oz with a recommendation), but secondary guides state there was no operational state-licensed dispensary in Nebraska as of writing; recreational remains illegal. HF 990 rulemaking notes Nebraska as the expected source of new Iowa out-of-state patients precisely because it lacks an operational programme: regulators said removing residency is “expected to primarily impact qualified Nebraska residents since Nebraska is the only bordering state that does not currently have an operational medical or adult-use cannabis program.” Verify Nebraska's current dispensary status — it may have changed since that filing. Secondary — Marijuana Moment, HF 990 rulemaking coverage — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Bordering-state dispensaries (§124E.14, historical): the original §124E.14 required HHS to use an RFP by 1 December 2017 to select up to two out-of-state dispensaries from a bordering state to sell to Iowa cardholders:
    “The department of public health shall utilize a request for proposals process to select and license by December 1, 2017, up to two out-of-state medical cannabidiol dispensaries from a bordering state…”Iowa Code §124E.14 (2021 Code text) — Justia mirror — Accessed 30 October 2026

  • Reciprocity (§124E.18) — disposition after HF 990: the enrolled HF 990 amends only §124E.4 (residency/card) and §124E.8 (dispensaries) — it does not amend §124E.18 in the enrolled text read. §124E.18 therefore still reads (2019 Code text, to be re-checked in Code 2026):

12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke / vape shops

12.3 Impaired driving and testing — per se metabolite rule and the medical defence

  • OWI statute: Iowa Code §321J.2 criminalises operating while (a) under the influence, (b) alcohol ≥0.08, or (c) “any amount of a controlled substance is present” in blood or urine. The Iowa Supreme Court in State v. Comried read “any amount” as any amount greater than zero, even if non-impairing; State v. Childs declined to overrule Comried on the Arizona-authority point. (Secondary synthesis of the case history — Verify in the Iowa Reports before citing as current law.) Secondary OWI guides agree: “Any detectable amount of a Schedule I substance — including THC metabolites — in blood or urine can support an OWI conviction, with no impairment showing required,” and “holding a card does not exempt a driver from drug-OWI prosecution.”
  • Medical defence — narrow, and it does NOT clearly cover OWI: §124E.12 gives a complete affirmative defence to a possession prosecution for cardholders/caregivers, plus a dismissal rule if a valid card is produced at/before trial. For OWI, Iowa has a prescription-drug affirmative defence in ch. 321J (substance prescribed, taken as directed, no alcohol, practitioner did not direct refraining from driving; the State must disprove it beyond reasonable doubt once raised — State v. Schories, 827 N.W.2d 659). Whether a medical certification (not a prescription) + medical cannabidiol counts as “prescribed” for that defence is Verify — critical gap: medical cannabidiol is certified, not prescribed, and secondary sources state cardholders have no OWI exemption. Do not advise a patient that they have a driving defence. Iowa Code §124E.12 (Justia) — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Testing / implied consent: by driving in Iowa, a driver gives implied consent to breath/blood/urine testing on reasonable grounds + trigger (§321J.6); refusal means revocation (1 year for a first refusal, per secondary summary — Verify in §321J.9). THC metabolites detectable days–weeks after use are the practical risk for patients. Commercial drivers: federal DOT rules prohibit medical cannabis use, with CDL loss risk (Secondary — Verify in the DOT rules).
  • Consumption places (related): medical cannabidiol may not be consumed in places open to the public (except a healthcare facility if its SOPs permit), on school property (unless school SOPs permit), or on dispensary/manufacturer property — 154.12 / HHS summary (Secondary summary of 154.12 — Verify in current 154.12).

12.4 Expungement and record clearing

No automatic cannabis clearing, and no cannabis-specific expungement statute

The automatic expungement proposed in HF 2206 (2026) is not law (§9.2). The routes below are the general ones.

“Upon application of a defendant convicted of a misdemeanor offense in the county where the conviction occurred, the court shall enter an order expunging the record … if the defendant has proven: a. More than eight years have passed since the date of the conviction. b. The defendant has no pending criminal charges. c. The defendant has not previously been granted two deferred judgments. d. The defendant has paid all court costs, fees, fines, restitution, and any other financial obligations…”Iowa Code §901C.3 (Code 2026) — legis.iowa.gov (PDF) — Accessed 30 October 2026
  • General misdemeanor expungement (§901C.3) is the only route for most marijuana misdemeanors. “A person shall be granted an expungement … one time in the person's lifetime” (multiple misdemeanors from the same transaction/occurrence may be in one application). Excluded misdemeanors include §321J.2 (OWI) convictions — so a marijuana OWI conviction is not expungeable under §901C.3 — plus alcohol, sex, assault and other listed offences; marijuana possession itself is not in the exclusion list read, but Verify the full §901C.3(2) list for any drug-specific exclusion.
  • Non-conviction records (§901C.2): 180 days after a not-guilty verdict / dismissal of all charges (except simple-misdemeanor ch. 321 / local traffic), the court may expunge on application by the defendant, prosecutor or court, if costs/fees are paid.
  • Deferred judgment (§§907.3 / 907.9): plead guilty, probation, successful completion → discharge without judgment/conviction and expungement of that record (on payment of obligations). This is the practical pre-conviction path for first possession cases; the eligibility bars for OWI deferred judgments (refusal, prior OWI conviction/deferred, injury, alcohol ≥0.15) are in §907.3 (Secondary summary — Verify in the Code).
  • Pardon: a governor's pardon remains the only route for convictions outside §901C.3 (e.g. felonies, OWI) — secondary consensus, Verify in Iowa Const. art. IV / ch. 914.
  • Penalties context (for expungement relevance): first marijuana possession: up to 6 months / $1,000 (§124.401(5)(b)); second: serious misdemeanor, up to 1 year / $430–$2,560; third+: aggravated misdemeanor, up to 2 years / $855–$8,540. (Secondary citing the Code.)

12.5 Landlord and tenant

No cannabis-specific landlord/tenant statute was found. General rules apply:

  • Leases may prohibit smoking, drug use, or illegal activity; a landlord may enforce a lease ban on smoking/vaping even for a medical patient, because medical use does not create a right to use on another's property and smoking itself is not an approved form. Verify for any disability-accommodation argument (federal Fair Housing / Iowa ch. 216) — no Iowa holding was located.
  • Clear-and-present-danger termination (§562B.25A): a landlord may terminate on 3 days' notice and sue for possession if a tenant creates a clear/present danger, including “Possession of a controlled substance unless the controlled substance was obtained directly from or pursuant to a valid prescription or order by a licensed medical practitioner…” Whether medical certification counts as a “prescription or order” for this paragraph is Verify and likely litigable. Iowa Code §562B.25A (2001 Code text) — Verify the current wording. Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Federally assisted housing: federal controlled-substance rules (including marijuana as federally controlled, subject to the Run 1 Schedule III medical split — Verify HUD guidance) can require eviction for drug-related activity regardless of Iowa medical status. No Iowa statute overrides this — Verify HUD / Iowa Finance Authority guidance.
  • Growing: home grow is illegal for everyone (Part 1); a lease ban on cultivation is therefore enforceable as a ban on illegal activity, not a cannabis-specific rule.
  • Consumable hemp: lawful hemp products are not controlled substances; a landlord ban on “smoking” would still cover inhalable hemp (itself banned for sale in Iowa), while a ban on “cannabis” in a lease is a contract question — Verify per lease.

12.6 Gun ownership conflicts for patients

  • Federal conflict (the operative risk): the federal Gun Control Act prohibits firearm possession/purchase by an “unlawful user of … any controlled substance” (18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3)); ATF Form 4473 asks about unlawful use. Iowa medical status does not override federal law. Secondary Iowa guidance: “Iowa gun law does not explicitly address medical cannabidiol cardholders… the state is a shall-issue state and defers to federal laws” and “Federal law restricts firearm possession and purchases by people who are unlawful users of controlled substances.” Secondary — iowacannabis.org, May 2026 — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
  • Schedule III twist — Verify, do not advise: the federal baseline splits state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III effective 28 April 2026. Whether that changes “unlawful user” status for Iowa cardholders under ATF guidance / §922(g)(3) case law (litigated post-rescheduling in other circuits) was not verified in Run 2 and must not be presented as resolved. 12.7 Pet and veterinary products

  • No Iowa pet/veterinary CBD statute or HHS pet-product rule was located. What is established:
    • HHS identifies animal products as prohibited under its consumable hemp programme (Secondary summary of HHS guidance). That is, a product marketed for animals is not a compliant human consumable hemp product; whether it may be sold as a non-consumable / animal supplement is Verify in the 156 definitions and FDA animal-drug law.
    • IDALS: “The 2018 Farm Bill did not override the FDA's regulatory authority to approve the use of hemp and hemp products in animal feed” (FAQ). IDALS hemp page — Accessed 30 Oct 2026
