How this audit was done. The auditor did not write the original report. Every claim cluster below was checked against primary sources where a primary source could be opened: the Kansas Statutes Annotated and bill pages on kslegislature.gov, the Kansas Administrative Regulations, the Kansas Department of Agriculture, the Kansas Department of Revenue, the Kansas Board of Pharmacy, the KBI, the Attorney General's official opinions (Kansas Register), Kansas Supreme Court and Court of Appeals material, county election offices and the Secretary of State, official tribal government sources, and official county, city and Unified Government sites and codes, plus the USDA plan list and USDA NASS releases. News, law-firm blogs, trade press and AI summaries were never used to confirm a claim; where only a secondary source exists, the verdict is Unverified. The federal baseline in the commission was used as given and was not re-audited, except to check that the report states it correctly and that Kansas's responses to it are accurate: the report does state it correctly, and Kansas marijuana remains Schedule I in Kansas because Kansas has no state-licensed medical programme.

Verdict counts (they sum exactly to the rows audited). Total rows: 115. Confirmed: 41. Corrected: 25. Removed: 2. Unverified: 47. 41 + 25 + 2 + 47 = 115.

1.4 CBD

What is legal: CBD itself is excluded from “marijuana” (65-4101(aa)(4) in the current definition; the report’s 2021 Supp. numbering had it at (aa)(3), with hemp at (aa)(4) — now (aa)(5)). Secondary “zero-THC” / “no detectable THC” descriptions of Kansas retail CBD are not adopted as a general rule; the statute sets a ≤0.3% total-THC limit for a lawful final hemp product (2-3901(4)). SB 282’s 0% figure is the narrower definition of a “CBD product” sold as an herbal supplement, not a general 0% rule for all retail CBD. Conflict flagged: the safest statute-supported statement is: CBD alone is not marijuana; any THC in a product is judged under 65-4105 / 2-3901 / 2-3908 and AG 2021-4 — not by a freestanding “CBD is always legal” rule. Verify with Board of Pharmacy / KDA whether a current “0%” enforcement standard is published.

1.5 Hemp and intoxicating hemp

  • Measurement: plant = ≤0.3% delta-9 THC dry weight (2-3901(7)); delta-9 concentration = combined % of delta-9 + optical isomers/salts/acids reported as free THC — dry weight for plant, % by weight for products/waste (2-3901(2)); final products = ≤0.3% total THC per 65-6235(b)(3) (2-3901(4)). That total-THC-for-products definition is why THCA counts toward the product limit in secondary analyses — lab method (post-decarboxylation) Verify in K.A.R./USDA rule.
  • Delta-9 in a lawful final hemp product ≤0.3% total THC: within hemp exception.
  • Delta-8 — OPINION 2021-4 (not statute), 2 Dec 2021, AG Derek Schmidt to KBI Director Kirk Thompson:
“Delta-8 tetrahydrocannabinol (Delta-8 THC) comes within the definition of a Schedule I controlled substance and is unlawful to possess or sell in Kansas unless it is made from industrial hemp and is contained in a lawful hemp product having no more than 0.3% total tetrahydrocannabinols (THC). Unlawful hemp products include cigarettes, cigars, teas, and substances for use in vaping devices. Delta-8 THC derived from any source other than industrial hemp is a Schedule I controlled substance…”

AG opinions are not binding law and this one has not, on sources found, been finally adjudicated on the merits — but KBI enforcement follows it (Oct 2025 operation). 10.2 Interstate issues

Hemp shipments (lawful hemp): 2018 Farm Bill §10114 (7 U.S.C. §1639o note): “(a) Nothing in this title… prohibits the interstate commerce of hemp… (b) No State or Indian Tribe shall prohibit the transportation or shipment of hemp or hemp products produced in accordance with subtitle G… through the State or the territory of the Indian Tribe.” (Quoted via a shipper policy page, secondary — verify the statutory text in the U.S. Code.) Practical limit: the protection covers hemp produced in accordance with an approved plan/USDA licence. A load testing >0.3% delta-9 (or, after the federal changes in the baseline, above the new total-THC / per-container limits) is marijuana, not protected hemp, and Kansas’s K.S.A. 2-3908 product bans apply to products marketed/sold/distributed in Kansas even if lawful where made. Documentation practice (not a Kansas statute): carry the producer licence, a COA from a DEA-registered lab, and a bill of lading — the same documents K.A.R. 22-26-13 requires of processors. No Kansas hemp-transport permit, manifest or pre-notification statute was found — Verify. USPS accepts hemp products only as permitted by applicable state and federal law; international shipment is prohibited (secondary summary) — Verify USPS Publication 52 on the day of use.

Kansas criminalises possession of marijuana/THC in Kansas (K.S.A. 21-5706) regardless of where it was bought or whether the purchase was lawful there. Part 11 — Operations Rules

“Growers” = USDA-licensed producers. “Product businesses” = Fire Marshal-registered processors + unlicensed hemp-product sellers. Where Kansas has no rule, this section says so rather than filling the gap.

11.1 Testing

Growers (7 CFR Part 990, Subpart C):

  • Pre-harvest sampling: a certified sampling agent must collect samples no more than 30 days before anticipated harvest, from the flowering tops (top ~5–8 inches including bud/flower material per USDA sampling guidelines as summarised in results); sampling must give 95% confidence that ≤1% of plants in the lot exceed the acceptable THC level.
  • Lab: only a DEA-registered laboratory may conduct compliance testing. Deadline: use the fixed federal baseline — USDA enforcement of DEA-registered-laboratory testing is delayed to 31 Dec 2026. An older USDA FAQ excerpt giving 31 Dec 2025 is not adopted. Labs must use validated methods, report measurement of uncertainty, and report results through HeMP (producer name, licence number, FSA lot ID, test date, pre/post-harvest).
  • Method: post-decarboxylation or similarly reliable method; total delta-9 THC (THC + THCA converted). KDA’s laboratory page confirms its method “measures both THC and its acid form [THCA]”.
  • Harvest window: harvest no later than 30 days after sample collection; if not completed, a second pre-harvest sample is required. Lots may not be commingled. A producer may request retesting if the result is believed erroneous (7 CFR 990.26).
  • Failed (“hot”) tests: lots exceeding the acceptable hemp THC level “constitute marijuana, a schedule I controlled substance” and must be disposed of via a DEA-registered reverse distributor or law enforcement, or disposed of on-site, or remediated (with post-remediation re-sampling/testing); the producer must notify USDA of intent to dispose/remediate (7 CFR 990.27). Negligent-violation tiers: 0.3–1.0% with reasonable efforts = no negligent violation (disposal still required); >1.0% or other negligent violations trigger a corrective action plan; 3 negligent violations in 5 years = 5-year ineligibility; intentional violations are reported to law enforcement — verify tier thresholds in the current eCFR text, as secondary summaries vary on the 1.0% line.
  • Legacy Kansas rule: former K.A.R. 4-34-24 (licensee “may not harvest any hemp before receiving notice that the test results show” <0.3% delta-9) is cited by a secondary source as still on the books — whether KDA still issues harvest-clearance notices under USDA licensing is Verify.
  • Recalls (growers): no Kansas hemp-crop recall procedure was found beyond disposal/remediation + USDA reporting.

Product businesses (processors): K.A.R. 22-26-16 requires the processor to submit samples to a testing laboratory at the Fire Marshal’s request and to allow the Fire Marshal to take samples on the premises, at the processor’s expense. No routine batch-testing, potency, contaminant (pesticide/heavy-metal/microbial) or labelling-COA mandate for Kansas processors was found in K.A.R. Article 26 — contrast SB 292 (proposed only), which would have required KDA lab testing/certification. Retail COAs are therefore a market practice, not a verified Kansas legal requirement — Verify before advising a retailer. Stop-sale analogue: Fire Marshal stop-sale, use or removal orders (K.A.R. 22-26-15) — inventory status quo maintained; no order valid >7 calendar days; plus cease-and-desist / criminal complaint under K.S.A. 31-139 for fire-code violations. Part 12 — Consumer and Social Rules

12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke/vape shop rules

  • Paraphernalia — K.S.A. 21-5709 (summary from statute summaries; verbatim text Verify on kslegislature.gov): unlawful to use or possess with intent to use drug paraphernalia to store, conceal, ingest, inhale or otherwise introduce a controlled substance into the body, and to manufacture/distribute paraphernalia. As summarised in multiple sources: personal-use possession = Class B nonperson misdemeanour (≤6 months / ≤$1,000); paraphernalia for manufacturing/distribution (or cultivation, except <5 marijuana plants) = drug severity level 5 felony; <5-plant cultivation paraphernalia = Class B misdemeanour; sale of paraphernalia is treated as a felony in secondary summaries (level stated as 3 in one summary — Verify in 21-5709(e) before citing the sale level); advertising paraphernalia for sale = Class A misdemeanour in one summary — Verify. Personal-use Class B and the (b)(1)/(b)(2) levels are confirmed on current statute text in this audit; only the sale level remains Verify. This corrects/complements Part 1.3, where the exact paraphernalia citation and penalty were left as Verify.
  • Hemp vs marijuana paraphernalia: paraphernalia for lawful hemp use is not paraphernalia for a controlled substance, but an item that can be used for marijuana (pipe, bong, papers, grinder, vape device) is routinely charged as marijuana paraphernalia when paired with marijuana or its residue — the item’s intended use is the issue. A shop selling a pipe is not thereby committing a paraphernalia offence unless the sale/use elements of 21-5709 are met — a charging-practice point, Verify with a Kansas criminal practitioner / case law; no Kansas paraphernalia-shop licensing case was found.
  • Smoke/vape shops — no Kansas hemp-shop licence: a shop selling only lawful hemp/CBD products needs no Kansas hemp licence (Part 7). It still needs general business registration, sales-tax registration, and — if it sells tobacco/e-cigarette products — KDOR tobacco licensing (below). Local zoning/business licensing applies generally — Verify per city.
  • Tobacco / e-cigarette retail (the licence that does exist): K.S.A. 79-3303(a): “No person shall engage in the business of selling or distributing cigarettes or tobacco products at retail without having first obtained a license for each place of business” (quoted in a secondary guide). Retail Cigarette Dealer / E-Cigarette Licence: $25 per establishment, biennial (2-year) term (K.S.A. 79-3304, per secondary summaries — Verify fee/term on KDOR’s current page); vending-machine distributor $50 + $25/machine (same summaries). Consumable-material (e-liquid) tax registration (Form EC-1, free) is additionally required for retailers selling untaxed consumable material; excise tax $0.05/ml of consumable material (K.S.A. 79-3399, per summaries). The Director of Revenue may suspend/revoke under 79-3309 (30 days to request a hearing; up to 1-year reapplication bar in one summary — Verify). All tobacco figures here are secondary (index, 30 Oct 2026).
  • Critical distinction: a KDOR tobacco/e-cigarette licence does not authorise sale of hemp vape liquids/solids/gases — those are banned hemp products under K.S.A. 2-3908(a)(1)(E) regardless of tobacco licensing. Conversely, a hemp processor registration does not authorise tobacco sales. Shops selling both must satisfy both regimes — the Oct 2025 AG/KBI raids targeted shops precisely at this intersection.

12.3 Impaired driving law and testing

  • Offence — K.S.A. 8-1567 (impairment standard, no per-se THC limit): DUI by operating/attempting to operate a vehicle “while under the influence of any drug [or alcohol + drug combination] to a degree that renders the person incapable of safely driving a vehicle” (NORML summary of 8-1567 — verify current subsection numbering). Kansas sets no THC blood-concentration per-se limit (contrast alcohol 0.08% BAC; under-21 alcohol 0.02% under 8-1567a — alcohol rules, not THC rules). Prosecution rests on observed impairment (field sobriety, Drug Recognition Expert evaluation) plus blood/urine confirmation of drugs/metabolites.
  • Habitual-user prong: older summaries cite a DUI prong for a person who is a “habitual user of any narcotic” — whether that prong still exists in current 8-1567 and whether it covers marijuana is Verify.
  • Defence bar (older summary, Verify): “the fact that the person is or has been entitled to use the drug under the laws of this state shall not constitute a defense” (former 8-1567(c)) — lawful use elsewhere is not a DUI defence in Kansas on the face of that provision; Verify current text. This matters for out-of-state medical use and for Claire and Lola’s Law CBD oil.
  • Penalties (secondary summaries — Verify in 8-1567(b)): 1st DUI = Class B nonperson misdemeanour; 48 consecutive hours–6 months jail (or 100 hours community service in lieu of the minimum, at court discretion) + $750–$1,000 fine; 3rd DUI within 10 years of a prior = felony, 90 days minimum (summaries vary on the look-back).
  • Testing — K.S.A. 8-1001: a person operating/attempting to operate a vehicle “may be requested… to submit to one or more tests of the person’s blood, breath, urine or other bodily substance to determine the presence of alcohol or drugs”, including quantitative and qualitative drug tests, at the officer’s direction on probable cause of an 8-1567 violation (plus arrest/collision conditions); oral + written notice must be given, including that refusal → licence suspension (1 year for a first refusal in the Justia 2025 text excerpt — Verify the full suspension schedule); refusal may be used against the person at trial; qualitative drug-presence results are admissible, accuracy going to weight. Breath testing does not detect THC — blood/urine is the practical THC test. No Kansas roadside oral-fluid THC test statute was found — Verify.
  • Commercial drivers: K.S.A. 8-2,137 implied consent for commercial motor vehicle drivers (blood/breath/urine for alcohol/drugs on probable cause). Federal DOT drug testing for CDL holders is separate federal law — not a Kansas rule.

12.4 Expungement and record clearing for marijuana offences

  • Statute: K.S.A. 21-6614 (expungement of certain convictions, arrest records and diversion agreements; procedure; restoration of firearm rights — title as amended, including by HB 2393, 2026, ch. 35). Petition-based only — Kansas has no automatic marijuana expungement / “clean slate” for drug convictions found. Do not describe Kansas as having automatic clearance.

12.6 Gun ownership conflicts

  • Federal prohibition (the operative conflict): 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) makes it unlawful for a person “who is an unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance” to possess/receive firearms or ammunition shipped in interstate/foreign commerce. ATF Form 4473 warns: “The use or possession of marijuana remains unlawful under Federal law regardless of whether it has been legalized or decriminalized for medicinal or recreational purposes in the state where you reside” (warning text as quoted in KBI testimony, 2 Mar 2023). Answering the 4473 unlawful-user question dishonestly is itself a federal felony; answering honestly as a marijuana user bars the purchase. KBI testimony states NICS treats medical-marijuana programme participation as a disqualifier and that Kansas NICS indices are managed by KBI.
  • No Kansas medical card exists, so the classic “card = prohibited person” scenario does not arise in Kansas — but actual unlawful marijuana use in Kansas (unlawful under both Kansas and federal law) squarely triggers §922(g)(3). Lawful hemp/CBD use is not use of a controlled substance (hemp is excluded from the federal CSA definition of marihuana) — but a hemp product that in fact exceeds the THC limit, or a positive drug test attributed to hemp, creates a fact dispute; no Kansas guidance resolving hemp-use vs §922(g)(3) was found — Verify with ATF/KBI before advising a hemp consumer on firearms.
  • Kansas Second Amendment Protection Act (2013) was held unconstitutional by a U.S. District Court (Feb 2015, per a law-firm summary) and does not protect marijuana users — Verify case citation before citing.
  • Expungement and firearms: 21-6614’s title includes “restoration of person’s right to keep and bear arms” — an expungement order may restore Kansas rights, but federal §922(g) disabilities are governed by federal law and are not automatically removed by a Kansas expungement — Verify.
  • Pending change (not law): a May 2026 ATF draft Form 4473 revision would remove the explicit medical-marijuana warning while keeping the unlawful-user question, with comments through 7 July 2026 (draft marked “Do Not Use”) — do not advise that the warning has been removed. A separate secondary claim (Jan 2026) that ATF narrowed “unlawful user” to require “regular, ongoing use” was not verified against the Federal Register — Verify before relying on any “single use is safe” reading.

12.7 Pet and veterinary products

  • No FDA-approved CBD (or other cannabis) product for animals exists. FDA’s position (via secondary summaries): CBD added to animal food is an unapproved food additive / unapproved new animal drug; the only FDA-approved cannabis-derived drug is Epidiolex (human epilepsy only). FDA CVM issued a January 2025 Request for Information on low-THC hemp products in animals — information-gathering, not approval (Verify on fda.gov). K-State veterinary summary: “At this time, the US Food and Drug Administration has not approved industrial hemp to be fed to livestock or pets.”
  • AAFCO: the single hemp-derived feed ingredient definition in the AAFCO Official Publication is hemp seed meal (mechanically extracted) for laying hens only, with limits (≤20 ppm CBD, ≤2 ppm THC per a 2026 testimony summarising the 2025 OP, T71.5) — Verify in the current AAFCO OP; do not extend it to dogs, cats or horses.
  • Kansas law: K.S.A. 2-3908(a)(1)(F) bans hemp products for human or animal consumption containing an ingredient prohibited under the Kansas Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act (K.S.A. 65-636 et seq.) or the Commercial Feeding Stuffs Act (K.S.A. 2-1001 et seq.), while stating that subparagraph “shall not otherwise prohibit the use of any such ingredient, including cannabidiol oil, in such hemp products” — i.e. the Kansas ban turns on whether the ingredient is prohibited under those Acts, not on a freestanding Kansas pet-CBD ban (Verify current text on kslegislature.gov). No Kansas pet-CBD registration, labelling or age rule was found.
  • Proposed (not law): HB 2168 (2023, as introduced) would have allowed hemp fibre, grain and seeds as food for livestock, poultry and pets; KDA testified in opposition and the introduced feed provision did not become law in that form. Do not present hemp pet/livestock feed as lawful in Kansas.
  • Veterinary practice: no Kansas veterinary-board guidance on recommending/prescribing CBD was verified — Verify with the Kansas Board of Veterinary Examiners. K-State’s cattle research (Part 11.9) is safety research, not a feeding approval.

12.8 CBD use in schools

  • No Kansas statewide statute, regulation or KSDE policy on CBD (or hemp-product) use by pupils in schools was found. Kansas has no “medicinal cannabis in schools” statute because it has no medical cannabis programme; Claire and Lola’s Law creates a possession defence (and a child-removal bar) but does not create a school-administration right, a school-nurse duty, or a pupil’s right to use CBD on school property — Verify before advising any school or family.
  • Excluded false hit: a school-boards policy document found in search (“Medicinal Cannabis… registered qualified patient… KRS 218B.045”) is Kentucky law (KRS = Kentucky Revised Statutes) and a Kentucky model policy. It is not Kansas law and must not be cited for Kansas schools.
  • What does apply: marijuana possession on school property remains a criminal offence (21-5706; school-zone enhancements, if any, Verify — none was verified); hemp products banned under 2-3908 (vape liquids, cigarettes, teas, etc.) are unlawful to market/sell/distribute in Kansas generally, including to/around pupils, while lawful hemp products are not banned by 2-3908 as such; research use by a state educational institution is expressly preserved (2-3908(d)(1)) — institutional research, not pupil use. Individual districts set student conduct / medication policies locally (including self-administration rules under K.S.A. 72-6283 et seq. for prescribed medication — whether CBD oil counts is Verify, as CBD oil is not a Kansas-prescribed drug). Follow-up: KSDE + large districts’ (Wichita USD 259, Shawnee Mission, Lawrence USD 497) student handbooks / medication policies — not retrieved.
