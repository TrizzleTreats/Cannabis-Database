Part 1 — Audit (Last audited: 24 October 2026)

Method: every factual claim was checked against Pennsylvania primary sources only — the General Assembly's statute and bill pages (legis.state.pa.us / palegis.us, including unconsolidated acts), the Pennsylvania Code and Pennsylvania Bulletin, the Department of Health Medical Marijuana Program, the Department of Revenue, the Department of Agriculture, the enacted budget/fiscal code material, official county and municipal codes, and Pennsylvania open-data portals. News, law-firm blogs, trade press and AI summaries were never used to confirm a claim; where only a secondary source exists the verdict is Unverified. The Federal Baseline in the brief was applied as given and was not re-audited; the report's federal box matches it on all six points. “LIVE” in the working audit notes means the auditor opened the page/PDF text; “snippet” means a search extract from a primary document, not a full open — snippet-supported items are marked accordingly in the verdict where it matters. Verdicts: Confirmed = primary source supports it as written; Corrected = wrong, incomplete or mislabelled, corrected wording given and applied in Part 2; Removed = wrong with no correct version found, deleted in Part 2; Unverified = no primary source confirmed it in this audit, flag retained in Part 2.

1.8 Employment and drug testing (§2103)

  • §2103(a): listed persons (patients, caregivers, practitioners, MMOs, etc.) shall not be subject to arrest/prosecution/penalty or denied any right/privilege solely for lawful programme activity.

  • §2103(b)(2): an employer need not accommodate use on its premises, and may discipline an employee for being under the influence in the workplace where conduct falls below the standard of care normally accepted for that position. §2103(b)(3): nothing requires an employer to violate federal law.
  • §2103(c): certification alone, or acting in accordance with the Act, shall not by itself be considered in custody determinations (§2103(c)).
  • §510 workplace restrictions (Act 16 text, confirmed in audit): §510(1) bars operating chemicals requiring a federal/state permit or high-voltage electricity/public utilities while under the influence with more than 10 ng of active THC per ml of blood in serum — the 10 ng threshold applies ONLY to §510(1); §510(2) bars duties at heights or in confined spaces, including mining, while under the influence with NO threshold stated; §510(3)–(4) allow an employer to prohibit life-threatening or public-health/safety-risk tasks while under the influence without that counting as an adverse employment decision even if it causes financial harm.

2.4 IRC §280E and PA decoupling — explained under the split baseline

Pennsylvania decouples in two different taxes, and the federal split changes what each is worth:

  1. PA Personal Income Tax (PIT): Revenue states: “Pennsylvania Personal Income Tax law does not contain a similar provision to IRC §280E. Consequently, Pennsylvania Personal Income Tax law allows ordinary, necessary, and reasonable business expenses incurred by businesses dealing in controlled substances.” (Answer 3903; Form REV-1841 via myPATH.) Sole proprietors/partnership pass-throughs were therefore never §280E-blocked at state level.
  2. PA Corporate Net Income Tax (CNIT): CNIT historically started from federal taxable income, importing §280E. Act 56 of 2024 (SB 654, signed 11 July 2024, P.L.674) added Tax Reform Code §401(3)1(b.2), an additional deduction — but as enacted it covered only G/P permit holders. Act 123 of 2024 (SB 1051, Act of 29 Oct 2024, P.L.1086) corrected it; verbatim operative effect: the additional deduction equals ordinary and necessary expenses that would be deductible under IRC §162 if no federal deduction was taken, and “medical cannabis business” means an MMO under Act 16 §103 holding an active grower/processor permit OR dispensary permit. Effective for taxable years beginning after 31 Dec 2023 (secondary for the Act 56 effective-date detail — Verify). Official Act 123 PDF: palegis.us — Accessed 2026-10-24 (index).

Meaning under the split baseline: PA's decoupling was enacted while state-licensed medical marijuana was still Schedule I federally, so it was the only expense relief available. Since 28 April 2026, state-licensed medical marijuana is Schedule III federally, and under the Baseline Treasury position §280E generally no longer bars those businesses federally — so the PA CNIT deduction now functions as a backstop: by its own terms it applies only “if no federal deduction was taken.” Because PA has no enacted adult-use programme, there are no state-licensed adult-use businesses to which the adult-use §280E exposure would attach; the split therefore does not create a two-tier PA tax problem on that basis. (This is not a statement that no Schedule I activity exists in Pennsylvania.) Hemp businesses (≤0.3% THC) were never §280E businesses.

Note: one secondary source attributed the corporate 280E fix to “SB 1501 (29 Oct 2024).” The official Act is Act 123 of 2024 (SB 1051), read as an official PDF. The SB 1501 claim is rejected: the official Act 123 of 2024 PDF is SB 1051, not SB 1501 (Research Note 26, resolved).

Part 3 — Local Rules

3.1 County vs municipal — who sets rules

  • The programme is state-run (DOH permits; PDA hemp permits). Act 16 gives counties no permitting or rule-making role. Counties play no cannabis permitting role in the Act text read.
  • Municipalities — cities, boroughs, townships, and PA's one incorporated town, Bloomsburg (Columbia County) — act only through zoning and land use under §2107, plus general police-power ordinances such as the decriminalisation citation schemes in §1.2D (which operate as enforcement policy and expressly do not supersede state law).

Source: Act 16 §§2107, 802, 601–616 (index).

3.2 Ban / opt-out / moratorium / local caps

  • No opt-in/opt-out mechanism exists in Act 16. Act 16 contains no opt-in/opt-out or ban provision; §2107 does not expressly state that a municipality may not ban or exclude the use, and that question is not settled by §2107 text alone — do not present a ban prohibition as settled law; §2107 instead requires that they be treated under the same zoning/land-use requirements as comparable uses in the same district (manufacturing for G/P; commercial for dispensary). A municipality therefore regulates where in a district and under what generally applicable conditions, not whether the use exists in the Commonwealth.
  • Local caps/moratoriums/fees: No official municipal cannabis-specific cap, moratorium or fee schedule was opened in this run — Unavailable (Research Note 22). DOH permit applications require evidence of compliance with municipal zoning requirements and a diversity plan (Biennial Report 2024), which is the practical point of municipal control.

10.2 Interstate commerce, compacts, reciprocity

Act 16 §2108 — verbatim (consolidated Act text — index, official):

“Section 2108. Notice. Upon amendment of the Controlled Substances Act (Public Law 91-513, 84 Stat. 1236) removing marijuana from Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, the department shall publish notice of the effective date of the amendment in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.”

  • Operative analysis (under the Federal Baseline — not a change to the baseline): The split rule (91 FR 22714, effective 28 Apr 2026) moves FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III but leaves all adult-use and all other marijuana in Schedule I. It therefore does not “remov[e] marijuana from Schedule I” as §2108 is written. §2108 is not triggered by the split alone, and no §2108 Pennsylvania Bulletin notice was found in Run 2. §2109(a) was deleted by Act 44 of 2021 (P.L.210, No.44, 30 June 2021) — confirmed in audit; §2109(b) (permit issuance upon PA Bulletin notice) remains. The Verify flag on this point is removed.
  • No interstate compact, interstate transfer, or import/export agreement for medical marijuana was found. Transport rules affirmatively prohibit it: §1151a.35 / §1161a.35 (see Part 11.4) — “may not transport … to any location outside of this Commonwealth.” Bringing adult-use marijuana purchased in NJ/NY/OH/MD into PA remains unlawful under PA law (secondary summaries agree; no reciprocity statute found).
  • Medical reciprocity: §2106 is marked “(Expired)” in the consolidated Act 16 text. The “730 days” pinpoint was not re-checked in audit — drop it unless the original §2106 text is re-read.   • Hemp interstate shipment: Federal rule 7 CFR §990.63 (states may not prohibit interstate transport of hemp lawfully produced under a state/tribal/USDA plan) is cited in secondary sources; PA plan summaries state: bulk hemp taken out-of-state must meet destination-state programme rules with documentation of destination compliance before purchase in PA (Art. “(1) An approved laboratory shall test samples from a harvest batch or harvest lot prior to using the harvest batch or harvest lot to produce a medical marijuana product. 11.5 Waste disposal

§1151a.40 (Cornell mirror — index, official text):

12.2 THC driving limits and impairment testing; medical-patient protections

  • No per-se THC nanogram limit in §3802 was found. The operative provisions are impairment-based (§3802(d)(2)–(3)) and per-se presence-based (§3802(d)(1)(i) — any amount of a Schedule I substance or metabolite in blood). Secondary law-firm summaries agree there is no numeric THC threshold for non-commercial drivers. Sharpened in audit: §3802(d)(1)(ii) covers Schedule II/III substances “not medically prescribed” — do not imply the federal split automatically gives patients a (d)(1)(ii) defence, because (d)(1) turns on the PA Controlled Substance Act schedules (§780-104) and medical marijuana is not a federal prescription.
  • Medical patients are NOT exempt. Commonwealth v. Stone (Pa. Superior Court, 2022, as summarised in secondary sources): medical marijuana remains Schedule I under PA law for DUI purposes; the Commonwealth need not prove THC came from non-medical marijuana under §3802(d)(1)(i). Act §2103 protections do not create a DUI defence (see also 75 Pa.C.S. §3810 (authorised use not a defence) — correction: §3804 was the wrong section number; Verify current §3810 text).
  • SB 363 CORRECTION (important): Secondary articles describe SB 363 as having “shifted” PA to impairment-only for patients. That is wrong as a statement of current law. Official General Assembly history (legis.state.pa.us bill history — index, official): SB 363 (2023–24), “Removal of DUI Penalties for Legal Medicinal Cannabis Use,” last action “Laid on the table (Pursuant to Senate Rule 9), April 10, 2024” — never enacted. Its amendment text (A03266) would have required proof of actual impairment for compliant patients and barred card-alone probable cause / chemical-test grounds, with a commercial/school-vehicle federal carve-out — proposed text only. Successor SB 63 (2025–26) was referred to Transportation 22 Jan 2025 (secondary summary) — not enacted in sources retrieved; Verify current status.
  • Federal-split interaction — Verify, do not assume: Under the Baseline, state-licensed medical marijuana is federally Schedule III, but §3802(d)(1)(i) turns on PA's Schedule I listing (§780-104) and on “unlawful” use concepts, not directly on the federal schedule. No PA amendment to §780-104 or §3802 in response to the split was found in Run 2. Until PA amends its own schedule/statute, the per-se exposure described in Stone should be assumed to persist — flagged as legal analysis requiring counsel confirmation, not as a verified post-split holding.
  • Impairment testing: No validated roadside THC impairment device / PA impairment-testing standard was found. Evidence in impairment cases is driving behaviour, officer observation, and field sobriety performance (secondary summaries). Blood THC/metabolites can persist days–weeks (secondary) — the reason per-se presence is so consequential for patients.

12.3 Expungement, sealing, pardons

  • Clean Slate — 18 Pa.C.S. §9122.2 (Act 56 of 2018, as amended by Act 83 of 2020 and Act 36 of 2023, P.L.334, No.36, 14 Dec 2023): Automated limited access (sealing, not destruction) for eligible records. Act 36 changes as summarised in Governor's-office / Senator Baker materials (secondary): third expansion — certain non-violent drug felonies automatically sealed after 10 years without another conviction; summary-offence waiting period reduced (reported as 10 → 5 years in one summary, and “7 years for many misdemeanours” in another — waiting-period table conflicts across secondary sources; Verify in §9122.2(a) full text before quoting a period for a specific offence); petition-based limited access under §9122.1 (7 years conviction-free for offences punishable by ≥1 year, per 2024 petition form summary — Verify).
  • Expungement vs sealing: Expungement (destruction, as if never occurred) of a conviction is generally available only after a pardon (see below) or for non-conviction dispositions; Clean Slate is sealing from public/non-criminal-justice view, not expungement (Senator Baker handout — secondary official-office material).
  • Pardons + automatic expungement (Act 36): 18 Pa.C.S. §9122(a.1) (as summarised in a 2024 petition guide citing Act 36): the Board of Pardons transmits eligible pardoned-conviction records quarterly to the Administrative Office of Pennsylvania Courts → court of common pleas → court orders expungement on confirmation of criteria. Previously a pardoned person had to petition separately.
  • Marijuana-specific pardon stream: Governor Wolf / Board of Pardons (Lt. Gov. Fetterman, chair) expedited process for marijuana convictions (misdemeanour possession/paraphernalia, and some felony possession in secondary summaries), with the $63 Board fee waived at launch (secondary summary of the 2019 programme, updated Nov 2025 — stale on fee). Current Board of Pardons marijuana-pardon fee, timeline and eligibility — Verify on the Board's current site (not retrieved). No cannabis-specific automatic expungement statute (without pardon) was found — relief runs through Clean Slate sealing + pardon/expungement.
  • §780-113 expungement eligibility: §780-119 (Probation Without Verdict — drug-dependent, non-violent, first-offence-type eligibility, record “promptly expunged” on completion) and general §9122 expungement for non-convictions/ARD/summary etc. are cited in secondary legal summaries. A §780-113-specific eligibility table was NOT established from official text in Run 2 — Verify in 18 Pa.C.S. §§9122–9122.2 and 35 P.S. §780-119 before advising.
  • Proposed, not law: HB 1180 (2021, Delloso — legalisation + marijuana expungement process) and HB 985/986 (2023, Rabb — tenant protections, see §12.5) did not become law in sources retrieved.

12.6 Gun ownership conflicts

  • Federal prohibition (Baseline + PSP guidance): 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) bars shipment/transport/possession/receipt of firearms/ammunition by an “unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance.” PSP Firearms Division guidance (as quoted in multiple secondary sources from psp.pa.gov — PSP page itself not opened in Run 2, Verify):

“According to the U.S. DOJ, possession of a valid Medical Marijuana Card and/or the use of medical marijuana makes you an ‘unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance’ who is prohibited by federal law from the purchase or acquisition, possession, or control of a firearm, according to 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) and 27 C.F.R. §478.32(a)(3). … the mere possession of a Medical Marijuana Card will give rise to an inference that you are an ‘unlawful user of or addicted to’ a controlled substance, according to 27 C.F.R. §478.11.”

  • ATF Open Letter 21 Sept 2011 to FFLs: any person who uses marijuana, regardless of state medicinal authorisation, is an unlawful user prohibited from possessing firearms/ammunition.
  • PA carry licence: 18 Pa.C.S. §6109(e)(1)(xiv) — a person prohibited from possessing/acquiring a firearm under U.S. statutes may not obtain/possess/renew a Pennsylvania License to Carry Firearm (LTC) (secondary summaries consistent).
  • Split-baseline interaction — Verify, do not assume a change: §922(g)(3) is not limited to Schedule I/II; it turns on “unlawful user” status. State-licensed medical use is still not a federally lawful prescription use (no FDA approval for dispensary marijuana). A secondary source reports an ATF Jan 2026 interim final rule narrowing “unlawful user” to regular use over an extended period continuing into the present, with no carve-out for state-legal marijuana — secondary only — Verify in the Federal Register rule.
