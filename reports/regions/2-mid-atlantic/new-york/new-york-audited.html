How to read the flags

Facts were confirmed against official pages. Where a fact could not be confirmed from an official source, it is flagged rather than filled in:

  • Verify — uncertain, conflicting, or not confirmed from official text; re-check before relying on it.
  • Unavailable — the information could not be retrieved or no official source was found.
  • Secondary only — reported only in news, trade press, or other non-official sources.

Federal baseline (used as given)

  • Scheduling is split. Under a final rule at 91 FR 22714 (effective 28 April 2026), FDA-approved cannabis products and state-licensed medical marijuana are in Schedule III. All other marijuana, including all adult-use, remains Schedule I. The broader 2024 rescheduling proposal is not final.
  • IRS §280E: Treasury has said rescheduling generally removes §280E as a bar for businesses no longer dealing in Schedule I/II substances (guidance forthcoming). Adult-use activity remains fully subject to §280E.
  • Hemp: Public Law 119-37 §781 rewrites the federal hemp definition to a total-THC standard (including THCA), with a 0.4 mg total THC per container cap for final hemp-derived cannabinoid products. It takes legal effect 12 November 2026, but Public Law 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026 to only the provisions excluding cannabinoids that are not naturally produced or are synthesized. The full definition applies after 11 December 2026.
  • USDA has delayed enforcement of the DEA-registered-laboratory hemp testing requirement until 31 December 2026.
  • The federal medical-marijuana appropriations rider is in force through 11 December 2026.
  • No federal cannabis banking law has been enacted.

Where a New York source conflicts with this baseline, this audited rewrite follows the baseline. The conflicting New York wording is corrected in place below and noted in the Audit.

Last audited: 24 October 2026. This audit was done by a separate fact-checking agent who did not write the original report. How conflicting figures were decided. Where the original listed several numbers for the same thing, one authoritative source was chosen and the others were removed from the main text (they appear only in this audit table). Adult-use sales and licence counts: OCM 2025 Annual Report, as of 30 November 2025. Cannabinoid-hemp counts: the same Annual Report, as of 30 November 2025. Personal details. The original report correctly did not reproduce personal phone numbers or emails for individual licence holders, and the rewrite keeps that rule. Local Rules

3.1 Which level of government sets rules

  • Only towns, cities and villages (“municipalities”) had opt-out and time/place/manner powers. Counties cannot opt out and cannot license cannabis businesses. The State (CCB/OCM) controls licensure.
    cannabis.ny.gov — Localities · Accessed 2026-10-24
  • Cannabis Law §131 (substance of the official text): only towns, cities and villages could opt out, by local law adopted on/before 31 Dec 2021 (or 9 months after the effective date, whichever later), subject to permissive referendum under Municipal Home Rule Law §24; a town opt-out law applies only outside any village in the town; no new opt-out law may be adopted after the deadline, but a repealing local law may be adopted after that date. §131(2) preempts all county/town/city/village bodies on the operation/licensure of ROs, adult-use and cannabinoid hemp businesses, except that towns/cities/villages may regulate the time, place and manner of retail dispensaries and on-site consumption sites if not “unreasonably impracticable” as determined by the CCB. §131(3) et seq. (2024 amendments) give counties/cities unlicensed-activity inspection and sealing powers.
    nysenate.gov — Cannabis Law §131 · Accessed 2026-10-24
  • Town/village interaction: because a town opt-out reaches only the area outside villages, a village inside an opted-out town could remain opted-in (and vice versa) — always check town and village status separately, and beware same-name town/village pairs.
  • NYC special case: New York City is a single municipality for opt-out purposes (it did not opt out); the five boroughs are not separate municipalities for §131. For Cannabis Law §76 municipal notification, notice in NYC goes to the community board for the district where the premises are located (elsewhere, to the municipal clerk), 30–270 days before filing a retail/ROD/on-site (or microbusiness-with-retail) application.
    cannabis.ny.gov — Localities · Accessed 2026-10-24

3.2 Opt-out: deadline, referendum, reversal, moratoria

Deadline: 31 Dec 2021; opt-outs were subject to permissive referendum; reversal is permitted at any time by a local law repealing the opt-out (for one or both activity types); a copy must be emailed to OCM; once repealed, a municipality cannot opt out again. Municipalities cannot opt out of other licence types, medical cannabis, or cannabinoid hemp, and cannot ban home cultivation (they may regulate it). Moratoria/local bans beyond §131 are preempted except via time/place/manner rules that are not “unreasonably impracticable”.

9.3 Ballot measures — New York has NO citizen initiative for cannabis

Finding: legislature-led only. No statewide cannabis ballot referendum, citizen initiative, or popular referendum has ever placed cannabis legalisation on a NY ballot, because NY has no statewide citizen-initiative process (the State Constitution provides no citizen-initiated statute/referendum at state level; local permissive referenda under Cannabis Law §131 for opt-outs are the only cannabis-related referendum mechanism, and they are local, not statewide). MRTA passed as S.854/A.1248 through the Legislature and was signed 31 Mar 2021. No contrary official record was found. This is a negative finding from the absence of any such measure in official sources searched; if a constitutional citation is needed, add NY Const. Art.   • No NY interstate-commerce agreement (compact with another state, e.g. on the California/Oregon/Washington model) was located. No dormant-commerce/export provision with a federal-legality trigger was located in Cannabis Law.

Conclusion: interstate cannabis commerce is not currently operative in NY; hemp moved interstate as a federally legal commodity under the pre-11 December 2026 definition; after 11 December 2026 the full PL 119-37 total-THC definition and 0.4 mg per container cap apply, subject to Part 114 §114.14(f) proof-of-origin + CoA rules for extract entering NY (Part 114 §114.14(f) was not re-opened in this audit).

  • Statute (secondary summary of Cannabis Law §127, consistent across sources): “no landlord may refuse to lease to, or otherwise penalize, an individual solely for conduct authorized under the chapter” / solely for lawful cannabis conduct, and custody/visitation may not be denied solely for permitted conduct (§127 also covers family court; Cannabis Law §127(5): denial of custody/visitation/parenting time solely for permitted conduct is barred unless best interest + impairment/imminent danger is shown by a fair preponderance, and not based solely on whether/when/how often a person uses cannabis). §127 text not opened verbatim in Run 2 — Verify.
  • Smoking vs possession vs growing — CONFLICT FLAGGED: OCM landlord guidance as summarised in one secondary source: landlord CAN ban smoking/vaping/growing on premises, but that does NOT reach non-combustible forms, and refusal/penalty is allowed only where the landlord would risk losing a federal benefit; registered medical patients have a stronger right to consume at home (incl. smoking/vaping flower) unless federal-benefit loss would result. A second secondary source states landlords CAN ban growing outright and tenants have no right to grow despite state legality, with lease bans enforceable as lease violations. A third (tenant-advice) source states landlords CANNOT bar legal growing/possession or evict for it, except public/federally-subsidised housing. These cannot all be correct in full; the OCM-FAQ-based formulation (ban smoking/vaping/growing as a premises rule, but not penalise lawful conduct / refuse tenancy solely for it, federal-benefit exception) is the most sourced, but the growing question is Verify against OCM's landlord FAQ + §127 text before advising a tenant or landlord. Practical points all sources agree on: no-smoking lease clauses apply to cannabis smoke; plants must not be plainly visible to public view and must be secure from under-21 access (home-grow rules, Run 1); no duty to disclose growing to a landlord is stated in tenant-advice sources (not a statute citation).
  • Public housing: Federally funded public housing (NYCHA, Section 8) follows federal law — cannabis use/possession/growing may be prohibited and may lead to eviction/loss of benefit, notwithstanding state legality. All secondary sources agree; NYCHA-specific policy document not opened Verify if a NYCHA citation is needed.

12.6 Gun ownership conflicts — state vs federal §922(g)(3)

The ban covers possession, not just purchase, and private sales do not avoid it. State medical registries are NOT automatically shared with NICS (secondary).
