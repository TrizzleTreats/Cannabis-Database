Federal baseline (given, not independently researched): Under 91 FR 22714 (effective 28 April 2026), FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana are Schedule III; all other marijuana including adult-use remains Schedule I. Treasury has said rescheduling generally removes IRC §280E for businesses no longer dealing in Schedule I/II substances (guidance forthcoming); adult-use remains fully subject to §280E. P.L. 119-37 §781 rewrites the federal hemp definition to a total-THC standard (incl. THCA) with a 0.4 mg/container cap — legal effect 12 Nov 2026, but P.L. 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 Dec 2026 to only the provisions excluding non-naturally-produced or synthesized cannabinoids; the full definition applies after 11 Dec 2026. USDA delayed enforcement of the DEA-registered-lab hemp testing requirement until 31 Dec 2026. The federal medical-marijuana appropriations rider is in force through 11 Dec 2026. No federal cannabis banking law has been enacted. Where a NJ source conflicts, it is flagged VERIFY and the baseline is unchanged.

Summary in brief: Adult-use is legal for 21+ under the CREAMM Act (P.L.2021, c.16; C.24:6I-31 et seq.). Medical patients pay 0% sales tax and are SEEF-exempt; adult-use pays 6.625% sales tax + SEEF + optional municipal transfer tax up to 2%. Hemp was rewritten by P.L.2025, c.215 (signed 12 Jan 2026, repealing P.L.2024, c.73), P.L.2026, c.7 (30 Mar 2026) and P.L.2026, c.13 (29 May 2026) — from 13 Apr 2026 products >0.3% total THC or >0.4 mg/container are cannabis; intoxicating hemp beverages survive only until 13 Nov 2026 in c.13 container forms. P.L.2025, c.325 renamed the Workplace Impairment Recognition Expert to Evaluator and created State Police unlicensed-business enforcement.

Current law — c.215 as revised by c.7 & c.13

Authoritative summary: CRC interim hemp FAQ

  • From 13 Apr 2026, NOT “hemp” (legally cannabis/marijuana): (1) viable seeds from a plant >0.3% total THC dry weight (total THC incl. delta-8, delta-10, THCA & comparable cannabinoids); (2) cannabinoids not capable of being naturally produced; (3) naturally-capable cannabinoids chemically synthesized/manufactured; (4) products > 0.4 mg total THC per container.
  • Immediately prohibited (from 13 Jan 2026): selling product marketed as hemp exceeding the federal THC level; online sale of any hemp-derived product or intoxicating hemp beverage; vending-machine sale of hemp beverages (≥$100/container civil penalty, C.2C:35-10f); any detectable-THC product to under-21s; chemically synthesized cannabinoids.
  • From 13 Apr 2026: products >0.3% total THC or >0.4 mg/container are cannabis — production/sale without a CRC licence is unlawful; hemp cultivators directed to Class 1/2. A “hemp-derived cannabinoid product” (<0.3% and <0.4 mg/container) may be sold by Class 5 Retailers if tested/packaged/labelled per CRC rules.
  • Beverages: produced before 12 Nov 2026 from hemp, may be sold until 13 Nov 2026 by ABC plenary wholesale / plenary retail distribution licensees, plenary retail consumption licensees (added by c.13), or Class 5 Retailers. From 31 May 2026 only in c.13 container forms (C.2C:35-10g): (a) can ≤10 mg total THC; or (b) resealable 750 mL bottle, 40 servings × 5 mg, ≤200 mg/bottle; each sale with a COA from an ISO 17025-accredited, DEA-registered lab per CRC procedures (90–110% compliant); non-conforming sale ≥$100/container. Title 33 sellers must segregate display, post 21+ notice, monitor, verify age/understanding (C.24:6I-48.5); ABC may enforce. $3.75/gallon wholesale excise (c.215 §13). The FAQ’s earlier 5 mg/serving + 10 mg/container formulation reflects c.215/c.7 and is superseded by c.13 — see Gaps.
  • From 14 Nov 2026: any hemp beverage >0.4 mg/container is cannabis — Class 2 Manufacturer production, Class 5 Retailer sale only.
  • Intermediate extracts: >0.3% extract may be possessed/transported only by licensed hemp producers / NJDA-authorised agents, only until 13 Nov 2026 (as extended by c.13; the c.7 window had ended 31 May 2026), if undergoing processing, not for direct use/consumption, handled per CRC safety/security rules, final product meets c.215 levels (C.2C:35-10f).
  • Penalties: C.2C:35-10f violations carry marijuana criminal penalties under N.J.S.2C:35-5 plus civil ≥$100 (1st), $1,000 (2nd), $10,000 (3rd+), each day separate. c.215 §14 (C.2C:35-10h) preserves lawful hemp transport & NJDA-authorised activity; §15 (C.2C:35-10j) directs AG law-enforcement guidelines.

NJ response to the federal definition: CRC FAQ: c.215 “revises New Jersey law in light of updated federal hemp definitions.” NJ adopted the same architecture (total-THC incl. THCA, 0.4 mg/container cap, synthesized/non-natural exclusion) but on NJ’s own earlier dates (13 Apr 2026 reclassification; 13–14 Nov 2026 beverage transition) vs the federal timeline (limited effect 12 Nov 2026; full definition after 11 Dec 2026). Do not conflate them — NJ’s stricter schedule governs in NJ. NJ’s DEA-registered ISO 17025 lab COA rule operates alongside the federal note that USDA delayed enforcement of the federal DEA-lab requirement until 31 Dec 2026.

3.2 Opt-in / opt-out

“The failure of a municipality to enact an ordinance prohibiting the operation of one or more classes … within 180 days after the effective date of P.L.2021, c.16 … shall result in [that class] … being permitted to operate therein as follows: [Classes 1–4 & 6] shall be permitted uses in all industrial zones …; and [Class 5 retail] shall be a conditional use in all commercial zones or retail zones…” — 24:6I-45(b)
“At the end of a five-year period following the initial failure … and every five-year period thereafter …, the municipality shall again be permitted to prohibit the future operation of any one or more classes … during a new 180-day period, but this ordinance shall be prospective only and not apply to any [business] operating … prior to [its] enactment.” — 24:6I-45(b)
  • Original window: 22 Feb 2021 → 21 Aug 2021. An opted-out municipality may opt back in at any time by new ordinance; the five-year windows (next window opens August 2026) let default-opt-in municipalities prohibit prospectively only, for a new 180-day period.
  • A municipality may prohibit classes, cap numbers, regulate location/manner/times (24:6I-45(a)); it may not prohibit delivery into the municipality; delivery hours are CRC-only.
  • New in 2026 (c.13 §3, new 24:6I-45(d)): for a Jake Honig dispensary co-locating Class 5 on existing premises where medical sales are permitted, “the Commission shall not require municipal review, consent, or approval” for the Class 5 licence; prior medical approval is deemed to authorise the retailer; a municipality may not prohibit retail by a medical dispensary open/lawful before 1 July 2023 without violation for ≥3 years before c.13.
  • Municipalities may impose separate local licensing/endorsement requirements and must notify CRC of approval/denial for each forwarded application (24:6I-45(c)(2)); CRC must send each application copy within 14 days (24:6I-45(c)(1)).

7.4 CRC / ABC hemp-derived product licences after c.215 / c.7 / c.13 — no new standalone “hemp licence”

There is no separate CRC hemp licence class. After P.L.2025, c.215 (signed 12 Jan 2026), P.L.2026, c.7 (30 Mar 2026) and P.L.2026, c.13 (29 May 2026):

  • Products that cease to be “hemp” become “cannabis” and fall under the existing CRC licensing regime: production by licensed Class 1 Cultivator / Class 2 Manufacturer and sale only through licensed Class 5 Retailer (secondary synthesis consistent with statute [Non-official]; statute text in P.L.2026, c.13 (PDF), amending C.2C:35-10f). From 13 Apr 2026, products >0.3% total THC or >0.4 mg total THC/container are cannabis (Run 1 baseline, unchanged).

10.3 Interstate commerce — cannabis: no enacted NJ law, no compact, not operative

  • No enacted NJ interstate cannabis commerce law was found. The only NJ proposal located is Senate President Scutari’s bill S3151, introduced 13 January 2026 (Senate Judiciary): it would empower the Governor to enter agreements with other states authorising medicinal and/or personal-use commercial cannabis activity between licensed entities, but only if federal law changes to explicitly allow interstate commerce, DOJ issues a tolerance memo, Congress blocks enforcement, or the NJ Attorney General opines implementation “will not result in significant legal risk” — and imported products would need rules equivalent to NJ’s on testing, packaging, labelling, marketing, tracking and quality assurance. The same reporting notes Scutari introduced similar bills in previous sessions and none was enacted. Marijuana Moment [Non-official] · Cultivated [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24.
  • That bill is NOT law. Do not present it as NJ interstate-commerce law, a compact, or a trigger law. No NJ interstate cannabis compact with any other state was found.
  • Operative analysis under the federal baseline as given: even if such a bill were enacted, its federal-change triggers are not met under the baseline: adult-use remains Schedule I (so interstate adult-use commerce remains federally prohibited); medical is Schedule III only for FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical marijuana, which does not by itself authorise interstate commerce, and no federal descheduling/legalisation, DOJ tolerance memo, or AG opinion satisfying the bill’s conditions was found in Run 2. Therefore no interstate cannabis commerce is currently operative in NJ. Transport within NJ is only between NJ-licensed premises (N.J.A.C. 17:30-9.15, §11.4). Taking NJ cannabis across a state line remains a federal offence and is not authorised by NJ law — visitors may purchase in NJ but may not lawfully transport it out of state under federal law (§12.5).
  • Case note (not a compact): NJ Appellate Division held CREAMM is not preempted by the federal CSA because it does not require any business to sell marijuana or any municipality to allow dispensaries, while warning residents/businesses “act at the risk that their activities might be prosecuted by federal authorities” — secondary reporting, Ganjapreneur [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24. This is about intrastate coexistence, not interstate commerce.

10.4 Interstate hemp transport

  • NJDA rule: N.J.A.C. 2:25-1.1(b) expressly excludes “transporters of hemp engaged in interstate commerce” from the NJDA chapter, and 2:25-2.2(v)/3.1(g) require a travel manifest and proof of authorisation (§7.2). Hemp (≤0.3% THC, federally defined) is an agricultural commodity that may move interstate under federal law, subject to the destination state’s rules and to the federal hemp definition changes in the baseline (full total-THC definition after 11 Dec 2026 — see federal baseline note in the header).
  • P.L.2025, c.215 §14: Run 1 (§1.5) summarised c.215 §14 (C.2C:35-10h) as preserving lawful hemp transport and NJDA-authorised activity. Run 2 did not open a §14 text and did not verify a §14 transport provision — the c.13 amendment text opened in Run 2 deals with intermediate-product transport (grace to 13 Nov 2026, §7.4) and online/vending bans, not a general §14 interstate rule. Mark c.215 §14 as VERIFY — do not summarise it beyond the Run 1 account without fetching the c.215 chapter law PDF and locating §14 verbatim.
  • Intoxicating hemp / cannabis transport across state lines: products that are “cannabis” under NJ law (>0.4 mg/container etc.) may not be transported interstate as hemp; they are subject to NJ cannabis transport rules (licensed distributor / licensee transport only, within NJ) and federal prohibition.

11.1 Testing

Required tests (CRC Testing Guidance, Feb 2025, and N.J.A.C. 17:30-19): Every batch of usable cannabis and every lot of cannabis products must be tested by a CRC-licensed testing laboratory. Required panel as summarised in CRC guidance / secondary testing-lab summaries [Non-official summary] — CRC Testing Guidance (PDF) · NewJerseyStateCannabis.org [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24:

  • Cannabinoid potency/profile: THCA, THC, CBDA, CBD, CBGA, CBG, CBN, plus delta-8 THC, THCVA, THCV, CBDVA, CBDV, CBCA, CBC (guidance list)
  • Terpene profile: linalool, terpinolene, alpha-humulene, beta-pinene, ocimene, d-limonene, beta-myrcene, beta-caryophyllene, alpha-pinene
  • Microbial contamination; mycotoxins (incl. aflatoxins, ochratoxin A)
  • Pesticides, fungicides, plant growth regulators
  • Residual solvents and manufacturing residue
  • Heavy metals
  • Additives / cutting agents / artificial flavourings incl. Vitamin E acetate (VEA); inactive ingredients
  • Stability (see below); moisture content / water activity (COA guidance)

Interim standards history: CRC Resolution 2021-9 (1 June 2021) designated Maryland testing standards as interim standards until CRC standards were established; CRC amended the Total Yeast and Mold limit eff. 12 Jan 2024 to <100,000 CFU/g for Raw Plant Material and Raw Prerolls to match Maryland — State Auditor report, pub.njleg.gov (PDF) — Accessed 2026-10-24. That Auditor report (2026) also found CRC “failed to oversee its testing laboratories, leading to questionable test results,” lacked tools to extract/analyse/monitor Metrc test results, had no reasonableness procedures, and had no dedicated independent means to test randomised samples. That is an official Auditor finding, not a lab list — cite it as such.

Sampling / retention / stability:

  • Representative sample per batch/lot; first harvest of a new cultivar segregated until written report shows specifications met; batch/lot may then be released (N.J.A.C. 17:30-19.1(e), Cornell LII republication — Accessed 2026-10-24).
  • Commission quality-control sampling: Commission may collect samples, maintain chain-of-custody accounting, use PHEL or a licensed lab; sample testing “may include tests for, among other things, cannabinoid content, the presence of pests, mold and mycotoxins, mildew, heavy metals, and pesticides to ensure the accuracy of labeling” (17:30-19.1(d), same source).
  • Stability: retention sample at 6- and 12-month intervals; stability testing for potency/purity, microbial contamination, water activity; for vaporised formulations in electronic smoking devices, heavy metals testing also required (CRC Testing Laboratory Requirements notice, CRC guidance PDF above).
  • Lab report: under N.J.A.C. 17:30-19.6, the lab must provide a written report detailing all testing for each sample to the cannabis business, the consumer, and the Commission, including methods/instrumentation, dates, results, batch/lot number, product form, name/address/licence of the business (same guidance PDF).
  • Sampling rules detail (exact sample size / increments): VERIFY — the full 17:30-19.2–19.7 text was not read line-by-line in Run 2; the 17 Dec 2025 resolution states those sections were put into effect by the sufficient-labs certification (below), so the operative sampling text is 17:30-19.2–19.7 as in effect after that certification. Failed tests and recalls:

Transfers / sales and ownership changes:

Any listing claiming a licence can be sold outright without CRC approval is misleading — VERIFY every such claim against 17:30-9.3.

Ownership caps / limits:

  • A person/entity shall be a principal of only one licence applicant/holder (17:30-6.8(l), Justia source above).
  • Principals may exercise control indirectly through intermediaries/shell/holding companies — that does not change who is the principal (17:30-6.8(m)).
  • Employees/volunteers may work for more than one applicant/holder (17:30-6.8(n)).
  • Owners/principals/passive investors/employees/volunteers must be 21+, except an heir under 21 inheriting from a deceased owner/passive investor (17:30-6.8(o)).
  • Vertical integration: “Recreational cannabis businesses may integrate vertically… hold any combination of a cultivator, manufacturer, retailer, and delivery license simultaneously or hold a wholesale and distributor license simultaneously. All recreational license holders can have only one business in each class.” — CRC businesses page. No statewide cap on number of licences: “There is no established limit on the number of cannabis business licenses available statewide” (same page).
  • Microbusiness limits: ≤10 employees at one time; physical plant ≤2,500 sq ft; (cultivator) ≤1,000 mature plants/month, canopy ≤2,500 sq ft and ≤24 ft high; manufacturer ≤1,000 lbs dried cannabis/month (secondary microbusiness summary — VERIFY against 17:30-6.7 / 10.x); distributors exempt from the 1,000-plant limit (CRC page). At least 10% of licences per class for microbusinesses / 25% overall is a statutory goal cited in secondary summaries — VERIFY against CREAMM §36.

Renewal:

  • Annual licences renew annually.   • Renewal fees per 17:30-7.17 (fee schedule above). Failure to satisfy renewal requirements may result in fines, suspension, revocation or renewal denial (same FAD).

  • Conditional licence: 120 days to convert (Run 1 baseline; the conversion application is under N.J.A.C. 17:30-7.8).   • Microbusiness-to-standard conversion: under 17:30-7.15, CRC approves if application complete and includes renovation/relocation plan (where applicable), 180-day sales/cultivation/manufacturing recitation + explanation, amended annual application with site/business plans, labour peace agreement attestation, conversion fee per 17:30-7.17, and any other relevant information — Justia republication — Accessed 2026-10-24.

Relocation: Location Change fee $10,000 standard / $1,000 micro (fee schedule above), per facility/premises relocated.   • On expiry without renewal or on revocation, the holder must: (1) immediately discontinue production/sale; (2) weigh inventory and update inventory; (3) destroy/dispose of unused/surplus inventory by providing it to the Commission for destruction within 72 hours of licence expiring (text in 17:30-9.14(d) as reproduced in NJLM rule PDF, njlm.org [Non-official host] — Accessed 2026-10-24); (4) create/maintain written disposal record; and within 10 business days after destroying, notify Commission in writing of amount destroyed (form, weight, quantity). 11.5 Waste disposal

N.J.A.C. 17:30-9.14 (Cornell LII republication) and DEP Cannabis Waste Guidance (dep.nj.gov (PDF)) — Accessed 2026-10-24:

  • Render unusable cannabis/waste irrecoverable, unrecognisable, useless for diversion in a secured, locked area visible to a security camera by: (1) finely shredding/grinding, and (2) mixing with safe, non-toxic, biodegradable material (soil, cat litter, compostable material) so resulting mixture is at least 50% non-cannabis waste by volume.
  • Store mixed waste in secure, locked container/space until disposal; container may be outside if within view of a security camera.
  • Dispose by: (a) transport (per below) to a DEP-permitted solid-waste disposal facility, or (b) compost on-site in secure, locked container/space as approved by Commission, or at a facility owned by the business and operated per DEP statutes/rules if all materials compostable, or transport compost to a DEP-authorised off-site compost facility; or (c) incineration at a DEP-authorised incinerator (transport per below).
  • Transport of non-hazardous solid waste (unusable cannabis/waste/items) for destruction/disposal: either register as Self-Generator Solid Waste Transporter (N.J.A.C. 7:26-3.2(a) and 16.3) or contract a licensed/registered Commercial (A-901) Solid Waste Transporter (N.J.S.A. 13:1E-126 et seq.). Comply with N.J.A.C. 7:26-2, 2B, 3 and the county/district Solid Waste Management Plan.
  • Hazardous waste determination per 40 CFR 262.11(a)–(d); hazardous waste disposed per DEP rules (N.J.A.C. 7:26G-6.1). Electronic smoking devices: shall not be incinerated or rendered unrecoverable as a device — remove vaporised formulation; all-in-one device with non-removable battery = hazardous waste (D003) per 40 CFR 262; multi-part device: battery as universal waste for recycling (40 CFR 273), remaining cartridge as solid waste per N.J.A.C. 7:26.
  • Written record of destruction/disposal, weigh cannabis and update inventory before destruction/disposal, including information required at 17:30-9.7.
  • Expiry/revocation disposal: see §11.2 (72 hours to Commission, 10 business days’ notice).

12.2 THC driving limits and impairment testing — impairment-based, no per se THC limit (verified in substance)

  • Statute: N.J.S.A. 39:4-50 makes it unlawful to operate a motor vehicle “while under the influence of intoxicating liquor, narcotic, hallucinogenic or habit-producing drug” (secondary statute summaries [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24). The statute sets a per se BAC limit for alcohol (0.08%) but no per se nanogram THC limit.
  • Official-state confirmation (Jersey Drives, NJ Division of Highway Traffic Safety public information):
    “Unlike alcohol, there is no set legal limit for cannabis. Impairment is determined through officer observation and evaluation.” — jerseydrives.com/impaired-driving — Accessed 2026-10-24.
    And: “Cannabis may be legal for adults 21+, but driving while impaired is illegal in New Jersey… It can slow reaction time, reduce coordination, and impair judgment and attention.”
  • Proof required: Prosecution must prove actual impairment (substantial deterioration/diminution of mental faculties or physical coordination — AG guidance as summarised in secondary sources), via officer observations, field sobriety testing (designed for alcohol, contested for drugs), Drug Recognition Expert (DRE) evaluation, and toxicology. A positive THC test alone does not prove impairment at time of driving, because THC persists after impairment ends (secondary [Non-official]).
  • State v. Olenowski (NJ Supreme Court, Nov 2023) — DRE limits (secondary summary): DRE testimony admissible with limits: DRE may testify evaluation is consistent with drug use, but not that drugs caused impairment; State must make reasonable attempt to obtain blood/urine toxicology, otherwise DRE opinion must be excluded absent persuasive reason; sample must be lawfully obtained (consent/warrant/genuine exigency); defence may cross-examine and present own expert. Cite Olenowski as secondary summary — VERIFY the slip opinion before quoting holdings.
  • Proposed per se bill is NOT law: A2776 (2018) would have created a per se DWI at ≥2 ng/mL delta-9 THC within 2 hours of driving — A2776 text (PDF) — Accessed 2026-10-24. Its own statement confirms current law is a basic impairment standard. Do not state NJ has a 2 ng limit.
  • Medical patients: Medical status is not a defence to DWI if impaired (secondary, same sources). Cannabis in a vehicle: secondary guidance says sealed original container or trunk; open cannabis in cabin may be cited as open container — VERIFY against 39:4-51 / CREAMM transport provisions before publishing as law.
  • Penalties: First drug-DWI uses the BAC 0.10%+ tier for fine/IDRC/jail exposure in secondary summaries, with 7-month to 1-year licence forfeiture for drug DWI — VERIFY against current 39:4-50 text, as penalty tiers have been amended over time.

12.3 Expungement and sealing — CREAMM, automatic expungement, Clean Slate

  • Vacatur by operation of law (22 Feb 2021): AG Directive 2021-1 directed prosecutors to dismiss pending marijuana cases where conduct was legalised, and the Administrative Office of the Courts would vacate by operation of law any guilty verdict, plea, diversionary placement or other entry of guilt for conduct before 22 Feb 2021 (secondary summary of Directive [Non-official]; AG page §9.2). Remaining sentences/supervision/unpaid assessments solely for the marijuana offence were also vacated.
  • Automatic sealing / expungement (N.J.S.A. 2C:52-5.2, 2C:52-6.1; §5/§6 of P.L.2019, c.269 as amended by 2021 law): System to seal records from the public “by operation of law” for specified indictable and disorderly marijuana/hashish offences, to be developed within 3 months of effective date. Eligible (secondary Collateral Consequences Resource Center summary, ccresourcecenter.org [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24):

    • Interim petition route (§2C:52-5.1): court may seal immediately on completion of sentence on petition.
  • Scale reported: NJ Courts reported 362,000 marijuana convictions expunged since decriminalisation law effective 1 July 2021? — secondary summary says “On September 16, 2021, the New Jersey courts reported that 362,000 marijuana convictions had been expunged since the decriminalization law became effective on July 1” (same CCRC source; the “July 1” reference is to the 2019 Clean Slate / marijuana sealing effective date context and is confusing — VERIFY the Courts’ 16 Sep 2021 release for exact figure/date before publishing 362,000 as a current total).

  • Clean Slate (general, not cannabis-specific): P.L.2019, c.269 (S4154) — petition process for “clean slate” expungement for residents with no offence in 10 years and no most-serious crimes; State to implement an automated clean slate system via a task force studying technological/fiscal/practical issues; low-level marijuana convictions sealed on disposition; e-filing, no filing fees for petition — Governor release 18 Dec 2019, nj.gov/governor — Accessed 2026-10-24. Larger distribution, >6 oz possession, home-grow, and non-cannabis offences are not covered by the automatic cannabis provisions and fall under general expungement (2C:52-2 etc., 5-year / early 4-year compelling-circumstances routes in secondary summaries — VERIFY).

  • Core position (secondary, but consistent across sources): Landlords may prohibit cannabis smoking/use on their property as a lease condition, including for medical patients as to smoking, and may enforce via lease-violation / eviction process.   • Medical anti-discrimination vs smoke-free policies — nuanced, VERIFY before giving legal advice: Jake Honig provides that qualifying patients/designated caregivers may not be discriminated against in renting/leasing real property solely on the basis of registry-cardholder status or authorised medical conduct (MPP summary of Jake Honig, mpp.org [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24). That is the landlord rule for commercial cannabis tenancies.

12.7 Gun ownership conflicts — federal 922(g)(3) vs NJ

  • Federal prohibition (statute): 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) prohibits a person who is “an unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance” from shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms/ammunition in/affecting commerce. ATF Form 4473 asks whether the buyer is an unlawful user; ATF’s 2011 open letter to federal firearms licensees states a medical cannabis cardholder may not be sold a firearm because the card is reasonable cause to believe the person is an unlawful user, even in a state where medical use is legal (secondary summaries [Non-official] — Accessed 2026-10-24).
  • Effect of Schedule III under the federal baseline: §922(g)(3) covers controlled substances in every schedule, not only Schedule I. Moving state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III therefore does not by itself remove the §922(g)(3) bar, because the substance remains a controlled substance and the “unlawful user” analysis turns on federal unlawfulness / lack of federal prescription status, not schedule alone. That analysis is from secondary NJ medical-gun summaries — flag as secondary legal analysis VERIFY with a firearms attorney / ATF guidance, not as an ATF statement. No ATF post-Schedule-III guidance on 4473 / medical cardholders was located in Run 2.
  • NJ state position: NJ does not independently prohibit holding both a medical patient card and a NJ Firearms Purchaser Identification Card (FPID) as an automatic state disqualifier in secondary summaries (same NJ medical-gun source: “NJ state law does not treat being a registered medical cannabis patient as an automatic disqualifying factor for firearms at the state level”). State FPID/carry applications ask about drug habits / mental health, and habitual use or admission of use may lead to denial on state criteria even without a federal charge (secondary gun-lawyer summary [Non-official]).
  • US v. Hemani context — flagged as instructed: No official NJ source (NJ Courts, AG, State Police) addressing US v. Hemani was found in Run 2. Secondary gun-lawyer coverage states Hemani 'centers on federal charges tied to 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3)' and 'leaves New Jersey's rules for issuing gun permits untouched.' Audit note: US v. Hemani was decided 18 June 2026 on a narrow as-applied basis (status-only §922(g)(3) prosecution of a regular user for in-home possession, with no danger showing, held inconsistent with the Second Amendment); it did not strike down §922(g)(3) generally. No official NJ source addressing Hemani was found. Therefore Hemani is reported ONLY as secondary coverage, flagged VERIFY / UNAVAILABLE for an official NJ source, and no Hemani holding is stated as NJ law. Do not state Hemani “overturned” §922(g)(3) or restored NJ gun rights for cannabis users.
  • Police / official-firearm nuance (secondary, do not generalise): A secondary report claims NJ appellate law allows police officers to use marijuana off-duty while retaining official firearms under the §925(a)(1) official-use exemption. That is not a civilian rule and must not be presented as one; VERIFY the appellate decision before citing it.
