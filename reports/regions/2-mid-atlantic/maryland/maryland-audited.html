Part 1 — Audit: method, and what changed

Method. The auditor did not write the original report. Every section was checked claim by claim against primary sources only where a primary source could be opened or found in search-index text of the official page itself: the Maryland Code and COMAR on mgaleg.maryland.gov and regs.maryland.gov, General Assembly bill/chapter pages on mgaleg, the Maryland Cannabis Administration (cannabis.maryland.gov), the Alcohol, Tobacco and Cannabis Commission (atcc.maryland.gov), the Office of Social Equity / Department of Social and Economic Mobility, the Comptroller of Maryland (marylandcomptroller.gov / marylandtaxes.com PDFs), the Department of Agriculture and OneStop, the State Board of Elections, the Maryland Manual, the Governor's office, USDA AMS, and the Bureau of Indian Affairs. News, law-firm blogs, trade press and AI summaries were NOT used to confirm anything; where only such a source exists, the verdict is Unverified and the flag is kept. Federal law is used exactly as given in the brief and was not re-audited; only the report's statements of it and Maryland's responses were checked. The report states the federal baseline correctly throughout — no federal-baseline correction was needed.

Headline corrections (read these first). (1) The original's most consequential stale claim — 'no MCA statement on Schedule III' — is wrong: MCA issued a notice on 23 April 2026 saying Maryland law 'remains fully operative and unchanged'. (2) The Question 4 official canvass is 1,302,161 / 635,572, not 1,302,180 / 635,580. (3) The operating-count arithmetic now closes exactly at 187 once the 4-lab MCA list of 2 March 2026 is used. (4) The cannabis-tax distribution formula applies to the NET after the initial 3% General Fund share and MCA/DoSEM operating allocations, not to the gross — the Q2 2026 official breakdown is now given in full. (5) §36-1102's hemp thresholds and tincture exception are now confirmed verbatim from the primary bill text (HB 12, 2025), not from a court quotation. (6) One untrusted mirror link is removed, and all staging/legacy MCA hosts are replaced with the canonical domain. (7) Four 2026 pending bills the original missed are added, all marked pending — none changes current law.

What was NOT upgraded. Sales totals (~$1.17bn for CY2025 and its split), the MCA dashboard/locator URLs, the MDA hemp registry, and most COMAR-quotation details that rest on search-index snippets remain flagged. This audit is honest about that boundary: a figure that only a secondary source reports is not promoted to 'official' because it appears in a table.

Verdict counts. Confirmed 57 · Corrected 18 · Removed 1 · Unverified 18 · Total 94. 'Removed' means the item (one mirror link) has no correct official version to substitute and is deleted, with the official index substituted. 'Unverified' means no primary source was found in this audit and the flag is kept in the rewrite.

1.3 Possession, home grow, public consumption, product types

  • Personal use amount (21+): 1.5 oz usable cannabis or 12 g concentrated cannabis or cannabis products ≤750 mg THC. MCA FAQ: “Adults 21 years and older may possess up to 1.5 ounces of cannabis flower, up to 12 grams of concentrated cannabis; or a total amount of cannabis products that does not exceed 750 mg of THC. This is known as the ‘personal use amount.’” Source: MCA FAQs (accessed 2026-10-24, index).
  • Civil use amount (over personal, up to): 2.5 oz / 20 g / 1,250 mg THC — civil fine regime (see §5-601.1 summary in appellate headnotes, cannabislaw.report headnotes (PDF) — secondary, VERIFY against Criminal Law text). Under-21 possession ≤2.5 oz: fine, drug-education order, assessment/treatment referral per MCA FAQ (same URL).
  • Home grow: 21+ may cultivate up to 2 plants per person, max 2 per household, out of public view; registered medical patient household may cultivate 2 additional plants, max 4 per residence. MCA FAQ verbatim: “A person 21 years or older may lawfully cultivate up to two plants in their home, out of public view. Likewise, a household may cultivate a maximum of two plants… medical patients registered with [MCA] can cultivate two additional plants, for a total of four plants; not to exceed more than four plants in a given residence. Landlords and property owners can prohibit growing.” Source: MCA FAQs (above). Dispensary sales of clones/seedlings/seeds for home cultivation are also regulated (2 clones/seedlings to adult, 4 to patient/caregiver 21+, 6 seeds — secondary summary at flowhub.com — VERIFY against COMAR).
  • Public consumption: prohibited except at a licensed on-site consumption establishment (none licensed as of Oct 2025 MCA FAQs v8 — secondary summary at catalyst-bc.com — VERIFY current status). No indoor smoking at on-site premises under SB 215 revisions; single-serving edibles + full food service (with local food approvals) are the SB 215 model (University of Maryland public-health summary, law.umaryland.edu summary (PDF) — secondary, VERIFY against §36-407).
  • Product types allowed: flower, pre-rolls, concentrates (kief/hash/oil/wax etc. per §36-101(f)), vapes, edibles, tinctures/capsules, topicals, beverages, sublingual pouches (from Sept 2026). Edibles: general rule “may not contain more than: (a) 10 milligrams of THC per serving; and (b) 100 milligrams of THC per package” (COMAR 14.17.13.05B, regs.maryland.gov COMAR 14.17.13.05). Each single serving in a multi-serving package must be physically separated; mere demarcations on an intact product do not suffice. Medical higher-potency: dispensary “shall only sell… capsule and tincture products with more than 10 mg THC per serving and 100 mg per package to qualifying patients or registered caregivers” (COMAR 14.17.13.06C, COMAR 14.17.13.06); secondary sources state medical edibles up to 40 mg/serving, 400 mg/package — that 40/400 figure is secondary only VERIFY against COMAR 14.17.13.05 medical subsection, do not quote as verified. Beverages: cannabinoid beverage ≤5 mg THC per serving, single-serving product, lawfully produced by licensee (§36-101(c-1)); liquid edible: one container = one serving, ≤10 mg THC (secondary summary — VERIFY). Sublingual pouch (Sept 2026): permeable pouch dissolving buccally/sublingually, 10 mg/serving, 100 mg/package unless otherwise authorised (secondary summary of NOFA — VERIFY against COMAR text).

1.5 Hemp

  • Current Maryland hemp definition/measurement (pre-federal-change): Cannabis sativa L. with delta-9 THC ≤0.3% on a dry-weight basis (Agriculture Art.; COMAR 15.01.17). Testing uses post-decarboxylation (total THC) methodology with measurement uncertainty: final-product batch must show “no more than a total 0.3 percent delta-9 THC on a dry weight basis using the method’s determined measurement uncertainty” (COMAR 15.01.17.12, Cornell mirror — secondary mirror, VERIFY against regs.maryland.gov). Negligent-violation tolerance: total THC over acceptable level but ≤1% delta-9 with reasonable efforts may be treated as negligent with corrective-action plan (COMAR 15.01.17.15, regs.maryland.gov COMAR 15.01.17.15).
  • Delta-8 / intoxicating hemp — MUST be sold only via cannabis licensees, in effect: §36-1102(c) bans products “not derived from naturally occurring biologically active chemical constituents.” Appellate outcome: Moore v. Maryland Hemp Coalition (Md. App. Ct. 2025) held delta-8 and delta-10 THC prohibited: “because hemp-derived psychoactive products, including delta-8 and delta-10 THC, are derived from a chemical process… these products are prohibited”; the court rejected the Article 41 anti-monopoly/common-right claim and reversed the lower-court injunction that had allowed limited unlicensed sales. Sources: cannabislaw.report case summary and opinion headnotes (PDF) (secondary summaries of opinion — VERIFY against official Md. App. opinion). A separate federal case, Charm City Hemp LLC v. Moore, No. 1:2025cv01744 (D. Md.), challenges §36-1102 / licensing — plaintiffs allege hemp retailers are required to obtain a recreational cannabis licence; status/outcome beyond the quoted excerpt VERIFY — Justia. Practical rule for this report: non-naturally-occurring / synthesised intoxicating cannabinoids (delta-8, delta-10) may not be sold as general hemp retail in Maryland; intoxicating cannabis products belong in MCA-licensed dispensaries. The narrow hemp-tincture exception (CBD:THC ≥15:1, ≤2.5 mg THC/serving, ≤100 mg/package, with Administration testing) is the only §36-1102 human-consumption hemp carve-out found — VERIFY full §36-1102(a)–(e).
  • THC drinks: Two distinct regimes — (i) cannabis cannabinoid beverages (licensee-produced, ≤5 mg THC/serving, single-serving) sold at dispensaries / on-site; (ii) hemp-derived THC drinks sold outside dispensaries fall under the §36-1102 ban if intoxicating/synthesised, and under the tincture-ratio rule only for tinctures. No separate Maryland “hemp THC beverage” retail regime was found — VERIFY if a hemp-beverage rule exists in COMAR 15.01.17 or MCA guidance.
  • Maryland response to new federal hemp definition (baseline dates — apply exactly): Federal law PL 119-37 §781 rewrites hemp to a total-THC standard (including THCA) with a 0.4 mg total THC per container cap for final hemp-derived cannabinoid products; legal effect 12 Nov 2026, BUT PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 Dec 2026 to only provisions excluding non-naturally-produced/synthesised cannabinoids; full definition applies after 11 Dec 2026. USDA DEA-lab testing enforcement delayed to 31 Dec 2026. Maryland official response: none found in this run UNAVAILABLE. Maryland’s current 0.3% delta-9 dry-weight standard and §36-1102 synthesised-cannabinoid ban are stricter/different from the incoming federal total-THC + per-container cap, and Maryland has not (in sources found) announced conforming legislation, MDA guidance, or an MCA statement. Do NOT state the full federal definition takes effect 12 Nov 2026 (that is the limited-effect date only). Consequences for Maryland growers/sellers after 11 Dec 2026 are therefore VERIFY — state statute/regulation amendment would be needed to align, and none was identified.

2.4 IRS 280E and Maryland decoupling — under the federal baseline split

  • Federal (baseline, given): From 28 Apr 2026, FDA-approved cannabis products + state-licensed medical marijuana = Schedule III, so 280E “generally removes… as a bar” for businesses no longer dealing in Schedule I/II substances (Treasury guidance forthcoming). Adult-use remains Schedule I and fully subject to 280E federally. A business with mixed medical + adult-use activity must therefore still segregate: medical activity escapes the federal bar (subject to guidance), adult-use activity does not. No federal banking law enacted.
  • Maryland state decoupling — what was actually found: a medical-only subtraction modification enacted 2022 (HB 217, 2021 session / Ch. 2022 — fiscal note mgaleg HB 217 fiscal note (PDF)): “The subtraction modification may be claimed if the deduction for ordinary and necessary expenses is disallowed under Section 280E” for “a certain medical cannabis grower, processor, dispensary, or independent testing laboratory,” claimable from tax year 2022, including “a reasonable allowance for salaries or other compensation.” Secondary firm summary: askfrost.com.
  • Conflict flagged: a trade guide claims Maryland “does not follow federal §280E for state income taxes” for both medical and adult-use (cannabiscpa.tax guide; CannBus table “Decoupled… for both medical and adult-use revenue”). That broader claim was NOT corroborated by a primary statute snippet in this run. The primary evidence found is medical-only. Decision for this report: report Maryland as having a verified medical subtraction modification; adult-use state decoupling is VERIFY against current Tax-General §§10-207/10-307 and any 2023 Cannabis Reform Act expansion. Do not advise that adult-use expenses are deductible on the Maryland return on the basis of the trade guide alone.
  • Interaction with Schedule III: if federal 280E no longer bars medical deductions, the Maryland medical subtraction becomes largely duplicative for federal-conforming state computation (Maryland starts from federal taxable income — mechanics VERIFY), but it remains relevant for any residual federal disallowance and for pre-28 Apr 2026 years. Adult-use: Maryland state treatment VERIFY as above; federally still barred.

Part 3 — Local rules

3.1 Who sets rules — county, municipal, or both? Both.

Maryland cannabis local control is exercised by “political subdivisions” — counties AND municipalities (cities/towns). Baltimore City is treated as a county-level jurisdiction for this purpose (it is an independent city, not part of Baltimore County — do not conflate Baltimore City vs Baltimore County; MCA county tables list them separately, e.g. Round 1 dispensary awards: Baltimore County 6, Baltimore City 11). Interaction: state law sets the licence, tax (state-only), and floor/ceiling rules; counties/municipalities set zoning (time/place/manner), local permits (especially on-site consumption), and local distribution of their tax share; municipalities inside a county exercise zoning within their boundaries, with county zoning applying in unincorporated areas. Where both a county and a municipality could regulate the same site, the municipality’s ordinance governs inside its limits — general Maryland home-rule principle VERIFY against specific county/municipal charters; no cannabis-specific preemption hierarchy beyond §36-405/410 was found.

3.2 Can locals ban outright? No — except on-site consumption.

  • General cannabis businesses: NO outright ban. Political subdivisions “may establish reasonable zoning requirements” but “may not… establish zoning or other requirements that unduly burden a cannabis licensee” (unduly burden includes a zoning requirement more restrictive than §36-410), may not impose disproportionate fees/requirements vs similar-impact businesses, may not prohibit transport/delivery through, may not block conversion of a compliant medical licensee, and may not extract payments/donations via agreement (SB 537 draft language summarising §36-405 — VERIFY enacted §36-405 text; MPP/DLS summaries agree: mpp.org summary; DLS SB 469 fiscal note (PDF)).
  • On-site consumption: YES, local opt-in / prohibition. “An onsite consumption establishment may not operate in a location unless the political subdivision affirmatively authorizes the operation by issuing a permit or license. The political subdivision may also place restrictions on or prohibit the operation of onsite consumption establishments.” (DLS SB 469 fiscal note, above.) SB 215 added local power to set on-site hours.
  • Moratoriums / caps: No statewide local moratorium regime found; no local cap numbers were verified from official county sources in this run. Any county claiming a “cap” must be checked against §36-405 undue-burden limits — a de facto ban via cap would be VERIFY litigable, not assumed lawful. Review dates: UNAVAILABLE — no local moratorium expiry dates found.
  • Local tax prohibition: §36-405(d) (Part 1) — locals may not impose a cannabis tax. Local fees: only non-disproportionate, similar-impact fees allowed; no cannabis-specific local licence-fee schedule was verified for any county in this run (see §3.4).

11.1 Testing

Framework: COMAR 14.17.08 (independent testing laboratories) + MCA Technical Authority for Cannabis Testing (Rev. 6, eff. Feb 2025), which “has the force and effect of law” per its introduction (index). Source: MCA Technical Authority Rev. 6 (PDF) — index (table garbled in index rendering; verify limits in the PDF itself before quoting a single limit).

Sampling / batch rules (COMAR 14.17.10/11, index):

Source: COMAR 14.17.10.03 — index.
  • Processor: same batch/lot logic for each unique lot of concentrate/infused product after validation. Source: COMAR 14.17.11.14 — index.

11.4 Security requirements

  • Grower premises (COMAR 14.17.10.02, index): motion-activated video surveillance “at all premises” that (a) records “in images of high quality and high resolution capable of clearly revealing facial detail”; (b) “Operates 24-hours a day, 365 days a year without interruption”; (c) date/time stamp on every frame; notices to visitors; cameras capturing each exit and each entrance to grow/test/cure/manufacture/process/store areas and each trim/package/cure/store area; recordings access-limited, stored under a security alarm independent of the main premises alarm, in an easily accessible format, retained ≥90 calendar days, made available to MCA/law enforcement for just cause within 48 hours (each missing day = separate violation).
  • Dispensary / micro-dispensary (COMAR 14.17.12 + MCA Policy FAQs May 2025, index): secure room; all cannabis outside packaging/dispensing hours kept in the storage facility (secure room may be open and inventory outside it only in the 2 hours immediately before/after posted hours, unless MCA regional investigator grants an exemption); access limited to owner/agents; lighting for surveillance; security alarm covering all perimeter entry points/portals, rooms holding cannabis, and on-site/off-site record locations; motion-activated video surveillance. Dispensary sales hours 8 a.m.–11 p.m. (sales must be made/recorded in that window; setup/closing/receiving outside it only while closed to the public and inventory locked in the secure room).
  • Transport: ITL sample transport: with oversight by ≥1 ITL employee registered with MCA, in a vehicle meeting COMAR 14.17.09.03 (COMAR 14.17.08.06, index). Micro-dispensary delivery vehicle (COMAR 14.17.12.03, index): enclosed vehicle, current registration, insured as required by law, GPS monitored for the entire route, locked/secure containers anchored inside, motion-activated surveillance capturing activity around all cannabis containers, containers concealed/not visible or identifiable from outside, no cannabis-related sign/illustration; delivery only on a prior order, only product on an active invoice/order/manifest, retail value in vehicle ≤$5,000 at any time; GPS records examined at regular intervals per SOP; electronic manifest for chain of custody (COMAR 14.17.09.03E–F). Transporter/security/waste registrants exist under COMAR 14.17.09 — registration (not full licence) model; exact vehicle/personnel rules for standard transporters beyond the above were not fully opened — VERIFY.
  • No separate “secure transport company licence”: MCA Growers FAQ (index): “No; the license must provide required agent ID cards to anyone transporting cannabis.”

11.5 Waste disposal rules

  • Cannabis (dispensary green-waste procedure, MCA bulletin, index — historic 2019/2022 bulletins, VERIFY for current SOP): waste entered in POS/Metrc with weight + reason; log must show date/time of disposal and removal, method (e.g. return to grower/processor, kitty litter, mulch, bleach), disposing agent/manager and verifying agent. Product returned to a grower/processor must be manifested back in Metrc within 7 calendar days. Other product “shall immediately be rendered unusable, entered into Waste log and placed into the waste container. This action must be clearly captured on video.” Flower/dry-leaf waste: ground to the smallest possible degree then mixed with non-cannabis material at 50:50 or higher non-cannabis content (alcohol, bleach, other solvent, kitty litter, mulch, dirt or other nonconsumable material). Non-flower waste emptied into a nonconsumable product. Final destruction ≤7 days after log entry, in a designated commercial waste bin; all disposal on video, verified by two dispensary agents on the log. Source: MCA green-waste SOP bulletin (PDF) — index.
  • Processor/grower waste (general): surplus/out-of-date/processing waste handled per the licensee's approved waste-disposal plan or sent to a licensed grower for destruction, tracked in Metrc (secondary POS summary — VERIFY against COMAR 14.17.10/11 waste sections, which were not fully opened). Edible-processing premises: non-green-waste containers adequate, labelled “not suitable for any green waste,” easily cleanable, on a hard impermeable surface; indoor containers leak-proof, emptied/cleaned at least daily; outdoor containers leak/vermin/insect-impervious, drained to a code-compliant sewerage system, covered, large enough to hold waste until off-site removal (COMAR 14.17.11.07, index).

12.2 THC driving limits and impairment testing

  • No per se THC limit in Maryland. Driving while impaired by cannabis is a criminal offence under Transportation §21-902 (drug-impaired driving: driving “while so far impaired by any drug… that the person cannot drive safely” — §21-902(c); CDS-impaired driving §21-902(d), secondary summaries). There is no ng/mL blood-THC threshold equivalent to the 0.08% alcohol per se limit (§21-902(a)(2)).
  • Impairment testing: standardised field sobriety tests, officer observation, Drug Recognition Expert (DRE) evaluation, and chemical testing (blood/urine) as evidence — but THC/metabolites persist days–weeks after impairment resolves, and the Maryland Cannabis Public Health Advisory Council's 27 Jan 2026 letter to the House Judiciary Committee (HB114, “Mateo's Law”) states: “there is no significant correlation between blood THC concentrations and driving impairment and thus there are no valid per se limits,” and warns against using presence alone for prosecutorial decisions. Source: mgaleg committee testimony (PDF) — index (official legislative testimony).
  • Vehicle consumption: smoking/consuming cannabis in the passenger area of a motor vehicle on a highway is prohibited (Transportation §21-903(c), secondary — VERIFY for text). MCA guidance (secondary summaries): wait at least 6 hours after consumption per a 2017 study cited by MCA; no cannabis breathalyser in use. MCA's 2025 Cannabis Use Biannual Study (secondary summary): drug-impaired-driving arrests down >70% over 7 years (all drugs); cannabis-positive DRE evaluations ~25% of all evaluations in 2023; self-reported driving within 3 hours of use spiked >20% after July 2023 then fell 5% — secondary, VERIFY against the MCA study itself.
  • HB114/SB110 (2026, “Mateo's Law”) status: proposed required testing after accidents — not stated as enacted in the sources found; do not present as current law — VERIFY for 2026 session outcome.

12.7 Gun ownership conflicts

This section reports the federal–state conflict; it does not give legal advice. Under the Federal Baseline, adult-use remains Schedule I and medical marijuana is Schedule III only as specified — the gun analysis must be read with that split.
  • Federal Gun Control Act, 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3) (secondary summaries): prohibits firearm/ammunition possession by a person who is an “unlawful user of or addicted to” a controlled substance. Maryland State Police licensing (secondary): firearm applicants are asked about medical-patient status and personal cannabis use; disclosure can block a regulated-firearm transaction. Source (secondary): legalclarity.org — VERIFY against ATF/MSP primary sources.
  • 2026 development (secondary, VERIFY): A Jan 2026 ATF rule revision is reported to have narrowed “unlawful user” so isolated/sporadic use no longer automatically triggers the prohibition, while a pattern of regular/ongoing use still does. Whether/how Schedule III medical status affects the §922(g)(3) analysis for Maryland medical patients was not resolved by any Maryland or federal source found in Run 2 — VERIFY/UNAVAILABLE for a definitive post-April 2026 gun position.
