Federal baseline — given, not re-researched (flagged where Utah material interacts)

  • Split scheduling: state-licensed medical marijuana and FDA-approved products moved to Schedule III effective 28 April 2026 (91 FR 22714). All adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. The broader 2024 rescheduling proposal is not final.
  • IRS §280E is generally lifted for Schedule III medical activity; adult-use remains fully subject to §280E.
  • Hemp: PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026. After 11 December 2026 the full total-THC hemp definition and the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply.
  • USDA enforcement of DEA-registered lab testing for hemp is delayed to 31 December 2026. The federal medical-marijuana appropriations rider runs through 11 December 2026. There is no federal cannabis banking law.

Utah application (analysis, not a Utah statute quoted here): Utah's programme is state-licensed (UDAF/DHHS — see Part 4), so on the given baseline its cultivators, processors and pharmacies' medical activity falls in the Schedule III / §280E-lifted category, while any non-medical possession remains Schedule I federally.

(b) Tribal question — Utah Code §4-41a-105 allows a governor–tribe agreement for a production establishment or pharmacy on tribal land, but expressly says the agreement “may not exempt any person from the requirements of this chapter” — whether a tribe operating under such an agreement counts as “state-licensed” for federal Schedule III purposes is Verify.

Part 1 — Laws

1.1 Legal status — medical only

  • Medical cannabis is legal for Utah residents (and registered non-Utah visitors — §1.4) with a qualifying condition, a recommending-provider recommendation, and a state card, purchased only from a licensed medical cannabis pharmacy (or via its home-delivery/courier channel). First pharmacy opened March 2020.
  • Non-patients: any possession / use / sale / cultivation of marijuana outside the medical programme is unlawful under Utah Code Title 58, Ch. 37 (§58-37-8) and Ch. 37a (paraphernalia). There is no adult-use, no home grow for anyone (see §1.3), and no reciprocity by out-of-state card alone (a visitor must register for a Utah non-resident card).
  • Hemp/CBD: legal only as a UDAF-registered cannabinoid product meeting THC/analog caps, sold by a UDAF-permitted retailer to buyers 21+ where THC is present; smokable hemp flower and conventional food/beverage additives are prohibited (see §1.5).

Sources: medicalcannabis.utah.gov Verified live; Ballotpedia — Proposition 2 (index); Marijuana Herald — Utah laws (index, Sept 2026 update). Accessed 2026-11-01.

1.3 Penalties, product forms, smoking ban, THC/dosage limits

Non-patient possession (§58-37-8, version shown 2024-07-01 — Verify current text)

  • Simple possession of marijuana (not amount-tiered in the excerpt for 1st/2nd offence): class B misdemeanor; 3rd conviction within 7 years = class A misdemeanor; 4th+ within 7 years = 3rd-degree felony. Secondary summaries state: <100 lb, 1st/2nd = class B (≤6 months, ≤$1,000); 3rd in 7 years = class A (≤364 days, ≤$2,500). Note conflict: older (pre-2014) text tiered by <1 oz; current text in index does NOT show a 1-oz tier — do not use the 1-oz tier as current law without re-checking le.utah.gov.
  • Manufacture / distribute / possess with intent to distribute marijuana (Schedule III/IV or marijuana category in §58-37-8(1)(b)(ii)): 3rd-degree felony first offence; 2nd-degree on subsequent. Secondary: ≤5 years / $5,000 first offence.
  • Home grow: PROHIBITED for all patients and adults — no home-cultivation licence exists; all product must come from licensed pharmacies.
  • Cultivation (unlicensed): treated as production/manufacture under §58-37-8(1)(a) (felony tier above); no separate “home grow” penalty tier found — Verify whether a distinct cultivation-amount table exists in current §58-37-8(2)(b) (the index excerpt for (2)(b)(i)/(ii) was truncated; drug-dealer liability tiers in §58-37e-101 use 25/50/75/100 plants and 1/4/8/16 lb thresholds for civil liability, NOT criminal possession tiers — do not conflate).
  • Paraphernalia (§58-37a-5): use/possess with intent to use = class B misdemeanor; deliver/manufacture with intent to deliver = class A misdemeanor; deliver to person <18 who is ≥3 years younger = 3rd-degree felony. A medical cannabis device possessed by a cardholder for medical use is excepted in practice (secondary) — Verify device definition in §26B-4-201.
  • Public consumption: prohibited except medical emergency (secondary summaries; statute §26B-4-?? / §58-37-3.9 — Verify section number). Transport in public must be in opaque bag/box with label (content, weight, purchase date, legal-use termination date, inventory-control barcode); 2025 change allows patient's own opaque bag/box.
  • Patient over-limit: possessing > legal dosage limit but ≤2× limit = infraction $100 first offence, class B misdemeanor repeat (secondary); >2× limit loses programme protections and faces §58-37-8 charges. Verify against §58-37-3.9 / §26B-4-245 current text.
  • DUI: driving under the influence remains illegal; affirmative defence if only substance is cannabis in medicinal dosage form ingested per programme (secondary).
  • Smoking: combustion prohibited even for cardholders; vaporisation (heating without flame) permitted; pharmacy may not sell cigarette-form product or flame device, but may sell a device that warms material into vapour without flame (§4-41a-1102(4), 2024 version). First smoking offence infraction ≤$100; second+ under Controlled Substances Act (secondary).

Allowed medical product forms (§26B-4-201(39), Board memo 2026-06-09)

Tablet; capsule; gelatinous cube/lozenge (cube or rectangular cuboid); concentrated liquid/viscous oil; liquid suspension ≤30 mL; topical; transdermal; sublingual; resin or wax; aerosol; suppository; soft/hard confection (uniform rectangular cuboid or spherical, homogeneous, single-serving); unprocessed flower in tamper-evident container for vaporisation only. ≤113 g unprocessed cannabis (flower) AND/OR ≤20 g total composite THC in all other dosage forms, subject to provider dosing guidelines (provider may set lower). Pharmacy may dispense in any one 28-day period up to the legal dosage limit (§4-41a-1102(2)). Sources: Medical Cannabis Patient Guide (PDF) (index); §4-41a-1102 (index).

1.4 Medical programme

  • Qualifying conditions (§26B-4-203(2), 2025-07-01 version): HIV/AIDS; Alzheimer's; ALS; cancer; cachexia; persistent nausea (not significantly responsive to traditional treatment; excl. pregnancy, cannabis-induced cyclical vomiting, cannabinoid hyperemesis); Crohn's / ulcerative colitis; epilepsy / debilitating seizures; MS or persistent debilitating muscle spasms; PTSD (treated/monitored by licensed mental-health therapist + VA or qualifying psychiatrist/psychologist/LCSW/PMH-APRN diagnosis/confirmation); autism; terminal illness <6 months; hospice; rare condition (<200,000 US, not adequately managed despite conventional non-opioid meds or physical interventions); pain >2 weeks not adequately managed despite conventional non-opioid meds/physical interventions; acute pain expected to last >2 weeks for a condition where opioids would otherwise be used (subsection (p) truncated in index — Verify full (p)/(q)). Sources: §26B-4-203 (index); board summary (index).
  • Providers: MD, DO, APRN, PA, podiatrist (DPM) licensed in Utah with controlled-substance licence. Pre-July 2025: Qualified Medical Provider (QMP, DHHS-registered, CE required, any age) vs Limited Medical Provider (LMP, unregistered, no CE, 21+ only, 15 active recommendations). 1.5 Hemp / cannabinoid products

  • Hemp definition (Utah, current): cannabis with <0.3% THC by dry weight (Title 4 Ch. 41; R68-32: “any part of the cannabis plant, whether growing or not, with a concentration of less than 0.3% tetrahydrocannabinol by dry weight”). Measurement: total THC = (Δ9-THCA × 0.877) + (Δ8-THCA × 0.877) + Δ9-THC + Δ8-THC for registration screening (UDAF guide). Federal interaction: after 11 Dec 2026 (per given baseline) the federal definition becomes total-THC based with a 0.4 mg/container cap — Utah's 0.3% dry-weight + 5 mg/serving regime will then be stricter on serving size but looser on container total than federal law for many products; a Utah-registered 5 mg gummy would exceed the federal 0.4 mg/container cap. No Utah statute responding to PL 119-37 / 119-103 was found in this run — Verify: Utah response to 12 Nov / 11 Dec 2026 dates = Unavailable (no UDAF guidance page opened; do not assume Utah will conform or decouple).
  • UDAF cannabinoid product registration: EVERY cannabinoid product marketed/sold in Utah (in-state or shipped in) must be registered with UDAF annually before sale, via agbusiness.utah.gov/s → Industrial Hemp → Product Registration Application; requires label + full-panel COA; fee per product (older secondary: $260 hemp-oil/extract products, $160 seed-only, $0 fibre — Verify in FY2027 fee schedule). Registration effective 12 months. Source: UDAF registration guide (index).
  • Retailer permit: a business selling cannabinoid products must hold a UDAF Industrial Hemp Retailer permit, renewed annually; HB 389 (2026) requires ID check 21+ and repeals the retailer video-surveillance requirement. Sources: HB 389 text (index); Hemp CBD Business Plans (index).
  • Processor licence: cannabinoid product processor licence (UDAF); medical processors exempt from an additional hemp-processor licence under HB 389 (2026). Source: HB 389 text (index).
  • Cultivation: USDA Hemp Producer Licence ONLY since HB 385 (2022); UDAF does not license cultivators. USDA AMS list (updated 17 Feb 2026) shows Utah = “USDA Hemp Producer License.” Source: USDA AMS state/tribal plan list (index).
  • THC limits (registered hemp products): combined total THC + THC analogs ≤5 mg/serving AND ≤150 mg/package AND ≤10% of total cannabinoid content (all must be met). Retail hemp extracts: tighter 10 mg/container + 5 mg/serving in one secondary summary — Conflict / Verify (may reflect extract vs product distinction). Sources: SEC filing (2024 annual report summarising HB 227) (index); LegalClarity — delta-8 (index).
  • Delta-8: banned for general sale — (i) Utah Controlled Substances Act lists “tetrahydrocannabinols” (incl. isomers/synthetic equivalents) in Schedule I (§58-37-4); (ii) Hemp Act prohibits selling a cannabinoid product containing a chemically converted cannabinoid (HB 52, 2024, defining cannabinoid to include chemically converted delta-8/9/10); (iii) artificially derived cannabinoids = adulterants (HB 227). The UDAF registration guide (older) allowed only Δ8-THC/Δ8-THCA as analogs in the 0.3% calculation — that guide is superseded in substance by the bans above; do not rely on it for legality. Exceptions noted in secondary: medical products via pharmacies; topical-only hemp extracts — Verify.
  • Delta-9 (hemp-derived): allowed ONLY within registered-product caps (5 mg/serving etc.) and 0.3% dry-weight; a heavy product (e.g. a drink) cannot use dry-weight maths to evade the mg caps in Utah (unlike the pre-2026 federal loophole).
  • THCA: “high-THCA” hemp products outside the medical system banned under HB 54 (2025) per secondary; smokable THCA/hemp flower banned (below). THCA counts toward total THC via the 0.877 decarboxylation factor in registration.
  • HHC, THCP, THC-O-acetate, delta-10, THCB: named non-compliant/prohibited (HB 54, 2025; UDAF guide). Sources: LegalClarity — delta-8 (index); MyCannabis (index); Drug Policy Watch (index).
  • Minimum age: 21+ to purchase any cannabinoid product containing any trace of THC (incl. full-spectrum CBD); HB 389 (2026) requires retailers to check ID for 21+. CBD with no THC: age rule Verify (secondary sources differ on whether 21+ applies to all hemp products or only THC-containing ones). Sources: MyCannabis (index); HB 389 text (index).
  • Prohibited formats: smokable form (incl. hemp flower for smoking); cannabinoids added to conventional food/beverages (incl. CBD water/sports drinks, baked goods as additives, chocolate-covered, candy); CBD vapes/e-cigarettes (HB 265, eff. 1 July 2026, excludes e-cigarettes/cannabinoid substances for them — medical devices separate). Sources: Herb.co (index); MyCannabis (index); Utah Cann (index).
  • THC beverages: conventional THC seltzers/drinks are effectively not legally sold in Utah as hemp products (beverage format prohibited), regardless of mg; the medical programme has no beverage dosage form (liquid suspension branded as beverage excluded). Source: Herb.co (index).
  • Testing/labelling: full-panel COA required for registration; labelling per R68-26-5 (and R68-37 testing standards per SEC summary); marketing must include notice the product is hemp, not cannabis/medical cannabis (HB 389 §4-41-?? marketing clause, index). Verify current R68-26 text — not opened in full. Source: SEC filing (index).

1.6 Packaging, labelling, advertising, delivery, zoning/buffers

  • Packaging (medical): tamper-resistant + tamper-evident container; pharmacy provides opaque bag/box for transport in public (patient may bring own since 2025); label must show THC/CBD amount; unprocessed flower in compliant labelled tamper-evident container. Source: §4-41a-1102 (index).
  • Labelling (medical): standard labelling format by UDAF rule (§4-41a-1102 / §4-41a-602); back-panel info on pharmacy websites from 1 July 2025; pregnancy/breastfeeding warning label from 1 Jan 2026 (secondary, DHHS summary). Sources: Hemp Gazette, Apr 2025 (index); HB 389 text via PMN (index).
  • Advertising — general ban with narrow exceptions: §4-41a-109(1) (2026 board memo):

“a person may not advertise regarding the recommendation, sale, dispensing, or transportation of medical cannabis, including: (a) a promotional discount or incentive; (b) a particular medical cannabis product, medical cannabis device, or medical cannabis brand, or medicinal dosage form; or (c) an assurance of a medical outcome.”

  • Pharmacies (§4-41a-1104) may: advertise jobs; exterior signage with ONLY name/logo/hours + green cross (notwithstanding local sign bans, but complying with local sign ordinances); advertise in any medium name/logo, location/hours, service, personnel, home-delivery status, best practices, educational material; hold educational events (no gifts/merchandise except educational materials, no product-specific marketing, no FDCA-violating claims); maintain non-promotional inventory info on website; targeted marketing per UDAF rule (§4-41a-604). Production establishments (§4-41a-403, eff. 2025-05-07): may NOT advertise to the general public; jobs + website (establishment/employees only; processors with 21+ age-gated website may show educational info incl. COA + where to purchase + targeted marketing); same green-cross signage rule; name/logo standards must ensure “medical rather than recreational disposition.” Sources: board memo 2026-08-18 (index); §4-41a-1104 via FindLaw (index); §4-41a-403 via Justia (index).
  • Delivery: home-delivery designation (§4-41a-1201) + courier licence (§4-41a-1202). A home-delivery pharmacy delivers shipments to fulfil electronic orders via the state central patient portal; courier contracts with home-delivery pharmacy; courier licence non-transferable; delivery allowed “notwithstanding any county or municipal ordinance” (§4-41a-1201(3)). Closed-door pharmacy (§4-41a-1206, eff. 2025-11-06): delivery-only, no walk-in; same 1,000 ft / 600 ft siting as production establishments (below). The Sept 2026 monthly report tracks delivery $ and deliveries by county (see Part 5). Sources: §4-41a-1201 via LawServer (index); §4-41a-1202 via LawServer (index); §4-41a-1206 (index).
  • Zoning/buffers (state): Production establishment (§4-41a-201(2)(c), eff. 2026-05-06): may NOT locate “(A) within 1,000 feet of a community location; or (B) in or within 600 feet of a district that the relevant municipality or county has zoned as primarily residential” — measured from nearest entrance by shortest ordinary pedestrian route to property boundary; licensing board may waive up to 20% if not reasonably feasible otherwise. Medical cannabis pharmacy (§4-41a-1001(2)(c), eff. 2026-05-06): may NOT locate “(A) within 200 feet of a community location; or (B) in or within 600 feet of a district… zoned as primarily residential” — same measurement + 20% waiver. Closed-door pharmacy: 1,000 ft / 600 ft (§4-41a-1206(6)(a)). “Community location” = public/private school, licensed childcare/preschool, church, public library, public playground/park (SLC ordinance definition, consistent with state usage). Pharmacy must be in a zone described in §4-41a-1105 (permitted in non-residential zones — see Part 3 pre-emption). 10.5 Interstate issues

Hemp shipments

  • Compliant hemp (≤0.3% delta-9 THC dry weight, pre-11 Dec 2026 federal definition) may move in interstate commerce under the 2018 Farm Bill, but Utah adds its own gates: product registration, retail permit, testing, labelling, and (for transport within Utah) a transport manifest for industrial hemp (secondary UDAF guide: “A transport manifest is required for all industrial hemp that is being transported in Utah” — Verify manifest form/rule citation). After 11 Dec 2026, per the given federal baseline, the full total-THC definition + 0.4 mg total THC per container cap apply — Utah's 5 mg/serving registered-product regime will then sit in tension with the federal finished-product cap; no Utah conforming amendment was found — Verify in the 2027 session.
  • Hemp that exceeds the federal/state THC definition is marijuana, not hemp, and may not be shipped as hemp. Law-enforcement roadside testing cannot distinguish hemp from marijuana — seizure/arrest risk even for technically compliant product (secondary legal summaries, consistent).

Utah residents buying in neighbouring states — what can lawfully be brought back: NOTHING containing marijuana

  • Colorado, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico (all adult-use + medical): a Utah resident aged 21+ may lawfully purchase and possess in that state under that state's law, but transport across the state line into Utah is a federal offence and a Utah state offence unless the person holds a Utah medical card and the product is in a Utah medicinal dosage form obtained from a Utah pharmacy — out-of-state product does not become lawful by crossing the border. Utah pharmacies/official guidance warn patients “not to import cannabis purchased elsewhere, regardless of where it was bought” (Herb guide summary of UDAF guidance, index).
  • Utah's metabolite DUI law (§41-6a-517, see §12.3) means even lawful use days earlier in Colorado/Nevada can create Utah driving exposure (subject to the inactive-metabolite exception and affirmative defence).
  • No interstate cannabis compact, reciprocity purchase agreement or import agreement involving Utah was found. Colorado's CANNRA / interstate-compact activity does not include Utah as a cannabis-trade partner (secondary Colorado summary). Interstate trade in marijuana remains prohibited even between two legal states.
  • Wendover warning: Wendover, UT is not a Nevada dispensary town. West Wendover, NV (across the border in Elko County) has Nevada-licensed adult-use sales; product bought there may not lawfully be carried back into Wendover, UT / Tooele County.

Out-of-state medical cards in Utah (visitor cards)

  • Utah does not recognise an out-of-state card for direct purchase at a Utah pharmacy. Instead, a visitor holding a valid home-state card and diagnosed with a Utah qualifying condition may apply for a non-Utah resident (visitor) card: valid 21 days, max 2 cards per calendar year; purchase/possession only within Utah dosing limits, only in Utah medicinal dosage forms, only from licensed Utah pharmacies; no interstate transport. Border enforcement

No Utah-specific border checkpoint programme for cannabis was found. Enforcement is via ordinary traffic stops, DUI/metabolite law, and federal enforcement on federal land (below). Utah Highway Patrol / DPS interdiction is not documented as a cannabis-specific border operation in Run 2 — Verify/Unavailable rather than asserting checkpoints exist or do not exist.

National parks and other federal land

  • Utah has 5 national parks (Zion, Bryce Canyon, Arches, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef), extensive national forest, BLM land and military installations — roughly two-thirds of Utah's land area is federal (secondary guides). A Utah medical card provides no protection on federal land. Federal simple-possession penalties (21 U.S.C. §844, per secondary summaries): first offence up to 1 year + minimum $1,000 fine; second 15 days–2 years + minimum $2,500. National Park Service rule 36 CFR §2.35 bans controlled-substance possession on park land with no medical exception (secondary). TSA at Salt Lake City International Airport operates under federal law and must refer discovered cannabis to law enforcement; hemp-derived CBD ≤0.3% THC is the stated TSA exception (secondary). These federal penalties are secondary summaries — verify 21 U.S.C. §844 / 36 CFR §2.35 text before advising.
  • Tribal land is not federal land for this purpose (see §10.1 — tribal law governs, with federal law as a backdrop), and state land / private property is where Utah medical law applies.

11.3 Cultivation rules — canopy, indoor/outdoor/greenhouse

Canopy (§4-41a-204(2), via UDAF Cultivation Inspection Checklist eff. 6 May 2026, ag.utah.gov PDF, index):

“(b) Except as provided in Subsection (2)(c)(i) or (c)(ii), a cannabis cultivation facility may not: (i) for a facility that cultivates cannabis only indoors, use more than 100,000 total square feet of cultivation space; (ii) for a facility that cultivates cannabis only outdoors, use more than four acres for cultivation; and (iii) for a facility that cultivates cannabis through a combination of indoor and outdoor cultivation, use more combined indoor square footage and outdoor acreage than allowed under the department's formula… (c)(i) Each licensee may apply… for: (A) a one-time, permanent increase of up to 20%…; or (B) a short-term increase, not to exceed 12 months, of up to 40%… (d) If a licensee describes an intended acreage or square footage… less than the limitation…, the licensee may not cultivate more than the licensee's identified intended acreage or square footage.”

  • Combined formula (R66-1-5, via checklist): no more than 50,000 sq ft indoors + 2 acres outdoors for a combined facility.
  • Indoor / outdoor / greenhouse: both indoor and outdoor cultivation are allowed under R66-1 (older R68-27). Outdoor facilities must outline measures to keep product from deterioration/contamination. Greenhouse is not separately defined as a third category in the excerpts found — a greenhouse would be counted as indoor or outdoor depending on department classification; Verify R66-1 definitions before advising a greenhouse operator. No plant-count limit was found (limits are by area, not plant number) — do not state a plant count.
  • Operating plan must include: cultivation practices incl. pesticide + plant-food use; acreage/sq ft + anticipated yield; drying/curing methods; pest management plan; fertiliser application timing/method; water usage + wastewater disposal procedures; waste disposal plan; recall/destruction plan; emergency procedures (fire, chemical spill, other). Facility must not be visible to the public from outside at ground level (secondary county guide citing §4-41a-501 — Verify); stored cannabis ≥6 inches off ground, away from chemicals/lubricants/pesticides/fertilisers/contaminants.
  • Local overlay example (secondary, Weber County): county code §108-7-34 allows licensed cultivation only indoors in A-2/A-3 zones, minimum 20 acres, 100-ft setback, 8-ft masonry wall or 6-ft berm screening — county-specific, not statewide; Verify Weber County Code before citing.
  • Odour: HB 343 (2025) requires production establishments to include odour-reduction methods in the operating plan and directs UDAF to create odour-control recommendations (bill summary, index) — Verify R66-1 odour rule text.

11.4 Security — cameras, alarms, storage, transport and courier vehicles

  • Video surveillance (R66 closed-door rule excerpt, utah.gov/pmn 1323193.pdf, index; cultivation/processing application requirements, index): cameras recording at resolution at least 640 × 470 or equivalent; coverage of single entrance/exits, product packaging and product destruction/disposal locations; capable of identifying activity in/adjacent to the building and identifying visitors/employees; record continuously 24 hours/day, 7 days/week or motion-activated; at least one 19-inch or greater call-up monitor; printer capable of an immediate clear still photo from any camera image. Storage of recordings: at least 45 calendar days; locally stored footage device secured. Areas recorded (processing application pattern): all entrances/exits and ingress/egress vantage points; all storage areas; all extraction areas; all manufacture/package/label areas; all waste movement/processing/storage/destruction areas; interior + exterior of entrances/exits. Recordings must display current date/time per NIST standards.
  • Security plan (every facility application): alarm system description; person(s) notified of breaches/alerts; video storage device location (local/cloud); procedures to give UDAF inspectors immediate access to current + archived footage; any additional measures exceeding the rule.
  • Storage (general): sanitary storage preserving integrity; short- + long-term storage protocols in the operating plan; cannabis stored ≥6 inches off ground, away from contaminants; pharmacy storage / PIC inventory duties under §4-41a-1101(12).
  • Transport — production (§4-41a-404, via checklist eff. 6 May 2026): the transporter (other than a cardholder transporting own treatment) must be employed by the licensed entity authorising transport + possess a manifest with unique identifier linking to the ICS, origin/destination, departure/arrival times/locations; manifest violation = infraction, $100 fine, not a Title 58 offence for that conduct alone. Only a cultivation-facility agent may transport unprocessed cannabis outside medicinal dosage form (to a processing facility or testing lab).
  • Transport — pharmacy/courier (R66-6, index): a printed manifest accompanies each transport (departure/receiving address + licence numbers, strain, weight, ICS unique ID, departure + estimated arrival date/time, name/signature of each accompanying licensee/agent; manifest may not be voided/changed after departure; receiving location verifies + records differences, reports immediately to the department; product shielded from public view, secured, temperature-controlled if perishable; licensee/agent must occupy each vehicle, no other individual; report a vehicle accident with product loss to the department within 24 hours).
  • Home delivery (R66-6-3/4, index): written home-delivery operating plan; transport in a fully enclosed box/container/cage secured inside the vehicle at appropriate storage temperature; vehicle make/model/colour/VIN/plate provided to the department; real-time delivery tracking; communication plan with central dispatch/security; emergency procedures; pharmacy-to-patient ICS tracking; delivery only to a cardholder or caregiver-facility employee; no delivery before 6 a.m. or after 10 p.m.; no product left unattended in a vehicle >60 minutes (unless the agent is staying overnight mid-route); no dosage/quantity change at delivery; no consumption while delivering; agent wears a name badge; PMP contact info/hours provided; payment made prior to delivery.

11.5 Waste disposal

Statute §4-41a-405 (index): “medical cannabis waste” = waste + unused material from cultivation/production. A production establishment shall: “(a) render medical cannabis waste unusable and unrecognizable before transporting [it] from the establishment; and (b) dispose… in accordance with: (i) federal and state laws… related to hazardous waste; (ii) RCRA, 42 U.S.C. §6991 et seq.; (iii) Title 19, Chapter 6, Part 5, Solid Waste Management Act…” (checklist excerpt truncates (b) — Verify full §4-41a-405).

Rule R66-1-13 (Cornell, index; adopted Bulletin 2024-10, eff. 13 May 2024):

“(1) Solid and liquid wastes… stored, managed, and disposed of in accordance with applicable state law. (2) Wastewater… disposed of in compliance with applicable state law. (3) Cannabis waste generated from the cannabis plant, trim, and leaves is not considered hazardous waste unless it has been treated or contaminated with a solvent, or pesticide. (4) Cannabis waste shall be made unusable before leaving the… facility. (5) Cannabis waste not designated as hazardous, shall be made unusable by grinding and incorporating the cannabis plant waste with other ground materials so the resulting mixture is at least 50% non-cannabis waste by volume, or by other methods approved by the department before implementation. … (7) Compostable waste… mixed with: (a) food waste; (b) yard waste; or (c) vegetable-based grease or oils. (8) Non-compostable waste… in a landfill or… incineration, mixed with: (a) paper waste; (b) cardboard waste; (c) plastic waste; or (d) soil. (9) Cannabis waste includes: (a) plant waste including roots, stalks, leaves, and stems; (b) excess… from QA testing; (c)… that fail to meet testing requirements; and (d)… subject to a recall.”

Testing-lab waste follows the same pattern (R68-30 / R66-4 excerpt): wastewater per state law; plant/trim/leaf waste not hazardous unless solvent/pesticide-treated; made unusable before leaving the lab by the same ≥50% mix.

  • Sampling (product samples): no rule authorising free product samples to patients was found. Part 12 — Consumer and Social Rules

12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke-shop rules

  • Definition (§58-37a-3, index via the 2011 enrolled text + a 2025 case quotation): “any equipment, product, or material used, or intended for use, to plant, propagate, cultivate, grow, harvest, manufacture, compound, convert, produce, process, prepare, test, analyze, package, repackage, store, contain, conceal, inject, ingest, inhale, or to otherwise introduce a controlled substance into the human body” — including metal/wooden/acrylic/glass/stone/plastic/ceramic pipes, roach clips, etc. Determination factors (§58-37a-4) include statements, prior convictions, proximity to controlled substances, residue, instructions, advertising, manner of display, ratio of sales, legitimate uses in the community, expert testimony. Intent/design/advertising can make an otherwise lawful object paraphernalia; a glass pipe in a tobacco shop is not automatically paraphernalia without intent evidence (secondary legal summary — Verify case law). Consistent with Run 1 §1.3.
  • Penalties (§58-37a-5, secondary summaries consistent): use/possession with intent to use = Class B misdemeanour, up to 6 months / $1,000; delivery / possession with intent to deliver / manufacture with intent to deliver = Class A misdemeanour, up to 1 year / $2,500; advertising paraphernalia for sale = Class B misdemeanour; delivery by a person ≥18 to a person <18 = third-degree felony, up to 5 years / $5,000. Sources: Brown Bradshaw (index); Southern Utah Defense (index).
  • Medical cannabis devices are distinct: a “medical cannabis device” (vaporizer warming without flame, etc.) obtained via a pharmacy for a cardholder's medicinal use is not treated as illicit paraphernalia for that lawful use — Verify the device definition + exemption language in §§26B-4-201 / 4-41a-102 before advising a retailer; devices that heat by flame (including dab nails heated by blowtorch) are prohibited for medical use in secondary pharmacy summaries.
  • Smoke shops / retail tobacco specialty businesses: tobacco / e-cigarette / nicotine products may not knowingly/intentionally be sold/given to a person <21 (§76-10-114, index — first violation infraction ≤$1,000 or compensatory service; subsequent Class C misdemeanour ≤$2,000); face-to-face sale required, minors not allowed in a tobacco specialty shop (§76-10-105.1, index). A smoke shop selling hemp/cannabinoid products needs, in addition, the UDAF retail permit + (from 6 May 2026) the Specialized Product Retailer licence + Tax Commission cannabinoid licence — see §7.2. No Utah “head shop licence” as a standalone cannabis licence exists.

12.2 Minimum age for hemp-derived products

  • Any THC / THC-analog cannabinoid product: 21+. HB 227 + §4-41-105(2)(a)(iv) underage-purchase investigation provision (§77-39-101(2)(a)(iii): a peace officer may direct a person <21 to attempt purchase of “a cannabinoid product that contains THC or a THC analog” — §77-39-101 via Justia, index). Full-spectrum CBD with even a trace of THC is therefore 21+ (secondary summaries consistent) — matching Run 1.
  • THC-free (0% THC) CBD / hemp products: no 21+ age gate in the excerpts found for a truly THC-free product — Verify (retailers commonly apply 21+ or 18+ store policies; store policy is not law). Tobacco/e-cigarette age is 21 regardless (above).
  • Medical cannabis patients: 21+ generally; 18–20 and minors only via the Compassionate Use Board (Run 1 + §4-41a-112). Non-resident visitor cards: adult visitor rules — minor visitor eligibility Verify (not found).

12.3 Impaired driving law and testing — per se metabolite rule + patient defence Conflict flagged

Statute §41-6a-517, Driving with any measurable controlled substance in the body (index via Brown Bradshaw / LegalClarity summaries of current text — the statute text itself was not opened line-by-line in Run 2, so treat the summaries as summaries and Verify the enrolled text):

  • Offence: operating / actual physical control of a vehicle with any measurable amount of a controlled substance or its metabolite in the body. No proof of impairment is required for this offence (as distinct from impairment DUI under §41-6a-502).
  • Inactive-metabolite exception — §41-6a-517(2)(b): “this charge cannot be brought if the only substance present in your body is 11-nor-9-carboxy-tetrahydrocannabinol (the primary inactive metabolite of marijuana).” Old THC-COOH alone does not support a metabolite charge; active THC / other metabolites can.
  • Affirmative defences — §41-6a-517(3): (a) involuntarily ingested; (b) legally prescribed by a practitioner for the accused's use; (c) cannabis or a cannabis product in a medicinal dosage form ingested in accordance with Utah's medical cannabis laws; (d) otherwise legally ingested. A defence is raised at trial — it does not prevent arrest/charge, and it does not defeat an impairment DUI if the driver was actually impaired.
  • Testing: blood test (not breath) in drug-DUI investigations; DRE (Drug Recognition Expert) 12-step protocol testimony admissible but challengeable; implied-consent refusal consequences (secondary summaries differ on 120-day vs 18-month figures — Verify §41-6a-520 / Driver License Division rules).
  • Penalties (secondary summaries — Verify against the Uniform Fine Schedule): a first metabolite DUI is described as a Class B misdemeanour, up to 6 months / $1,000, 120-day licence suspension in one summary; another summary cites 18-month revocation for refusal. Do not quote a single penalty figure without checking the current fine schedule + §41-6a-517 penalty subsection.
  • Conflict: Cardon Stuart (updated 20 Oct 2026, index) states “Utah's medical cannabis program does not provide a defense to metabolite DUI.” That contradicts the §41-6a-517(3) defence summaries from Brown Bradshaw and LegalClarity and the statute structure. Resolution: the statute summaries prevail here; Cardon Stuart's statement is flagged as incorrect/incomplete on this point (it may reflect the position that a card does not prevent a charge, which is true, but it overstates it as “no defense”). Verify enrolled §41-6a-517(3) before advising a patient.
  • Practical consequence for interstate travellers: lawful use in Nevada/Colorado days earlier can leave active THC/metabolites detectable in Utah; the inactive-metabolite exception + medicinal-dosage defence help only if (a) only inactive metabolite remains, or (b) use was in a Utah medicinal dosage form per Utah law — out-of-state recreational use satisfies neither.

12.4 Expungement and record clearing for marijuana offences

No cannabis-specific automatic expungement programme was found. Relief is via Utah's general expungement / Clean Slate system, Title 77, Ch. 40a:

  • Definitions (§77-40a-101, le.utah.gov PDF, index): “Automatic expungement” = expungement without a petition; “Clean slate eligible case” = eligible under §77-40a-205; “Drug possession offense” (§77-40a-101(15)) = §58-37-8(2) possession except (i) ≥100 lbs marijuana, (ii) correctional-facility enhancement, (iii) pre-May 2022 DUI-with-controlled-substance causing serious injury/death; plus §58-37a-5(1) paraphernalia use/possession, §58-37b-6 imitation substance, and substantially similar local ordinances. “Expunge” = remove from public inspection by sealing / restricting access (not destruction).
  • Petition route: BCI certificate of eligibility (§77-40a-303) → petition in the court where proceedings occurred; the court may expunge a Class A misdemeanour / felony drug possession 5 years after sentence completion (National Reentry Resource Center summary citing former §77-40-105(3)(c)(iii), index — Verify current §77-40a-303 waiting periods, which were recodified); hearing if an objection is filed; after expungement the person may respond to inquiries as though the arrest/conviction did not occur.
  • Automatic (Clean Slate) route — HB 431 (2019), signed 28 Mar 2019, Gov. Herbert: agencies, not the individual, identify and clear eligible cases; no petition/fee for the individual. Eligibility (Crime and Justice Institute summary, index): correct number/type of offences under existing law + crime-free for 5 years (Class C misdemeanour), 6 years (Class B), 7 years (Class A drug possession only). All felonies and certain misdemeanours are ineligible for automatic expungement, including sex, violent, DUI and person-on-person offences (CJI). Source: Crime and Justice Institute (index).
  • Pause/resumption (secondary brief — Verify with Utah Courts): automatic identification/clearing paused Oct 2024–Dec 2025, resumed 1 Jan 2026 (Drug Policy Watch Utah brief 2026, index). Unpaid restitution / court debt and conviction-count limits exclude cases (same brief).
  • Marijuana-specific petition path (secondary Herald expungement guide, Sept 2026, index): Utah is said to provide a marijuana-specific path for pre-programme medical-purpose possession if the person had a qualifying condition + eligible amount — that summary is truncated in the excerpt and was not verified against a statute section — Verify/Unavailable; do not advise on it without the statute citation.

12.5 Consumption rules

Public view ban (§26B-4-216(2), eff. 6 May 2026, le.utah.gov PDF, index):

“(b) Except as described in Subsection (2)(c), a medical cannabis patient cardholder, a provisional patient cardholder, or a nonresident patient may not use, in public view, medical cannabis or a cannabis product. (c) In the event of an emergency medical condition [§31A-1-301 definition], an individual… may use, and the holder of a… guardian card or… caregiver card may administer…, in public view, cannabis in a medicinal dosage form… (d) [Violation:] for a first offense: (A) guilty of an infraction; and (B) subject to a fine of up to $100; and (ii) for a second or subsequent offense: (A) guilty of a class B misdemeanor; and (B) subject to a fine of $1,000.”

  • “Public view” is broader than “public place” (LegalClarity visitor guide, index): a hotel balcony or other place where use can be seen may count; the only exception is a genuine emergency medical condition.
  • Possession mechanics (§26B-4-216(1)): the cardholder must carry the card at all times + a pharmacy label linking the product to the ICS; possess only up to the legal dosage limit (Run 1: 113 g flower / 20 g THC other forms per 28 days — note the statute says “legal dosage limit” per provider dosing guidelines up to that ceiling; secondary summaries differ on 28 vs 30 days — Run 1's 28-day figure is retained; Verify the §26B-4-201 “legal dosage limit” definition); only in the container received from the pharmacy; may not alter/remove the §4-41a-602 label. Possession in violation: infraction $100; over limit up to 2× limit: first infraction ≤$100, second+ Class B $1,000.
  • Smoking/combustion banned; vaporisation allowed (Run 1, confirmed by multiple secondary summaries + §26B-4-216 smoking references; first smoking offence by a cardholder described as an infraction ≤$100 in the Safe Access summary — Verify the §26B-4-216 smoking penalty subsection). Dabbing by flame-heated nail is prohibited in pharmacy summaries.
  • Forms: gelatinous cubes / lozenges (cube/rectangular only for edibles), tablets, capsules, concentrated oil, liquid suspension, resin/wax, topicals, transdermal patches, sublingual preparations, unprocessed flower in child-resistant containers for vaporisation (MPP modes table, index, secondary). Chocolate/cookies/brownies/candies as conventional edibles are not permitted forms — consistent with Run 1.
  • Federal land / schools / government buildings: use on federal land is a federal offence (see §10.5); use in public view at/near schools is doubly barred (public-view ban + community-location buffers for facilities, not a patient-use buffer — do not invent a patient distance rule). Workplace: no employer must permit on-duty use/impairment (see §12.11).

A LegalClarity patient-protection summary citing §26B-4-214/215 for housing is misleading on its face (§26B-4-214 in current code is the caregiver-card section; housing protections, if any, are elsewhere — Verify).

  • Federally assisted / public housing: cannabis remains federally prohibited; a housing authority may initiate eviction for manufacture/possession in federally assisted housing regardless of a state card (HUD guidance summary in a LegalClarity home-grow article, index — secondary, Verify the HUD notice).

12.8 Gun ownership conflicts for patients

  • Utah state law (secondary summaries — Verify enrolled text): §58-37-3.8 (cited in one secondary source as §68-37-3.8; the le.utah.gov text is Title 58, Ch. 37, §3.8, renumbered 6 May 2026 — check the post-renumbering number before citing) (cited by UtahStateCannabis): “a law enforcement officer may not use local or state resources to enforce a law that limits a person's right to own a firearm solely for possessing or using marijuana in line with state cannabis laws.” §76-10-528 exception (cited by a secondary blog): state-compliant medical cardholders are exempt from the state charge of carrying a dangerous weapon while under the influence of drugs. Utah does not strip firearm rights for becoming a patient under state law; permitless concealed carry for 21+ is not conditioned on patient status in the summaries. Caveat: state law does not authorise handling a firearm while impaired as a safety matter, and impairment can defeat a self-defence reasonableness claim — practical advice, not a statute.
  • Federal law — the conflict: 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3): unlawful for an “unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance” to possess firearms/ammunition. ATF (2011 open letter to FFLs, repeatedly reaffirmed in summaries): a person who uses cannabis “regardless of whether his or her state has passed legislation authorizing [cannabis] use for medicinal purposes, is an unlawful user… and is prohibited by Federal law from possessing firearms or ammunition.” ATF Form 4473 asks: “Are you an unlawful user of, or addicted to, marijuana…?” with the warning: “The use or possession of marijuana remains unlawful under Federal law, regardless of whether it has been legalized or decriminalized for medicinal or recreational purposes in the state where you reside.” Answering yes = denial; lying = federal felony (up to 10 years / $250,000 in summaries). Non-medical possession + firearm can also trigger Utah “restricted person” status (§76-10-503 pattern in a secondary law-firm summary — Verify).
  • 2026 developments — UNRESOLVED, secondary only (Leafwell, index, updated Oct 2026): Jan 2026 ATF narrowed “unlawful user” to require evidence of regular unlawful use; 28 Apr 2026 state-licensed medical moved to Schedule III but a state card is still not a federal prescription; June 2026 United States v. Hemani (Supreme Court, unanimous, narrow) held prosecuting a man who used marijuana a few times a week for gun possession was inconsistent with the Second Amendment; an ATF proposed revised Form 4473 is not final. None of these makes it “clearly legal” for a medical cardholder to own a gun (Leafwell's own conclusion). Treat federal gun eligibility for Utah patients as Verify/unresolved in 2026 — do not advise a patient they are federally safe. Schedule III alone does not resolve §922(g)(3), because “unlawful user” status turns on whether use is unlawful under federal law, not solely on schedule — a state card is not a federal prescription.

12.10 Medical cannabis in schools

No statewide school-administration statute found

12.11 Employment drug testing (cross-reference)
