Wyoming Cannabis & Hemp — Audit and Audited Final Report

Last audited: 1 November 2026. Research date 1 November 2026. The 3 November 2026 general election had not taken place on this date, so no 2026 certified result is reported anywhere in this file. The federal baseline in the user's instruction was used as given and was not re-audited; only the report's statements of it, and Wyoming's responses to it, were checked.

How the audit was done. Primary sources only for law: wyoleg.gov statutes and bill pages, Wyoming Administrative Rules (Chapter 61), the Department of Agriculture, USDA AMS, the Department of Revenue rate chart, the Attorney General, the Secretary of State, Wyoming and federal court records, and official tribal/USDA lists. Where a consolidated statute text could only be opened in a republication (Justia/FindLaw) consistent with wyoleg amendment history, that is noted in the row. News, law-firm blogs, trade press and AI summaries were never used to confirm a legal claim; anything resting only on them is marked Unverified. Verdicts: Confirmed = primary source agrees; Corrected = primary source says something different (corrected wording given); Removed = wrong with no correct version, or a claimed source that does not exist; Unverified = no primary source found.

Audit totals: 94 rows. By first verdict word: {'Confirmed': 52, 'Corrected': 26, 'Unverified': 15, 'Removed': 1}. The headline corrections are: HB0187 died (the §1011(d) rule came from HB0171); SF0055 is SEA 65 / Chapter 149, not Chapter 65; HB0108 is HEA 66 / Chapter 99, age 18; paraphernalia requires delivery or intent to deliver; the school-zone enhancement is §1036, not §1038, and §1038 does not apply to §1031(c); the §1039 penalty is ≤6 months/$750 (90 days/$100 was pre-2004); Cheyenne and Casper are 5%, Laramie 6%, Jackson/Teton 7% and the resort districts 9% on the DOR chart effective 1 October 2026; the 2018 WYSAC 'no jail' figure is 69%, not 29%; Chapter 61 corrective action is §61-14, not §61-13; and the AG's Schedule III Final Decision is officially listed (its PDF details remain Verify).

PART 1 — LAWS

1.1 Legal status

Recreational: illegal. Medical (comprehensive): illegal / none. CBD-only (narrow, historical): repealed as a registry; hemp-derived CBD within the hemp definition is lawful. Overall classification: ILLEGAL for marijuana, with lawful hemp (≤0.3% THC, no synthetic substance) as the only cannabis-plant route. No out-of-state medical card is recognised — there is no statute creating recognition, and W.S. 35-7-1031(c) bars practitioner prescribing/dispensing of marihuana/THC except for an FDA-approved drug. A Colorado or Montana purchase does not create a Wyoming defence.

Wyoming follows federal scheduling only through the objection procedure. W.S. 35-7-1011(d) (verbatim, from the directly cited text):

“If any substance is designated, rescheduled, or deleted as a controlled substance under federal law and notice thereof is given to the commissioner, the commissioner shall similarly control the substance under this act in the same manner as federal law within thirty (30) days after receiving notice of the change but not later than thirty (30) days after the first publication of the change in the Federal Register” — through an emergency rule followed by a permanent rule. “If the commissioner objects to the designation, rescheduling or deletion of a substance, the commissioner shall within the same period required to control the substance publish the reasons for objection and afford all interested parties an opportunity to be heard” and publish a final decision, which is final unless altered by statute; on publication of an objection, control is stayed until that final decision.

Source: W.S. 35-7-1011, accessed 1 Nov 2026. The 30-day conforming language in its current form was enacted by 2019 HB0171, HEA 110, Ch. 173, §2 (effective immediately), not by HB0187: 2019 HB0171 enrolled text. 2019 HB0187 (“Controlled substance schedules—federal uniformity”) died on 4 February 2019 (“H Did Not Consider in COW”) and never became law: HB0187 digest. The post-HB0171 §1011(d) also provides that control is through an emergency rule followed by a permanent rule, and that on publication of an objection control is stayed until the Commissioner publishes a final decision, which is final unless altered by statute. 1.4 CBD — what the law allows now

  • Route: hemp-derived CBD that meets the Chapter 51 hemp definition. No registration card, no qualifying-condition list, no in-state dispensary system, no patient possession limit stated in statute for compliant hemp products. The 2015 conditions (intractable epilepsy, neurologist certification, ≥5% CBD, $150 card) are historical only — repealed 2019.
  • THC limit: ≤0.3% THC on a dry-weight basis using post-decarboxylation or another similarly reliable method, where “THC” now means delta-9 THC plus psychoactive analogues and psychoactive structural/optical/geometric isomers (see §1.5). A CBD product that is >0.3% on that expanded measure, or that contains a synthetic substance, is not “hemp”.
  • Who may possess: “the possession, purchase, sale, transportation and use of hemp and hemp products by any person is allowable except as provided in W.S. 11-51-103(f) and 14-3-310” — W.S. 11-51-102(b), as amended by SF0032 (the pre-2024 wording “without restriction” must not be quoted as current law). The 14-3-310 exception is the under-18 edible/vaping rule.
  • Out-of-state medical cards: not recognised. No statute was found creating recognition, and the practitioner bar in §1031(c) points the other way.
  • FDA-approved drugs: dronabinol and other FDA-approved cannabinoid drugs are the exception to the practitioner bar and are not “marijuana” for Wyoming patient purposes in the same way — Verify their current Wyoming scheduling (Schedule II/III listings in §§1016/1018) before advising a patient.

None for retail hemp/CBD. Wyoming has no Metrc-style seed-to-sale system for hemp or cannabis, because it has no cannabis market. 1.8 Employment and drug-testing protections

No cannabis employment protection. Wyoming has no medical or adult-use programme to protect, no statute barring an employer from acting on a marijuana-positive test, and no general private-employer drug-testing statute. Secondary employment guides (Baker Donelson, Sept 2025; US Drug Test Centers) agree: employers have broad latitude under a consistent written policy; federal DOT testing (49 CFR Part 40) applies unchanged to safety-sensitive workers. PART 3 — LOCAL RULES

3.1 Authority and pre-emption

Wyoming cities and towns have general police and zoning powers under Title 15, and counties have land-use powers under Title 18, but no local government can legalise marijuana: possession, manufacture and delivery remain state crimes under Title 35, Chapter 7 regardless of a local ordinance, and there is no state cannabis-licensing scheme for a locality to opt into or out of. The “local opt-out” framework found in some secondary guides is therefore misleading for Wyoming — it describes a hypothetical future market, not current law. Conversely, a locality can regulate the time, place and manner of lawful businesses (including hemp/CBD and smoke/vape shops) through general business licensing, zoning and nuisance/smoking rules, and can enact a local ordinance mirroring or supplementing state drug offences within its municipal-court jurisdiction (e.g. the historical Casper ordinance cited in old college materials). Whether a specific local hemp/CBD restriction would be pre-empted by W.S. 11-51-102(b) (“allowable except as provided in 11-51-103(f) and 14-3-310”) has not been litigated in a source located in Run 1 — Verify for a specific ordinance. That sentence is the strongest pre-emption argument for compliant hemp products, but it is a Chapter 51 provision, not an express local-pre-emption clause.

PART 7 — HEMP LICENCES IN DETAIL

Scope note. This part expands Part 4, it does not change it. Wyoming issues one hemp licence type (producer and/or processor, activity stated on the application). There is no separate producer-only licence, processor-only licence, retail hemp licence, or cannabis licence. All Chapter 61 citations below are to 010-61 Wyo. Code R. Ch. 61, effective 23 May 2024 (ref. 010.0005.61.05232024), unless a different date is stated.

7.1 Who must be licensed, and who need not be

  • Must be licensed: any person who produces or processes hemp in Wyoming, at a licensed area stated on the licence (W.S. 11-51-102(a), 11-51-103). A licence may authorise more than one location for the same licensee (Part 4).
  • Need not be licensed: a person who possesses hemp/hemp products only to test THC levels (W.S. 11-51-103(e) as compiled, Part 4); a retailer who only sells compliant hemp products (W.S. 11-51-102(b)); a person who only possesses, purchases, sells, transports or uses compliant hemp products (same paragraph). General business licensing and sales-tax registration still apply to retailers.
  • Research: W.S. 11-51-105(c) permits the Department, the University of Wyoming and Wyoming community colleges to produce/process hemp for research purposes — see Part 11 §11.7. W.S. 11-51-105(b) also permits the Department to enter agreements with tribal governments on hemp production/processing. The research route is not a commercial licence shortcut.

7.2 Application — step by step (Chapter 61, §61-3)

“Each applicant for a license shall submit a signed, complete, accurate, and legible application. In addition to submitting the Department's application form, an applicant must submit the following: (i) A copy of the applicant's government issued photo identification or, if a business entity, a Wyoming Secretary of State certificate of good standing. (ii) An aerial map or photograph (e.g., from Farm Service Agency, Google, or Bing) of all the Licensed Areas on which the applicant plans to grow hemp, showing the boundaries and dimensions of each Lot in acres or square feet. (iii) An aerial map or photograph … of all the Licensed Areas on which the applicant plans to process hemp, showing the boundaries and dimensions of each licensed facility in acres or square feet. (iv) An official Criminal History Report for all Key Participants of the applicant dated within 60 days prior to the application submission date.” — §61-3(a)

Source: Cornell LII, §61-3 (directly opened 1 Nov 2026). In addition, the Part 4 application contents (name/address, physical address + legal description of all land/property, no controlled-substance felony statement, Department access authorisation, Wyoming residency/organisation verification) apply from W.S. 11-51-103. Note: the 2020 §61-3(a) also listed a processor product description and an intended end-use statement; those two items do not appear in the 2024 §61-3(a)(i)–(iv) text and must not be carried as current §61-3 requirements without re-checking the current application form. Fee first: “The Department will not begin reviewing an application until it receives the fee specified in W.S. § 11-51-103(c)(ii)” — §61-3(c). Fee: $750/year, $500 nonprofit/educational.

Key Participant (Chapter 61, §61-2(n)): “a sole proprietor, a partner in a partnership, a member of a limited liability company, a director of a corporation, or a person with executive managerial control in a corporation. A person with executive managerial control includes persons such as a chief executive officer, chief operating officer, chief financial officer, principal, officer, member, manager, and director. This definition does not include non-executive managers such as farm, field, or shift managers.” Source: Cornell LII, §61-2. Only Key Participants need the 60-day criminal-history report; farm/field/shift managers do not, under that definition.

7.3 Term, renewal, amendment, transfer

  • Term: “A license shall be valid for the remainder of the current calendar year in which the application was approved. All licenses will expire on December 31 of each year.” — §61-3(g). A licence bought in, say, October is therefore not a 12-month licence; it expires 31 December of that year.
  • Renewal: Chapter 61 §61-3 does not state a separate renewal procedure in the text opened; renewal is by a fresh annual application/fee for the next calendar year. Whether the Department treats a renewal as a new application for criminal-history-report purposes (new 60-day report each year) was not confirmed from a Department instruction page in Run 2 — Verify with WDA (agrhemp@wyo.gov) before a renewal deadline.
  • Amendment: “To amend a license, including changing a Licensed Area or activity, a licensee must submit the Department's amendment request form and receive the Department's approval.” — §61-3(e). Contact-information changes must be reported within 10 days on the amendment form, by email, mail, contract carrier or fax — §61-3(f).
  • Transfer: “Licenses cannot be assigned or transferred.” — §61-3(d). A sale of a hemp business does not carry the licence; the buyer must apply in its own name. This is a common trap in business-sale listings — see Part 8.
  • Denial: Chapter 61 §61-15 (current 2024 numbering; in the 2020 rules this was §61-13) denies an application if the applicant made a materially false statement, failed to submit the fee, failed to submit a correct/complete application, or if any applicant/Key Participant was convicted of or pled nolo contendere to a state or federal controlled-substance felony within the past 10 years — with an exception for a person lawfully growing hemp under the federal Agricultural Act of 2014 before 20 December 2018 whose conviction/plea also occurred before that date. Source: WDA Chapter 61 PDF (2024 rules). §61-15 numbering is the current (2024) numbering; the 2020 numbering for denial was §61-13.

7.4 Pre-harvest process — harvest reports, sampling, testing

  • Acceptable Hemp THC Level (§61-2): the laboratory reports total delta-9 THC on a dry-weight basis with measurement of uncertainty; a lot is compliant when applying the measurement of uncertainty produces a range that includes 0.3% or less. This is a measurement-uncertainty rule, not a flat 0.3% line on the reported number alone.
  • Harvest report (§61-4, 2024 rules): submit no more than 30 days but not less than 15 days before the anticipated harvest, with the estimated harvest date and the location of each lot. The superseded 2020 version said simply 15 days — use the 2024 15-to-30-day window.
  • Sampling (§61-5): the Department schedules a Sampling Agent; each lot must be sampled before harvest; the licensee must not harvest any portion of a lot before sampling; notify the Department immediately of a harvest-date change of more than 5 days (additional sampling may be required); all licensees are subject to inspection/sampling.
  • Retest: if a licensee believes an Official Test Result is in error, the Department will retest the sample originally collected if the licensee requests retest within 5 days of notification, pays all retest costs, and payment is received within 15 days of the request (Chapter 61, §61-5(g)(i)–(iii)).
  • Records: licensees must keep specified records (including acquisition of Cannabis plants) for at least 3 years, available for inspection — processor records §61-8(c) and producer records §61-4(b) each say “at least three years”; §61-8(b) also requires processor intake/disposal/processing records kept and updated monthly.

7.5 Processing rules (§61-7)

Each batch gets a unique identifier at processing; THC extracted from hemp must be destroyed under a Department-approved plan in accordance with 21 CFR Part 1317, accounted for by batch number; a processor must have a Department-approved plan for disposing of batches exceeding the Acceptable Hemp THC Level; a processor may accept hemp only from a person licensed by the Department, USDA, or a state/tribe with a USDA-approved plan; if Cannabis is found at a processing licensed area without records proving such a source, it (and any commingled Cannabis) must be disposed of under 21 CFR Part 1317; a licensee may not process/handle Cannabis that is not hemp, and may process/handle hemp only on/in licensed areas. Source: Cornell LII, §61-7.

7.6 Non-compliance, remediation, destruction (§61-6; W.S. 11-51-104)

  • If the Official Test Result exceeds the Acceptable Hemp THC Level, all Cannabis/products in the lot/batch/unit must be disposed of unless remediation is permitted; non-compliant lots cannot be further handled except as allowed; the licensee bears disposal cost and USDA is notified.
  • Remediation methods: (i) removing and destroying flower material while retaining stalk, stems, leaf material and seeds; or (ii) shredding/blending the entire plant into a biomass-like material. Remediated material must be re-sampled and tested. Deadline: within 60 days of Department notification or by the end of the current calendar year, whichever is first.
  • Destruction methods: plowing/tilling/disking into soil; mulching/composting/chopping/bush mowing into green manure; burning; burying under at least 12 inches of soil. The licensee must provide proof of destruction unless a Department employee observes it. Only persons authorised by 21 CFR Part 1317 may move/transport Cannabis subject to disposal.
  • Lot harvested before sampling must be disposed of. No violation is issued for a lot over the level where the Department determines resampling was required because the Department collected the sample incorrectly or it was damaged in transport to the Certified Laboratory — but the lot must still be disposed of.
  • Source: Cornell LII, §61-6; WDA Chapter 61 PDF.

7.7 Violations and corrective action plans (§61-13/§61-14; W.S. 11-51-104(b)–(e))

  • The Department will initiate a corrective action plan or revoke the licence of any licensee who violates the Hemp Statute or Chapter 61. (§61-13 Violations; §61-14 Corrective Action Plan; §61-15 License Denial, Revocation, and Appeals — 2024 numbering.)
  • A corrective action plan includes a reasonable date to correct the violation and a requirement to provide all requested compliance reports for 2 years following the violation (Chapter 61, §61-14 — not §61-13; §61-13 is Violations in the 2024 numbering). It may also include reporting requirements, additional inspections, suspension, steps to restore a licence, disposal requirements, or notice to known creditors (W.S. 11-51-104(b)).
  • Negligent violations: examples include failure to provide a legal description of land, failure to obtain a licence, and producing Cannabis over the THC level. A negligent violation is not subject to criminal enforcement by federal/state/local government, except for §61-14(f) “Culpable Mental State Greater Than Negligence” (intentionally/knowingly/willfully/recklessly), which must be reported to USDA, the U.S. Attorney General, the local prosecuting attorney and the Wyoming Attorney General, then a corrective plan or revocation follows.
  • Three strikes: a licensee or Key Participant who violates the Hemp Statute/rules three times in a 5-year period is ineligible for a licence for 5 years from the third violation; the Department shall revoke the licence (W.S. 11-51-104(d); §61-13(b) in the 2024 numbering — §61-13 is Violations).
  • Intentional violation: misdemeanour, fine ≤$750 and/or imprisonment ≤6 months (W.S. 11-51-104(c)).
  • Synthetic-substance violation: any person who violates Chapter 51 by producing, processing or selling hemp/hemp products containing any synthetic substance is ineligible for licensure (W.S. 11-51-104(e), as amended by SF0032) — this is in addition to the three-strikes rule and applies on a single synthetic violation, on the text as quoted in Part 1.

7.8 What a hemp licence does NOT authorise

A Wyoming hemp licence does not authorise: growing/processing marijuana; growing/processing hemp outside a licensed area; selling a product >0.3% THC on the expanded post-2024 measure; adding a synthetic substance; retailing under a “hemp licence” (no retail licence exists); or transferring the licence on a business sale. A licence also does not exempt the holder from general law — water, pesticide, food-safety, tax, employment and local zoning law still apply (see Part 11).

10.5 Federal land inside Wyoming — Yellowstone and Grand Teton

Possession of a controlled substance in a national park is prohibited by 36 CFR §2.35(b)(2), “unless such substance was obtained by the possessor directly, or pursuant to a valid prescription or order, from a practitioner acting in the course of professional practice or otherwise allowed by Federal or State law.” Adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I federally, so a Colorado/Montana purchase does not satisfy that exception inside Yellowstone or Grand Teton National Park. Secondary law-firm guidance (Puzzle Group; Teton Attorney) describes the park charge as a Class B misdemeanour (up to 6 months / $5,000) and notes collateral-forfeiture schedules (one Colorado-district schedule lists $100 for <1 oz marijuana possession and $150 for ≥1 oz, but that is a Colorado-district schedule, not the District of Wyoming schedule) — the District of Wyoming collateral amount for a park marijuana notice is Verify before quoting any dollar figure, and a paid notice's record consequences (firearms-form, immigration, background-check) are beyond this file's scope and need federal counsel. The same federal-law logic applies on other federal land (national forests, BLM land) under federal drug law, but the specific charging regulation differs by agency — Verify for a specific parcel. Regulation text: 36 CFR §2.35 — the prohibition is “unless such substance was obtained by the possessor directly, or pursuant to a valid prescription or order, from a practitioner acting in the course of professional practice or otherwise allowed by Federal or State law” (that final clause must not be dropped; Wyoming state law does not allow marijuana, so it does not save possession in Wyoming parks). The Class B maximum (up to 6 months / $5,000) is the statutory maximum under 36 CFR §1.3 / 18 U.S.C. §§1865, 3581(b)(7), 3571(b)(6), not a scheduled fine. Secondary park-practice sources: Puzzle Group (secondary); Teton Attorney (secondary).

12.1 Impaired driving — W.S. 31-5-233 (no per se THC limit)

Wyoming has no per se THC blood limit for driving. The drug prong is impairment-based. Verbatim, W.S. 31-5-233(b)(iii): “No person shall drive or have actual physical control of any vehicle within this state if the person: … (iii) To a degree which renders him incapable of safely driving: (A) Is under the influence of alcohol; (B) Is under the influence of a controlled substance; or (C) Is under the influence of a combination of any of the elements named in subparagraphs (A) and (B).” “Controlled substance” for this section includes any drug/substance defined by W.S. 35-7-1002(a)(iv), toxic vapours, and any drug/psychoactive substance capable of impairing faculties (§31-5-233(a)(ii)). The 0.08% and 0.05% figures in the statute are alcohol presumptions only (0.05% or less: presumed not under the influence of alcohol; 0.05–0.08%: no presumption, other evidence considered). Source: W.S. 31-5-233 (Justia, 2025 code).

Other DUI points. (i) Lawful use under Wyoming law is not a defence to a §31-5-233(b) charge: §31-5-233(d) — “The fact that any person … is or has been entitled to use the controlled substance under the laws of this state shall not constitute a defense against any charge under subsection (b)” (confirmed in the consolidated text). That point is largely moot in Wyoming because there is no lawful marijuana use to be “entitled” to, but it matters for FDA-approved drugs and for out-of-state medical use. (ii) Implied consent: W.S. 31-6-101–106, with the refusal penalty in W.S. 31-6-107 (not in the 101–106 range): a driver on a public street/highway is deemed to have consented to chemical testing (blood/breath/urine) incidental to a lawful arrest on probable cause. 12.3 Expungement and first-offender relief

  • Drug-specific first-offender — W.S. 35-7-1037: a person with no prior drug conviction who pleads/is found guilty of possession under 35-7-1031(c) (or 35-7-1033(a)(iii)(B), or use/under-influence under 35-7-1039) may, with their consent, have proceedings deferred and be placed on probation without entering a judgment of guilt; on successful completion the court discharges and dismisses; discharge/dismissal is not a conviction; only one such discharge per person. Source: consolidated §35-7-1037 text (republication consistent with wyoleg amendment history; a wyoleg consolidated-code page for this section was not fetched in this audit, so cite the wyoleg published statutes before advising a defendant). The discharge is without adjudication of guilt and is not a conviction, including for §1038 second-offence penalties, and only one discharge per person is allowed.
  • Non-conviction records — W.S. 7-13-1401: petition ≥180 days after arrest/dismissal, no filing fee; but records with a disposition under 35-7-1037 (or 7-13-301) are ineligible under §1401 (National Reentry Resource Center comparison, secondary). That ineligibility does not leave a §1037 discharge unrelieved — the discharge itself is not a conviction — but it does mean §1401 is not the route for a §1037 record.
  • Misdemeanour convictions — W.S. 7-13-1501; felony convictions — W.S. 7-13-1502: secondary template (ezel.ai) gives 5 years after sentence for a non-status misdemeanour offence / 1 year for a status offence, $100 filing fee (§1501); 10 years, $300 fee (§1502). Both fee/timing details are confirmed in the consolidated text and the Wyoming Judicial Branch expungement guidance: §1501 — $100 filing fee, ≥1 year after the end of sentence for a status offence, ≥5 years for a non-status offence, no gun involvement, not a substantial danger, only one expungement under the section, and deferred-adjudication-then-dismissed cases do not qualify under §1501; §1502 — $300 filing fee, ≥10 years after the end of sentence including probation, completion of any court-ordered programme and full restitution, no other felony plea/conviction, no gun used, not a substantial danger, listed violent/sexual/domestic/child/weapons and other felonies excluded, only one expungement under the section.
  • No marijuana-specific or automatic expungement law exists in Wyoming. The 2021 HB0209 automatic-expungement idea was tied to its proposed $50 civil penalty and died with the bill (Part 1 §1.9). There is no “clean slate” marijuana expungement in current law.

FirearmsFederal law prohibits firearm possession by an “unlawful user of or addicted to any controlled substance” (18 U.S.C. §922(g)(3)); ATF Form 4473 asks about marijuana use in terms. The concealed-carry permit statute is W.S. 6-8-104 (not §6-8-102), whose eligibility cross-refers to ineligibility under 18 U.S.C. §922(g) or §6-8-102. W.S. 6-8-102 itself is the felony firearm-possession ban: a person who pleaded guilty to / was convicted of a violent felony or a felony under §6-5-204(b) and “has not been pardoned” who uses/knowingly possesses a firearm commits a felony (≤3 years and/or ≤$5,000). Wyoming's Firearms Freedom Act (W.S. 6-8-404(a)) states that a personal firearm/accessory/ammunition manufactured in Wyoming and remaining exclusively within Wyoming “is not subject to federal law, federal taxation or federal regulation” — do not present that as an effective exemption from federal §922(g)(3) enforcement. Any restoration procedure beyond the §6-8-102 pardon wording is Verify (the 5-year non-violent-felony restoration detail in a secondary summary was not verified). Because Wyoming has no medical programme, there is no Wyoming medical-patient firearms carve-out to apply, and the Schedule III question for a future programme is Verify. This paragraph is not firearms legal advice.
Pets / veterinary CBDNo Wyoming veterinary cannabinoid rule was located (Board of Veterinary Medicine rule not opened — Verify). A compliant hemp CBD product (≤0.3% THC, no synthetic substance) is a lawful hemp product generally; marijuana/THC products are unlawful generally and veterinary guidance (secondary: AVMA/ASPCA summaries via pet-CBD guides) treats THC as toxic to dogs/cats. A veterinarian cannot prescribe marijuana in Wyoming (practitioner bar, §1031(c)) — that is the statute's consequence, not a veterinary-board rule found in Run 2.

Parents/guardians: CBD oil ≤0.3% THC to a parent/guardian for their child is excepted from the under-18 edible/vaping ban (14-3-310).
Public consumptionNo separate statewide public-consumption offence was located (Part 1 §1.3); public use is already unlawful possession/use, and local smoking-in-public-places codes (e.g. Cheyenne Ch. 8.64) may separately apply to smoking.

What Part 12 does not do. It does not create a Wyoming “patient rights” chapter, because Wyoming has no patients in a state programme: there are no hospital-use, organ-transplant, child-custody, or public-housing cannabis protections in Wyoming statute to list, and none are invented here. Secondary “patient access” pages that list such protections for Wyoming are describing other states' law or a hypothetical programme — Verify any such claim against Wyoming statute before repeating it.
