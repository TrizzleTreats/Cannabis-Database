Fixed federal baseline (supplied, not re-researched)

  • State-licensed medical marijuana and FDA-approved products moved to Schedule III effective 28 April 2026 (91 FR 22714). All adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. The broader 2024 rescheduling proposal is not final.
  • IRS §280E is generally lifted for Schedule III medical activity; adult-use remains fully subject to §280E.
  • Hemp: PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 November 2026, but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 December 2026. After 11 Dec 2026 the full total-THC hemp definition and the 0.4 mg total THC per-container cap apply.
  • USDA enforcement of DEA-registered lab testing for hemp is delayed to 31 December 2026. The federal medical-marijuana appropriations rider runs through 11 December 2026. There is no federal cannabis banking law.

The Illinois interaction — the point that matters most

Schedule III and §280E apply to medical activity only. Only state-licensed medical marijuana activity (and FDA-approved products) moved to Schedule III on 28 April 2026; all adult-use activity remains Schedule I and remains fully subject to §280E. Illinois licenses many businesses for both markets — and from 10 Sep 2026 an adult-use dispensary in good standing may also hold a medical licence at the same site (Part 4, §15-37) — so a dual-market business must split its activity: sales to registered medical patients under the state medical licence = Schedule III activity (federal §280E relief as supplied); sales to adult-use customers at the same premises, under the same roof = Schedule I activity (§280E fully applies). A single blended till cannot support the medical claim — the split must be documented by patient allotment, medical tax rate and separate licence. No source in Run 2 contradicts that split; secondary summaries that say “280E is gone in Illinois” without the medical-only qualifier are oversimplifications — flag them, do not rely on them.

At Illinois state level the distinction is largely moot for deductions, because Illinois decoupled from §280E for state tax for both markets from 1 Jan 2023 (Part 2) — a separate state rule that does not change the federal treatment above. Federal hemp dates vs Illinois: federally, PL 119-37 §781 takes effect 12 Nov 2026 but PL 119-103 §2019 limits it until 11 Dec 2026, after which the full total-THC definition and 0.4 mg/container cap apply. Illinois does not wait for 11 Dec 2026: its own Illinois Hemp Act takes effect 12 November 2026 and reclassifies products over 0.4 mg/container as dispensary-only cannabis on that date (Parts 1 §5 and 7). The new Act's §65 federal-conflict clause — if the Act conflicts with 7 U.S.C. 1639p as of 13 Nov 2026, the federal provision controls to the extent of the conflict — does not change that Illinois enforcement start date.

Part 1

Laws

1.1 Legal status — what is legal, for whom

  • Adult-use legal since 1 Jan 2020 under the Cannabis Regulation and Tax Act (CRTA), 410 ILCS 705, for persons 21+. CRTA §1-5 findings: “the use of cannabis should be legal for persons 21 years of age or older and should be taxed in a manner similar to alcohol” and regulated “in a manner similar to alcohol” — proof of age before purchase; sales to under-21s remain illegal; DUI remains illegal. ilga.gov CRTA full text — index/open.
  • Medical legal since 2014 under the Compassionate Use of Medical Cannabis Program Act, 410 ILCS 130, administered by IDPH (registry) + IDFPR (medical dispensaries, now including §15-37 opt-ins) + IDOA (medical cultivation). Patients 18+ (minors with caregiver) may use with a qualifying condition and certification.
  • Residents vs non-residents — adult-use: both may purchase from a licensed Illinois dispensary (no Illinois-residency requirement to buy adult-use); the difference is the possession limit (residents double non-residents) and the sales split (Part 6: out-of-state buyers ~19–21% of sales). Non-residents may not lawfully take cannabis out of Illinois — interstate transport remains federally illegal and neighbouring-state law applies on arrival; Illinois law does not authorise export.
  • Gifting: CRTA permits transfer without remuneration within possession limits between adults 21+. Verify the exact gifting clause (410 ILCS 705/10-5/10-25) before relying on it for a commercial “gift” model — “gifting” as a sale substitute is not authorised.
  • Public use: prohibited — see §1.3. Private residence is the reliably lawful place; landlords, hotels, employers and private businesses may prohibit use on their property.
  • What is NOT legal: adult-use home grow (medical patients only); delivery to consumers (no state delivery licence); on-site consumption except where a local government authorises it; sale of cannabis/hemp >0.4 mg outside licensed dispensaries from 12 Nov 2026; possession above limits; use in a vehicle, school, public place, or near under-21s as listed in §1.3.

1.6 Packaging, labelling, advertising, delivery, zoning & buffers

  • Packaging/labelling (CRTA Art. 55, esp. §55-21): sealed, odour-proof, child-resistant, tamper-evident, opaque packaging; universal cannabis symbol; health-warning statement; net weight, serving size, total THC/CBD; lab-testing results; Metrc UID tag/RFID; no packaging/marketing designed to appeal to minors; no misleading marketing or packaging mimicking consumer brands/children's products. Product registration with IDOA before packaging use (secondary — Verify).
  • Advertising: “Advertise” is defined in CRTA §1-10 as promotional activities including newspaper, radio, Internet/electronic media, television, fliers, billboards and window/interior signs — excluding exterior signage displaying only the licensed business name. SB 3222 adds bans on misleading marketing and packaging that “rips off consumer brands to appeal to children”. Verify Art. 55 advertising restrictions on ilga.gov — not captured in Run 1.
  • Delivery: no home delivery to consumers is authorised under CRTA. Transporter licences are business-to-business only. Curbside/drive-through pickup at the dispensary is authorised by SB 3222 (medical priority) — that is pickup, not delivery.
  • Zoning / buffers — state minima: only two statewide setbacks in secondary summaries: (1) dispensaries ≥1,500 ft from another dispensary (property line to property line); (2) craft growers ≥1,500 ft from other craft growers and cultivation centres. Municipalities may impose buffers only within 410 ILCS 705/55-25(4) — they may not regulate zoning, time-place-manner-number or on-site consumption more restrictively than the State, while 55-25(5) allows ordinances to prohibit or significantly limit a business establishment’s location; additional buffers as such are not an unqualified state power — see Part 3. Old medical distance rules (1,000 ft / 2,500 ft) were amended in 2019 to eliminate distance requirements for medical dispensing organisations registered on/after 1 July 2019 (municipal summary — Verify).
  • On-site consumption: local governments may authorise on-site consumption establishments — CRTA creates no state lounge licence, and consumption on dispensary premises is otherwise prohibited. Whether any Illinois municipality has actually authorised a lounge was not confirmed in Run 1.

1.8 Employment & drug-testing protections

  • Employers may adopt and enforce reasonable zero-tolerance or drug-free workplace policies and policies on drug testing, smoking, consumption, storage or use in the workplace or while on call; may discipline/terminate for violating them. “On-call” requires at least 24 hours' notice (secondary summary — Verify). Policies must be applied in a nondiscriminatory manner.
  • An employer may consider an employee impaired if it has a good-faith belief the employee manifests specific, articulable symptoms while working that lessen performance, and must give the employee a reasonable opportunity to contest that determination.
  • §10-50(e)(1) no-cause-of-action clause (quoted in §1.2): no cause of action for actions under a reasonable workplace drug policy, including reasonable and nondiscriminatory random drug testing and discipline/termination/withdrawal of an offer for failing a drug test. A federal district court has read this to authorise zero-tolerance policies not limited to in-workplace conduct (secondary case summary — Verify citation).
  • Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act (820 ILCS 55): CRTA amended it so “legal products” includes state-lawful cannabis and “off duty” includes non-on-call hours. The practical tension is resolved toward employer policies that are reasonable and nondiscriminatory, with impairment (not mere metabolite presence) as the safer basis for for-cause action. This is not a general “cannot be fired for off-duty use” protection for adult-use cannabis in Illinois — do not state it as one.
  • Medical patients: secondary summaries state employers generally cannot fire/discipline solely for being a registered medical patient, but the CRTA text found in Run 1 does not contain a standalone medical-patient employment-protection clause — Verify on 410 ILCS 130 and §10-50. Federal contractors, CDL holders and safety-sensitive/federally regulated positions remain subject to federal rules regardless.
  • Cannabis-industry workers: SB 3222 “removes certain barriers for individuals seeking to work at dispensaries” and ties most agent badges to the person, not the dispensary — a worker changing dispensaries need not re-badge. Verify on IDFPR guidance.

Local rules

3.1 Authority — who does what

  • Municipalities and counties (for unincorporated areas) may, under CRTA Art. 55 (§55-25 and related provisions, via municipal summaries): (1) adopt reasonable zoning ordinances that do not conflict with CRTA and do not unreasonably prohibit home cultivation/use; (2) adopt ordinances governing time, place, manner and number of cannabis businesses (caps, hours, conditional/special-use permits, buffers); (3) regulate consumption consistently with CRTA; (4) prohibit cannabis businesses entirely (opt-out) by ordinance; (5) impose a local cannabis retailers' occupation tax (Part 2); (6) set local civil penalties (not exceeding CRTA penalties for the underlying conduct, per county guidance).
  • What they may NOT do: ban personal possession or personal use that CRTA authorises. “Local governments cannot ban individual possession of recreational cannabis but they can, however, ban cannabis retail and cultivation within their borders.” A possession ban would conflict with CRTA's statewide-concern finding (§1-5(c)). Verify §55-25 text for exact preemption language.
  • Counties: authority runs in unincorporated areas; inside a municipality, municipal zoning/tax rules govern (county cannabis tax inside municipalities capped at 3% vs 3.75% unincorporated).
  • Townships: no township cannabis-business licensing/zoning authority was located. Townships generally do not zone in Illinois. Unavailable/Verify — if a specific township rule is needed, check that township's code and the county zoning ordinance, and always give the county, because township names repeat across counties. No township-level prohibition is asserted in this report.
  • Medical vs adult-use asymmetry: under the original Compassionate Use Act, municipalities could not prohibit lawful medical cannabis businesses, though they could restrict them to certain zoning districts. Under CRTA, municipalities may prohibit adult-use businesses. Whether a municipality that opted out of adult-use can be compelled to accept a §15-37 medical opt-in at an existing site: no source in Run 1 answers this.
  • Referendums: CRTA does not, on the sources found, require a referendum to opt out or in — it is by ordinance. Some municipalities held advisory referendums/surveys first (e.g. Woodridge, 2019) — advisory, not the legal act.

11.1 Testing

Statute: 410 ILCS 705/50-5. Rule: 8 Ill. Adm. Code 1300.700 (IDOA). Regulator: IDOA (not IDFPR) approves laboratories; IDOA may also select random samples to verify label information.

“Immediately prior to manufacturing or natural processing of any cannabis or cannabis-infused product or packaging cannabis for sale to a dispensary, each batch shall be made available at the cultivation center, craft grower, or infuser, for an employee of an approved laboratory to select a random sample, which shall be tested by the approved laboratory for: 1) Microbiological contaminants; 2) Mycotoxins; 3) Pesticide, active ingredients; 4) Residual, solvent; 5) Heavy, metals; and, 6) An, active ingredient analysis.” — 8 Ill. Adm. Code 1300.700(a), via ilga.gov JCAR (index)

The statute at 410 ILCS 705/50-5(c) lists the same tests except heavy metals, which is added by rule.

  • Sampling: the sample is selected by an employee of the approved laboratory (not licensee self-sampling) at the cultivation centre / craft grower / infuser premises; the laboratory holds chain of custody. Representative sample: minimum 7 g, not required to be more than 15 g per batch, consisting of different parts of the batch. Cured flower batches: maximum 20 lbs. A representative sample must be taken from each final batch and must pass all testing requirements before being offered for sale. The laboratory must immediately return or dispose of cannabis on completion of testing/use/research, in compliance with Subpart I. Laboratories must file electronic copies of passing results to IDOA and the cultivation centre at the same time and keep records of all test results for at least 5 years (secondary summary of the rule — Verify on the 1300 text).
  • Hemp sampling (contrast): under the old hemp programme a sampling agent collects each lot within 30 days before reported harvest; growers may not sample their own crop; no harvest before sampling (secondary summary). New Hemp Act sampling/testing rules are by rule — Verify.
  • Failed tests: pesticide failure — the entire batch is recalled as provided by rule. Other failures (microbiological, mycotoxin, heavy metals, solvent residue): the batch may be used to make a CO2-based or solvent-based extract; after processing, the extract must still pass all required tests (410 ILCS 705/50-5(f); 8 Ill. Adm. Code 1300.700(g)). Cultivation centres/craft growers/infusers must send a 7-day notice to IDOA before destroying any cannabis product.
  • Mandatory recalls issued: ZERO. IDFPR Annual Report 2025: “0 product recalls issued in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025. A voluntary-recall list, if any, was not located — Verify on IDFPR/IDOA recall notices.

11.5 Waste disposal

Rules: 8 Ill. Adm. Code 1000.460 (cultivation centres), 8 Ill. Adm. Code 1300.840 (craft growers/infusers, added 44 Ill. Reg. 10364, eff. 3 June 2020), 68 Ill. Adm. Code 1291.325 (dispensaries).

“The allowable method to render cannabis plant waste unusable is by grinding and incorporating the cannabis plant waste with other ground materials so the resulting mixture is at least 50% non-cannabis waste by volume. Other methods … must be approved by the Department before implementation.”

Compostable mixed waste (compost / anaerobic digester / jurisdictional health-department-approved facility): food waste, yard waste, vegetable-based grease/oils, or other Department-approved wastes (agricultural material, biodegradable products/paper, clean wood, fruits/vegetables, plant matter). Non-compostable mixed waste (landfill / incinerator / health-department-approved facility): paper, cardboard, plastic, soil, or other Department-approved wastes (non-recyclable plastic, broken glass, leather). Procedure: cannabis waste may not be sold; all waste/unusable product is weighed, recorded and entered into the inventory/tracking system before mixing/disposal; verification by a supervisor, conducted in an area with video surveillance; cultivation centres must give IDOA and ISP at least 7 days' notice through the traceability system before rendering product unusable/disposing of it (1000.460(e)); dispensaries destroy on the same day/time weekly unless otherwise approved, with the day/time notified at initial inspection and changes communicated ≥3 business days before implementation (1291.325(b)). Liquid waste: per the Illinois Environmental Protection Act and 35 Ill. Adm. Code. Hazardous/chemical waste: per federal/state/local law. Hemp waste / hot hemp: disposal of plant matter exceeding lawful THC levels under the new Hemp Act is by Department rule including a retest option for minor violations (Part 7.3) — Verify the rule.

11.7 Sampling, giveaways, discounts, promotions

  • Sampling (consumer): dispensary licensees may not open a packaged flower, make it a sample, and/or allow customers to look at it — 410 ILCS 705/15-80 (all tampered cannabis must be destroyed); all cannabis must be stored/dispensed from the restricted-access area during operational hours, accessible only to agents (IDFPR FAQ document — index; note that FAQ also states drive-throughs were prohibited under §15-70(p)(7) — that drive-through prohibition was changed by SB 3222; do not cite the old FAQ on drive-throughs).
  • Giveaways / promotions (410 ILCS 705/55-20(d)): “No cannabis business establishment nor any other person or entity may encourage the sale of cannabis or cannabis products by giving away cannabis or cannabis products, by conducting games or competitions related to the consumption of cannabis or cannabis products, or by providing promotional materials or activities of a manner or type that would be appealing to children.” The official FAQ confirms: “Using samples or free products to promote sales is not allowed by the CRTA (410 ILCS 705/55-20(d)).”
  • Gifting (non-commercial, consumer-to-consumer): allowed only if purely non-commercial, recipient 21+ (or an authorised medical patient), within possession limits, not in public, and not used to promote sales. Medical home-grow patients may NOT gift cannabis/products they grew, and may not give/sell seeds to non-patients (410 ILCS 705/10-5, per the official FAQ gifting §5). Bundling: dispensaries are prohibited from selling cannabis products bundled together for a single price, and from selling alcohol (except tinctures ≤100 ml), tobacco/nicotine products, clones/live plant material (secondary dispensary guide — Verify on 68 Ill. Adm. Code 1291).
  • Discounts: price discounts / loyalty pricing / medical-patient discounts are not prohibited by §55-20(d) as quoted — the ban is on giving away product and games/competitions. Dispensary discounting to medical patients who show a card (without using the official medical system) is reported by CROO as occurring but is not the same as a medical-system purchase (no medical tax rate, no allotment credit). Verify any specific discount programme on IDFPR guidance; do not advise that a discount = a medical sale.
  • Advertising (410 ILCS 705/55-20, secondary summary): no false/misleading statements, no promotion/depiction of overconsumption, no depiction of actual consumption, no health/medicinal/therapeutic claims, no cannabis leaf/bud image, no images/characters/cartoons/toys/animals/phrases appealing to under-21s, no candy/food-packaging imitation; no advertising within 1,000 ft of school grounds, playgrounds, recreation centres, child-care centres, public parks, public libraries or game arcades not restricted to 21+; no advertising on/in public transit vehicles/shelters or publicly owned/operated property; mandatory health/legal warnings. 12.1 Paraphernalia and smoke shops

Drug Paraphernalia Control Act, 720 ILCS 600: “drug paraphernalia” means equipment/products/materials “peculiar to and marketed for use” in planting/propagating/cultivating/growing/harvesting/manufacturing/compounding/converting/producing/processing/preparing/testing/analysing/packaging/repackaging/storing/containing/concealing/injecting/ingesting/inhaling or otherwise introducing cannabis or a controlled substance into the body in violation of the Cannabis Control Act / Controlled Substances Act. Unlawful use/possession (720 ILCS 600/3.5(a)): knowingly using or possessing with intent to use paraphernalia for those purposes — a Class A misdemeanour in the pre-2019 text cited in case law (People v. Easley, People v. Bostic).

CRTA amendment — cannabis paraphernalia excluded: the Illinois Municipal League FAQ (2019) states: “The criminal penalty for possession of drug paraphernalia was amended to exclude items historically and customarily used for injecting, ingesting or inhaling of cannabis. (720 ILCS 600/3.5 and 4(b)).” Practical meaning (with caution): ordinary cannabis consumption devices (pipes, bongs, grinders, vaporizers) used for lawful adult-use/medical cannabis are not treated as criminal paraphernalia in the way the pre-2019 case law treated them; devices still used for unlawful cannabis (e.g. under-21 use, unlicensed manufacture) or for other controlled substances remain within the Act. Verify the current 720 ILCS 600/2(d), 3.5 and 4(b) text on ilga.gov before advising a smoke shop — secondary criminal-defence pages that quote the pre-amendment definition without the cannabis exclusion are stale.

12.2 THC driving limits and impairment testing

  • Per se limit (625 ILCS 5/11-501(a)(7) + 11-501.2(a)(6)): a person shall not drive or be in actual physical control of a vehicle while having, within 2 hours of driving / being in actual physical control, a THC concentration of 5 nanograms or more of delta-9 THC per millilitre of whole blood, or 10 nanograms or more per millilitre of another bodily substance. At ≥5 ng/ml whole blood, impairment is presumed (11-501.2(b-5)); below 5 ng/ml there is no presumption either way but the result may be considered with other evidence. (ISP Forensic Science Commission statement quoting (a)(7) verbatim — index; secondary legal summaries. Last amendment to §11-501 cited in one summary: P.A. 102-982, eff. 1 July 2023 — terminology “accident” → “crash”, did not alter the cannabis provisions — Verify on ilga.gov.) This resolves Run 1's Verify flag on the 5 ng/ml / 10 ng/ml thresholds: the figures are as stated here, subject to the medical-patient exception below.
  • Impairment (non-per-se) DUI: separately, 625 ILCS 5/11-501(a)(4) prohibits driving under the influence of any drug/combination “to a degree that renders the person incapable of safely driving”, and (a)(5) covers combined alcohol + drugs — no blood number is required; officer observations, driving, statements and roadside tests may suffice.
  • Medical-patient exception to per se (not to impairment DUI): registered medical cannabis patients are exempt from the per se limit — they may only be charged if actual impairment is shown; the State may not rely solely on a THC blood/urine result for a patient (secondary legal summaries). Verify on 625 ILCS 5/11-501.2(b-5) — the exception's exact wording matters (some summaries phrase it as “not subject to the per se presumption” rather than a full exemption).
  • Sample timing: the whole-blood / bodily-substance sample must be collected within 2 hours of driving / actual physical control (ISP statement). Chemical analysis must be performed per ISP standards (11-501.2(a)(1)); the defendant/attorney is entitled on request to “full information concerning the test or tests” (11-501.2(a)(4)).
  • Penalties (secondary — Verify on 625 ILCS 5/11-501 / 730 ILCS 5/5-4.5-55 before quoting): first cannabis DUI = Class A misdemeanour, up to 364 days' jail, fines up to $2,500, minimum 1-year revocation of driving privileges; second offence adds a mandatory minimum 5 days' jail or 240 hours' community service; prior convictions / serious-injury crash may elevate to a felony.
  • Vehicle possession/consumption: cannabis may not be used in a motor vehicle (driver or passengers); transport only in a secured, sealed, odour-proof, child-resistant container in original packaging (official FAQ, opened; 625 ILCS 5/11-502.15, Class A misdemeanour for non-compliant transport). SB 3222 clarified that lawfully purchased cannabis in such a container does not have to be inaccessible in the vehicle (secondary summary — Verify on enrolled text).

The DUI Cannabis Task Force was created by P.A. 101-27 to study driving under the influence of cannabis. No task-force final impairment standard (e.g. an oral-fluid threshold) has been enacted as a per se rule in the sources located — Unavailable / Verify. 12.6 Landlord and tenant rules

410 ILCS 130/40 (medical-patient anti-discrimination, now in the permanent medical Act — Verify the current section number on ilga.gov; the quoted text carries a “scheduled to be repealed on July 1, 2020” note in the old pilot Act): “No school, employer, or landlord may refuse to enroll or lease to, or otherwise penalize, a person solely for his or her status as a registered qualifying patient or a registered designated caregiver, unless failing to do so would put the school, employer, or landlord in violation of federal law or unless failing to do so would cause it to lose a monetary or licensing-related benefit under federal law or rules. This does not prevent a landlord from prohibiting the smoking of cannabis on the premises.”

Adult-use landlord rules (410 ILCS 705/10-30): (b) “No landlord may be penalized or denied any benefit under State law for leasing to a person who uses cannabis under this Act.” (c) Nothing requires a property possessor to allow a guest/client/lessee/customer/visitor to use cannabis on/in the property, including state-owned/managed land. (a) Cannabinoid metabolites, paraphernalia possession, or lawful cannabis conduct by a parent/guardian/foster parent shall not form the sole/primary/supporting basis for child-welfare, adoption, foster-parent, guardianship, conservatorship, trusteeship, will or estate actions, unless the person's cannabis actions created an unreasonable danger to a minor's safety or incompetence is shown by clear and convincing evidence (P.A. 101-27, eff. 6-25-19; 101-593, eff. 12-4-19).

Practical synthesis (Illinois Legal Aid, secondary but consistent with statute): private landlords can ban smoking cannabis in units (including for medical patients) and can restrict other forms (e.g. vaporising) if explicitly stated in the lease; tenants may still use medical cannabis in other forms (edibles, tinctures) if not violating lease terms; breach of a lease cannabis rule can lead to eviction; federally subsidised public housing: cannabis (including medical, all forms) is prohibited — use/possession can lead to eviction or denial, including for guests and past use/charges; the federal smoking ban also applies; condominium association rules can limit smoking but cannot universally ban all forms for a medical cardholder; rules can prohibit use in public/common areas; landlords may impose lease rules stricter than association rules; cannabis may not be possessed/used in a residence used as licensed childcare/foster care/social-service care. Verify lease-specific advice on the current lease + 765 ILCS / local ordinance. Employment overlay: the same “solely for status” protection applies to employers, subject to the federal-law / federal-benefit exception and to drug-free workplace policies (410 ILCS 705/10-50).

12.9 Medical cannabis in schools — Ashley's Law

  • Original Ashley's Law (P.A. 100-0660, HB 4870, signed 2018, eff. 1 Aug 2018): named for Ashley Surin, a student who sued to use medical cannabis for epilepsy at school. Requires all schools — public, charter and nonpublic — to authorise a parent/guardian (registered designated caregiver) to administer a cannabis-infused product (not smokable/vapable) to a registered minor patient on school property or on a school bus, provided both parent and child are cleared under the medical cannabis law. School staff were not required/permitted to administer under the original law; the caregiver must come to school, administer, and remove the product afterwards; the school may restrict time/place/manner if disruptive or exposing others; the school may not discipline or deny enrolment solely because the student needs medical cannabis during the school day; both patient and caregiver registry cards must be current. School Code citation: 105 ILCS 5/22-33 (“Ashley's Law”).
  • Expansion (P.A. 101-0370, SB 455, signed 12 Aug 2019, eff. 1 Jan 2020): school administrators and school nurses (in district, public, charter and nonpublic schools) may administer medical cannabis infused products to eligible students on school property during the school day, during school-sponsored activities and before normal school activities (including riding a school bus or attending district childcare); eligible students may self-administer under supervision of an administrator/nurse; the product may be stored on school grounds in the same manner as other medications (securely, direct access only by school personnel).
  • Limits that remain: CRTA generally prohibits possession/use on school grounds (official FAQ: possession prohibited on school grounds / school bus unless registered as a cannabis patient or caregiver). Ashley's Law is the exception for registered minor patients + infused products only. Smoking/vaping on school grounds is not authorised. No school-use statistics (how many students, how many administrations) were located — Unavailable. Verify the current 105 ILCS 5/22-33 text on ilga.gov — the 2018/2019 summaries above are secondary; the statute is the authority.

12.10 Employment drug-testing overlay (cross-reference to §11.8)

CRTA §10-50: employers may adopt reasonable zero-tolerance / drug-free workplace policies (drug testing, smoking, consumption, storage, use in the workplace or while on call) applied non-discriminatorily; nothing requires an employer to permit use on the job or while on call; there is no private right of action against an employer for reasonable testing, for discipline based on a good-faith belief of workplace/on-call use/impairment, or for third-party injury where the employer neither knew nor had reason to know of impairment.
