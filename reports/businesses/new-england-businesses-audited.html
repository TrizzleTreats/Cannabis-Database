State-licensed operators (growers, manufacturers, dispensaries, labs, caregivers) are excluded by design; they come from official licence data. National firms appear only where a New England office was verified, and are marked National.

How to read this directory

  • “Not published” means the field was not published in the source(s) cited for that entry — it was not guessed or left blank.
  • [Verify] flags a legal name, licence number, base or status that needs confirmation on the firm’s own site or a state register before you rely on it.
  • No duplicates: a business serving several states is listed once, under its home or local-office state; every state it serves is shown in its States served field. Two pointer rows — CohnReznick in Category 1 and One Call Cannabis in Category 3 — are cross-references to that business’s single full entry in its primary category (Category 2 and Category 6 respectively), not second listings. Run 2 adds three more cross-reference rows: Integrated Security Group / Category 6 firms in Category 7, the Category 1 law firms in Category 10, and One Call Cannabis in Category 11. Vermont Grow Barn (Category 11, grow supplies) and Vermont Hempicurean (Category 12, CBD retail) share an owner and a Brattleboro complex but are distinct services in different units, each listed once and cross-noted; their licensed sister dispensary, Vermont Bud Barn, is excluded throughout. Massachusetts is the regional hub — check States served before concluding a state is unserved.
  • Evidence: each entry links the cannabis practice page, association listing or project report that evidences its cannabis work, plus a primary source link and the date it was accessed (2026-10-23 or 2026-10-24).
  • Contact details are only those the business or person publishes publicly for business purposes.
  • Social media links were asked for where published, but very few sources in this run published a social URL in the text retrieved (Vicente LLP’s LinkedIn is cited as its evidence source). Social links are therefore omitted rather than guessed in both runs, and should be harvested from firm footers in a follow-up pass.
  • Count note: Run 1’s summary cites 31 law and 11 insurance entries; the Run 1 tables as delivered contain 30 and 10 business rows. Run 2 adds 37 deduplicated business listings (3 transport, 3 waste, 1 staffing, 5 compliance, 12 marketing/grow, 13 hemp/CBD) plus 14 note rows. Page counts here count business listings only (125 combined) — cross-reference pointers, documented gaps, closed/dormant rows and excluded-national notes are shown in place but not counted as listings.
  • Run 2 sourcing: every Category 7–12 entry was sourced from web-index material read 2026-10-23 / 2026-10-24 (its access date is marked “index”) and was not re-verified in a live browser — re-check commercially important entries before use.

Gaps by state and category

  • Maine: Run 1 law and accounting strong and local; insurance essentially Allen Insurance & Financial plus nationals; security essentially project-named firms plus nationals. Run 2: Category 7 gap (no dedicated transporter — licensees self-transport under OCP manifest rules); Category 9 gap; Categories 8, 10, 11, 12 covered — ME is Run 2’s second-strongest state.
  • New Hampshire (thinnest state overall): therapeutic cannabis only (4 ATC licences, no adult-use), which structurally suppresses the ancillary market. Run 1 verified only Sheehan Phinney (law), AAFCPAs / Puff Puff (accounting, out-of-state based), Appletree (insurance, genuine Windham NH office), Jewett / Vantage / Interstate (construction, regional) and American Global (security, licensed, no NH address found). Run 2: Categories 7, 8, 9, 10 all gaps; Category 11 one grow store (Natural Roots, Nashua); Category 12 one maker (Ridge Hemp, Littleton). NH hemp is USDA-administered, so no state hemp list exists to mine.
  • Vermont: Run 1 law is the exception (Vermont Cannabis Solutions, Gravel & Shea); no VT-based CPA firm, real estate broker, GC or guard firm with a dedicated cannabis page was verified. Run 2: Categories 7, 8, 9 gaps; Category 10 gap for non-law consultants (law firms fill it); Categories 11–12 comparatively strong (CannaPlanners; Sunsoil, Luce Farm, Sunset Lake CBD, Vermont Hempicurean) — VT punches above its size in hemp brands.
  • Massachusetts (the regional hub in both runs): most multi-state entries are MA-based. Run 2 strongest state in every category except Category 9 (still only 1 verified firm + 1 dormant). Category 8 has no verified MA-headquartered cannabis-only hauler — a real gap in the largest NE market.
  • Connecticut: Run 1 strong law (Shipman, Pullman, Carmody) and a genuine CT Social Equity accounting reviewer (CohnReznick, Hartford), ISG security, Fortunato / VALERE build-out — but no CT-based commercial broker marketing cannabis-zoned property. Run 2: Categories 7, 9 gaps; Category 10 gap for non-law consultants; Categories 8 (BioServ), 11 (Harvest Moon — thin evidence) and 12 (New England Hemp Farm) have a single entry each.
  • Rhode Island: Run 1 law, accounting and security are real (SecurityRI — Licence #1; One Call). No RI-based cannabis real estate broker or GC was verified. Run 2: Categories 7, 8, 9, 10 all gaps for in-state firms; Category 11 two thin-evidence grow entries; Category 12 one strong entry (Lovewell). RI is Run 2’s thinnest state — consistent with a small, late-opening retail market.

Thin categories overall — and why

Categories ranked by thinness across both runs: Category 9 Staffing (1 active entry in 6 states — thinnest in the whole project) > Category 7 Transport (3, and 2 are national generalists) > Category 8 Waste (3) > Category 4 Real Estate (10 in Run 1) > Category 3 Insurance (11 in Run 1). Categories 1 (Law) and 12 (Hemp/CBD, 13) are the deepest.

  • Staffing: cannabis recruiting is dominated by national online platforms (Vangst, Viridian, HempStaff, Marijuana Jobs Cannabis Careers) with no NE offices. Local firms either closed/dormant (THC Staffing) or are very small and web-invisible (Cannabis Staffing Group — no own website found).
  • Transport: in ME/VT/CT, regulation makes transport a licensee activity, not an ancillary market; in MA only a handful of third-party transporter licences exist (Plymouth Armor states it was the second granted; CCC listing shows MT281667). Cash transport is supplied by national armoured carriers whose cannabis acceptance is stated nationally, not branch-by-branch.
  • Waste: most operators render waste on-site (a regulatory requirement) and then use ordinary haulers/composters, so “cannabis waste companies” as a distinct local trade barely exists in New England outside Maine Labpack and BioServ’s cannabis service line.
  • The single largest structural finding of Run 2: most nationally advertised cannabis transport, waste, staffing and compliance brands serving New England have no New England office and therefore fail the scope rule.

Most productive sources

  • Both runs: firms’ own cannabis practice/service pages (Run 1: Foley Hoag, Verrill, Shipman, Pullman, AAFCPAs, PATCO, ISG, SecurityRI, One Call, Appletree, Allen; Run 2: Plymouth Armor, Maine Labpack, Complynt Canna, CannaPlanners, Cannabis Creative, Lovewell, Sunsoil, Luce Farm, Healing Rose).
  • Massachusetts CCC public documents — uniquely good for verifying MA licences, addresses and consultant correspondence (Plymouth Armor MT281667; Blue Skies Unlimited’s Northampton address/email/phone).
  • NEREJ / Bisnow project reporting for construction (named GC, electrical, HVAC, project addresses).
  • 420Property professional directory for real estate (the only source giving a broker licence number — Cannavision).
  • State programme/regulatory material for gap-proofing (CT Social Equity Council — CohnReznick; VT CCB insurance FAQs — PurpleRisk; ME OCP and CT DCP transport rules; MassDEP organics-site list).
  • Trade press (Cannabis Business Times for ERG Strategies; MJBizDaily for Blue Skies).
  • Justia/Avvo cannabis directories — useful for solo/small-firm lawyers and CPAs, but entries from those directories are flagged and should be re-confirmed on the firm’s own site.

Least productive sources

  • NECANN exhibitor material — no usable current exhibitor directory was retrieved (a 2019 exhibitor manual and 2026/2027 event pages were all that surfaced).
  • NCIA member directory in Run 2 — one useful hit (Brink’s/PAI), but the address/phone on the profile belong to the member’s payment partner, requiring care.
  • State hemp programme lists (ME DACF, VT CCB, MA MDAR, CT DOAG, RI CCC) — no public, searchable licensee list from any of them was retrieved in Run 2, which is why Category 12 licence numbers are mostly “Not published”.
  • Generic “hydroponics near X” searches — heavy contamination with Portland, Oregon and national chains; every Category 11 grow entry needed state disambiguation.
  • Generic “state cannabis” directory sites (mainecannabis.org and sister sites) — they list national CPA/insurance names with no local address, phone or principal.
